# Page Audit — https://learn.modernagecoders.com/

Skill: `superseo:page-audit` · Run 2026-09-03 · Competitors fetched and read end to end.

## Content Identity

The homepage is a **catalogue-hub landing page wearing an editorial skin**. It is engineered to do
three jobs at once: rank for category head terms, act as the internal-link distribution hub for ~760
pages, and convert a demo booking. Stage: solution-aware to product-aware. Intent: commercial
investigation with a transactional CTA.

**The mismatch:** it is structured as a hub (20 H2s, 40+ H3s, ~8,500 words, 200+ internal links) but
written as a brochure. Every claim on it is self-reported. Nothing on the page is verifiable by a
third party except the Google review link. That is the whole finding of this audit.

## Competitive Position

Primary keyword: **online coding and maths classes** (from title, H1 context, meta description and
the ItemList schema). Verified live: the homepage ranks top-10 for it and **is already cited by name
in the AI answer**, correctly, with the 6-to-67 positioning.

Top 3 read in full:

| | Codingal | 98thPercentile | Codeyoung |
|---|---|---|---|
| Words | ~8,500 | ~3,200 | ~3,800 |
| Angle | Accredited, YC-backed scale | "Seven technologies, one habit" | Bloom's 2-Sigma, 1:1 |
| Third-party proof | STEM.org, Trustpilot 4.6, Y Combinator, IIT/BITS/HP/Times partners | IKCC placements, FIRST Robotics, NASA OSTEM, 1,518 reviews across 3 platforms | STEM accredited (credential.net), USA Today, Forbes India, LiveMint, GSV Cup Top 25 |
| Founder credentials | IIT Roorkee, HackerEarth co-founder | Curriculum Lead named | less than 1% mentor acceptance |
| Press | YourStory, Indian Express, The Print, Mint, Business Standard | — | USA Today, Forbes, Business Wire |

**The #1 thing competitors do better:** every one of them proves its claims with a source that is not
itself. Codingal does not say "we are good"; it says *Y Combinator, STEM.org, IIT Roorkee and the
Times of India think so*. That is the currency both Google's E-E-A-T raters and every LLM run on.
Language models cite entities that **other documents corroborate**. This site is described accurately
by exactly one document: itself.

**The #1 gap to exploit:** every competitor stops at 18. Codingal is grades 1-12. 98thPercentile is
grades 3-8. Codeyoung is kids. BYJU's FutureSchool is 6-18. WhiteHat Jr is 6-18.

Modern Age Coders teaches **6 to 67, and coding *plus* maths, in one place**. There is no competitor
in this SERP for "the same school taught my 8-year-old and my 41-year-old." That is a category of one,
and the homepage currently buries it in a title-tag suffix.

## Detailed findings

### 1. Information Gain & Originality — 4/10 (Red)

**Works:** "Our Certificates are earned, never given" is a genuine position. The student projects
(FreshTrack food-expiry tracker, Baby Website, Word Counter) are real artefacts no competitor can
copy. The coding-plus-maths thesis is a real differentiator.

**Doesn't:** Nothing on the page could only have been written by this school. The three student
projects are named but not linked to a working demo, a build story, or the student's age and starting
point. "Why Coding + Maths Together?" asserts the thesis and never proves it with a case.

**Recommendation (non-obvious):** the single highest information-gain asset available and currently
unused is the **age span itself**. Nobody in this market has taught the same curriculum to a 6-year-old
and a 67-year-old. Publish what that actually taught you: which concepts collapse by age, where adults
are slower than children and where they are faster, what a 60-year-old beginner gets wrong that a
10-year-old never does. That is a document that cannot be fabricated, cannot be copied, and gets cited.

### 2. Semantic Depth — 7/10 (Yellow)

**Works:** entity coverage is genuinely broad. Scratch, Python, Java, C++, web dev, AI/ML, DSA, data
science, Olympiad maths, CBSE/ICSE, IP and CS Class 11-12 are all named. Predicates are mostly right
(build, ship, debug, master, compete).

**Missing nodes that all three competitors have:**
- **Instructor-to-student ratio as a named number.** 98thPercentile leads with 1:4. The page says
  "Group Classes / Mini Batch / Personalized 1-on-1" without the ratios in prose. `brand-facts.json`
  holds 5-8, 3-4, 1:1. Put the digits on the page.
- **Class length in minutes.** Competitors state 50 minutes. The page says "one full hour of real
  teaching" in an H3 but never as an extractable fact next to the format.
- **Pedagogical authority anchor.** Codeyoung cites Bloom's 2-Sigma. Nothing here anchors the method
  to a named body of research.
- **Named competition or exam outcomes.** IKCC, FIRST, NASA OSTEM, USACO. The site has a USACO page
  but the homepage does not surface a single named result.
- **Accreditation predicate.** "accredited", "certified by", "recognised by" appear nowhere.

### 3. E-E-A-T — 4/10 (Red)

**Experience:** thin. No first-person teaching observation anywhere on the page. Parent quotes exist
but are testimonials, not experience signals from the school.

**Expertise:** the founder exists in schema (`Shivam Khemka`, Founder & Lead Mentor) but is invisible
in the rendered page. No instructor is named, credentialed, or photographed on the homepage. All three
competitors name people.

**Authoritativeness:** the site is its own only witness. One press mention exists in the wild
(`insidernewstimes.co.in`, Apr 2026) and is not linked from the site.

**Trustworthiness:** genuinely good and worth protecting. Real 4.9 across 547 Google reviews, a live
link to the actual Google review page, no fabricated AggregateRating on the homepage, a single
consistent set of numbers. That last one is rarer than it sounds and it is why the AI answer quoted
this site's figures back correctly.

### 4. Structure & Time-to-Value — 6/10 (Yellow)

**Works:** H1 lands the thesis in five words. "Why families abroad choose us" is above the curriculum,
which is the correct order for the international audience.

**Doesn't:** 20 H2s and 40+ H3s on one page. Six sections are variations of "why us" (Why Coding +
Maths Together, Why Choose Modern Age Coders, The Modern Age Coders Advantage, Coding and Maths are
for Everyone, Why Learn Coding & Mathematics, Start Your Coding Journey Today). An H2-only skim reads
as repetition, which is exactly how an extractive model skims.

Four H3s repeat verbatim: "Olympiad & Competition Math" appears four times, "Group Classes" / "Mini
Batch" / "Personalized 1-on-1" three times each. Duplicate headings degrade passage indexing because
each passage competes with its own siblings.

### 5. Technical On-Page — 7/10 (Yellow)

**Title tag — 80 characters, over the 60-char / 580px limit.** Google truncates after "for All Ages"
and drops "| Learn Worldwide" entirely. Group A element, so this is the highest-value single fix.

**Meta description — ~205 characters**, truncates mid-sentence at "Book a free dem…".

**Schema — two real defects:**

1. **Broken entity edge.** Both `Service` nodes at `index.html:255` and `index.html:277` declare an
   inline `provider` of `{"@type": "EducationalOrganization", "name": "Modern Age Coders"}` with **no
   `@id`**. That creates two orphan organization nodes that never join the main `#organization` node.
   A parser sees three organizations with the same name instead of one with two services. This is
   precisely the ambiguity that stops a knowledge-graph entity from consolidating.
   Fix: replace each inline provider with `{"@id": "https://learn.modernagecoders.com/#organization"}`.

2. **`sameAs` points at a person for an organization.** The org node's `sameAs` includes
   `linkedin.com/in/shivam-khemka-modern-age-coders`, a personal profile. An organization's `sameAs`
   should carry the **company** page. The personal URL belongs only on the `founder` Person node,
   where it already correctly is. Also absent: X/Twitter, Crunchbase, Trustpilot, and any Wikidata or
   Wikipedia anchor. `sameAs` breadth is the main lever a site controls over entity reconciliation.

**Good:** canonical present, `robots: index, follow`, `link rel="alternate" type="text/markdown"` for
AI agents (genuinely ahead of the field), FAQPage with 14 Q&A pairs, BreadcrumbList, WebSite with
SearchAction, no fabricated AggregateRating.

**Confirmed non-issue:** `www.modernagecoders.com` 301s to `learn.modernagecoders.com/courses`. The
stale `www` result still in the index will drop out on its own.

### 6. Engagement & Discoverability — 5/10 (Yellow)

OG tags present. `theme-color` matches the brand amber. Hero is typographic rather than a 1200px+
photographic image, which suits the editorial identity but costs Google Discover eligibility. No
quotable statistic formatted for extraction. The 6-to-67 span is the most tweetable fact on the site
and appears nowhere as a standalone pull quote.

### 7. Conversion — 7/10 (Green)

Value prop lands in under 5 seconds. "Book a Free Demo" and "Watch a Real Class" is a strong pairing:
one high-commitment, one low. Social proof sits near the CTA. Urgency is genuine, not fabricated.

Friction: 13 distinct CTA labels compete. "Book a Free Demo", "Get Free Consultation", "Call Me Back",
"Contact Us", "Choose Plan" and "Send Message" are five doors to one room.

## Semantic gap analysis — what to add

Entities, attributes and predicates present in the top 3 and absent here:

| Missing node | Competitor holding it | What to add |
|---|---|---|
| Batch ratio as digits | 98thPercentile (1:4) | "5-8 per group, 3-4 in a mini batch, or 1:1" in prose from `brand-facts.json` |
| Class duration as digits | all three (50 min) | "60-minute classes" next to the format, not in a decorative H3 |
| Named instructor + credential | all three | one named mentor with a real credential, above the fold |
| Third-party accreditation | Codingal, Codeyoung (STEM.org) | apply, or state plainly what you have instead and why |
| Named student outcome | 98thPercentile (IKCC, NASA) | one named student, one named competition, one verifiable result |
| Press logos | Codingal, Codeyoung | start with the one real mention that already exists |
| Pedagogical research anchor | Codeyoung (Bloom 2-Sigma) | anchor the coding-plus-maths thesis to named research |
| Adult learner entity | **nobody** | this is the unclaimed node. Own it. |

## Top 5 quick wins

1. **Title tag.** FROM `Modern Age Coders - Online Coding & Maths Classes for All Ages | Learn Worldwide` (80)
   TO `Online Coding & Maths Classes, Ages 6-67 | Modern Age Coders` (60). Leads with the keyword,
   puts the one uncopyable differentiator in the visible portion, keeps the brand.

2. **Meta description.** FROM 205 chars truncating mid-word TO
   `Live online coding and maths classes for ages 6 to 67. Small batches, real projects from class one. Rated 4.9 across 547 Google reviews.` (136).
   Every number in it is defensible and comes from `brand-facts.json`.

3. **Fix the two orphan `provider` nodes** (`index.html:255`, `:277`) to reference
   `#organization` by `@id`. Ten minutes, and it is the difference between one entity and three.

4. **Move the founder's personal LinkedIn out of the organization's `sameAs`** and add the company
   profiles instead.

5. **Put the ratio and duration digits into prose.** "60-minute live classes, 5-8 students per group."
   Both facts are already true and already in `brand-facts.json`; they are simply not extractable.

## Top 5 strategic improvements

1. **Claim the adult-learner category.** Every competitor stops at 18. Build the hub the site does not
   have: coding and maths for adults, 25-67, career changers and returners. Detailed in
   `08-topic-cluster-planning.md`.

2. **Publish the age-span dataset.** What six years of teaching 6-to-67 actually taught you about how
   learning changes with age. Unfabricatable, and the most linkable asset this brand owns.

3. **Build third-party corroboration** — the real constraint on both rankings and AI citation. See
   `10-linkbuilding.md`.

4. **Name the humans.** One named mentor with a verifiable credential on the homepage, and a real
   `/team` page, moves E-E-A-T more than any on-page tweak.

5. **Collapse the six "why us" sections into two.** Fewer, denser passages index better than many
   thin ones that repeat each other.
