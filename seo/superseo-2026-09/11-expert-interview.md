# Expert Interview — questions for the owner

Skill: `superseo:expert-interview` · Run 2026-09-03

## Why this file is questions and not answers

`expert-interview` is the one skill in the set with no research step. It exists to pull out things
only a person who has done the work knows, precisely because those things cannot be found on the web
or generated.

**So I have not answered them.** If I had, I would have manufactured exactly the class of claim that
this session spent its first hour removing from 11 pages: the "world's first" line that was
unverifiable, sat inside FAQ schema, and had to be deleted. Generating an adult student's story to
fill a content brief is the same mistake with better grammar.

Three deliverables are blocked on these answers and should not be written until they exist:
`06-content-brief.md` (E-E-A-T requirements), `09-write-content.md` (the `NEEDS-OWNER` sections), and
the Experience fixes in `02-eeat-audit-about.md`.

The questions are ordered by how much content they unlock. Answer 1, 4 and 7 first if time is short.

## The eight questions

### 1. What do most people get wrong about teaching an adult to code?

*Why it matters:* the entire adult SERP says the same thing, which is "age is no barrier, you can do
it." Every result. That consensus is a vacuum: whatever is actually true and slightly against the
grain is both more useful and far more citable. This answer becomes the opening argument of
`/coding-classes-for-adults` and probably the reason anyone links to it.

*Follow-up if the answer is short:* what do adults do in the first three weeks that children never do?

### 2. Across six years and 10,000+ students, what did teaching 6-year-olds and 60-year-olds the same material actually teach you?

*Why it matters:* this is the single most valuable unwritten asset the business owns. No competitor
can write it, because no competitor teaches this range. It supports a data-led PR pitch
(`10-linkbuilding.md`), an information-gain section on the homepage (`01-page-audit-homepage.md`), and
the adult hub.

Specifically: which concepts collapse by age and which do not? Where are adults slower than children,
and where are they faster? What does a 60-year-old beginner get wrong that a 10-year-old never does?

### 3. Name one adult student. What age, what were they doing before, what did they build, where are they now?

*Why it matters:* every testimonial on the site today is a parent of a child. There is not one adult
outcome anywhere, and the adult cluster cannot carry E-E-A-T without one. One real story with a real
number beats five generic quotes.

*Needed:* permission to publish, and the specifics. A first name and a city is enough.

### 4. How long does it actually take one of your adult beginners to build something that works?

*Why it matters:* `09-write-content.md` has a section reserved for this and it must not be filled with
an industry average. The honest number from your own batches is the differentiator, especially if it
is less flattering than the marketing average. It is also the highest-value featured-snippet target in
the adult cluster.

### 5. Who should NOT take these classes?

*Why it matters:* scope limits are the strongest trust signal available and almost nobody publishes
them. `/best-age-to-start-coding` already does this well with its "not yet" rows, which is why that
page is one of the best on the site. The adult hub needs the same move.

### 6. What surprised you about the adults who enrolled?

*Why it matters:* `08-keyword-deep-dive.md` assumes five adult personas (career changer, upskiller,
returner, parent learning alongside a child, later-life learner). That list is inferred from the SERP,
not from your enrolment data. If it is wrong, the cluster is aimed at the wrong people, and only you
can say.

### 7. Is Java genuinely more important for your Kolkata students because West Bengal is ICSE-heavy, or is that an argument that sounds better than it works?

*Why it matters:* I assembled this argument from three facts already on your Kolkata page and shipped
it into the new `#python-kolkata` section. The facts are each verified. **The causal claim joining
them is mine, not yours**, and you are the only person who can confirm it holds in practice. If it
does not, say so and I will cut it.

### 8. What is the real story behind "maths and coding taught together"?

*Why it matters:* the line "coding without maths is incomplete, and maths without coding is abstract"
is good, and it is currently asserted and never demonstrated. Codeyoung anchors its method to Bloom's
2-Sigma research. What is the moment that convinced you? A specific student, a specific topic where
the two clicked together.

This also replaces what the "world's first" claim was doing rhetorically. That claim was removed
because it was false; the thing it was gesturing at is real and needs evidence rather than a
superlative.

## How to use the answers

Reply in plain prose. Do not tidy them up, and do not write in marketing voice: the raw phrasing is
the point, and `write-content` is built to preserve it. Two or three genuinely detailed answers are
worth more than eight tidy ones.
