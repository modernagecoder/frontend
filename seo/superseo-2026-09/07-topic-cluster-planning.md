## The publishing order is inverted here, on purpose

The skill's default rule is: publish 3 to 4 spokes first, then the hub, so the hub does not launch as a
link-less orphan.

**That rule does not apply, because the spokes are already live and already linked.** Measured:

| Existing spoke | Inbound internal links |
|---|---|
| `/summer-coding-camp-adults` | **181** |
| `/ai-classes-for-adults` | 23 |
| `/java-classes-for-adults` | 22 |
| `/ai-and-machine-learning-for-working-professionals` | 22 |
| `/category/professionals` | 11 |
| `/online-maths-classes-for-adults-in-usa`, `-in-uk`, `/maths-classes-for-adults-uae` | live |
| `/winter-coding-camp-adults` | live |

Nine or more adult pages, indexed, internally linked, and pointing at **nothing**. There is no
`/coding-classes-for-adults`. The equity is spread across nine URLs and concentrates on none, which is
why the page currently surfacing for adult intent is the generic `/courses` catalogue.

**So: build the hub first.** It is the one move in this whole plan that costs one page and pays
immediately, because the links to point at it already exist.

## Hub

**URL:** `/coding-classes-for-adults`
**Format:** pillar page with chapter links. Not an ultimate guide, because the intent is commercial
investigation, not research.
**Target keyword:** coding classes for adults
**Length:** 2,200 to 2,800 words

### H2 structure

1. Who this is for (career changer, upskiller, returner, parent learning alongside a child, retiree)
2. What a live adult class actually looks like (formats, batch digits, 60-minute sessions)
3. Choosing your first language (Python, Java, JavaScript, and honestly when *not* to pick each)
4. The maths question, because it is the real blocker
5. Evening and weekend scheduling across time zones
6. How long it actually takes to get to a first working project
7. Adult learner paths, linking to every spoke
8. What it costs (region-switched, and **not** repeated in FAQ text where it leaks into schema)
9. FAQs

**What the hub must NOT do:** go deep on any single language or audience. Those are spokes. The hub's
job is breadth plus the link graph.

earns its keep through the rest of the cluster rather than through its own traffic.

## Internal link map

- **Every spoke → hub**, contextual, in the first body paragraph, anchor `coding classes for adults`
  or a close variant. The first link from a page carries the most weight, so it must be in the body
  prose, not in the footer mesh or a related-pages block.
- **Hub → every spoke**, from the "Adult learner paths" section.
- Sensible cross-links only: 6 ↔ 9 ↔ 12 (the age and career-change trio), 7 ↔ 8 (the beginner pair),
  8 ↔ 13 (the maths pair), 10 → kids pages (the parent-child bridge, which also connects this cluster
  to the site's main business).

Spoke 10 is worth calling out structurally: it is the only page in the plan that links the adult
cluster to the kids cluster, which is exactly the connection the 6-to-67 claim needs in the link graph
to be more than a slogan.

## External links from the hub

Cite three to five real authorities, none of them competitors:

- **US Bureau of Labor Statistics**, Occupational Outlook for software developers (the 17% growth
  through 2033 figure that the whole adult-career-change SERP quotes second-hand). Cite the primary.
- **Benjamin Bloom, "The 2 Sigma Problem" (1984)**, for the tutoring effect. Codeyoung leans on this
  and this site does not, despite running smaller batches.
- **MIT OpenCourseWare** or the **Raspberry Pi Foundation**, for the beginner curriculum position.
- **Andragogy / Malcolm Knowles** on adult learning, which would ground the pedagogy for a cohort the
  rest of the site's copy does not address.

## The honest risk

The adult SERP is a different and tougher competitive set than the kids SERPs where this site wins.
General Assembly, MIT xPRO and Coursera have authority this domain does not. The cluster is still worth
building, for three reasons: the spokes are already paid for, the kids-coding competitors are entirely
absent from this space, and none of the bootcamps offer maths. But nobody should expect the head term
in 90 days. The long-tails are where this cluster earns, and it already ranks on some of them.
