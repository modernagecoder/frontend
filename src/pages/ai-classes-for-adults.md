## The 7:58 pm monologue, and the 8:52 pm revision

Before the first class: everyone will be twenty-two, everyone will know things, I will freeze, my typing is embarrassing, it is too late, there is probably maths, I will give it one session. After it: the batch was a nurse, two managers, a shop owner and someone's grandfather, all beginners, all relieved; I asked the stupid question and it was everyone's question; the machine typed, I decided, my chart appeared from my numbers; there was no maths; same time next week.

The unfair adult advantage nobody credits: the scarce skill of the AI era is knowing what you want, stating it precisely, and judging what came back. Decades of emails, budgets and running rooms is training in exactly that. Teens out-click you; you out-specify them; the specifier wins. What adults genuinely find harder is being publicly mid-learning, and the fixes are structural: adult-only rooms, and a one to one option for doing the clumsy fortnight in private.
