# GCSE maths resits explained: the rules, the dates and a plan

**The short answer.** In England, 16 to 18 year olds on a study programme without a grade 4 in maths keep studying it, and full-time students with a grade 3 must resit the GCSE. Resits run in May and June and, for students who were at least 16 on the previous 31 August, in November. All three papers are retaken each time.

## The post-16 rule, from the Department for Education.

The rule is called the maths and English condition of funding. It applies to students aged 16 to 18 (up to 25 with an education, health and care plan) on a study programme of 150 hours or more who do not hold "a GCSE grade 9 to 4 (a level 2 pass grade)" in maths by the end of Year 11.

For 2026 to 2027, full-time students with a grade 3 must study towards the maths GCSE, because they are "closest to achieving level 2". Students with grade 2 or below can study towards Functional Skills Level 2 or the GCSE. Part-time students can study any approved qualification on their way to a grade 9 to 4.

Colleges must plan at least 100 hours of maths teaching a year for full-time students, and are encouraged to add an extra 35 hours of maths in 2026 to 2027.

### The rule at a glance

| GCSE maths grade at 16 | Full-time student, aged 16 to 18 |
| --- | --- |
| 9 to 4 | No maths requirement |
| 3 | Must study towards the GCSE |
| 2 or below | Functional Skills Level 2 or the GCSE |

The November series

## A second chance each year, with an age rule.

| Board | November GCSE maths | Who can enter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AQA 8300 | Yes | Students who were at least 16 on the previous 31 August |
| Edexcel 1MA1 | Yes | Students at least 16 years of age on the preceding 31 August |
| OCR J560 | Yes | Learners who reached at least 16 on or before 31 August of that year |

November is quicker, but it is not always wiser. The Department for Education asks colleges to "carefully consider" whether students with a low grade 3 or below should sit in November, looking at attendance, engagement and progress first. A summer entry gives more teaching time.

Whichever series you choose, a resit means sitting all three papers again, at one tier, in that one series. OCR's specification says learners "retake all components of the qualification", and AQA and OCR both allow as many retakes as a learner wishes.

Adults

## Resitting GCSE maths as an adult.

### Cost of the course

Gov.uk says that if you're over 16 and have left school, you normally do not have to pay to study maths to GCSE level. Colleges and adult education centres run these courses; the National Careers Service can help you find one.

### Sitting the exam

The exams are sat at an exam centre. If you study with a tutor rather than at college, ask local schools, colleges and exam centres whether they accept private candidates for your board.

### GCSE or Functional Skills

If your employer or course accepts Functional Skills Level 2, it can be a shorter route. Our [comparison](/functional-skills-maths-vs-gcse-maths) sets out the differences.

A resit plan that works

## Diagnose, repair, then rehearse.

### One full paper, marked properly

Every lost mark is sorted into a gap in knowledge, a method slip or timing. That map decides the plan.

### Repair the big leaks first

Number and ratio carry about half of the Foundation marks on every board, so they usually come first.

### Practise the non-calculator paper weekly

It is a third of the GCSE, and arithmetic slips there are often quick marks to win back.

### Full papers in your board's format

Timed, marked with the mark scheme, so method marks are banked and nothing on the day is a surprise.

Where resit marks hide

**Increase 240 by 15%.**

10% of 240 = 24, 5% = 1215% = 36240 + 36 = **276** or 240 × 1.15 = 276

Percentages appear in many forms on Foundation papers. Two methods, one answer, and each checks the other.

Common questions

## GCSE maths resits: what students and adults ask.

Do you have to resit GCSE maths if you get a 3?

If you are aged 16 to 18 on a full-time study programme, yes: students with a grade 3 must study towards the maths GCSE.

What if I got a grade 2 or below?

Students aged 16 to 18 with grade 2 or below can study towards Functional Skills Level 2 or towards the GCSE.

When can I resit GCSE maths?

Every May and June, and in the November series if you were at least 16 on the previous 31 August.

Can a 15 year old sit the November GCSE maths exam?

No. All three boards restrict November entry to students who were at least 16 on the relevant 31 August.

Do I have to retake all three papers?

Yes. GCSE maths is linear, so a resit means sitting all three papers again at one tier in one series.

How many times can you resit GCSE maths?

As many times as you wish. AQA and OCR both state this in their specifications.

Can adults resit GCSE maths for free?

Gov.uk says that if you're over 16 and have left school, you normally do not have to pay to study maths to GCSE level.

Can I resit on Higher tier?

Yes, if your working level supports it. Our [Foundation vs Higher guide](/gcse-maths-foundation-vs-higher) explains the trade-off.

Is Functional Skills easier than a GCSE resit?

It is shorter and more practical. Whether it counts for you depends on what your employer or course accepts.
