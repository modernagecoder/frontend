In short

The Latymer School in Edmonton is Enfield's selective grammar school, and it sets its own test. For September 2027 entry, sat on 3 to 5 September 2026, the maths was a 30-minute multiple-choice booklet by GL Assessment, paired with 30 minutes of verbal reasoning, then a 60-minute English paper set by the school; the English was marked only for the top 700 on maths and verbal reasoning combined. For September 2028 entry the school has switched to Quest Assessments, and the assessment has three sections: Mathematics, English Comprehension and English Writing. Quest lists mathematics as Paper One, multiple choice, with registration from March to June 2027 and the test in September 2027. The school expects Key Stage 2 competence. We teach that maths, live online. Book a first lesson at no charge; a group place after that is USD 100 a month, and one to one lessons USD 150 a month.

The cycle just finished

How the maths worked in September 2026

Families with older children, or those reading older advice, will know the previous arrangement. Here it is, as the school published it, so the difference is clear.

The papers

For 2027 entry, the school's admission procedures describe "a Mathematics questions booklet and a Verbal Reasoning questions booklet; both will be multiple-choice format set by GL Assessment. It will be 60 minutes in duration (30 minutes Mathematics & 30 minutes Verbal Reasoning)". The English paper, 60 minutes of reading and creative writing, was set by the school.

The test guidance notes added practical details: a clock visible in the room, no calculators, no rulers, and "Rough paper will be provided for workings out", which is more generous than several other areas' tests.

The order of marking

Maths and verbal reasoning were scored first, by machine, combined and ranked. The procedures say that "In normal terms the top 700 applicants will be deemed selective", and that the English paper is marked for those applicants, with a wider threshold for looked-after children.

In that system, 30 minutes of maths decided, with verbal reasoning, whether a child's English was even read. Under the new structure the sections change, and the school will publish how they are combined. What does not change is that the maths has to be secure.

The admission procedures also limit entry to applicants living in the school's published Inner Area postcodes. That is admissions, and we leave it to the school's documents, but it is worth checking before any preparation begins.

A slice of Paper One

Year 5 fractions, the topic that trips most children

The school expects "competence in skills appropriate to Key Stage 2 English and Mathematics". Fractions are where that competence is most often thinner than it looks.

Year 5 fraction skills from the national curriculum, with our own practice questions
Year 5 skillOur questionAnswer and the idea
Compare and order fractions whose denominators are multiples of the same numberWhich is larger, 3/4 or 7/12?3/4, because 3/4 = 9/12
Mixed numbers and improper fractionsWrite 17/4 as a mixed number.4 1/4: four wholes and a quarter
Add fractions, including answers above 1What is 2/5 + 4/5?6/5 = 1 1/5, the curriculum's own example
Multiply fractions and mixed numbers by whole numbersWhat is 4 × 1 3/4?7: four lots of 1 and four lots of 3/4
Decimals as fractionsWrite 0.71 as a fraction.71/100, again the curriculum's example
Order a setPut 1/2, 3/8, 3/4 and 5/8 in order, smallest first.3/8, 1/2, 5/8, 3/4, all in eighths

Every row uses one idea: rewrite fractions so they share a denominator, then compare, add or order the numerators. The Year 5 curriculum phrases it carefully, asking children to compare and order fractions "whose denominators are all multiples of the same number", which is exactly the condition that makes the rewriting easy.

On a multiple-choice paper, the wrong options are usually the answers produced by the classic errors: adding the denominators (2/5 + 4/5 = 6/10), or comparing by the size of the numbers alone (7/12 looks bigger than 3/4 because 7 and 12 are bigger). A child who knows why those are wrong discards them at once.

The multiplication row is the one Year 5 children find newest. 4 × 1 3/4 can be done as 4 lots of 1 plus 4 lots of 3/4, which is 4 + 3 = 7. We teach it with a picture first, as the curriculum suggests, "supported by materials and diagrams", before any rule.

Fractions also sit inside word problems about measures and money, where a child may not notice them. A question about three quarters of a 2 metre ribbon is a fractions question in disguise, and we practise finding the fraction hidden in the words.

Source: national curriculum in England, mathematics, Year 5 fractions (including decimals and percentages), statutory requirements and examples. The practice questions are ours.

Preparing for a new test

What to do when the format has just changed

A change of provider unsettles families. For maths, it changes much less than it seems.

Content holds

The school expects Key Stage 2 competence under either provider. The maths a child needs in September 2027 is the maths of Year 5 and before, whoever prints the paper.

Format is published

Quest has said the maths is multiple choice, on an answer sheet, as Paper One. It also offers familiarisation booklets with sample questions and an answer sheet. Use them when they are available.

Old papers mislead

Practice built around a 30-minute maths-and-verbal booklet trains the wrong rhythm. We practise maths as a paper in its own right, not as half of one.

Quest's advice to families is sensible and we repeat it: a strong grounding in what the child is already learning at school, wide reading, and short, regular learning rather than long sessions. For maths, that means steady weekly work through Year 5, not a crash course in the summer.

We do not reproduce Quest familiarisation material, GL papers or Latymer's specimen English papers. Our questions are our own, written to the Year 5 curriculum and the published format.

The English sections, comprehension and writing, now make up two of the three. We do not teach English for the assessment, and families should plan for that separately.

Enfield has only one selective school. The council's guide notes that Enfield Grammar School, despite the name, describes itself as "a truly comprehensive school", so it does not use this or any selection test.

Latymer questions

What Enfield families ask us

Is the Latymer test changing?

Yes. The school says that from September 2027, for 2028 entry, its assessments will be provided by Quest Assessments and will have three sections: Mathematics, English Comprehension and English Writing.

Is there still verbal reasoning?

Verbal reasoning is not among the three sections the school lists for 2028 entry. For 2027 entry it was a 30-minute booklet alongside 30 minutes of maths.

What is the maths paper like for 2028 entry?

Quest describes mathematics as Paper One of two papers sat in a single session, with multiple-choice questions. It has not published the length of the paper.

What maths does Latymer expect?

The school says applicants are expected to show competence in skills appropriate to Key Stage 2 English and Mathematics. For a test early in Year 6, that is the primary curriculum up to the end of Year 5.

When is the next Latymer test?

Quest lists registration from March to June 2027 and the test in September 2027, with results in October 2027. The school will publish exact dates.

Is a calculator allowed?

The school's 2026 test guidance said no calculators, and that rough paper would be provided. Check the guidance for 2027, which may differ under the new provider.

Is Enfield Grammar School selective?

No. In Enfield Council's guide it describes itself as a truly comprehensive school despite the name. Latymer is the borough's selective school.

Do you teach the English sections?

No. We teach mathematics only, and we do not advise on registration, the Inner Area or places.

Do you use Quest or GL papers?

No. We write our own questions and do not reproduce any provider's or the school's material.

How much do lessons cost?

You pay nothing for the first lesson. Regular lessons then cost USD 100 a month in a group or USD 150 a month one to one, and you can leave whenever you like.
