## The non-calculator paper on each board.

| Board and tier | Non-calculator | Calculator allowed | Each paper |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| AQA 8300, both tiers | Paper 1 | Papers 2 and 3 | 1 hour 30 minutes, 80 marks |
| Edexcel 1MA1, both tiers | Paper 1 | Papers 2 and 3 | 1 hour 30 minutes, 80 marks |
| OCR J560, Foundation | Paper 2 | Papers 1 and 3 | 1 hour 30 minutes, 100 marks |
| OCR J560, Higher | Paper 5 | Papers 4 and 6 | 1 hour 30 minutes, 100 marks |

Each paper is a third of the GCSE, so the non-calculator paper carries the same weight as each calculator paper. A student who drops 20 marks there cannot simply make it up by being careful later; those marks are part of the same total.

The boards are explicit that topics are not split between papers. AQA's description of every paper reads "Content from any part of the specification may be assessed", and OCR states: "All content may be assessed on either the calculator or non-calculator papers."

The non-calculator paper

## What to practise when the calculator stays in the bag.

The Department for Education's GCSE content asks students to "calculate exactly with fractions, surds and multiples of π" and to "estimate answers" and check calculations by approximation. Those are the skills the non-calculator paper leans on hardest.

Higher students also need the exact values of sin and cos for 0°, 30°, 45°, 60° and 90°, and of tan for 0°, 30°, 45° and 60°, because a calculator cannot be used to look them up on this paper.

### The non-calculator checklist

- Long multiplication and division, including decimals
- Fractions: all four operations and mixed numbers
- Percentages of amounts and percentage change
- Negative numbers, powers and roots
- Estimating by rounding to 1 significant figure
- Surds and exact answers in terms of π (Higher)
- Exact trigonometric values (Higher)

Decimals

**0.6 × 0.07**

6 × 7 = 423 decimal places in total**= 0.042**

Multiply as whole numbers, then place the point.

Fractions of amounts

**3/8 of 96**

96 ÷ 8 = 1212 × 3 = **36**

Divide by the bottom, multiply by the top.

Estimation

**Estimate (49.7 × 3.12) ÷ 0.48**

≈ (50 × 3) ÷ 0.5= 150 ÷ 0.5**≈ 300**

Round each number to 1 significant figure first, then dividing by 0.5 is doubling.

The calculator papers

## A calculator does not think. It only does what you type.

Calculator papers are two thirds of the GCSE, and they still need working shown. OCR's specification says learners are expected to "support their answers with appropriate working" in every paper. A correct method can earn marks even when the final number is wrong.

In our lessons, calculator marks are more often lost to technique than to missing topics: brackets left out, the fraction key avoided, or answers rounded halfway through a problem so the final answer drifts.

Round at the end

**A town of 2,400 people grows by 3% a year. How many people after 5 years?**

2,400 × 1.03⁵ = 2,782.25...≈ **2,782 people**rounding 1.03⁵ to 1.16 first gives 2,784, which is wrong

Keep the full value in the calculator and round once, at the end.

### Learn your own calculator

Use the same model in every practice paper, so the fraction, power and standard form keys are second nature.

### Write before you type

Write the calculation down first. If the answer looks wrong, you can see where the typing went astray.

### Estimate to check

A quick rounded estimate catches a misplaced decimal point or a missing bracket.

## Calculator and non-calculator papers: what students ask.

Which GCSE maths paper is non-calculator?

Paper 1 on AQA and Edexcel. On OCR it is Paper 2 on Foundation tier and Paper 5 on Higher tier.

How many GCSE maths papers allow a calculator?

Two of the three papers on every board and tier.

Can any topic come up on the non-calculator paper?

Yes. AQA and OCR both state that content from any part of the specification can be assessed on any paper.

How much of the GCSE is the non-calculator paper worth?

One third. Each of the three papers carries an equal share of the marks.

What type of calculator can I use in GCSE maths?

A scientific calculator is the usual choice. OCR permits a scientific or graphical calculator on its calculator papers, and calculators must follow the JCQ exam rules. Check with your school before buying.

Do I need to show working on the calculator paper?

Yes. Boards expect working to support answers, and method marks can be earned even if the final answer is wrong.

How do I get better at the non-calculator paper?

Practise written methods for fractions, decimals, percentages and long multiplication and division little and often, and estimate before calculating.

Do I need to learn trigonometry values for GCSE maths?

Higher students need the exact values of sin and cos for 0°, 30°, 45°, 60° and 90°, and of tan for 0°, 30°, 45° and 60°.

Is there a formulae sheet on the non-calculator paper?

Ofqual requires a formulae sheet in GCSE maths exams, and has decided it continues for the remaining lifetime of the current specifications.
