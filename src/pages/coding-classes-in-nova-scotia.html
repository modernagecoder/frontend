Coding classes in Nova Scotia

Where can a Nova Scotia student get real coding lessons with a live teacher?

Modern Age Coders gives Nova Scotia students live online lessons in coding, Python, Java and AI, for anyone aged 6 or older, taught one to one or in a shared group of fifteen to twenty. Python and AI for Kids is the common first course for younger children; high school students often choose Java for Teens, which matches Computer Programming 12. Lessons run on Atlantic time, and the first is free.

In Nova Scotia, coding is woven into the early grades as information technology, sits beside math and science in the Grade 7 and 8 numeracy block, and becomes a course of its own in Computer Programming 12, which the province's virtual school teaches in Java. The graduation rules give technology a place too, through one compulsory credit that can come from science or technology courses. What the system cannot give every student is a programming teacher every week from Grade 4 to Grade 12. This page sets out the credit rules and the 2026 to 2027 assessment calendar from the department's own pages, turns the province's public library statistics into a Python exercise, and explains how live classes on Atlantic time fit around a Nova Scotia school year.

Coding in Nova Scotia schools

Where programming fits in a Nova Scotia education, Primary to Grade 12

The Department of Education and Early Childhood Development publishes the Public School Program and the graduation rules on its EDnet site. Everything here was read on 22 October 2026. Words in quotation marks belong to the department; the plain-English summaries were written by us.

The virtual school describes Computer Programming 12 by what students do: they “will be expected to understand and apply the basic skills and processes of problem solving using computer programming”, plan solutions and work together on a realistic problem. It runs in the second semester and uses CodeHS in Java, with other development tools allowed for project work. Students who meet Java for the first time that semester spend much of it on syntax; students who arrive fluent can spend it on the project.

Next to the school course, not instead of it

Our classes support students in Computer Programming 12, in math courses and in grades with no programming course at all, and we do not grant Nova Scotia credits. The Mathematics 10 exam

Students write it at the end of Mathematics 10, Pre-IB Mathematics 10 or their French equivalents, on paper, in one two-hour session. Teachers score it with a provincial marking guide, and the department says the exam counts for 20% of the final course mark.

Grades 6 and 8 go online

The department says students will complete the Grade 6 and 8 assessments online using school devices instead of on paper. The Grade 6 math portion comes in four parts of about 30 minutes, so typing and reading a screen matter as much as arithmetic.

Not a report card mark

For Grade 8, the department states that results are not used to determine report card grades or grade level placement decisions. They guide teaching. The Grade 10 exam is different: it is part of the course mark.

We do not coach students on assessment items, and we never reproduce them; the department publishes its own sample questions for Mathematics 10. What we do is teach the mathematics properly, in the order a Nova Scotia course covers it, with problems we write ourselves. For a student heading to Grade 10 math, a few weeks of algebra worked through short Python programs, where every equation has to be typed exactly and every answer checked, tends to show where the gaps are faster than a worksheet.

Nova Scotia data project

Which library system lends its collection the most? Rates, not totals, on Nova Scotia's open data

The province publishes annual statistics for its nine regional public library systems. Our students download the file and learn the most useful idea in data work: divide by the right thing before you compare. Every figure below is our own calculation.

The script below produces every number in the table from the downloaded CSV, using only the Python standard library:import csv with open("tnu4-55e9.csv", encoding="utf-8") as f: rows = list(csv.DictReader(f)) def number(text): return float(text.replace(",", "")) latest = [r for r in rows if r["Year"] == "2024-2025"] earlier = {r["Library region"]: r for r in rows if r["Year"] == "2022-2023"} for r in sorted(latest, key=lambda r: number(r["Borrowing"]) / number(r["Items owned"]), reverse=True): turnover = number(r["Borrowing"]) / number(r["Items owned"]) per_hour = number(r["Annual in-person visits"]) / number(r["Hours open"]) before = number(earlier[r["Library region"]]["Annual in-person visits"]) change = 100 * (number(r["Annual in-person visits"]) - before) / before print(r["Library region"], round(turnover, 2), round(per_hour, 1), round(change, 1))

1. Pick a denominator

Halifax borrows far more items than any other system, which says little on its own because it also owns far more. Dividing borrowing by items owned gives loans per item, a measure of how hard a collection works. On that measure Halifax still leads, at 6.99, but Annapolis Valley is close behind at 5.34.

2. Compare like with like

Percent change needs the same region at two dates. The script builds a dictionary of 2022-2023 rows keyed by region, then looks each 2024-2025 row up in it. Most systems gained visitors over those two years; Annapolis Valley fell by 12.6%.

3. Question the odd one

Eastern Counties shows 2.1 in-person visits per hour open, far below every other system. The file's definition of hours open includes branches, bookmobiles and satellite locations, so students ask whether the hours and the visits measure the same places before drawing any conclusion.

What not to do with this file

We never add the nine regions together to make a provincial figure; the department publishes regions, and a sum of them is not a number it stands behind. The usage note also warns that closures in the early years and the Same Page shared catalogue, introduced in April 2023, changed borrowing and data collection, which is why our comparison starts in 2022-2023.

A Grade 12 extension: plot public access computers against program attendance for the nine systems and decide whether a straight line is honest with only nine points. Halifax and Cape Breton

The time zone database lists Cape Breton under its own zone name, America/Glace_Bay, but with the same offsets as Halifax, so Sydney and Halifax always share a clock. We confirm every class in Atlantic time.

Phone numbers

Nova Scotia numbers use the 902 and 782 area codes, which Prince Edward Island shares. Both are accepted by our booking form, which already carries the +1, so only the ten-digit number is needed.

Coding and AI

An AI tool can total a column. Knowing not to is the skill

The library project turns on one judgment, which numbers to divide and which never to add, and that judgment is exactly what students need when AI does the arithmetic.

Ask an AI assistant which Nova Scotia library system is busiest and it may hand back raw borrowing totals, which only say that Halifax is biggest. A student who has worked the file knows to ask a better question, loans per item or visits per open hour, and to check an odd figure such as 2.1 visits an hour before believing it.

So our students build their own analysis first and compare it with a tool's answer second. Teenagers use AI tools only within each tool's age rules, with a parent's permission where the tool requires it. Children under 13 learn how AI works by writing their own small programs rather than using tools built for adults.

Computer Programming 12 asks students to identify problems and select effective strategies; choosing the right denominator is one of the first strategies a programmer working with data has to learn. The longer argument is in is coding still worth learning in 2026.

FAQ

Nova Scotia coding questions

What Nova Scotia families most often ask before the first class.

Do you have coding classes in Nova Scotia?

Yes, live and online. There is no Nova Scotia centre; students join from home with a teacher, in a group batch of fifteen to twenty or one to one.

Is there a computer programming course in Nova Scotia high schools?

Yes. Computer Programming 12 is offered through the Nova Scotia Virtual School, in its Technology Education category, with course materials in Java. Ask your school how students enrol.

How many credits does a Nova Scotia student need to graduate?

At least 18, according to the department. No more than seven can be Grade 10 courses, at least five must be Grade 12 courses, and three must be mathematics, one at each grade level.

Do your classes count toward the Nova Scotia graduation diploma?

No. We do not grant Nova Scotia credits. Diploma credits come from school courses; our classes build the skills alongside them.

When is the Mathematics 10 exam?

The department lists 23 June 2027 for Mathematics 10 and Mathématiques 10. The exam is written on paper in one two-hour session and counts for 20% of the final course mark.

Should my teen learn Java or Python first?

For a student planning on Computer Programming 12, Java is a sensible early choice because the virtual school course uses it. Younger students usually start with Python, then add Java.

What time are classes for Nova Scotia families?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. All times are confirmed in Atlantic time.

Can a Nova Scotia student write the Canadian Computing Competition?

Yes, if their school places an order with CEMC, which has set the 2027 contest for Wednesday 17 February 2027. Java, Python, C and C++ are all allowed.

Do 902 and 782 numbers work in your form?

Yes. Enter the ten digits of any Nova Scotia number; the country code is already in place.

How young can a child start?

From about age 6, in Primary or Grade 1, with block coding. Typed Python usually begins around Grade 4, and adults are welcome at any stage.

Where are the fees listed?

Further up this page, under Fees. You will see three lines, for the free introductory lesson, the group rate per month and the one-to-one rate per month, each shown in your currency. Payment only starts after the introductory lesson.
