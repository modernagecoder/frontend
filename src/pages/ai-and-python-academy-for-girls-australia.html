What does not change

The syllabus, the standard and the expectations are identical to every other course. Girls do the same Python, the same maths and the same AI as anyone else.

Classes are our normal small groups, matched by level, or one to one. This academy does not use separate girls-only batches.

Why AI needs more people building it

The Australian Curriculum makes the point itself: "training a model on data collected from a single demographic group may not produce correct outputs for a more diverse population".

AI is better when the people building and checking it bring different experiences. That starts with more students learning how it works.

A first AI project

A rock, paper, scissors AI that learns

One of the first AI projects in the academy. It fits in a few dozen lines of Python and teaches the core idea behind machine learning: an AI can only learn patterns that are really there.

The program keeps a table of what its opponent played after each of their previous moves. Each round it predicts the most likely next move and plays whatever beats it. We tested it against four kinds of player, 20 games of 1,000 rounds each, with fixed random seeds so the results can be repeated.

The learning player against four opponents (our simulation, 14 October 2026)
OpponentAI winsAI losesDraws
Plays completely at random33.6%33.0%33.3%
Repeats their own last move 70% of the time79.9%10.1%10.0%
Cycles rock, paper, scissors in order99.7%0.1%0.1%
Picks rock half the time49.5%25.0%25.6%

Patterns are everything

Against a player in a rut, the AI wins four rounds in five. Against a fixed cycle it almost never loses. The more predictable the data, the better the model.

Randomness wins

Against a truly random player the AI does no better than chance: 33.6% wins against 33.0% losses. The simplest strategy of all, playing at random, cannot be beaten.

What students try next

Remembering two moves instead of one, forgetting old games, and playing against each other's programs. Each change is measured the same way.

Why this is a real AI lesson

The table of counts is a tiny model trained on data, the opponent's moves. Its predictions are only as good as the patterns in that data, the same reason a large AI system can be confidently wrong.

Students see that directly, in numbers they produced themselves.

Where it goes next

The same students later train classifiers on real datasets in AI classes for teens, and learn why the training data matters as much as the code.

Younger students build the game first in Python and add the learning part once loops and lists feel easy.

The program, seeds and results are our own. Curriculum quote from ACARA, Digital Technologies curriculum content, Years 7 to 10, read 14 October 2026.

Why choice matters

A project she chose gets finished. Finishing things, and showing them to someone, builds confidence faster than any amount of exercises.

The syllabus skills are the same whichever project carries them.

Sharing the work

Finished projects are shown to the group, and older students can publish theirs online with a parent's agreement.

Some go on to student hackathons or programming competitions; others simply keep building.

For families

Encouragement without pressure

Curiosity grows when coding feels like making things, not like another test. Praise for finishing and for sticking with a hard bug goes a long way.

There is no need to push for competitions unless she wants them.

When interest dips

Interest rises and falls with school terms and other activities. A change of project can bring it back faster than a change of course.

Teachers can suggest a new project that fits what she enjoys now.

Girls and parents ask

Questions about the AI and Python academy for girls

What is the AI and Python academy for girls?

A way into AI and Python for Australian girls aged 9 to 18, built on open access, role models and real projects. The syllabus is the same as every course.

Are classes girls only?

Not in this academy. Students learn in our normal mixed small groups matched by level, or one to one if they prefer.

Do you teach girls differently?

No. We do not assume girls learn differently. The content and standard are identical; what we focus on is removing practical barriers and showing real work.

What is a good first AI project?

A rock, paper, scissors player that learns its opponent's habits. In our tests it won 99.7% against a predictable player and only 33.6% against a random one, which shows that AI depends on patterns in its data.

What age can start?

From 9 with Python and AI for kids, or from 6 with Scratch. Teenagers can start Python from zero at any point.

Does my daughter need experience?

No. Every starting course begins from zero, and groups are matched by level.

Where can she meet other girls who code?

The Girls' Programming Network runs free full-day workshops around Australia each term. Our GPN next-step guide explains how they fit with regular lessons.

Which AI tools will she use?

Only tools whose own terms allow her age. Building AI in Python, the core of the academy, needs no AI account at all.

Will this help her career?

We make no career or salary promises. The academy builds real skills and a portfolio of projects she can show.

What are the fees?

A first class at no charge, with no card. Ongoing monthly fees for groups and one-to-one lessons are in the fees panel.
