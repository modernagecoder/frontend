# Coding Classes in Kuwait

> Kuwait's general secondary certificate, the Shahadat Al-Thanawiya Al-A'ama, is cumulative. Grade 10 contributes 10 per cent of the final mark, Grade 11 contributes 20 per cent and Grade 12 contributes 70 per cent. Modern Age Coders teaches live online coding and mathematics to ages 6 to 67 in Kuwait, in groups of five to eight or one to one, at USD 100 and USD 150 a month. The first class is free.

## How is school structured in Kuwait?

State education in Kuwait runs in three stages:

| Stage | Grades | Typical ages |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | 1 to 5 | 6 to 10 |
| Intermediate | 6 to 9 | 11 to 14 |
| Secondary | 10 to 12 | 15 to 17 |

Schooling is compulsory from age 6 to age 14, which is to say through the end of the intermediate stage. The Ministry of Education supervises secondary education in both government and private schools. From Grade 11 a learner chooses between a Science and an Arts specialisation, with religious and special education routes also available.

At the end of Grade 12 the learner is awarded the **Shahadat Al-Thanawiya Al-A'ama**, the general secondary education certificate.

## Why does the Grade 10 mark matter so much in Kuwait?

Because the certificate is not decided in one year. The final mark accumulates across the whole secondary stage:

| Grade | Contribution to the final certificate mark |
|---|---:|
| Grade 10 | 10 per cent |
| Grade 11 | 20 per cent |
| Grade 12 | 70 per cent |

This is the most useful thing a family in Kuwait can know before adding anything to a teenager's week. Two consequences follow directly.

**The pressure is not evenly spread.** A Grade 12 learner has 70 per cent of the certificate live in a single year. A Grade 10 learner has 10 per cent live and 90 per cent still open.

**So the cheapest year to build a skill is Grade 10**, the year most families treat as the quiet one. By Grade 12, adding two evenings a week is expensive in a way it simply is not two years earlier.

It also changes what an honest provider should say about workload. Two lessons a week is the normal pattern here. For a Grade 12 learner in Kuwait the sensible pattern is fewer, and saying so in advance is worth more than promising that everything fits.

## Does CBSE in Kuwait match what you teach?

Closely. Kuwait has a well established group of Indian schools running the CBSE curriculum, among them Bhavans and the Indian Educational School, alongside the American, British and Kuwaiti national systems.

For a CBSE family the practical consequence is specific: the Class 9 to 12 computer science sequence, the vocabulary and the way questions are phrased are the same ones Modern Age Coders teaches in India every day. A learner is not translating between two systems, and a Kuwait-based CBSE student can sit in the same group as a student in India without either being held back. That is why a small country does not limit the group a learner can be placed into.

## The Kuwait student data project

Every market page here has one analytical trap the class actually works through. Kuwait's is the one that has broken more real software than any exotic statistical error: **the inclusive-range off-by-one.**

The task looks trivial. Write a small program that answers a question Kuwaiti families genuinely ask: how many school terms are left before the next tranche of the certificate closes?

Almost every first attempt is wrong by exactly one, and it still looks right. Ask how many terms from the start of Grade 10 to the end of Grade 12 and the instinct is to subtract: 12 minus 10 is 2, times three terms, gives 6. The real answer counts both ends, so it is 3 years and 9 terms. Subtracting two boundary numbers gives you the gaps between them, never the things themselves.

It is the fence problem. Ten panels in a straight run need eleven posts. Programmers meet it as the off-by-one: a loop that runs one time too few, a range that quietly drops its last element, a report short by a single row and therefore never questioned.

What makes it a good first lesson in checking is that the wrong answer is plausible. Six terms is not absurd, so nobody notices it the way they would notice six hundred. The learner finds it only by testing the smallest case by hand: how many terms from the start of Grade 12 to the end of Grade 12? If the formula says zero, it is broken, and now they know before it matters.

Every figure in the classroom exercise is the learner's own. The certificate weighting is the only fact taken from the system.

## Does coding still matter when AI can write it?

Ask an AI to count the terms between Grade 10 and Grade 12 and it will hand back a confident, well-formatted, plausible answer. Sometimes nine. Sometimes six. It will not tell you which, and it will not sound less certain when it is wrong.

The bottleneck was never typing the loop. It is knowing that an inclusive range needs checking, testing the smallest case by hand, and being unwilling to publish a number you have not verified. That is what the project above trains, and it is why we teach logic before syntax.

A learner who has personally been wrong by one, found it and fixed it will check the machine's arithmetic for the rest of their life. A learner who has only ever accepted a generated answer has no reason to.
