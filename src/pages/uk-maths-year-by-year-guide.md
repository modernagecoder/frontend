## What maths is taught in each year in England?

One row per school year. Ages and checks come from gov.uk's year table; the content column is our short pick from that year's statutory statements.

The government describes the shape simply: "The national curriculum is organised into blocks of years called 'key stages' (KS)." Reception has early learning goals, not a programme of study. Years 1 to 6 each have their own statutory list. Years 7 to 9 share one Key Stage 3 programme and Years 10 and 11 one Key Stage 4 programme, so for those five rows the split by year is our illustration, not a legal one.

**Maths in England, year by year (ages and checks from gov.uk; content selected by us from the DfE programmes of study and the EYFS framework)**

| Year | Stage | Age | Headline maths that year | National check |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reception | EYFS | 4 to 5 | Numbers to 10 and how each is made; subitising up to 5; counting aloud past 20; comparing quantities up to 10 | Reception baseline (first 6 weeks); EYFS profile (April to June) |
| Year 1 | KS1 | 5 to 6 | Counting to and across 100; one more and one less; halves and quarters; adding and subtracting in one step | None in maths |
| Year 2 | KS1 | 6 to 7 | Addition and subtraction facts to 20 recalled fluently; the 2, 5 and 10 tables; thirds and quarters; time to five minutes | Optional KS1 tests, May |
| Year 3 | Lower KS2 | 7 to 8 | The 3, 4 and 8 tables; column addition and subtraction; counting in tenths; perimeter; clock numerals I to XII | None |
| Year 4 | Lower KS2 | 8 to 9 | All tables to 12 × 12; counting back through zero; decimal forms of a quarter, a half and three quarters; Roman numerals to C; area by counting squares; first-quadrant coordinates | Multiplication tables check, June |
| Year 5 | Upper KS2 | 9 to 10 | Numbers to at least 1 000 000; square and cube numbers; primes; long multiplication; thousandths; percentages | None |
| Year 6 | Upper KS2 | 10 to 11 | Numbers to 10 000 000; long division; ratio; first algebra with simple formulae; the mean; parts of a circle; all four quadrants | KS2 national tests, May |
| Year 7 | KS3 | 11 to 12 | Algebra used to generalise arithmetic; the four operations with negatives, fractions and mixed numbers; prime factors (order set by the school) | None |
| Year 8 | KS3 | 12 to 13 | Linear equations; percentages above 100%; proportion; straight-line graphs; angle facts (order set by the school) | School check of maths progress expected (no start year yet) |
| Year 9 | KS3 | 13 to 14 | The nth term of arithmetic sequences; standard form; Pythagoras' theorem and trigonometric ratios (order set by the school) | None |
| Year 10 | KS4 | 14 to 15 | Quadratic and simultaneous equations; vectors; for higher attainers, surds and the sine and cosine rules | Some take GCSEs |
| Year 11 | KS4 | 15 to 16 | The rest of the KS4 programme, then revision for three GCSE papers | GCSE Maths, May and June |

Sources: gov.uk, [The national curriculum](https://www.gov.uk/national-curriculum) (year, age and assessment table); DfE, [Mathematics programmes of study](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/national-curriculum-in-england-mathematics-programmes-of-study) for key stages 1 to 4; DfE, [EYFS statutory framework](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/early-years-foundation-stage-framework--2) from September 2026. All read on 1 November 2026.

Each primary stage opens with a one-line aim. Key Stage 1 seeks "confidence and mental fluency with whole numbers, counting and place value"; lower Key Stage 2 wants pupils to "become increasingly fluent with whole numbers and the four operations"; upper Key Stage 2 is meant to "develop the connections that pupils make between multiplication and division with fractions, decimals, percentages and ratio". From Year 7 the order is the school's, since the KS3 text says "Decisions about progression should be based on the security of pupils' understanding". And gov.uk adds that "Other types of school, like academies and private schools, do not have to follow the national curriculum."

## Which school years have a national maths test or check?

Reception (two of them), Year 2 (optional), Year 4, Year 6 and Year 11. The dates below are those published for 2026 to 2027.

gov.uk puts it in one line: "Your child will be tested at the end of some key stages." The DfE's assessment calendar adds that "Assessment arrangements for the academic year 2026 to 2027 will remain unchanged", and that "Schools must keep these periods free for administering the assessments." GCSE dates come from the exam boards; the Year 11 row uses AQA's.

**National assessments that include maths, school year 2026 to 2027 (dates from DfE Primary assessments: future dates and the AQA 8300 key dates page)**

| Year | Assessment | Maths content and format | When in 2026 to 2027 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reception | Reception baseline assessment | A short task, designed to take around 20 minutes, on early maths and language; no expected standard and no pass or fail | First 6 weeks after a child enters Reception |
| Reception | EYFS profile | Teacher judgement against the early learning goals, including Number and Numerical Patterns | April to June 2027, completed no later than 30 June |
| Year 2 | Optional KS1 tests | Maths Paper 1 arithmetic (about 20 minutes) and Paper 2 reasoning (about 35 minutes) | Recommended during May 2027 |
| Year 4 | Multiplication tables check | 25 on-screen questions, 6 seconds each, tables 2 to 12 | Two weeks from Monday 7 June 2027 |
| Year 6 | KS2 national tests | Paper 1 arithmetic, Papers 2 and 3 reasoning | Maths on Wednesday 12 May and Thursday 13 May 2027 |
| Year 11 | GCSE Mathematics (AQA 8300 as example) | Three papers of 1 hour 30 minutes, Paper 1 without a calculator; Foundation grades 1 to 5, Higher 4 to 9 | 14 May, 27 May and 14 June 2027, mornings |

Sources: DfE, [Primary assessments: future dates](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/primary-assessments-future-dates), updated 12 October 2026; STA optional KS1 and tables check guidance; AQA, [GCSE Mathematics 8300 key dates](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/mathematics/gcse/mathematics-8300/key-dates). Edexcel and OCR sit on the same three mornings.

Only the KS2 tests and GCSE carry real weight for a family. KS2 results are scaled scores, which the STA says "are used to report national curriculum test outcomes", and they pass to the secondary school. The Year 2 papers are the school's choice, and the times tables check is brief: the STA leaflet says "On average, the check should take no longer than 5 minutes to complete." The DfE already lists the 2028 KS2 tests for Monday 8 May to Thursday 11 May 2028.

## How does maths work year by year in Scotland?

Scotland does not use year groups or key stages. It uses stages (P1 to P7, then S1 upwards) and Curriculum for Excellence levels, and there is no national maths exam before the senior phase.

In Scotland a child works through numeracy and mathematics at Early, First, Second, Third and Fourth level. Progress is reported at four stages, which also sit the online National Standardised Assessments.

**Scottish stages, expected CfE level and numeracy results, 2024-25 (Scottish Government, Achievement of CfE Levels 2024-25, published 9 December 2025)**

| Stage | Expected level | Numeracy: share achieving it, 2024-25 | National Standardised Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Early level | 85 per cent | Yes, numeracy and literacy |
| P4 | First level | 78 per cent | Yes |
| P7 | Second level | 79 per cent | Yes |
| S3 | Third level or better | 91 per cent | Yes |
| S3 | Fourth level | 68 per cent | Same assessment |

Sources: Scottish Government, [Achievement of CfE Levels 2024-25](https://www.gov.scot/publications/achievement-of-curriculum-for-excellence-cfe-levels-2024-25/), chapter 1, tables 1 and 2; National Standardised Assessments for Scotland, parents and carers page and questions and answers page. All read on 1 November 2026.

Those percentages are "based on teachers' professional judgements", not on a test score, and the standardised assessments have no pass mark. The question-and-answer page for those assessments is blunt about it: "There is no pass or fail." ## How does one maths idea grow from Year 2 to GCSE?

Most topics return each year, deeper. Here is one thread of ours, three quarters, through the programmes of study.

1. **Year 2: a part of a whole.** The Year 2 list includes finding thirds and quarters "of a length, shape, set of objects or quantity". Our question: three quarters of 8 sweets. Thinking: 4 equal groups of 2; 3 groups is 6.
2. **Year 4: a decimal.** Year 4 asks children to "recognise and write decimal equivalents to" a quarter, a half and three quarters. Thinking (ours): a quarter of 100 hundredths is 25 hundredths, so three quarters is 0.75.
3. **Year 6: a percentage and a ratio.** Year 6 expects pupils to "recall and use equivalences between simple fractions, decimals and percentages" and to solve "problems involving the relative sizes of two quantities". Our question: three quarters of a class walk to school; give the percentage and the ratio of walkers to the rest. Thinking: 75%, and 3 : 1.
4. **Key Stage 3: an operator above 100%.** The KS3 programme asks pupils to "work with percentages greater than 100%" and to "interpret fractions and percentages as operators". Our question: a plant is 40 cm tall and grows to 175% of that height. How tall is it? Thinking: 175% is 1.75, so 40 × 1.75 = 70 cm.
5. **Key Stage 4: an exact value.** At GCSE, higher-attaining students learn to "change recurring decimals into their corresponding fractions". Our question: show that 0.7575... (75 repeating) is 25/33. Thinking: let x = 0.7575...; then 100x = 75.7575...; subtracting gives 99x = 75, so x = 75/99 = 25/33.

A child who stalls at step four usually has a gap at step two. When Year 8 goes wrong, the fix is often a Year 4 idea, and the map shows where to look.

## How do you find the right year page for your child?

Five steps, in order.

1. **Confirm the school year, not just the age.** gov.uk says "Most children start reception full-time in September after their fourth birthday." Our reading: a year group runs on birthdays from September to August.
2. **Check which nation's curriculum applies.** The map on this page is England's. Scotland is above; Wales and Northern Ireland are not covered here.
3. **Find the next national check.** A Year 3 child has the tables check next; a Year 9 student has GCSE two years out.
4. **Read the row before your child's row.** If the previous year's headline content is not secure, start there.
5. **Then open the year page, or book a free class.** A free class shows which row your child is really working at.

**A worked example (ours).** A child was born in March 2018. Which year are they in during 2026 to 2027, and which national check comes next? They turned 4 in March 2022, so under the gov.uk rule they started Reception in September 2022. Counting on one year each September gives Year 1 in 2023 to 2024, Year 2 in 2024 to 2025, Year 3 in 2025 to 2026 and Year 4 in 2026 to 2027. The Year 4 row carries the multiplication tables check, which the DfE lists in the two weeks from Monday 7 June 2027, so the pages to open are Year 4 and the tables check.

## Is the maths curriculum in England changing?

Yes, but not this school year.

The November 2025 government response says it will "publish the final revised national curriculum by spring 2027" and implement it "for first teaching from September 2028". For maths it promises to review Key Stages 1 to 3 "with a particular focus on resequencing content (particularly in the 'number' domain)", so some rows above may move. GCSEs are to be updated "for first teaching from 2029 onwards".

**Announced changes and their timing (Government response to the Curriculum and Assessment Review, November 2025; DfE Primary assessments: future dates, October 2026)**

| Change | Timing as published | Who it affects first (our reading) |
|---|---|---|
| 2026 to 2027 assessments | Unchanged | Every year group this school year |
| Revised curriculum first taught | September 2028 | Schools from that September |
| Year 8 maths progress check by schools | Expectation announced; no start year in the text we read | Future Year 8 groups |
| Updated GCSEs first taught | 2029 onwards | Year 7 or below in 2026 to 2027, at the earliest |

The last column is our arithmetic: a child in Year 8 now reaches Year 10 in September 2028 and sits GCSE on the current specification. On Year 8 itself the response says: "We will expect all schools to assess pupil progress in writing and maths in Year 8".

## Questions parents ask about maths in each school year

### What maths is taught in each year in the UK?

In England: numbers to 10 in Reception; place value to 100 and the first tables in Years 1 and 2; written methods and all tables to 12 × 12 in Years 3 and 4; large numbers, percentages, ratio and early algebra in Years 5 and 6; then Key Stages 3 and 4 towards GCSE.

### Which year groups have maths tests in England?

Reception has the baseline assessment and the EYFS profile, Year 2 has optional KS1 tests, Year 4 has the multiplication tables check, Year 6 has the KS2 national tests and Year 11 sits GCSE Maths. The other years have no national maths test.

### What age is each school year in England?

gov.uk lists Reception as 4 to 5, rising a year at a time to Year 11 at 15 to 16. Most children start Reception in the September after their fourth birthday.

### When are the 2027 KS2 SATs and the times tables check?

The DfE lists the KS2 tests from Monday 10 May to Thursday 13 May 2027, with the maths papers on 12 and 13 May. The multiplication tables check runs in the two weeks from Monday 7 June 2027.

### Are KS1 SATs still compulsory?

No. They are optional, and the STA recommends May 2027 for schools that use them.

### Why is there no Year 7, 8 or 9 maths list?

Key Stage 3 is one programme for all three years, and each school decides the order.

### Is there a Year 8 maths test now?

Not a national one. In November 2025 the government said it will expect schools to assess maths progress in Year 8; no start year has been given.

### How is Scottish maths different from England?

Scotland uses stages (P1 to P7, then S1 upwards) and CfE levels from Early to Fourth. P1, P4, P7 and S3 take online assessments with no pass or fail.
