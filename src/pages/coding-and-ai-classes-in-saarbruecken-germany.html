Saarland schools

How much Informatik does a Saarbrücken pupil get at school?

More than in most Länder, and it starts in grade 7. The figures below come from the Gesellschaft für Informatik and the Kultusministerkonferenz, read on 2 November 2026.

Compulsory from grade 7

According to the Gesellschaft für Informatik's Informatik-Monitor 2026/27, Informatik is compulsory at Gemeinschaftsschulen and at the nine-year Gymnasium in Saarland: two lessons a week in grades 7 and 8, and one lesson a week in grades 9 and 10.

On the national page of the same monitor, Saarland is one of only three Länder, with Mecklenburg-Vorpommern and Thüringen, that already meet the medium-term target of six compulsory hours recommended by the KMK's Standing Scientific Commission for 2030.

What that leaves open

Six hours across four school years is a solid start, but it is shared between a whole class at one speed. Before grade 7 there is no fixed computing lesson at all, which is the gap younger Saarbrücken children fall into.

The Gymnasium is also changing: the monitor notes that Saarland moves to the nine-year G9 route by 2026/27. In the Oberstufe, Informatik is a course a pupil chooses; nationally, about 15 percent of pupils in the Qualifikationsphase take it.

Taught in English

Are the classes really in English, and does that work for a German speaker?

Yes, every lesson is live and in English, and for most learners in Saarbrücken that turns out to be an advantage rather than a hurdle.

No German lessons, said early

We teach in English only. A family that needs teaching in German should know that before the first class, so we say it here and again in the questions below. Mentors are based in India and teach over live video.

The mentor adapts

Mentors slow down, rephrase, and wait. A nine year old who answers half in German is understood and handed the English word; an adult who wants technical vocabulary gets it written in the chat. Accent and pace are matched to the learner, not the reverse.

Programming is already English

Python is written with words like if, while, print and return. The error messages, the documentation and most of the help online are in English too, so a learner who practises the language of programming in class reads it more easily everywhere else.

Saarbrücken sits a few kilometres from the French border, and many families here already live with more than one language. A lesson in English adds one more, in a subject where the vocabulary is small and the screen does much of the explaining.

A real Saarbrücken project

Can a model guess tomorrow's temperature at Ensheim from days that looked like today?

This is the project our teenagers and adults build in their first machine-learning month. It uses one weather station inside the city, a simple idea called nearest neighbours, and one mistake that almost everyone makes on the first try.

The data

The Deutscher Wetterdienst runs station 4336, Saarbrücken-Ensheim, at the airport in the south-east of the city, 319 metres above sea level. Its Climate Data Center publishes the daily observations from 1951 to 2025 under the Creative Commons BY 4.0 licence. We downloaded the file on 3 November 2026.

We keep four columns from 31 December 1990 onwards: the date, the day's highest temperature, the mean air pressure and the relative humidity. That leaves 12,783 days with all three values present, saved as ensheim_daily.csv.

The idea

To guess tomorrow, find the 20 past days that looked most like today and average what happened the day after each of them. Nothing is hand-written about weather; the model only remembers and compares. Each day is described by four numbers: the day of the year, its highest temperature, how much the pressure changed since yesterday, and the humidity.

The model learns from 1991 to 2024 and is tested on 2025, a year it has never seen. It has to beat the laziest guess there is: tomorrow will be as warm as today.

The model is only six hundredths of a degree better than copying today. The reason is in how distance is measured. In raw units, a gap of 30 days in the calendar counts the same as a gap of 30 degrees, and a gap of 13 humidity points the same as 13 degrees. So the model mostly looks for days at the same time of year with similar humidity, and hardly notices whether today was hot or cold.

Dividing each difference by that feature's typical spread turns every feature into the same unit: "how unusual is this gap". Now a day that was 8.5 degrees warmer is as far away as one that sits 52.7 days apart in the calendar. The average error falls to 2.39 degrees, a fifth of a degree better than copying today. Nothing about the data changed. Only the ruler did.

Data: Deutscher Wetterdienst, Climate Data Center, daily climate data (KL), station 4336 Saarbrücken-Ensheim, licence CC BY 4.0, downloaded 3 November 2026. The column selection, the training and test split and all errors are our own. Two days before 2025 lack one of the values and are left out; 2025 contributes 364 test days because the last day of the year has no tomorrow in the file.

  1. Children, 9 to 12. Write five cards with a day's temperature and the next day's. For a new card, find the most similar old card and copy its answer. That is a nearest neighbour.
  2. Teenagers. Run Version 1, then change the humidity column to fractions (0.75 instead of 75) and run it again. Explain why the answer moves.
  3. Adults. Try k of 5, 20 and 80 after scaling, and plot the error. Then leave out one feature at a time and find the one the model needs most.
  4. Everyone. Write in one sentence why "it will be like today" is so hard to beat for the weather, and what kind of day it gets most wrong.

The AI contest

The Bundeswettbewerb Künstliche Intelligenz is run by the Tübingen AI Center. A team hands in written documentation, runnable code and a short video pitch, and the project must use machine-learning methods. Projects may be submitted in German or in English.

The 2026 round closed for projects on 20 September 2026 and ends with the final in Tübingen on 13 November 2026. A pupil who wants to enter the next round can start building now; a project like the Ensheim model is the right size for a first attempt.

The puzzle contests

The Informatik-Biber is a free online quiz of logic puzzles that schools run in a lesson. In 2026 the Biberwochen are 9 to 20 November. Ask your child's teacher whether the school takes part.

In class we never use real contest tasks. We write our own problems in the same spirit, label them as ours, and point families to the organisers' own free archives.

How the gap works: German law (the Einheitenzeitgesetz) sets MEZ, one hour ahead of UTC, for the winter months and MESZ, two hours ahead, for the summer. The clocks went back on 25 October 2026 and go forward on 28 March 2027. India never changes its clocks and sits five and a half hours ahead of UTC, which puts a mentor four and a half hours ahead of Saarbrücken until the spring change and three and a half hours ahead after it. The slots above illustrate the conversion; none of them is a fixed class.

Learners from Völklingen, St. Ingbert, Sulzbach or Dudweiler join the same way, because everything happens online. When you book, the time is shown in your own clock, and the mentor sees it in theirs.

Questions from Saarbrücken

Coding and AI classes in Saarbrücken: questions and answers

Are the classes in German?

No, never. Lessons happen live in English, and only in English. Our mentors work from India; they slow down, choose plain words and get used to each learner's accent, which is why a ten year old with school English and an adult who learnt it long ago can both keep up.

Who can join coding and AI classes in Saarbrücken?

Children from 6, teenagers and adults. Children start with Scratch or Python and AI for Kids, teenagers with Python or AI and Machine Learning for Teens, and adults with the Python Masterclass or the AI and ML Masterclass.

Do Saarland schools already teach Informatik?

Yes. The Gesellschaft für Informatik reports that Informatik is compulsory at Gemeinschaftsschulen and the nine-year Gymnasium in Saarland, two lessons a week in grades 7 and 8 and one lesson a week in grades 9 and 10. Our classes run alongside school; the school course is the one that is graded.

When are classes in Saarbrücken time?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Most learners here pick a late afternoon, an early evening or a weekend morning.

Does my teenager need an AI chatbot account for these classes?

No. Nobody under 18 is asked to open one. Claude may only be used by adults, and ChatGPT requires a parent to agree for anyone under 18, so our teenage courses avoid both. Pupils write small models of their own instead, which shows them what a chatbot is doing far better than prompting one.

Can you help with the Bundeswettbewerb Künstliche Intelligenz?

We teach the skills a project needs: Python, data, machine learning and honest testing. The BWKI accepts projects in German or English. The 2026 final is in Tübingen on 13 November 2026, and the work for each entry must be the team's own.

Is the Ensheim weather project real data?

Yes. It uses the daily observations of Deutscher Wetterdienst station 4336, Saarbrücken-Ensheim, published under the CC BY 4.0 licence and downloaded on 3 November 2026. The model, the test on 2025 and the error figures are our own.

How many learners share a class?

A group class has between fifteen and twenty people, put together by age for children and by level for teenagers and adults. Someone who prefers undivided attention can take the one-to-one plan, with two private lessons each week.

How much does it cost, and in which currency?

There is one monthly fee. The page displays it in euro, worked out from the reference rate of the European Central Bank; the card is then charged in US dollars, and your bank turns that into euro on the statement, so a small difference is normal. Your first class costs nothing.

Can an adult with no coding background start with AI?

Yes, but through Python first. The AI and ML Masterclass begins with the basics of programming, then data, then models. Most adults are writing and testing their own small model within the first two months.
