## When do Hannover children get Informatik at school?

Compulsory Informatik arrives in grade 9. Before that it depends on the school type and on what a child chooses. The details come from the GI Informatik-Monitor 2026/27.

### The compulsory part

Since the school year 2024/25, the monitor says, Informatik has been taught as a compulsory subject in grades 9 and 10 at every general school type in Lower Saxony. That is a real step, but it comes late: in a G9 system, where the Oberstufe begins with the Einführungsphase in grade 11, grades 9 and 10 are the last years of Sekundarstufe I.

Grundschule covers grades 1 to 4, and there is no Informatik subject there.

### Earlier, if chosen

At a Hauptschule, Realschule or Oberschule, a two-hour Informatik elective can be offered from grade 6. An integrated Gesamtschule can offer it from grade 6 and a cooperative one from grade 7. At a Gymnasium without a profile, Informatik is an optional two-hour subject; Gymnasien with a profile timetable can run it as an elective from grade 8.

Whether any of this exists at your child's school is a question for the school.

**Informatik in Lower Saxony by grade, 2026/27**

| Grades | Who gets it | Form |
|---|---|---|
| 1 to 4 | Nobody | No Informatik subject in Grundschule |
| 6 to 8 | Pupils who choose it, where offered | Two-hour elective at Haupt-, Real- and Oberschule; from grade 7 at a KGS |
| 9 and 10 | Every pupil | Compulsory Informatik at all general school types since 2024/25 |
| 11 | Oberstufe pupils who choose it | Elective in the Einführungsphase |
| 12 and 13 | Oberstufe pupils who choose it | Courses at basic or raised level; written Abitur set centrally |

Source: GI Informatik-Monitor 2026/27, national comparison and Niedersachsen page, read 2 November 2026. Lower Saxony has five Sekundarstufe I school types: Hauptschule, Realschule, Oberschule, Gesamtschule and Gymnasium. The GI counts it among the Länder where only about one Oberstufe learner in twelve takes Informatik.

For a family with a primary-age child this is a long runway. A child of eight has five years before the first guaranteed Informatik lesson, which is plenty of time to discover whether they enjoy building programs, and to arrive in grade 9 able to help the person sitting next to them.

## When does Hannover get its first frost? Ninety years of weather in a few lines of Python

The German weather service has measured the temperature at its Hannover station every day for decades and publishes the whole record for free reuse. Children use it to find the first frosty night of each autumn, and learn how a program can stop looking once it has found what it wants.

### The data, and why it is not from the city

The Landeshauptstadt Hannover publishes maps and lists as open data, but no daily record that suits a question like this one. The Deutscher Wetterdienst (DWD) does: its Climate Data Center offers the daily observations of station 2014, "Hannover", under the Creative Commons BY 4.0 licence. We downloaded the file on 3 November 2026.

It has one line per day from 1 January 1936 to 31 December 2025, with a gap for 1941, 1942 and 1945, years that are missing from the file entirely. The column TNK holds the lowest air temperature of the day, measured 2 metres above the ground.

### The question

The DWD calls a day a Frosttag when the minimum temperature falls below 0 °C. So: on which date did each autumn bring its first frosty night, and has that date moved since the 1950s?

A child's first idea is to look at every day and remember the frosty ones. The better idea is to stop looking for a year as soon as its first frost is found.

**Version 1: the first frost of every autumn** (Python)
```python
import csv, datetime

first_frost = {}
readings = open("hannover_tag.txt", encoding="latin-1")
for day_row in csv.DictReader(readings, delimiter=";", skipinitialspace=True):
    day = datetime.datetime.strptime(day_row["MESS_DATUM"], "%Y%m%d").date()
    if day.month < 7 or day.year in first_frost:
        continue                      # spring, or this autumn is already done
    if float(day_row["TNK"]) < 0:
        first_frost[day.year] = day   # the first frosty night after 1 July

print("Autumns found:", len(first_frost))
print("Earliest:", min(first_frost.values(), key=lambda d: (d.month, d.day)))
print("Latest:", max(first_frost.values(), key=lambda d: (d.month, d.day)))
```
Our program. It prints Autumns found: 87, Earliest: 1977-09-19 and Latest: 2000-12-18.

The line day.year in first_frost does the stopping: once a year has its first frost, every later day of that year is skipped. The program finds 87 autumns, which is the 90 years from 1936 to 2025 minus the three missing ones, and a good check that nothing has gone astray. The earliest first frost came on 19 September 1977; the latest waited until 18 December 2000.

Comparing decades needs one more idea. Dates do not average well as text, so Version 2 turns each date into a number of days after 30 June, averages those numbers, and turns the result back into a date.

**Version 2: the typical date in three decades** (Python)
```python
def days_after_june(d):
    return (d - datetime.date(d.year, 6, 30)).days

for start in (1950, 1980, 2010):
    years = [y for y in first_frost if start <= y < start + 10]
    mean = sum(days_after_june(first_frost[y]) for y in years) / len(years)
    typical = datetime.date(2026, 6, 30) + datetime.timedelta(days=round(mean))
    print(str(start) + "s:", len(years), "autumns, first frost around", typical.strftime("%d %B"))
```
Our program, run after Version 1. It adds 1950s: 10 autumns, first frost around 21 October, 1980s: 10 autumns, first frost around 02 November and 2010s: 10 autumns, first frost around 27 October.

Here comes the lesson children remember. In this record the 1980s had a later typical first frost than the 2010s. If you only compared those two decades you might say the frost is coming earlier. With every decade side by side, as in the table, the pattern looks different again. Ten autumns are a small sample, single years jump around by weeks, and a careful scientist looks at the whole series before saying what it shows.

**Typical date of the first autumn frost at the DWD Hannover station, by decade (our calculation)**

| Decade | Autumns in the file | First frost around |
|---|---|---|
| 1950s | 10 | 21 October |
| 1960s | 10 | 21 October |
| 1970s | 10 | 12 October |
| 1980s | 10 | 2 November |
| 1990s | 10 | 24 October |
| 2000s | 10 | 1 November |
| 2010s | 10 | 27 October |
| 2020 to 2025 | 6 | 13 November |

Data: Deutscher Wetterdienst, Climate Data Center, daily climate data (KL) for station 2014 Hannover, licence CC BY 4.0, downloaded 3 November 2026. "First frost" is our choice: the first day on or after 1 July with a minimum air temperature below 0 °C, following the DWD definition of a Frosttag. Averages and dates are ours. September first frosts appear in 1936, 1939, 1969, 1977 and 1979.

1. **Ages 6 to 8, at the window.** From October, check the car roofs or the grass each morning and mark the first white morning on a calendar. That is your own first-frost record.
2. **Ages 8 to 10, in Scratch.** Make a list of ten temperatures and let a sprite say the position of the first one below zero, then stop. Add a "stop this script" block and see why it matters.
3. **Ages 10 to 12, in Python.** Run Version 1, then change it to find the last frost of each spring instead. Hint: you can no longer stop at the first match.
4. **Going further.** Add the 1960s, 1970s, 1990s and 2000s to Version 2 and rebuild the table above. Then count, for each decade, how many autumns had their first frost in September.

## When is the Känguru der Mathematik in 2027?

On 8 April 2027, which is unusual. The contest normally falls on the third Thursday in March, but in 2027 that date sits just before the Easter holidays in almost every Land, so the organisers moved it.

### What it is

The Känguru der Mathematik is a multiple-choice maths contest, normally held on the same day in all participating countries. In 2026 more than 920,000 pupils from over 13,000 schools in Germany took part.

Everyone works alone for 75 minutes. Grades 3 and 4, and grades 5 and 6, each get 24 problems; from grade 7 there are 30.

### How to enter

Online registration opens on 1 December 2026 and closes on 31 January 2027. The 2026 figures speak of more than 13,000 schools, so children normally take part through their school: ask the class teacher in December.

In Lower Saxony the Easter holidays in 2027 run from 22 March to 3 April, so the contest falls in the first week back.

We have no connection with the Känguru organisers and do not reuse their problems. The thinking it rewards is the thinking in the frost project: read the question slowly, notice the one detail that changes the answer, and check before you commit.
