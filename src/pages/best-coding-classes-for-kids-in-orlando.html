What are the best coding classes for kids in Orlando?

The best coding class for an Orlando child is live, small enough for the teacher to see every screen, and placed by level rather than age. Modern Age Coders teaches kids aged 6 to 12 live online: blocks and Scratch, then Roblox games, Python and AI basics, in batches of fifteen to twenty or one to one, on Eastern Time. The first lesson is free.

Orlando parents have a lot of choice: camps near the theme parks, after-school clubs, apps that promise to teach coding through games. This page is a buyer’s guide written from the teacher’s side of the screen. It sets out what to look for in a coding class for a young child, what Florida law says schools should offer, and what an eight-year-old can actually build from public data about their own city. Our Orlando project uses the City of Orlando’s record of every annexation since 1950: 1,012 rows, one per piece of land the city took in. Sorted into decades and drawn as a histogram, it shows a child how their city grew, with a program they wrote themselves.

Florida schools

What Florida says young children should get

Our plain-English reading of section 1007.2616 of the Florida Statutes, as published by the Florida Legislature and checked on 18 October 2026.

Florida law defines computer science as “the study of computers and algorithmic processes”, including how hardware and software are designed, how they are used and how they affect society, and it says coding and programming are part of it. For younger children the statute is permissive rather than strict: school opportunities may include coding instruction in elementary school and middle school, and elementary and middle schools may set up digital classrooms where students practise skills such as coding and working with images. Firm course requirements start in middle and high school. Further up the ladder, fewer than half of Florida high schools had a foundational course in 2023 to 2024: 463 of 1,192, according to Code.org (2025), 2025 State of AI + CS Education. For an Orlando parent of an eight-year-old, that means early coding at school is possible but not guaranteed, which is where an outside class helps.

A note on sources

The Florida Department of Education site refused our request on 18 October 2026, so we quote the statute itself. Ask your child’s school what it offers in the elementary grades.

Signature project

How did Orlando grow? A decade-by-decade histogram

The City of Orlando publishes a record of every annexation, each time the city took in new land, with the year it happened. Children count them by decade and draw the bars themselves.

1. Count the rows

The dataset holds 1,012 records from 1950 to 2026. A child loads the year column and checks the first and last years before doing anything else, because checking the edges is how good programmers start.

2. Make the bins

Integer division does the magic: year // 10 * 10 turns 1973 into 1970 and 2002 into 2000. A dictionary then counts how many records land in each decade.

3. Draw the bars

Printing one # for every ten records gives the text histogram above. Older children redraw it with matplotlib and compare: does the picture tell the same story as the numbers?

The busiest single years in the file are 1973, with 55 records, and 2002, with 48. The 2000s bin is the tallest, at 229. Then comes the question we always ask: does the shortest bar mean Orlando stopped growing in the 2020s? No. That decade has only run from 2020 to 2026, so its bin is unfinished. Spotting an incomplete bin is a grown-up data skill, and nine-year-olds get it once they see it.

Our counts, not official statistics

These are our counts of records in one download, not the city’s official history of its growth. One annexation record can be a tiny lot or a large tract, so more records does not always mean more land, and 78 of the records before 1970 list zero acres, which most likely means the size was not recorded. The City of Orlando states no licence on this dataset; we link to it and quote counts only.

That zero is a lesson for older kids too. If they add up the acres column, the early decades will look tiny, not because Orlando grew slowly but because the information is missing. A child who learns to ask whether zero means none or unknown has learned something many adults never do.

Coding and AI

Should an Orlando child learn to code if AI can do it?

Yes, for the same reason children still learn arithmetic after calculators.

A child who asks an AI to draw the annexation histogram will get a picture. They will not know that the last bar is unfinished, or that the zeros in the acres column mean missing information. A child who wrote the twelve lines of Python that make the bars will notice both, because they saw every number go in.

So we teach AI as part of coding, not instead of it. Children learn what an AI tool is, which ones are meant for their age, and how to check what it says. The tools come after the thinking, never before it.

The aim for a young Orlando coder is simple: build things, ask questions, and never take a confident answer on trust. The longer argument is in is coding still worth learning in 2026.

Orlando kids coding questions

What Orlando parents ask us most.

What makes a coding class good for a young child?

A live teacher in every lesson, placement by level rather than age, a small group or one to one, and real projects. A free first lesson lets you check all four.

What age can my child start coding?

We teach children from about age 6. Younger ones start with blocks and puzzles; by around third or fourth grade many are ready for Scratch games or Roblox.

Are the classes in person in Orlando?

No. All classes are live and online, taught from India. There is no Orlando center, so children join from home.

What time are the classes?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Times are always confirmed in Eastern Time.

What happens when the clocks change?

Your class keeps its Orlando time. Our teachers adjust, because India has no daylight saving, and we message the confirmed Eastern Time ahead of November 1, 2026 and again ahead of March 14, 2027.

What is the Orlando histogram project?

Children count the city's 1,012 annexation records by decade and draw a bar for each. In our 18 October 2026 count, the 2000s were the busiest decade with 229 records.

Do Florida elementary schools teach coding?

Florida Statutes section 1007.2616 says school opportunities may include coding in elementary and middle school, with firm course requirements from middle school. Ask your school what it offers.

Can my child learn about AI safely?

Yes. Our AI literacy course explains how AI works and where it goes wrong, and only uses tools suitable for the child's age. Children never need an adult-only account.

Will my child need a tablet or a computer?

A laptop or desktop works for every course. Some early blocks lessons run on a tablet, but typed code and Roblox Studio need a computer.

How do I find the prices?

Look for the fees panel just above the booking form: a no-cost first class, then a monthly group price and a monthly private price, shown in US dollars and payable only after that first class.
