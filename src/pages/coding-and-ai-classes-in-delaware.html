Coding and AI classes in Delaware

What does Delaware law say about computer science in high school?

Title 14, section 4139 of the Delaware Code says that by the 2020-2021 school year all public high schools, including charter schools, shall offer at least 1 computer science course, and from 2018-2019 a qualifying course may count as a math credit beyond Algebra I, Geometry and Algebra II. Modern Age Coders adds live online classes for Delaware students, in batches or 1-on-1.

Delaware settled its computer science policy in a single section of state law, and it is short enough to read in a minute. It sets a course-offering duty with a deadline, lets the right kind of computer science course replace one of the required math credits other than Algebra I, Geometry and Algebra II, and allows a school to ask for a waiver. Because the state has only a few dozen high schools, its published access percentages jump around, and its two Code.org figures for the same year do not agree. That makes Delaware the perfect place to teach percentages carefully, which is what the state parks project on this page does.

The law

Section 4139, Delaware's computer science law, in three parts

Quoted from the Delaware Code, Title 14, Chapter 41, read on the state's official Code site on 17 October 2026. The Department of Education's own site blocked our requests, so the statute is the primary source here.

14 Del. C. 4139, Computer science (enacted by 81 Del. Laws, c. 185)
SubsectionWhat it says
(a) OfferingBy the 2020-2021 school year, all public high schools, including charter schools, shall offer at least 1 computer science course
(a) StandardsThe Department of Education was directed to develop computer science standards and seek State Board approval no later than December 31, 2017
(b) Math creditFrom the 2018-2019 school year, an advanced placement, honors, college prep or integrated computer science course meeting the computer science and mathematics standards may satisfy a mathematics graduation credit
(b) The exceptionsIt cannot replace Algebra I, Geometry, Algebra II or the equivalent courses
(c) WaiverA high school may ask for a waiver of the offering duty if it would be a burden financially or programmatically; the Secretary of Education or designee decides

Who sets diploma rules

Under section 122(b)(3) of the same title, rules on certificates and diplomas are proposed by the Secretary and approved by the State Board, so the detailed credit list lives in regulation, not in section 4139.

Four course types count

The math option is limited to advanced placement, honors, college prep or integrated computer science courses that meet both sets of standards. An introductory elective alone does not qualify under the text.

A waiver is possible

Because subsection (c) allows waivers, "every high school offers a course" is the rule rather than a guarantee. A family should confirm what their own school runs this year.

What we could not read: the state standards the Department was told to develop, and any list of waivers granted. Both would be on education.delaware.gov, which returned a security block. So this page does not print a standards adoption date or say how many schools hold waivers.

They disagree

For 2024 to 2025 the trend tab says 61% while the Most Recent tab says 36 of 57. Dividing 36 by 57 gives 63.2%. One school separates them, and the sheet does not explain which is current.

A survey, not a register

The Most Recent tab names its Delaware basis as "School Course Catalogs and Teacher Surveys", so it depends on what catalogs list and teachers report.

Enrollment stops in 2022

Participation values are 4.0% for 2020 to 2021 and 4.9% for 2021 to 2022, with nothing published after that, so current enrollment is unknown from this source.

With 57 high schools in the count, each school is worth 1.75 percentage points, by our arithmetic. That is why Delaware's line jumps from 68% to 40% and back to the low sixties: a handful of schools adding or dropping a listed course moves the share a lot. The College Board shows a steadier picture of advanced courses, with 69 Delaware schools giving AP exams in both 2023 and 2024 and the public share rising from 45 to 48. Signature project

Shares of Delaware state park programs, and the missing two percent

The Division of Parks and Recreation publishes every public program in Delaware State Parks as public-domain open data. Students turn program counts into percentages and discover that rounding has rules of its own.

Programs by park, five largest values (our counts from the State Park Programs dataset, 4,077 rows, downloaded 17 October 2026)

1. Count and divide

The dataset reports 4,077 rows and the download holds 4,077. Students count programs per park, divide each count by the total, and multiply by 100.

2. Round, then add

Round each of the 26 shares to a whole number and they sum to 98. Round to one decimal and they sum to 99.7. Nothing is wrong with the data; small remainders were dropped 26 times.

3. Fix it honestly

The largest remainder method hands the missing points to the parks with the biggest fractional parts, so the rounded shares total exactly 100. Students code it and compare.

Our percentages, public-domain data

Every count and share here is ours, from one download of a public-domain dataset. The table describes programs listed in the data over its whole span, not attendance and not a current calendar, and it is not a Division of Parks and Recreation statistic.

Two data checks come before any chart. Five of the 26 park values are fragments with one or two rows each, such as "Cape", "Delaware" and a park name typed twice in a row, which belong to larger parks and need cleaning. And while most program start dates fall from 2022 to 2024, two rows say the year 0203, five say 2001 and one says 2028. A student who finds those before publishing a percentage has learned the habit that matters most.

Coding and AI

Will an AI notice that its percentages add up to 98?

Usually not unless asked. Noticing is a skill students build by doing the arithmetic themselves.

An assistant asked for "the share of programs at each park" will round each value and return a tidy table. It will rarely mention that the column totals 98, or that five of the park names are fragments of others. Both problems are invisible unless someone adds the column and reads the labels.

Students who have written the rounding code know to total every percentage column, to say how values were rounded, and to clean labels before grouping. With those habits, AI tools speed them up instead of slipping errors past them.

Delaware made computer science count toward math for good reason: done well, it is careful quantitative reasoning. The longer argument is in whether coding is worth learning when AI can code.

Delaware questions, answered

What Delaware parents and students ask most.

Must every Delaware public high school offer computer science?

Section 4139 of Title 14 says that by the 2020-2021 school year all public high schools, including charter schools, shall offer at least 1 computer science course, though a school can request a waiver.

Can computer science count as a math credit in Delaware?

Yes. From 2018-2019, an AP, honors, college prep or integrated computer science course meeting the standards may satisfy a math credit, except Algebra I, Geometry or Algebra II.

Who decides a Delaware waiver request?

The Secretary of Education or a designee, after the school shows that offering the course would be a financial or programmatic burden.

What share of Delaware high schools offer computer science?

The 2025 Code.org sheet gives 36 of 57 high schools for 2024 to 2025 on one tab and 61% on another; the two do not match.

How many Delaware students take computer science?

The latest published Code.org value is 4.9% of high school students for 2021 to 2022. Nothing newer appears in the sheet.

When are the classes?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays.

Will your classes count toward a Delaware diploma?

No. Diploma credits come from school courses under state rules. Our lessons add depth, projects and AP practice alongside them.

Are AI tools used with younger students?

Only age-appropriate tools, and only after children understand how a computer follows instructions. Older students learn to check AI output line by line.

Can we meet a teacher in person in Delaware?

No. Teaching is live online from India, and we have no Delaware office or classroom.
