Behind the number

Six things that change what a maths tutor costs

Teacher training

The Australian Tutoring Association separates teacher-trained tutors, who hold a degree in education, from tutors who are university students or graduates without one. Training is one of the biggest drivers of the hourly rate.

Primary or secondary

The Association notes that primary-trained teachers as tutors are typically paid less than secondary-trained ones. Senior maths, such as Year 11 and 12 courses, usually costs more than primary help.

One to one or a class

One-to-one sessions cost the family more; a class spreads the teacher across many students, so each pays less and gets less individual attention.

Where you live

The Association says rural rates are typically lower than urban ones. Online lessons take location out of the price altogether.

Experience and standing

Years of teaching and recognised professional contributions push rates up. The Association adds that simply attending workshops is not, of itself, a sign of professional contribution.

Exam pressure

Demand rises before senior exams and selective tests. Starting earlier, and spreading tuition over terms, usually costs less than an intensive burst at the end.

Read the small print

The costs families tend to miss

Payment in advance. Some providers hold several sessions in advance on an ongoing basis. Cluey Learning's published schedule, for example, keeps two or four sessions paid ahead depending on the plan. Know how much is held and how it is refunded.

Notice periods. Ask how much notice you must give to cancel a session or a plan without being charged. A long notice period is a cost if your child's needs change.

Assessments and materials. Some centres offer a free first assessment; others charge for it, or for workbooks. Ask for the total first-term cost.

Travel. A weekly drive to a centre or a tutor's home adds time and fuel across a whole year, especially outside the capitals.

Exam-season surges. Extra sessions crammed in before tests cost more than steady weekly work spread over the year.

Price the teaching, not the hour

How to judge whether maths tuition is worth the money

  • Does the tutor look at your child's working, or only mark the final answers?
  • Can they name the specific ideas your child is missing, such as fractions with unrelated denominators?
  • Is it the same tutor every week, who remembers what went wrong last time?
  • How many students does the tutor teach at once?
  • Is the total cost for a term clear, including anything paid in advance?
  • What happens if your child is not benefiting after a few weeks?
  • Can you watch a lesson?
  • Will you get a progress report that names topics, not just a percentage?

Several of these echo questions the Australian Tutoring Association suggests parents ask any tutoring provider, read 7 October 2026.

Choosing

Group or one to one, and which maths course?

Choose a small group if

  • your child needs steady practice and routine more than one-off rescue
  • they are at a similar level to others in their year
  • you want the lowest monthly cost for weekly maths

Groups hold 15 to 20 learners at a similar level.

Choose one to one if

  • there is one clear gap to close quickly
  • your child is anxious about maths or far ahead
  • a senior student has exams or a selective test coming

Maths tutor costs: what Australian parents ask

How much does a maths tutor cost in Australia?

It depends mostly on the format and the tutor. The Australian Tutoring Association publishes a typical range for teacher-trained tutors, and online and centre-based providers publish per-session prices; one-to-one lessons cost more than classes. The comparison table sets out the published figures we could read at their source.

Why do maths tutors charge such different rates?

The Australian Tutoring Association lists factors including whether the tutor is teacher-trained, primary or secondary trained, their experience and professional standing, and the area they work in, with rural rates typically lower than urban ones.

Is group maths tuition cheaper than one-to-one?

Usually, per child. The Association notes that one-to-one sessions tend to levy a higher fee, and that the individual fee may be significantly lower in a class. The trade-off is less individual attention.

Are there extra costs on top of the session fee?

Sometimes. Check whether sessions are paid in advance, the notice needed to cancel, assessment or materials fees and travel. With us there is no joining fee and no term contract.

How many hours of maths tutoring does a child need?

There is no fixed number. One or two hours a week through a term is common; a child closing one specific gap may need less, and a student preparing for senior exams may need more.
