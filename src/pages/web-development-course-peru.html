What you learn

What should a web development course teach in 2026?

The three classic layers, plus two things that used to be optional and are no longer: accessibility and version control.

Structure and style

HTML that describes what each part of a page is, a heading, a list, a form, and CSS that makes it look right on a phone and a desktop. Good structure is also the first half of accessibility.

Behaviour

JavaScript for interaction, then React for larger interfaces built from components. Learners write the logic themselves before reaching for a library.

The server

A back end in Node.js or Python, a database, logins and the security habits that go with them. Learners deploy at least one complete site that other people can use.

Accessibility

Text alternatives, keyboard use, contrast, clear link text and the page language, checked against WCAG 2.2. In Peru this is now the legal floor for public services.

Version control

Git from week one: small commits, branches, pull requests and code review with the mentor.

Reading the specs

MDN, the W3C Recommendation and framework docs are in English. Learning to read them is part of the course, not homework.

Our Spanish page, curso de desarrollo web en inglés, explains front end, back end and full stack in detail, with INEI's figures on how quickly Peruvian homes got online and a lesson on escaping user text. This page concentrates on the rule that changed Peruvian web work in 2025.

Peru's 2025 rule

Do websites in Peru have to be accessible?

Public ones do. On 24 June 2025 the Secretariat of Government and Digital Transformation of the PCM approved a guideline that obliges public bodies to adopt WCAG 2.2 and later versions as the minimum accessibility standard for their digital services, replacing the 2009 rules.

Deadlines to apply Peru's 2025 digital accessibility guideline, by type of public body
Public bodyDeadline, months
Executive, Legislature and Judiciary12
Autonomous constitutional bodies12
Regional governments and public universities12
Public companies of regional and local governments or under FONAFE12
Local governments, Type A and Type C18
Other bodies covered by Ley 27444 not listed above18
Local governments, Type B24

Source: Resolución de Secretaría de Gobierno y Transformación Digital N° 001-2025-PCM/SGTD, Lima, 24 June 2025, article 2 ("plazo no mayor de"). Deadlines run from the resolution; other bodies apply it "en función a sus capacidades y recursos". The earlier rules, RM 126-2009-PCM, were repealed by RM 100-2025-PCM on 13 May 2025.

What the guideline actually requires

Section 6.2 of the guideline says public bodies "incorporan de forma obligatoria" the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines, WCAG, "en su versión 2.2 y posteriores versiones, como estándar mínimo", applied progressively. It defines level AA as the one "que se solicita generalmente a las administraciones públicas".

It goes further than the international text in one Peruvian direction: a section on accessibility in Peruvian Sign Language and in Peru's original languages, and a description of level AAA that mentions an interpreter of "lengua de señas peruana" for multimedia.

Who does the work

The guideline names the team roles every public body needs for accessibility, at minimum: a front-end and back-end developer, a user experience writer, and a UX/UI designer. Bodies may also designate a Coordinador de Accesibilidad Digital, and gob.pe shows municipalities and public universities naming one through 2025 and 2026.

There is also a public reward: a Sello de Accesibilidad Digital. On 23 December 2025, gob.pe reported that Conadis, Peru's council for people with disabilities, received the 2025 seal at level AA for its digital document desk.

For a learner, the point is practical. The developer role in that guideline is exactly the job a web course prepares you for, and the state now expects that developer to know WCAG. Private companies are not bound by this guideline, but many supply software to public bodies, and accessible sites are simply better sites. We teach it to everyone.

WCAG 2.2

What is WCAG 2.2, and what does level AA mean?

WCAG is the W3C's Web Content Accessibility Guidelines. Version 2.2 is a W3C Recommendation, in its current form dated 12 December 2024, and it is the version Peru's guideline names.

How WCAG 2.2 is organised, and the three conformance levels as Peru's guideline describes them
PartWhat it isExample
4 principlesPerceivable, operable, understandable, robustRobust: works with assistive technology

Success criteriaTestable statements, each with a level1.1.1 Non-text Content, level A
Level AThe minimum level of conformance (Peru's guideline, 5.19)Every image has a text alternative
Level AAThe level generally asked of public administrations (5.20)1.4.3 Contrast (Minimum): at least 4.5:1
Level AAAThe high level; includes a Peruvian Sign Language interpreter for multimedia (5.21)Extended audio description

The criteria beginners break most

In our experience, first websites fail the same few criteria again and again: images without a text alternative (1.1.1), links that say "click here" (2.4.4, Link Purpose), a page with no declared language (3.1.1, Language of Page) and pale grey text on white (1.4.3, Contrast). The first three are level A, the most basic level.

All four are cheap to get right on the first day and tedious to fix on a finished site with hundreds of pages. That is why we teach them in the HTML module, not at the end.

A detail that tells you the version

WCAG 2.2 marks success criterion 4.1.1, Parsing, as "Obsolete and removed". Older courses and checklists still teach it as a requirement. A learner who notices that, and knows why browsers made it unnecessary, has read the actual Recommendation rather than a summary of a summary.

Reading the source is a habit we push throughout: when a learner asks whether something is required, the mentor sends them to the W3C text first.

A lesson from the course

How can a program check whether a web page is accessible?

By reading the page as structure, the way a browser does, rather than searching it as text. Our learners discover the difference with a small page about tours in Cusco that has four deliberate mistakes.

The page

The page has four images. One has a proper description. One, a logo, has no alt attribute at all. One, a route map, has a data-alt attribute, which looks like alt text to a human but is ignored by screen readers. One, a decorative divider, has an empty alt="", which is correct: it tells assistive technology to skip it.

There is also no lang on the html element, and one link whose whole text is "click here".

The first attempt

Version 1 does what most beginners try: count the images, count the occurrences of alt=", and subtract. It runs, prints a reasonable answer and gives a false sense of safety.

Before running Version 2, the mentor asks the learner to list every problem by eye. Most find the logo; fewer catch the map, the language or the link.

Python's html.parser hands the program each tag with its attributes already separated, so "alt" in a means exactly "this image has an alt attribute", whatever else is written nearby. It also lets the empty alt="" through, because the attribute is present, and that is the right behaviour for a decorative image. A text search can do none of this reliably.

The wider lesson reaches far beyond accessibility. HTML, JSON, CSV and dates all look like text, and every one of them has a parser in the standard library. A developer who reaches for the parser instead of a search writes code that survives the next page, not just this one. Professional tools such as browser accessibility audits are built on the same idea, and once a learner has written a small checker, they understand what those tools can and cannot see: no program can tell you whether "Machu Picchu at sunrise" is a good description of the photo. A person still has to judge that.

  1. Fix the page. Correct all four problems in the HTML and run Version 2 again until it prints 0 problems.
  2. Add a check. Flag any image whose alt text is the file name, such as alt="logo.png".
  3. Spanish and English. Our Cusco page mixes a Spanish heading with English text. Which lang should the page declare, and how does WCAG 3.1.2 handle a passage in another language?
  4. Go further. Read success criterion 1.4.3 in the W3C text and write a function that computes the contrast ratio of two colours.

The page, code, output and tasks are ours, written for this page and run with Python 3 on 9 November 2026. The tour page is invented.

Our plan, adjusted to each learner. Our full stack course for adults runs 12 to 14 months from zero; the HTML and CSS course is four months. We promise no job or salary.

Can AI build the website for me?

AI tools now produce working pages quickly, and our professional courses show how to use them. But generated pages fail accessibility checks often, because the model reproduces the average of the web, and the average web is not accessible.

The developer who knows WCAG can review what the tool produced and fix it. The one who does not cannot even see what is wrong. Our page on vibe coding, in Spanish, covers this balance.

Language of the classes

Are the classes in Spanish?

No. Our classes are live and online, and they are taught in English only, by mentors based in India who adjust to each learner.

How the English works in class

Mentors use plain sentences, slow down for new terms, and say a thing twice in different ways when a learner hesitates. Questions in unpolished English are welcome; most of the hour is spent looking at the same code and the same browser window.

The vocabulary of the web is English: element, attribute, selector, request, response. Even Peru's own guideline refers to WCAG "por su abreviatura en inglés" and links each criterion to its English page.

Where Peru stands in English

EF's English Proficiency Index 2025 gives Peru 519 points overall, which EF calls moderate, and gives Lima 555, which falls in its high band.

A learner near the national level will find the first weeks demanding and then easier. A learner in Lima's band will mostly notice that the documentation finally makes sense. The free class is the honest test.

Questions from Peru

Web development course in Peru: questions and answers

Are the classes in Spanish?

No. We teach web development in English only, live and online. Mentors based in India use short, clear sentences, take your pace and accent into account and explain any term again in other words. The web's own vocabulary and documentation are English.

Can I learn web development from zero?

Yes. The full stack course and the HTML and CSS course both start from nothing. You build a first page in the opening weeks.

Do websites in Peru have to meet WCAG 2.2?

Public ones do. Since 24 June 2025, RS 001-2025-PCM/SGTD and its guideline oblige public bodies to adopt WCAG 2.2 and later versions as the minimum, with deadlines of 12 to 24 months by type of body. Private sites are not covered by that guideline.

What is WCAG level AA?

The middle of WCAG's three conformance levels. Peru's guideline describes it as the level generally asked of public administrations; it includes, for example, a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1 for normal text.

Do I need to learn React?

Not at first. We teach HTML, CSS and plain JavaScript properly, then React. Learners who start with React often cannot explain what it is doing underneath.

Python or Node.js for the back end?

Both are good. Node.js keeps one language from front to back; Python suits people who also want data or automation. The free class helps you choose.

What time are classes?

Any day, any time, flexible: choose a batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at the hour that suits your family. India is 10 hours 30 minutes ahead of Peru all year, so 18:30 in Peru is 05:00 the next day for the mentor.

How long until I can build a real site?

A simple accessible site in the first months; a full stack project with a database within about a year for a beginner, depending on practice. We do not promise jobs or salaries.

How much does it cost?

Look at the fee section: the monthly price is in US dollars, the card is charged in dollars, and soles appear only after your bank converts it. Visitors from India see rupees. The first class costs nothing and needs no card.

Will I get a certificate?

Our own certificate of completion after the final exam. It is not a university degree, and it is not the Peruvian state's Sello de Accesibilidad Digital, which public bodies apply for.

Are you connected with the PCM or Conadis?

No. We quote their public documents to explain the rules; we have no connection with the PCM, its Secretariat of Government and Digital Transformation, Conadis or any public body.
