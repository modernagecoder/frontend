## What should a parent do with that number?

It points both ways, and honest reading matters more than spin.

Fourteen or more hours a year is a serious, structured introduction, and more than most education systems anywhere specify. **It is also roughly one afternoon a term.** A weekly class is not competing with it. It is a different quantity of contact time.

The school starts the subject. Whether anything follows is a separate decision, and it should be made with the number in front of you rather than in the dark, which is where most families comparing after-school options are.

The other useful detail is the name. Not programming, not app building. **Computational thinking**, with coding as the thing you do to practise it. That ordering is deliberate, and it is the same ordering we use.

## What does P6 leave a learner able to do?

A pupil who has been through three levels usually arrives with the reasoning in place and the fluency absent.

**They can** talk about sequence, condition and repetition, and look at a problem and see parts.

**They generally cannot yet** write typed code from a blank file, read an error message and act on it, or find their own mistake without somebody beside them.

That gap is not a failure of the school. It is what fourteen hours a year buys, and it is a good use of fourteen hours. Closing it is a fluency problem, and fluency needs frequency, which is the one thing a school timetable cannot give a single subject.

| Stage | What we would do |
|---|---|
| P1 to P3 | Before the formal allocation. Ordering, counting, explaining what a thing will do before it does it |
| P4 to P6 | Alongside the school's own work. Stay out of its way, build the typing fluency it has no room for |
| Junior secondary | The best window in the system. Blocks to Python, no public examination pressure yet |
| Senior form | Reduce rather than add. One lesson a week and a narrow goal, or a deliberate pause |

## The Hong Kong student data project

Every market page here works through one analytical trap. Hong Kong's is hiding inside the figure this page is built on: **a bound is not a value.**

The learner is given the published line and asked how many hours of coding a Hong Kong pupil gets across P4, P5 and P6.

Every learner answers 42. Fourteen times three. Arithmetic they could do at seven, from a primary source, and the number is wrong.

The published figure is not 14. It is **14 or more**, a floor with nothing above it. The honest answer is "at least 42 hours, possibly a great deal more, and the source does not say". Forty-two is the smallest total the data permits, presented as though it were the total.

Dropping the plus is the most common error in reading published statistics, and it is almost invisible because the result looks like a normal number. "10,000+ students" becomes 10,000. "Ages 6 to 67" becomes 67. A minimum wage becomes a wage. Once the qualifier is gone nobody downstream can tell it was ever there, and every calculation built on it inherits a false precision.

The second error is quieter. Even if the figure were exactly 14, multiplying by three assumes all three years are identical, and the source says three *levels* of sequential material, which is a reason to expect they are not.

So the learner writes the program twice: once returning a single number, once returning a lower bound with a note about what is unknown. Then they are asked which version they would want if they were the parent making the decision. Nobody picks the single number.

This page states "14 or more" everywhere the figure appears, including the table above, for exactly that reason.

## Does coding still matter when AI can write it?

Hong Kong's own curriculum gives the shortest answer available. It did not name the subject programming. It named it **computational thinking**, and put coding underneath as the way you practise it.

Ask an AI how many hours of coding a Hong Kong primary pupil receives and it returns 42, cleanly, with the multiplication shown. It will not mention that it silently converted a floor into a value, because it was not asked to and the question did not invite it. The output is well formed and the reasoning inside it is unavailable for inspection.

What is left for a person is noticing the plus sign, refusing to multiply a bound, and writing down what is unknown. That is the actual work, and it gets more valuable as generated answers get more fluent, not less.

## Why is the schedule easy from Hong Kong?

This is the one market where the clock is never a problem.

Hong Kong Time is **UTC+8 with no daylight saving**. Indian Standard Time is **UTC+5:30 with none either**. So Hong Kong is exactly two and a half hours ahead in every month of the year, and an after-dinner class here lands in the late afternoon for the teacher.

A slot agreed in September is still the right slot in March. Most of our markets need a conversation twice a year when somebody's clocks change; this one does not.

- The school week is **Monday to Friday**, so the weekend is the ordinary one
- Classes are in **English**; the teacher slows on terminology rather than assuming it
- **Twice a week**, normally eight lessons a month
- Groups of **five to eight**, or **one to one**
- A laptop or desktop, stable internet, working audio, a current browser
