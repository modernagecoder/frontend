In short

Twelve states require a high school computer science course for graduation, according to Code.org (2025), 2025 State of AI + CS Education: Alabama, Arkansas, Indiana, Louisiana, Nebraska, Nevada, North Carolina, North Dakota, Rhode Island, South Carolina, Tennessee and West Virginia. No state yet requires a course that combines AI and computer science. Start years differ: Arkansas began with ninth graders in 2022 to 2023, West Virginia begins with ninth graders in 2027 to 2028. How to read a "No"

A "No" does not mean a state ignores computer science. Many states fund teacher training, publish standards or expect schools to offer a course without making it compulsory for every student, and those policies are separate from the diploma rule shown here.

It also does not mean nothing is coming. Bills and board rules move every session, so a state marked "No" in the 2025 data may have adopted a rule since. Check your state education department before you plan a schedule around this table.

How to read a "Yes"

A "Yes" means a trackable computer science credit is required for a diploma, but the details are set by each state: how much credit, which courses qualify, which graduating class it starts with, and who is exempt.

Those details are in the next section for the twelve states, with the wording we could read on a state site and a plain note where a state page could not be reached on 17 October 2026.

Start years are not the same thing as adoption years

A state can pass a rule years before it bites. North Carolina’s law dates from 2023, but it applies to students entering high school in the 2026 to 2027 school year. West Virginia’s bill passed in 2025 and applies to ninth graders entering in 2027 to 2028.

For a family, the useful question is not "does my state have a rule?" but "does it apply to my child’s graduating class?" A freshman who started before the first covered year is usually outside it.

Two rules that look unusual

North Dakota is described in the 2025 report as "the only state with a computer science and cybersecurity graduation requirement." The state’s own summary lets a cybersecurity unit meet it, and waives it for a student who completed the school’s integration plan.

Rhode Island sets a half credit as the default and lets it be earned in middle or high school, tied to proficiency in the state CS standards rather than seat time alone.

What the numbers say

In 2024 to 2025, 76% of high schools in requirement states offered a foundational CS course, against 58% elsewhere. The share of high school students actually enrolled was 10.4% against 5.3%.

Nationally the report puts high school access at 60% and enrollment at 6.1%, the published national figures, not an average we worked out from the states.

What they do not say

States that adopt a rule may already have been investing in teachers and courses, so some of the gap may predate the rule. The report itself words it as more growth in requirement states, not proof of cause.

The report also notes participation data were missing for Nevada and incomplete for North Carolina, North Dakota and Rhode Island, so the requirement-state averages rest on fewer states in some years.

Math or science credit

When a CS course counts toward math or science

After the start year, this is the rule families ask about most often, because it decides whether computer science squeezes out another class or replaces one. Here is what the state sources we could read say.

Ways a CS credit can count, from the state sources we could read (17 October 2026)
StateWhat the CS credit can stand in for
North DakotaOne of the three science requirements; before the 2025 change, one of the math requirements
West VirginiaOne math credit or one personalized education plan credit, earned in grades 8 to 12
ArkansasThe memo says the rule does not necessarily increase the number of credits a student needs
Rhode IslandA half credit that can be earned in middle school or high school

Why it matters for planning

Where a CS course can replace a fourth math credit or a science credit, a student can meet the requirement without giving up an elective they care about. Where it cannot, computer science competes with band, a language or a second science.

West Virginia’s bill adds a detail families will appreciate: a math-substituting CS course is to be shown as equivalent to a high school math course on the transcript for admission to a state higher education institution.

What counts as computer science

States draw the line differently. West Virginia’s bill says computer science "does not include the study of everyday uses of computers and computer applications, such as keyboarding". A typing or office-software class will not meet a rule written that way.

Ask the school counselor for the state’s list of qualifying course codes. Arkansas, for example, checks transcripts for course codes in specific ranges rather than course titles.

AI and the requirement

Is AI part of any state graduation requirement yet?

Not in the 2025 data. The report’s headline count is "0 out of 50" states requiring an AI plus CS course for graduation.

Where it is heading

The report’s rubric says a state would meet an AI plus CS graduation requirement only if every qualifying course includes what AI is, how it works, how to design AI applications and its impact on society.

West Virginia is the nearest case in the report: the state plans to let a computer science course that includes AI count toward its new requirement.

What that means for a student now

A student meeting a CS requirement today is mostly learning programming, data, networks and the impact of computing. Those are exactly the foundations an AI course builds on, so nothing learned now is wasted if AI is added later.

For younger teens, the safest AI learning is about how models work, using tools whose terms allow their age. We never put an under-18 student on an adults-only chatbot.

Computer science is still worth a credit. Ask the counselor whether a CS course can stand in for a math, science or elective credit in your state; where it can, it costs no extra slot.

Try it yourself

Count the table with ten lines of Python

A small exercise our high school students do with any published table: copy it into a CSV file, then let code do the counting so you can check a report’s headline figure yourself.

  1. Copy the first two columns into a spreadsheet and export them as CSV with the two column names in the comment.
  2. Run the script with Python 3. csv.DictReader turns each row into a dictionary keyed by the header.
  3. Read the counts. Counter tallies every distinct value in a column in one pass.
  4. Ask a new question, such as how many of the twelve a state source confirmed, by adding the third column and changing one condition.

The sorted list is the same idea as an alphabetical class roster: once the data are in a file, ordering, filtering and counting take one line each. Adding the third column turns the exercise into a check on our own work, because a student can count how many of the twelve we confirmed at a state source (five) and how many rest on the report alone (seven).

CS graduation requirements: common questions

How many states require computer science to graduate?

Twelve, according to Code.org (2025), 2025 State of AI + CS Education: Alabama, Arkansas, Indiana, Louisiana, Nebraska, Nevada, North Carolina, North Dakota, Rhode Island, South Carolina, Tennessee and West Virginia. The data were finalized on December 1, 2025.

Does any state require an AI course to graduate?

Not in the 2025 report, which counts zero of 50 states with an AI plus computer science graduation requirement. West Virginia plans to let a CS course that includes AI count toward its new requirement.

When does the North Carolina requirement start?

The NC Department of Public Instruction says it applies to all students entering high school in the 2026-27 school year.

When does the West Virginia requirement start?

House Bill 2411, as enrolled in 2025, applies beginning with the entering ninth grade class in the 2027-2028 school year. The credit can be earned in grades 8 through 12.

Can computer science count as a math or science credit?

Often, but it depends on the state. North Dakota lets the required unit satisfy one of its three science requirements, and West Virginia lets it substitute for one math credit or one personalized education plan credit. Ask your school counselor for your state's rule.

Does a keyboarding or office software class count?

Usually not. West Virginia's law, for example, says computer science does not include the study of everyday uses of computers and computer applications, such as keyboarding. Check the state's list of qualifying courses.

My state has no requirement. Should my child take CS anyway?

It is worth it for most students. Ask whether a CS course can stand in for another subject in your state, and remember that the programming and data skills carry into science, math and many college majors.

Is this table up to date?

It reflects the 2025 report, with data finalized on December 1, 2025. Laws passed since then will not appear, so confirm with your state education department or school counselor before planning around it.

What times are your classes?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. We teach US students in every time zone.

Can you help with a school CS assignment?

We teach the concepts behind the assignment so the student can solve it themselves. We do not write graded work for students.

Is Modern Age Coders connected with Code.org or any state?

No. We are an independent online school. This page reports their published information and links to it.
