Q2. What are the stages?

What are the stages of NLP in Class 10?

The five stages of NLP are lexical analysis, syntactic analysis, semantic analysis, discourse integration and pragmatic analysis, in that order. Each one checks the text at a deeper level than the one before: words, then grammar, then meaning, then the link between sentences, then the intended purpose.

StageOne-line definitionExample that passes or fails it
1. Lexical analysisBreaks a long text into paragraphs, sentences and words, and identifies each word"Rohan bought mangoes. They were sweet." becomes two sentences and their words
2. Syntactic analysis (parsing)Checks that the words follow the grammar of the language"Mangoes bought Rohan sweet were" fails: the order breaks the rules
3. Semantic analysisChecks that the grammatically correct sentence actually makes sense"The mango sang loudly" passes grammar but fails meaning
4. Discourse integrationLinks a sentence with those before and after itWorking out that "They" in the second sentence means the mangoes
5. Pragmatic analysisFinds the intended meaning in the real situation"Can you pass the salt?" is a request, not a question about ability

How students lose marks here

  • Mixing up syntactic and semantic. Syntax is grammar; semantics is meaning. A sentence can pass the first and fail the second, never the reverse in a useful exam example.
  • Calling discourse "the meaning". Discourse is about how sentences connect, especially pronouns like it, he and they.
  • Skipping the example. A definition alone usually earns half the marks for a stage.
  • Writing "parsing" for lexical. Parsing is the other name for syntactic analysis.

A one-mark form of the same idea: "Which stage would reject a grammatically correct sentence that makes no sense?" Answer: semantic analysis.

Q3. Applications and chatbots

Applications of NLP, and script bot vs smart bot

A chatbot is a program that holds a conversation with a person in text or speech. A script bot follows a fixed, pre-written flow; a smart bot uses NLP and machine learning to understand open questions and improves as it sees more data.

Point of differenceScript botSmart bot
How it worksFollows a script: buttons, menus or set keywordsUnderstands free text using NLP
Language processingLittle or noneLarge: intent, context and meaning
LearningDoes not learn; changes only when someone edits the scriptLearns from more data and conversations
Data it usesA small set of fixed repliesLarge databases and other resources
Building itEasy, often with no codingNeeds coding and training
ExampleA school fee bot that offers "1 for dates, 2 for receipts"A voice assistant that answers "Will I need an umbrella in Pune tomorrow?"

A two-mark question asks for two differences; four rows of this table cover a four-mark one. Write each difference as a pair, script bot first, so the examiner can tick both halves.

Applications of NLP the syllabus names

Voice assistants

Turn speech into text, find the intent and reply, such as setting an alarm when told "wake me at six".

Auto-generated captions

Speech in a video is converted to text on screen as it plays, which helps viewers with hearing loss.

Language translation

Text in one language is rewritten in another while keeping its meaning, not word by word.

Sentiment analysis

Decides whether a review or post is positive, negative or neutral. This is also the practical case study in Orange.

Text classification

Puts text into categories, such as sorting emails into spam and not spam, or news into sport and politics.

Keyword extraction

Pulls the most important words out of a long text, so a report can be tagged or summarised quickly.

Why is any of this hard for a computer? Human language is ambiguous ("I saw her duck"), the same idea can be said many ways, and it keeps changing. Those three features of natural language are a common two-mark question in their own right. Short contrasts of this kind are collected on CBSE Class 10 AI difference between questions.

Q4. Normalise this text

Text normalisation class 10: the steps, worked on real text

Text normalisation is the cleaning of a whole corpus so a machine can work with it: the text is cut into sentences and tokens, low-value words and symbols are dropped, case is made uniform and each word is reduced to a base form. Here it is done on a two-document corpus, step by step.

  1. Sentence segmentation

    Each document here is one sentence, so the corpus gives two sentences.

  2. Tokenisation

    Every word and every punctuation mark becomes a token. Document 1 gives 8 tokens (7 words and the full stop); Document 2 gives 11 (9 words, a comma and an exclamation mark). 19 tokens in all.

  3. Removing stopwords and special characters

    Dropping the, at, we, were, to, her, and, and all punctuation leaves "Grandma tells best stories night" and "listening stories laughed".

  4. Converting to a common case

    Everything becomes lower case, so "Grandma" and "grandma" can never be counted as two different words.

  5. Stemming or lemmatisation

    Each word is cut to its base: tells to tell, listening to listen, laughed to laugh, stories to story (lemma). The result: grandma, tell, best, story, night, listen, laugh, 7 unique words.

WordAfter stemmingAfter lemmatisationWhat it shows
storiesstoristoryA stem need not be a real word; a lemma always is
listeninglistenlistenBoth methods agree when the suffix is regular
betterbettergoodOnly lemmatisation knows the dictionary root
happilyhappilihappyRules chop endings without checking the result is English

Stemming against lemmatisation, in one answer

Stemming removes affixes by rule, so it is fast but may leave a word that does not exist. Lemmatisation finds the proper dictionary form, so it is slower but always gives a meaningful word. The usual exam choice: lemmatisation when meaning matters, stemming when speed matters.

What comes next: Bag of Words and TFIDF

The 7 normalised words become the dictionary for Bag of Words, and each document becomes a row of counts. TFIDF then scores every word by how special it is to one document. The complete worked tables, three documents from raw text to final TFIDF values, are on Bag of Words and TFIDF for Class 10 AI.

Q5. Practise by mark band

NLP class 10 questions and answers, by marks

Original questions in the shapes the paper uses. Try each before opening the answer. The one-mark answers are short on purpose; the two- and four-mark ones show the length the scheme rewards.

One mark each

  1. Which stage of NLP works out that "she" in the second sentence refers to Asha in the first?
    Answer Discourse integration, which links each sentence with its neighbours.
  2. How many tokens are there in "Is it raining? Take an umbrella, Riya."?
    Answer 10: seven words plus the question mark, the comma and the full stop.
  3. Words such as "is", "an" and "the" that are removed because they add little meaning are called
    Answer Stopwords.
  4. A bank's chatbot only lets you tap one of four buttons. Is it a script bot or a smart bot?
    Answer A script bot: it follows a fixed flow and understands no free text.
  5. Name the NLP application that turns spoken words in a video into text on screen.
    Answer Auto-generated captions.
  6. "Doctors said the new medicine works. It works fast!" How many tokens?
    Answer 11: nine words, the full stop and the exclamation mark. "works" counts twice because tokens are not unique words.
  7. Which gives a meaningful base word: stemming or lemmatisation?
    Answer Lemmatisation.
  8. What is the whole body of text taken from all the documents together called?
    Answer The corpus.

Two marks each

  1. Give two differences between a script bot and a smart bot.
    Answer A script bot follows a pre-written script and has little language processing, while a smart bot understands free text using NLP. A script bot does not learn, while a smart bot improves with more data.
  2. Differentiate between syntactic and semantic analysis with an example.
    Answer Syntactic analysis checks grammar; semantic analysis checks meaning. "The mango sang loudly" is grammatically correct, so it passes the syntactic stage, but it makes no sense, so it fails the semantic stage.
  3. Why is text converted to a common case during normalisation?
    Answer A machine treats "School" and "school" as different words because their characters differ. Lowercasing everything makes them one word, so counts and vectors are correct.
  4. State any two features of natural language that make NLP difficult.
    Answer It is ambiguous: one sentence can have more than one meaning. It is redundant and changing: the same idea can be said in many ways, and words and usage change over time.

Four marks each

  1. Explain any four applications of NLP with one example each.
    Answer Voice assistants turn speech into an action, such as setting an alarm. Translation rewrites text in another language, such as a Hindi sign read in English. Sentiment analysis labels a review positive or negative, such as rating feedback on a product. Text classification sorts text into groups, such as moving spam out of the inbox.
  2. Normalise "Riya plays football. Her brothers play cricket!" Treat "her" as the only stopword. Show each step and give the final dictionary.
    Answer Segmentation: two sentences. Tokenisation: 9 tokens, seven words plus the full stop and the exclamation mark. Remove "Her" and both punctuation marks. Lower case: riya plays football / brothers play cricket. Lemmatise: plays to play, brothers to brother. Dictionary of unique words: riya, play, football, brother, cricket, so 5 words.

Questions about NLP in Class 10 AI

NLP class 10: what students and parents ask

What are the important NLP class 10 questions and answers for 2026-27?

The questions that recur are: the five stages of NLP with examples, differences between a script bot and a smart bot, four applications of NLP, the steps of text normalisation on given text, stemming against lemmatisation, and a Bag of Words or TFIDF calculation on a small corpus. This page answers each of them in the length the marks deserve.

How many marks is NLP in CBSE Class 10 AI?

Unit 6, Natural Language Processing, carries 8 of the 50 theory marks in CBSE 417 for 2026-27. In the 2025-26 sample paper it appeared as five one-mark objective items, one two-mark question and one four-mark question, with choice across units. The sentiment analysis case study is assessed in the practical instead.

What are the stages of NLP class 10?

There are five: lexical analysis splits text into sentences and words; syntactic analysis checks grammar; semantic analysis checks meaning; discourse integration links each sentence with those around it; pragmatic analysis finds the real intent. Learn them in this order with one example each.

What is the difference between a script bot and a smart bot?

A script bot follows a fixed, pre-written flow, has little or no language processing, does not learn and is easy to build. A smart bot uses NLP to understand free text, learns from more data, works with large databases and needs coding. A menu-based fee bot is a script bot; a voice assistant is a smart bot.

What are the steps of text normalisation in class 10?

Sentence segmentation, tokenisation, removing stopwords and special characters, converting to a common case, and stemming or lemmatisation. The output is a clean list of base words for the whole corpus, which then becomes the dictionary for Bag of Words. In a four-mark answer, show the text after every step, not just the final list, because each step usually carries its own share of the marks.

What are the applications of NLP for class 10?

The curriculum names voice assistants, auto-generated captions, language translation, sentiment analysis, text classification and keyword extraction. For a four-mark answer, explain four of them with one real example each, such as spam filtering for text classification. Sentiment analysis is worth knowing best, because it is also the case study you build in Orange for the practical.

Are punctuation marks counted as tokens?

Yes. In tokenisation every word, number and special character, including commas, full stops and exclamation marks, becomes a separate token. Punctuation is only removed in the next step, along with stopwords. Most token-count mistakes come from forgetting this. So "Stop, Riya!" is four tokens: two words, a comma and an exclamation mark.

Yes. TFIDF is listed under text processing in the theory part of Unit 6 for 2026-27, alongside Bag of Words and text normalisation. A small corpus calculation is a natural four-mark question, which is why we give it a page of its own with full tables.

Is sentiment analysis asked in the written paper?

As an application of NLP, yes: you may be asked what it is or for an example. The hands-on sentiment analysis case study in Orange is listed under the practical part of Unit 6, so it is assessed through the practical exam and viva rather than written out in full.
