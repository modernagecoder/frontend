# Math and coding competitions for kids in the USA, by grade

Most families hear about US math and computing competitions one at a time, from a flyer or a friend. This guide puts them side by side: who organizes each one, which grades it serves, what a sitting looks like, and when the 2026 to 2027 season falls. Every row comes from the organizer&rsquo;s own website.

Why grade 6 is a crossroads. Sixth grade is the one year where nearly every math contest overlaps: a sixth grader can sit MOEMS in either division, the Noetic grade 6 paper and a first MATHCOUNTS year. It is also the last ACSL Elementary year and the first Science Olympiad Division B year. Below grade 2, few contests exist, and puzzles, counting games and a first block-coding activity are the honest start.

## Which US math contests should elementary and middle schoolers try?

These contests differ less in topic than in rhythm: monthly through the winter, once in March, or twice a year. Pick the rhythm your child&rsquo;s week can carry, then open its guide.

MOEMS. The [MOEMS preparation guide](/moems-math-olympiad-preparation) suits a fourth to eighth grader who likes a short, deep puzzle, since each sitting holds only five problems. It lays out the five 2026 to 2027 windows, the award ladder and a strategy toolkit, and every problem in it was written by our teachers.

Noetic. Noetic starts at grade 2 and writes a separate problem set for every grade, so a second grader never meets fourth grade content. Our [Noetic contest guide](/noetic-math-contest-preparation) covers the fall and spring windows, the official At-Home Edition, and bar-model drawing for comparison word problems.

Math League. Two separate organizations share this name, which confuses many parents. The [Math League contest guide](/math-league-contest-preparation) untangles mathleague.com from mathleague.org, explains this year&rsquo;s calculator reversal, and shows how a scoring rule with a penalty changes when a student should guess.

Continental Math League. CML asks students to choose between two divisions that differ mainly in how much reading the questions demand. Our [CML preparation guide](/continental-math-league-preparation) sets out the meet dates for grades 2 to 9 and teaches a read, restate, then solve habit for wordy problems.

Several contests compared. If you are weighing MATHCOUNTS against Math Kangaroo and MOEMS for a middle schooler, [our middle school math competitions comparison](/math-competitions-for-middle-school-students) puts the three side by side and shows how much time each format gives a single problem.

Math circles. Not every strong young mathematician wants a stopwatch. The [online math circle guide](/online-math-circle-for-kids) explains what a circle is, where the US network came from, and how an exploration session differs from contest practice,.

Art of Problem Solving. Many contest families use AoPS books or classes. Our [neutral AoPS comparison](/art-of-problem-solving-alternative) sets the options out by criteria rather than claims, so a parent can decide whether live small-group teaching, a self-paced platform or a campus program fits their child.

What changed this year. The Math League (mathleague.com) first said calculators would be banned from its 2026 to 2027 contests, then posted a notice dated July 4, 2026 allowing them this school year and moving the ban to the next. MATHCOUNTS, meanwhile, says it is developing a standalone program for grades 3 to 5; no dates for it were posted on November 1, 2026.

## What coding and robotics competitions are there for US kids and teens?

Computing contests split into three families: written puzzle contests that need no programming (Bebras, ACSL Elementary), programming contests judged by automatic tests (ACSL upper divisions, USACO), and team builds where a robot or device has to work on the day (FIRST, RECF, Science Olympiad).

Bebras. Bebras is the gentlest door into computer science competitions because it is free and needs no coding at all. Our [Bebras practice guide](/bebras-challenge-practice) explains the November 2026 dates, the six age groups and how the scoring rewards leaving a hard task blank, with a stack puzzle of our own.

FIRST LEGO League. A team coach or parent preparing for the last FLL season will want [our FIRST LEGO League coding help](/first-lego-league-coding-help-usa), which reads the BIOGLOW rulebook closely and covers the program&rsquo;s end in 2027 and what replaces it, plus how to plan when only the highest of three matches counts.

FIRST Tech Challenge. High school teams writing Java or Blocks for a robot controller can turn to the [FTC programming help page](/ftc-programming-help). It works through OpModes, the AUTO and TELEOP periods in this season&rsquo;s manual, and a joystick shaping lesson that makes a robot easier to drive precisely.

VEX IQ and RECF Engage. Robotics clubs built on VEX IQ hardware face a new split this season between the REC Foundation and VEX Robotics. The [VEX IQ programming help page](/vex-iq-programming-help) sets out what that split means for events and teaches autonomous routines in short segments with sensor checks.

Science Olympiad. Of the 23 events in each division, only a handful involve code or circuits. Our [Science Olympiad coding events guide](/science-olympiad-coding-events-help) picks those out, such as Codebusters and Electric Vehicle, and teaches the modular arithmetic behind affine ciphers.

ACSL. ACSL rewards students who can trace code and reason about number systems, Boolean algebra and graphs by hand. The [ACSL preparation guide](/acsl-competition-preparation) covers all five divisions and the four contest windows, and pairs hand tracing with short Python checkers.

Middle school and high school roundups. For a single list by school stage, see [coding competitions for middle school students](/coding-competitions-for-middle-school-students), which leans toward first contests and number bases, and [coding competitions for high school students](/coding-competitions-for-high-school-students), which adds USACO, CyberPatriot and the AI challenges and teaches running-time estimates.

Two changes to know about. FIRST says the 2026 to 2027 BIOGLOW season will be the final season of FIRST LEGO League, run in two editions, Founders and Future, and LEGO Education says its own LEGO League launches in August 2027. Separately, the REC Foundation now states that it is not authorized to organize VEX Robotics competitions and that its events will not qualify teams for the 2026 to 2027 VEX Robotics World Championship; RECF runs its own world championship in St. Louis in 2027.

Written, judged or built. A written contest such as Bebras or ACSL needs a quiet half hour and a supervising adult; a judged contest such as USACO needs a long block of time and a working compiler; a robot season needs a team, a coach and months of build time. An ACSL year is four sittings; an FTC team meets several afternoons a week from September.

## What comes after a first contest: projects, clubs and next steps?

After timed contests, many students want something longer and their own: a research project, a club, or a plan for the whole year.

Regeneron ISEF. A high schooler with a software, math or robotics research idea should read the [ISEF computer science project mentoring guide](/isef-computer-science-project-mentoring). It covers the affiliated-fair route, this season&rsquo;s AI-use rules and fair comparisons across seeded trials; the research stays the student&rsquo;s.

Girls Who Code. A student finishing a free club year often asks where to go next. The [Girls Who Code next step page](/girls-who-code-next-step) maps the Clubs and Pathways programs by grade and shows how to move from block code to Python one block at a time.

After the Hour of Code. Since the Hour of Code became the Hour of AI, many teachers and parents are unsure what follows a one-hour activity. [Our after Hour of Code guide](/after-hour-of-code-next-steps) lists the free CodeAI courses by grade and teaches loops by spotting repetition, including the classic fencepost slip.

The calendar. Families entering three or more programs should keep the [US competitions calendar for 2026 to 2027](/us-coding-math-and-ai-competitions-calendar) open while planning, since it lists every dated deadline month by month and marks which ones need an adult to register.

Where independence rules apply. Society for Science says an ISEF project should reflect independent research done by the student and asks that any mentor&rsquo;s part be set out in the research plan; FIRST&rsquo;s participation rules say all work presented at events is the work of the children on the team. We follow both: a teacher explains methods and questions a plan, but never writes the code, the paper or the robot program.

## Can my child enter if their school does not take part?

Usually, but each organizer has its own route, and some results count only when a school runs the sitting. Check before the registration deadline.

**Routes for a student without a participating school (organizers&rsquo; websites, read October 31 and November 1, 2026)**

| Competition | What the organizer says |
|---|---|
| MOEMS | Open to public, private, parochial and home schools, institutes and, with restrictions, individuals |
| Noetic | An At-Home Edition exists, but its scores are not official and do not qualify for awards |
| The Math League | A homeschool package is sent in May for testing at home; official participation runs through an accredited local school |
| mathleague.org | Parents or outside coaches may act as the point of contact for students at a school that will not take part |
| Continental Math League | A student whose school does not participate may enter as a Home School, which need not mean schooled at home |
| Bebras | A homeschool teacher registers as coordinator under a name such as Home School plus initials |
| ACSL | The team advisor can be any teacher, parent or other adult with computer science experience |
| USACO | Open to everyone online, with no fee |

Worked example (ours): a homeschooled sixth grader. A grade 6 student studies at home and wants official results. MOEMS gives five sittings, the Noetic school edition two (only through a school team; the At-Home Edition would be unofficial), Bebras one and ACSL four, so a year with all four holds 5 + 2 + 1 + 4 = 12 official sittings by our count. Counting items: MOEMS is 5 x 5 = 25 problems, Noetic 2 x 20 = 40, and Bebras 15 questions according to its students page, so 80 timed items before ACSL. Our advice would be to drop Noetic if no school team is available, since the home edition earns no awards, and to keep MOEMS (homeschools are named as eligible) with Bebras and ACSL, where a parent can coordinate. Arithmetic and advice are ours.

## How do you choose the right competition for your child?

Five steps, in this order, with the master table open.

1. Find the grade band. Read your child&rsquo;s row in the grade table above and cross off anything outside the organizer&rsquo;s published range.
2. Pick the kind of challenge. Short puzzles (MOEMS, Bebras), longer timed papers (Noetic, Math Kangaroo), team contests (MATHCOUNTS, Science Olympiad), coding with automatic judging (ACSL, USACO), or builds (FIRST, RECF).
3. Check the entry route. Ask the school first. If the school does not take part, use the routes table to see whether a parent, a homeschool group or an outside coach can register, and whether the result would be official.
4. Count the season. Mark every sitting and deadline on one calendar. A student entering three contests can easily face two in one week in November 2026, when MOEMS, Noetic and Bebras windows all open.
5. Keep one, add one. Commit to one contest for the year and try one more for a single sitting. After the season, keep whichever your child would choose to do again without being asked.

November 2026 is crowded. Bebras runs November 9 to 20, MOEMS contest 1 runs November 9 to 30 and the Noetic fall window runs November 12 to 25, all in 2026, while ACSL contest 1 has been open since October 19. For a younger child, two sittings in one fortnight is enough; spring is lighter, with Noetic&rsquo;s second window from April 1, 2027 and The Math League&rsquo;s grade 4 and 5 contests on or after April 15.

Our rule in class. No real contest item, sample question or answer key is put in front of a student. Each lesson takes one idea (an organized list, working backward, a loop, a base change), shows it once on problems our teachers wrote, then mixes problems so the student must choose the idea alone. The organizer&rsquo;s own free samples are kept for a final timed run.

## US math and coding competitions: common questions

### What math competitions are there for elementary school students in the US?

For grades 2 to 5 the main options are the Noetic Learning Math Contest (grades 2 to 8), MOEMS Division E (grades 4 to 6), the Continental Mathematics League (grades 2 to 9), Math Kangaroo USA (grades 1 to 12) and the mathleague.org elementary contests.

### What is the easiest coding competition for a beginner?

The Bebras Challenge is the usual first step. It is free, open to ages 6 to 18, takes 45 minutes, and its organizers say the tasks can be done without any preparation or studying. ACSL's Elementary Division, grades 3 to 6, can follow.

### Is FIRST LEGO League ending?

Yes. FIRST says the 2026 to 2027 BIOGLOW season is the final season of FIRST LEGO League. FIRST is preparing new K to 8 programs, and LEGO Education says its own LEGO League launches in August 2027, so teams can finish this season and then choose a successor.

### What happened to the Hour of Code?

Code.org's organization now calls itself CodeAI, and its site says the Hour of Code is now the Hour of AI, at hourofai.org. Activities still fit one class period, and CSEdWeek runs December 7 to 11, 2026.

### Are VEX IQ competitions still run by the REC Foundation?

Not in the same way. The REC Foundation now states it is not authorized to organize VEX Robotics competitions and that its events will not qualify teams for the 2026 to 2027 VEX Robotics World Championship. RECF runs its own events, including RECF Engage, with a world championship in St. Louis in 2027.

### Can homeschooled students enter these competitions?

Many can. MOEMS names home schools as eligible, Bebras lets a homeschool teacher register, ACSL accepts a parent with computer science experience as advisor, and USACO is open to anyone online. Noetic's At-Home Edition is unofficial and earns no awards, so check each organizer's rule.

### What days and times are your classes?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays.
