# Boston exam schools math prep

Entry to Boston&rsquo;s three exam schools does not hang on one test. Boston Public Schools combines a student&rsquo;s grade point average, weighted at 70 percent, with a MAP Growth test score, weighted at 30 percent, and then invites students within four socioeconomic tiers. That means the math that matters most is the math on report cards this fall and winter, with the January 2027 test as the smaller part. This guide explains the process from the BPS exam school pages as they stood on 31 October 2026, works the composite through with our own numbers, and closes with math practice for students in grades 6 and 8.

At a glance: Schools: BLA, BLS and the O&rsquo;Bryant; Entry grades: 7 and 9; a few seats in 10 at the O&rsquo;Bryant; GPA rule: B or higher; Composite: 70% GPA, 30% MAP Growth; Weekend MAP test: January 9 to 10, 2027; In-school MAP test: January 11 to 15, 2027; Decisions: Late April 2027; Start: A free first class. ## Who can apply to the Boston exam schools for September 2027?

Boston residents entering grade 6, 8 or 9 in September 2026 can apply for September 2027. To be considered, a student needs a GPA of B or higher, must rank at least one exam school, and must have a valid MAP Growth score.

**Boston exam school admission criteria for 2027 to 2028 (Boston Public Schools exam schools pages, read 31 October 2026)**

| Requirement | What BPS publishes |
|---|---|
| The schools | Boston Latin Academy, Boston Latin School, and the John D. O&rsquo;Bryant School of Mathematics and Science |
| Entry grades | All three admit new students in grades 7 and 9; the O&rsquo;Bryant also takes a small number in grade 10 |
| Residency | Both the parent and the student must live in the City of Boston |
| GPA | B or higher, from unweighted report card marks |
| Test | MAP Growth, Reading and Math; both sections must be completed |
| Choice | At least one exam school ranked on the school choice form |
| Composite | 70 percent GPA and 30 percent MAP Growth, plus 10 points for some students |
| Not eligible | Students on a modified curriculum, in MCAS Alternate Assessments, or enrolled in SLIFE |

### What changed for this cycle

BPS says that after a five-year review of its exam school policy, the School Committee accepted revisions in fall 2025, effective from the 2026 to 2027 admissions cycle. School-based points were eliminated.

The 10 extra points now go only to students experiencing homelessness, living in Boston Housing Authority public housing, or in the care of the Department of Children and Families, and they apply within the tier-based share of invitations.

### One invitation, no appeals

A student receives an invitation to only one exam school, set by the composite score, the tier, the ranked choices and the seats. Once invited, the student is assigned to that school; BPS says families cannot make a final decision afterwards.

BPS states that "exam school decisions are final, and we do not accept appeals." Sibling priority does not apply.

Sources: Boston Public Schools [Exam Schools](https://www.bostonpublicschools.org/academics/exam-schools), [How to Apply](https://www.bostonpublicschools.org/academics/exam-schools/how-to-apply) and [Admissions FAQs](https://www.bostonpublicschools.org/academics/exam-schools/admissions-faqs), read 31 October 2026. ## How is the Boston exam school composite score calculated?

In BPS&rsquo;s words, "Students will receive a composite score that is based 30% on MAP Growth and 70% GPA." The GPA is worked out on an 11-point scale and then scaled to 100 by dividing by 11 and multiplying by 100.

**Which marks make up the GPA for 2027 to 2028 entry (BPS How to Apply page, read 31 October 2026)**

| Applying for | Marks used |
|---|---|
| Grade 7 | Grade 6 ELA, Math, Science and Social Studies from Quarter 1 and 2, or Trimester 1 or Semester 1; grade 5 ELA and Math from the last marking period |
| Grade 9 | Grade 8 ELA, Math, Science and Social Studies from Quarter 1 and 2, or Trimester 1 or Semester 1; grade 7 ELA and Math from the last marking period |
| Grade 10 | Grade 9 ELA, Math, Science and Social Studies from Quarter 1 and 2, or Trimester 1 or Semester 1; grade 8 ELA and Math from the last marking period |

Worked example (ours): scaling a GPA. Suppose, after BPS converts a grade 6 student&rsquo;s letter marks, the average on the 11-point scale comes out at 9.9. BPS scales it to 100 by dividing by 11 and multiplying by 100: 9.9 / 11 = 0.9, and 0.9 x 100 = 90. The letter-to-point table itself is on the BPS composite score fact sheet, so check your own child&rsquo;s marks there; the 9.9 here is an invented figure.

Worked example (ours): combining the two parts. Keep the GPA at 90 and suppose the MAP Growth part works out at 80 on a 100-point scale. Then the composite is 0.7 x 90 + 0.3 x 80 = 63 + 24 = 87. Now see what each part is worth. Raising the GPA from 90 to 95 adds 0.7 x 5 = 3.5 points. Raising the test part from 80 to 85 adds only 0.3 x 5 = 1.5 points. How BPS turns a MAP Growth percentile into its 30 percent share is set out in its fact sheet, which we have not reproduced, so the 80 is an assumption for the arithmetic.

### Why the GPA deserves most of the effort

Because the GPA is weighted more than twice as heavily as the test, a steady report card in grade 6 or grade 8 does more for the composite than a strong January morning. And unlike the test, the marks build over months, so effort shows.

BPS gives no extra points for advanced classes and uses unweighted marks. An A+ counts the same as an A, so the target is a consistent A or B+ across all four subjects, not a single star subject.

### Where the marks deadline falls

The FAQs say BPS cannot calculate GPA until early March, after the second quarter marking period closes on January 29, 2027. For a grade 6 or grade 8 student, that means the Quarter 1 and 2 marks being earned right now are the ones that count.

The grade 5 or grade 7 marks used are only ELA and Math from the last marking period, already fixed. So this autumn&rsquo;s math class is where help can still change the picture.

Source: Boston Public Schools [Admissions FAQs](https://www.bostonpublicschools.org/academics/exam-schools/admissions-faqs) (11-point scale, scaling, 70/30 split, unweighted marks) and How to Apply page, read 31 October 2026. Both worked examples use invented figures and are ours.

## When is the MAP Growth test for Boston exam schools in 2027?

BPS students in grades 6 and 8 at non-exam schools take MAP Growth in school between January 11 and 15, 2027. Everyone else who needs the weekend test sits it on January 9 to 10, 2027, after registering by December 4, 2026.

**Boston exam schools timeline for 2027 to 2028 entry (BPS exam schools pages, read 31 October 2026; weekdays checked against the calendar)**

| Date | Step | Who |
|---|---|---|
| Tuesday, October 13 to Friday, December 4, 2026 | Weekend MAP registration; residency verification and ranked choices | Students not in BPS, and BPS grade 9 students for the test |

### Two chances at the test

BPS says students have two opportunities to take MAP Growth, in the spring and winter, and that if a student tests twice, BPS uses the higher achievement percentile in each subject, Reading and Math, separately.

For a grade 6 student, that means a spring MAP taken at school and the January administration can both count. Ask your school which administrations apply to your child.

### Families outside BPS

Private, parochial, Commonwealth charter, METCO and homeschooled students must verify City of Boston residency between October 13 and December 4, 2026, through apply.avela.org/boston, and submit ranked choices at the same time.

BPS stresses this applies even if the family has other children in BPS, receives BPS special education services, or works for the city.

Source: Boston Public Schools [How to Apply](https://www.bostonpublicschools.org/academics/exam-schools/how-to-apply) and Admissions FAQs, read 31 October 2026. BPS links a MAP Growth demo by NWEA for students to try the testing platform.

## How do the socioeconomic tiers and invitations work?

Every Boston address falls in one of four socioeconomic tiers. BPS gives 20 percent of each school&rsquo;s invitations to the highest-ranking students citywide and 80 percent to the highest-ranking students within each tier.

**Invitations, tiers and waitlists (BPS Exam Schools page and Admissions FAQs, read 31 October 2026)**

| Point | What BPS says |
|---|---|
| Citywide share | 20 percent of invitations at each school, to the highest-ranking students citywide |
| Tier share | 80 percent, to the highest-ranking students in each of the 4 socioeconomic tiers |
| Tier assignment | By home address; students compete only with others in their tier for the tier share |
| Seats per tier | Approximately the same number in each tier |
| Order of rounds | Invitations go out in several cycles, with the lowest socioeconomic tier first in each round |
| Tier updates | Every winter, pending data |
| Waitlists | Grades 7, 9 and 10 only, one per school and grade, capped at 100 students, until November 30 |
| Waitlist order | Composite score and a random number within the tier |

### Rank in true order

BPS encourages students to apply to all three exam schools for grades 7 and 9, and to rank them, along with any other BPS schools, in true order of preference. A student who is not invited keeps their current BPS seat where that grade is offered.

Because an invitation is automatic and final, rank only schools you would really accept, in the order you would choose them.

A family cannot change its tier, the seat count or the ranking of other students. The only parts in a student&rsquo;s hands are the report card marks and the MAP Growth result.

## Three worked math examples for grades 6 and 8

Problems we wrote ourselves, two at grade 6 and one at grade 8. They are not MAP Growth items and are not drawn from any BPS or NWEA material.

Example 1 (grade 6): a weighted average. A student&rsquo;s quarter math grade is 60 percent tests and 40 percent homework. Test average 82, homework average 95. What is the quarter grade? Reasoning: 0.6 x 82 = 49.2 and 0.4 x 95 = 38, so the grade is 87.2. The same idea runs the exam school composite, which is why we teach it early: a heavier weight moves the total more.

Example 2 (grade 6): dividing fractions in context. A recipe needs 3/4 cup of oats per batch. How many full batches can be made from 5 cups? 5 divided by 3/4 is 5 x 4/3 = 20/3, which is 6 and 2/3. So 6 full batches, with 2/3 of a batch of oats left, which is 2/3 x 3/4 = 1/2 cup. The trap is answering 6 and 2/3 when the question asks for full batches.

Example 3 (grade 8): a line from two points. A taxi charges a fixed amount plus a rate per mile. A 4-mile ride costs 14 dollars and a 10-mile ride costs 29 dollars. What does a 7-mile ride cost? The slope is (29 minus 14) / (10 minus 4) = 15 / 6 = 2.5 dollars per mile. The fixed part is 14 minus 4 x 2.5 = 4. A 7-mile ride costs 4 + 7 x 2.5 = 21.50 dollars. Check: the answer sits halfway between 14 and 29 because 7 sits halfway between 4 and 10.

### Why these three

Weighted averages, fraction division and linear rates are three ideas that appear constantly in grade 6 and grade 8 classwork, so they serve the GPA and the test together.

Each has a check that needs no calculator: a total between the two parts, a leftover that makes sense, a midpoint that matches.

### How a lesson uses them

The student predicts the answer roughly before working it out. For example 1, "between 82 and 95, nearer 82": a prediction catches a swapped weight at once.

We then ask the student to invent a similar problem for a classmate. Writing a problem is a stronger test of understanding than solving one.

## Ten original practice problems for Boston applicants

Problems 1 to 6 suit a grade 6 applicant; 7 to 10 suit grade 8. Written by our teachers; work them without a calculator, then check against the table.

1. A car travels 135 miles in 2.5 hours at a steady speed. How far does it go in 4 hours?
2. Compute 2 1/2 divided by 5/6.
3. The temperature is negative 6 degrees and rises 14 degrees, then falls 9. What is it now?
4. A box is 5 cm by 4 cm by 3 cm. How many 1 cm cubes fill it, and what is its surface area?
5. Write an expression for 3 less than twice a number n, then evaluate it for n = 8.
6. In a class of 25, 60 percent walk to school. How many do not?
7. Solve 5x minus 7 = 2x + 11.
8. Find the slope of the line through (2, 3) and (6, 11).
9. A right triangle has legs 9 and 12. How long is the hypotenuse?
10. Simplify 2 to the 5th times 2 to the 3rd, divided by 2 to the 6th.

**Answer key with the one-line method (ours; verified in Python)**

| No. | Answer | Method |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 216 miles | 135 / 2.5 = 54 miles an hour, times 4 |
| 2 | 3 | 5/2 x 6/5 = 30/10 |
| 3 | Negative 1 degree | Negative 6 + 14 = 8; 8 minus 9 |
| 4 | 60 cubes; 94 square cm | 5 x 4 x 3; 2(20 + 15 + 12) |
| 5 | 2n minus 3; 13 | 2 x 8 minus 3 |
| 6 | 10 | 40 percent of 25 |
| 7 | x = 6 | 3x = 18 |
| 8 | 2 | (11 minus 3) / (6 minus 2) = 8 / 4 |
| 9 | 15 | 81 + 144 = 225, and the square root of 225 |
| 10 | 4 | 2 to the 8th over 2 to the 6th is 2 squared |

### Common slips

Problem 4 asks for two things; students often give the volume and stop. Problem 6 asks who does not walk, so 15 is the answer to the wrong question.

In problem 10, adding exponents to get 8 and then subtracting 6 is right; multiplying them is the usual error.

### Turning practice into marks

Take the problems from the unit your child is studying now and bring the ones they missed to class. Fixing a school unit is worth more than any extra test drill.

Before January, add one MAP Growth demo session for the platform itself.

## Boston exam schools: what families ask

### What are the three Boston exam schools?

Boston Public Schools lists Boston Latin Academy, Boston Latin School, and the John D. O'Bryant School of Mathematics and Science. All three admit new students in grades 7 and 9, and the O'Bryant takes a small number in grade 10.

### Is there an entrance exam for Boston Latin School?

Yes, in the sense that BPS requires the MAP Growth test for all exam school applicants. It counts for 30 percent of the composite score, and the GPA counts for 70 percent.

### How is the exam school composite score calculated?

BPS combines the GPA, weighted 70 percent, with the MAP Growth score, weighted 30 percent. The GPA is worked out on an 11-point scale and scaled to 100 by dividing by 11 and multiplying by 100.

### When is the MAP test for Boston exam schools in 2027?

BPS grade 6 and 8 students at non-exam schools test in school between January 11 and 15, 2027. The weekend test is January 9 to 10, 2027, with January 16 to 17 held in case of severe weather.

### What GPA do you need for a Boston exam school?

BPS requires a GPA of B or higher. For grade 7 entry it uses grade 6 marks in ELA, Math, Science and Social Studies from the first two quarters, plus grade 5 ELA and Math from the last marking period.

### Do advanced classes or an A+ give extra points?

No. BPS uses unweighted marks and gives no extra points for advanced coursework. Its FAQs say an A+ is treated like an A.

### Can my child take the MAP Growth test twice?

BPS says students have two opportunities, in the spring and winter, and it uses the higher achievement percentile in each subject. Both Reading and Math sections must be completed to get a score.

### Can we appeal an exam school decision?

No. BPS states that exam school decisions are final and that it does not accept appeals. Students not invited to their top choice may be placed on waitlists, which last until November 30.

### Do you teach MAP Growth questions?

No. We teach grade-level math and reasoning with our own problems, and point students to the free MAP Growth demo by NWEA that BPS links for platform practice.

### What days and times are classes?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays.
