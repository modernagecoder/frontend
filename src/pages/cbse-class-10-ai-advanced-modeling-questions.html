Ans. The short version

Advanced Concepts of Modeling in AI is Unit 2 of CBSE Class 10 AI (417), worth 11 of the 50 theory marks in 2026-27. It covers AI, machine learning and deep learning; features, labels and training and testing data; rule-based against learning-based models; supervised learning (classification and regression), unsupervised learning (clustering and association) and reinforcement learning; and neural networks, with ANN, CNN and the perceptron. Most questions ask you to define, differentiate or identify the model type in a scenario. Modern Age Coders teaches it live in a Monday and Wednesday 9:00 PM IST batch, or one to one.

What is in advanced concepts of modeling in AI class 10?

Features and labels in one example

A table of past students lists hours studied, attendance and mock score, and a last column saying whether each student passed. The first three columns are features, the facts the model looks at. "Passed" is the label, the answer it learns to predict. Data that carries that last column is labelled data; without it, it is unlabelled.

Q2. Rule-based or learning-based?

Rule based vs learning based AI: what is the difference?

In a rule-based model, a person writes the rules and the machine only follows them. In a learning-based model, the machine works out its own rules from examples, and can improve when given new data.

Q3. The classify-this drill

Supervised, unsupervised or reinforcement learning? Fifteen class 10 scenarios

For each scenario, name the type of learning and, where it applies, the sub-type. Ask two questions: are the right answers given in the data, and is the output a category, a number, a group, a pattern or a reward?

  1. A bank labels past loan applications "repaid" or "defaulted" and trains a model to judge new ones.
    Answer Supervised, classification. Labels are given and the output is one of two categories.
  2. A model predicts how many litres of milk a dairy will sell next Sunday.
    Answer Supervised, regression. The output is a quantity, not a category.
  3. A music app sorts millions of songs into groups by tempo and mood, with no genres given.
    Answer Unsupervised, clustering. No labels; similar songs are grouped.
  4. A stationery shop finds that pencil boxes and erasers are usually bought together.
    Answer Unsupervised, association. It finds items that occur together, not groups of customers.
  5. A computer learns chess by playing itself, winning points for victories and losing them for defeats.
    Answer Reinforcement learning. No labelled moves; rewards and penalties guide it.
  6. An app reads a photo of a leaf and names the plant species.
    Answer Supervised, classification. Trained on photos labelled with species names.
  7. A model estimates a used scooter's resale value from its age and kilometres driven.
    Answer Supervised, regression. The output is a continuous value.
  8. A school groups students by their study habits so that each group gets its own revision plan, with no groups decided in advance.
    Answer Unsupervised, clustering. The groups emerge from the data.
  1. A robot vacuum learns the quickest path round a room, scoring points for clean floor and losing them for bumps.
    Answer Reinforcement learning. It improves through reward and penalty.
  2. A model predicts tomorrow's air quality index as a number.
    Answer Supervised, regression. A number, learnt from past readings with known values.
  3. The same model instead says whether tomorrow's air will be "good", "moderate" or "poor".
    Answer Supervised, classification. Same data, but now the output is a category. The output decides, not the topic.
  4. A streaming site notices that viewers who watch one cooking show often watch a particular travel show next.
    Answer Unsupervised, association. A rule of the form "if this, then often that".
  5. An email service learns from mails users have marked as spam.
    Answer Supervised, classification. Users' marks are the labels.
  6. A news site gathers thousands of untagged articles into topics such as sport and politics on its own.
    Answer Unsupervised, clustering. Even if the groups look like topics, nobody labelled them.
  7. A game character learns to cross a level, earning points for coins and losing a life for each fall.
    Answer Reinforcement learning. Trial, error and a score.

Q4. The two pairs students mix up

Classification vs regression, and clustering vs association, class 10

Classification predicts a category and regression predicts a number; both are supervised. Clustering forms groups of similar items and association finds items that occur together; both are unsupervised.

Result looks likeGroup 1, group 2, group 3If A, then often B

The output test

Cover the scenario's topic and look only at what comes out. A category means classification, a number means regression. Questions 10 and 11 above use the same data to prove it.

The groups or links test

If the result is a set of groups of people or things, it is clustering. If it is a link between items, it is association.

Clustering is not classification

Both end in groups, but classification sorts into groups decided in advance using labels; clustering discovers the groups with no labels at all.

Q5. Neural networks

Neural network class 10: layers, ANN and CNN, and the perceptron

A neural network is a model made of layers of connected nodes, loosely modelled on the brain: an input layer takes the data, hidden layers process it, and an output layer gives the result. Deep learning uses networks with many hidden layers.

Q6. Written answers

Advanced modeling class 10 questions and answers, by marks

Ten original questions, from one mark to four. The bold words in each model answer are the points an examiner looks for; an answer that misses one usually loses that mark.

One and two marks

  1. Which is the widest of the three: AI, machine learning or deep learning?
    Answer AI. ML sits inside AI and DL sits inside ML.
  2. In a dataset of houses with area, rooms and price, which column is the label if we want to predict price?
    Answer Price. Area and rooms are features.
  3. Name the type of learning in which an agent learns from rewards and penalties.
    Answer Reinforcement learning.
  4. Give two differences between supervised and unsupervised learning.
    Answer Supervised learning uses labelled data; unsupervised uses unlabelled data. Supervised predicts a known kind of answer (a class or a number); unsupervised discovers structure such as groups or associations.
  5. Why is a learning-based model better suited to spam filtering than a rule-based one?
    Answer Spammers keep changing their wording, so fixed rules soon miss new spam. A learning-based model learns from newly marked emails and adapts without anyone rewriting rules.
  6. State one difference between classification and clustering.
    Answer Classification sorts data into categories defined in advance, using labels; clustering finds its own groups in unlabelled data.

Four marks

  1. Explain the three categories of machine learning, with one example each.
    Answer Supervised: learns from labelled data, such as predicting exam results from past marked records (1). Unsupervised: finds patterns in unlabelled data, such as grouping customers (1). Reinforcement: learns by trial and error with rewards, such as a robot learning to walk (1). One clear example for each (1).
  2. A city wants to (a) predict the number of buses needed each hour and (b) group bus stops with similar crowd patterns. Name the model type for each and justify.
    Answer (a) Regression, supervised (1): the output is a number learnt from past hourly records (1). (b) Clustering, unsupervised (1): stops are grouped by similarity with no labels given (1).
  3. Draw and explain the structure of an artificial neural network.
    Answer Diagram of three columns of nodes joined by lines (1). Input layer receives the features (1). Hidden layers process the data using weights and bias (1). Output layer gives the prediction (1).
  4. A perceptron has inputs 1 and 1, weights 0.4 and 0.3 and bias -0.5. It outputs 1 if the total is above 0. Find the total and the output, and say what would happen with a bias of -0.8.
    Answer Total = 0.4 + 0.3 - 0.5 = 0.2 (1), above 0, so output 1 (1). With bias -0.8 the total is 0.7 - 0.8 = -0.1 (1), so output 0: a more negative bias makes a yes harder to reach (1).

Questions about Unit 2

What students ask about advanced concepts of modeling

What is advanced concepts of modeling in AI class 10?

It is Unit 2 of CBSE Class 10 Artificial Intelligence (417). It explains how AI models learn: AI, machine learning and deep learning, features and labels, rule-based and learning-based models, supervised, unsupervised and reinforcement learning with their sub-types, and neural networks including ANN, CNN and the perceptron.

How many marks is Unit 2 in CBSE Class 10 AI?

Advanced Concepts of Modeling in AI carries 11 of the 50 theory marks in the 417 curriculum for 2026-27, the most of any unit. In the 2025-26 sample paper it supplied four of the objective items in the AI part, with the rest of its marks in written answers.

What is the difference between supervised, unsupervised and reinforcement learning?

Supervised learning trains on labelled data, so the right answers are known. Unsupervised learning works on unlabelled data and finds its own structure, such as groups. Reinforcement learning has no dataset of answers at all: an agent tries actions and learns from rewards and penalties.

What is the difference between classification and regression in class 10?

Both are supervised learning. Classification predicts a category, such as spam or not spam, or pass or fail. Regression predicts a continuous number, such as a temperature, a price or a delivery time. Look at the output: a label means classification, a quantity means regression.

What is the difference between clustering and association?

Both are unsupervised. Clustering places similar records in the same group, for example customers with similar spending. Association finds items or events that tend to occur together, for example people who buy notebooks often buy pens. Groups mean clustering; if-this-then-that links mean association.

What is the difference between rule based and learning based AI?

In rule-based AI, a developer writes every rule and the system follows them, so it cannot cope with cases the rules did not foresee. In learning-based AI, the model works out patterns from training data and can improve as new data arrives. A fixed-reply help-desk bot is rule-based; a recommendation system is learning-based.

What is a neural network in class 10 AI?

A neural network is a model made of layers of connected nodes: an input layer that takes the data, one or more hidden layers that process it using weights and bias, and an output layer that gives the result. Networks with many hidden layers are what deep learning uses.

What is the difference between ANN and CNN?

An artificial neural network (ANN) is the general layered network used for many tasks, such as predicting values. A convolutional neural network (CNN) is a special kind designed for images: it slides small filters over the picture to detect edges and shapes, which makes it the standard choice in computer vision.

How do I identify the type of learning in a scenario question?

Ask first whether the data comes with correct answers. If yes, it is supervised; then check if the output is a category (classification) or a number (regression). If no labels and the result is groups, it is clustering; if it is items found together, association. If learning comes from rewards, it is reinforcement.
