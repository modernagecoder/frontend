# Keystone Algebra 1 prep: the exam, module by module

The Keystone Algebra I is the end-of-course exam Pennsylvania students take when they finish Algebra I, often in grade 8 or 9. It is written to a published list: six assessment anchors, broken into eligible content statements (18 for Module 1 and 15 for Module 2), many with notes that say exactly how far a question can go. A trinomial to factor will have a leading coefficient of 1. A system will have two equations, never three. Knowing those limits changes how you prepare, because you can stop worrying about what is not on the test. This page reads PDE&rsquo;s test definition and anchor document closely, then teaches both modules with problems of our own.

At a glance: Exam: Keystone Algebra I; Run by: Pennsylvania Department of Education; Modules: Two, 30 points each; Question mix: About 60% MC/TE, 40% CR; Time limit: None; Next waves: December 2026, January and May 2027; Our classes: Batch or 1-on-1; Start: A free first class. ## How is the Keystone Algebra 1 exam structured?

In two modules of equal weight. Each module carries 18 points from multiple-choice and technology-enhanced items and 12 points from three constructed-response items, which makes 30 points per module.

**Keystone Algebra I module design (PDE Keystone Exams Test Definition, Algebra I, read 31 October 2026)**

|  | Module 1: Operations and Linear Equations & Inequalities | Module 2: Linear Functions and Data Organization |
|---|---|---|
| Core MC/TE items | 18 points | 18 points |
| Core constructed response | 3 items, 4 points each, 12 points | 3 items, 4 points each, 12 points |
| Module total | 30 points | 30 points |
| Eligible content | 18 | 15 |
| Embedded field-test items | 5 MC/TE and 1 CR | 5 MC/TE and 1 CR |
| Testing time / administration time | 75 / 85 minutes | 75 / 85 minutes |

### Sixty points, forty percent written

PDE&rsquo;s test definition sums it up: &ldquo;a total of 60 points (Module 1 and Module 2 combined) with approximately 60% MC/TE items and 40% CR items.&rdquo;

That 40 percent is unusually high for an end-of-course exam. Six constructed responses, each scored out of 4, decide a large share of the result, so writing out algebra clearly is not optional.

### Field-test items

Each module also carries 5 field-test multiple-choice or technology-enhanced items and 1 field-test constructed response, which do not count. So a student sees 23 MC/TE items and 4 constructed responses in each module, and cannot tell which are field tests.

Treat every question as if it counts. That is the only sensible strategy when you cannot see the difference.

## What is on the Keystone Algebra 1 exam?

Six assessment anchors, three per module. PDE counts 18 pieces of eligible content in Module 1 and 15 in Module 2, and its anchor document lists every one, with notes that limit how hard a question can be.

**Keystone Algebra I assessment anchors and selected eligible content limits (PDE Assessment Anchors and Eligible Content, Algebra I, April 2014 final, read 31 October 2026)**

| Anchor | What it covers | A limit PDE writes into the eligible content |
|---|---|---|
| A1.1.1 Operations with Real Numbers and Expressions | Comparing reals, square roots, GCF and LCM of monomials, exponents, estimation, polynomials, factoring, rational expressions | Polynomial products: &ldquo;Nothing larger than a binomial multiplied by a trinomial.&rdquo; |
| A1.1.2 Linear Equations | Writing and solving linear equations, justifying steps by properties, systems | Systems: &ldquo;Limit systems to two linear equations.&rdquo; |
| A1.1.3 Linear Inequalities | Compound inequalities on a number line, systems of inequalities by graphing | Compound inequalities may include absolute value inequalities |
| A1.2.1 Functions | Patterns, whether a relation is a function, domain and range, linear functions in tables, graphs and equations | Relations may be given as ordered pairs, a graph or a table |
| A1.2.2 Coordinate Geometry | Rate of change, slope, writing linear equations, trend lines | Slope and intercept read from an equation or a graph |
| A1.2.3 Data Analysis | Range, quartiles and interquartile range, predictions from displays, compound probability | Probabilities shown as a fraction, decimal or percent |

### The limits that save time

Factoring: trinomials are limited to the form ax&sup2; + bx + c where a equals 1 after factoring out all monomial factors, in PDE&rsquo;s wording. So 2x&sup2; + 10x + 12 can appear (it becomes 2(x&sup2; + 5x + 6)), but a trinomial needing the &ldquo;ac method&rdquo; with a leading 3 cannot.

Exponents: PDE notes that exponents should be integers from -10 to 10. No fractional exponents, so radicals are handled as square roots, as in the anchor document&rsquo;s own example, &ldquo;Simplify square roots (e.g., √24 = 2√6).&rdquo;

### What surprises students

Module 2 is not all functions. Data Analysis is a full anchor: quartiles, box-and-whisker plots, stem-and-leaf plots, scatter plots and compound probability all live there.

And Module 1 asks students to identify the algebraic property that justifies a step in solving an equation. Knowing the names (distributive, additive property of equality) earns points here.

Source: [Keystone Exams: Algebra I Assessment Anchors and Eligible Content](https://www.pa.gov/content/dam/copapwp-pagov/en/education/documents/instruction/assessment-and-accountability/keystone-exams/keystone-exams-aa-ec-wosample-items/keystone%20content%20module%20standard%20blueprint--algebra%20i.pdf), PDE.

## Is a calculator allowed on the Keystone Algebra 1, and what is on the formula sheet?

Students may use a calculator that meets PDE&rsquo;s calculator policy, and they get the Algebra I formula sheet both on paper and inside the online test.

### Calculator rules

PDE&rsquo;s policy bans phones and other devices, wireless or computer algebra system (CAS) calculators, and sharing. A test administrator wipes the memory both before the session starts and once it ends.

### Paper formula sheet

The 2026 to 2027 winter handbook says Algebra I students should receive a paper copy of the formula sheet, which is also available within the online test.

### What the sheet holds

Slope, point-slope, slope-intercept and standard forms of a line, the arithmetic properties, and rectangle and prism formulas.

The sheet also says: &ldquo;You may use calculator π or the number 3.14 as an approximation of π.&rdquo; More usefully for Module 1, it lists the properties by name, the distributive, commutative, associative and identity properties and the additive and multiplicative properties of equality, so a student asked to justify a step can find the right word on the page in front of them. We teach with the real sheet from the first week.

Sources: [Algebra I Formula Sheet](https://www.pa.gov/content/dam/copapwp-pagov/en/education/documents/instruction/assessment-and-accountability/keystone-exams/administration-materials/algebra%20i%20formula%20sheet.pdf) and the [2026 to 2027 Winter Keystone Handbook for Assessment Coordinators](https://www.pa.gov/content/dam/copapwp-pagov/en/education/documents/instruction/assessment-and-accountability/keystone-exams/administration-materials/2026-2027%20winter%20keystone%20handbook%20for%20assessment%20coordinators.pdf), PDE.

## How do you answer a Keystone Algebra 1 constructed-response question?

Every part answered, every step shown, and the answer interpreted in the situation. Here is a Module 1 problem we wrote around a compound inequality, eligible content A1.1.3.1.1 and A1.1.3.1.3.

1. Our problem (not a Keystone item). A climbing gym charges a 30 sign-up fee plus 12 per visit. Maya wants to spend at least 150 and at most 240 this season. Part A: write a compound inequality for v, the number of visits. Part B: solve it and graph the solution on a number line. Part C: explain what the solution means for Maya, and say whether 19 visits is possible.
2. Part A. The cost is 30 + 12v, so 150 &le; 30 + 12v &le; 240.
3. Part B. Subtract 30 from all three parts: 120 &le; 12v &le; 210. Divide all three parts by 12: 10 &le; v &le; 17.5. On the number line, closed dots at 10 and 17.5 with the segment between them shaded.
4. Part C. Visits must be whole numbers, so Maya can make 10, 11, 12 and so on up to 17 visits. 19 visits is not possible: 30 + 12 x 19 = 258, which is more than 240. Interpreting the solution in context, as A1.1.3.1.3 asks, is where the whole-number limit comes from.
5. Where the four points come from, as we teach it. A correct inequality; correct solving with the operation done to all three parts; a correct graph; and an interpretation that rounds down to 17 and rejects 19 with a calculation. Spelling does not count; PDE&rsquo;s FAQ says scores are based on content only.

### The slip we see most

Subtracting 30 from the middle only, or from only one side. A compound inequality is three expressions at once, and every operation goes to all three.

### The habit that fixes it

Write the three parts in columns and act on each column in turn. It looks slow and it is fast.

## What does a Keystone Module 2 question on data and trend lines look like?

Module 2 joins linear functions to data. Eligible content A1.2.2.2.1 asks students to write an equation for a line of best fit, and A1.2.3.2.3 to predict from it. Here is our example.

1. Our data (invented). A plant&rsquo;s height in centimeters over weeks: week 1, 4 cm; week 2, 7 cm; week 4, 12 cm; week 6, 18 cm. Part A: estimate a trend line. Part B: interpret the slope. Part C: predict the height at week 8.
2. Part A. Use two points near the trend, say (2, 7) and (6, 18). Slope = (18 - 7) / (6 - 2) = 11/4 = 2.75. Point-slope form from the formula sheet: y - 7 = 2.75(x - 2), so y = 2.75x + 1.5.
3. Part B. The slope 2.75 means the plant grows about 2.75 cm per week. The intercept 1.5 is the model&rsquo;s height at week 0, which may not match reality, and saying so shows understanding.
4. Part C. At x = 8: y = 2.75 x 8 + 1.5 = 23.5 cm. Prediction beyond the data assumes the trend continues, which a good answer mentions.

Different students may pick different points and get slightly different lines. That is expected with a trend line: what matters is a reasonable line, the formula used correctly, and a prediction read from it with the right units.

## Do you have to pass the Keystone Algebra 1 to graduate in Pennsylvania?

Proficiency on the Algebra I Keystone is one way to meet the statewide requirement, but not the only one. Since the class of 2023, Pennsylvania has offered five Pathways to Graduation.

### What PDE says

PDE&rsquo;s graduation page states: &ldquo;Effective with the graduating class of 2023, Pennsylvania high school students have the option to demonstrate their postsecondary preparedness and meet statewide requirements through one of five Pathways to Graduation.&rdquo;

Its parent FAQ adds that students should demonstrate proficiency on the Algebra I, Literature and Biology Keystones, or meet one of the other pathways, to graduate.

### Banked, and retakeable

Keystone results are &ldquo;banked until their junior year for accountability purposes and until their senior year for graduation purposes&rdquo;, according to the FAQ. A grade 8 result can count years later.

The FAQ is equally clear that &ldquo;All students who take a Keystone Exam may retake the exam.&rdquo; A score below proficient is a reason to plan a later wave, not the end of the road.

Historically, PDE explains, the Keystones replaced the 11th-grade PSSA in math, reading and science in 2012 to 2013. Students still take the Algebra I Keystone for federal accountability whichever graduation pathway they use. Because pathways and local rules vary, ask the school counselor which route your teenager is on.

Sources: [Statewide High School Graduation Requirements](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/education/programs-and-services/instruction/elementary-and-secondary-education/assessment-and-accountability/graduation-requirements) and the [Keystone Exams parent FAQ](https://www.pa.gov/content/dam/copapwp-pagov/en/education/documents/instruction/assessment-and-accountability/keystone-exams/administration-materials/english%20parent%20faq%202026%20keystone.pdf) (Spring 2026), PDE.

## Keystone Algebra 1: common questions

### When is the Keystone Algebra 1 exam in 2027?

PDE lists Winter Wave 2 from January 4 to 15, 2027, Spring from May 17 to 28, 2027 and Summer from July 26 to 30, 2027, after Winter Wave 1 in December 2026. The school decides which wave a student takes.

### How many questions are on the Keystone Algebra 1?

Each module has 23 multiple-choice or technology-enhanced items and 4 constructed responses, including field-test items. The scored part is 18 MC/TE points and three 4-point constructed responses per module, 60 points in all.

### How long is the Keystone Algebra 1 exam?

There is no time limit. PDE's parent FAQ says a typical student needs two to three hours, and each module about one to one and a half hours.

### What is on Module 1 and Module 2 of the Algebra 1 Keystone?

Module 1 covers operations with real numbers and expressions, linear equations and linear inequalities. Module 2 covers functions, coordinate geometry and data analysis, including probability.

### Is a calculator allowed on the Algebra 1 Keystone?

Yes, one that follows the state calculator policy. Phones, wireless calculators, computer algebra system calculators and shared calculators are not allowed, and the memory is cleared before and after each session.

### Is there a formula sheet for the Algebra 1 Keystone?

Yes. Students receive a paper copy and can also open it in the online test. It includes the forms of a linear equation, slope and the arithmetic properties.

### Do you need to pass the Keystone Algebra 1 to graduate?

Proficiency is one route, but since the class of 2023 Pennsylvania has offered five Pathways to Graduation. Check with the school counselor which pathway applies to your student.

### Can you retake the Keystone Algebra 1?

Yes. PDE's parent FAQ says all students who take a Keystone Exam may retake it, and results are banked until senior year for graduation purposes.

### Where can I find Keystone Algebra 1 practice tests?

PDE posts Item and Scoring Samplers on its Keystone Exams page, with the 2021 set revised from 2017. Our twelve questions here add two more for each anchor.

### When are your classes?
