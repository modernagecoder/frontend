Recommendation engines, customer segmentation and many AI tools group things into families of "similar" items without being told what the families are. One classic way to do it is hierarchical clustering: start with every item on its own, repeatedly merge the two closest groups, and draw the whole history as a tree called a dendrogram. The catch is that "closest groups" can be defined in several ways, called linkages. This project clusters all 296 districts of England by the age make-up of their residents from the 2021 census, looks at where Cannock Chase lands, and finds that changing the linkage can turn a sensible set of families into one enormous group and three outcasts.

## Which districts are like Cannock Chase? Hierarchical clustering and its linkages

Describe every district by its age make-up, build a family tree of districts four ways, and compare the families.

Using the Nomis API, the learner describes all 296 English districts by the same 18 numbers: what fraction of residents falls in each five-year age band at the 2021 census. On that measure, the single districts closest to Cannock Chase are Barnsley, Wakefield, Ashfield, Wigan and Bolsover, none of them in the West Midlands. Hierarchical clustering then goes further than nearest neighbours: it builds a complete family tree, merging the two most similar groups again and again until only one is left.

The linkage decides what "most similar groups" means. Single linkage uses the closest pair of members, complete linkage the furthest pair, average linkage the average of all pairs, and Ward's method merges whichever pair of groups adds least spread. Cutting each tree into four families gives startlingly different results.

**Family sizes when England's 296 districts are cut into four groups by age profile, our Python run on Census 2021 TS007A, 29 September 2026**

| Linkage | Sizes of the four families |
|---|---|
| Single | 293, 1, 1, 1 |
| Complete | 162, 93, 32, 9 |
| Average | 229, 57, 9, 1 |
| Ward | 159, 77, 33, 27 |

Single linkage suffers from chaining: districts join the big group one after another through a chain of close neighbours, so almost everything ends up in one family and the only other "families" are three districts with unusual age profiles, the Isles of Scilly, the City of London and Tower Hamlets. That is technically correct and practically useless. Ward's method gives families of comparable size, one reason it is a common choice for this kind of task. With Ward and eight families, Cannock Chase sits in a family of 92 districts drawn from every English region outside London, including 23 from the South East and 16 from the East Midlands.

There is also a way to score how faithfully each tree reflects the original distances, the cophenetic correlation: 0.797 for average linkage, 0.714 for single, 0.696 for Ward and 0.562 for complete. The tree that looks most useful is not the one that scores highest. Choosing a clustering method means deciding what the groups are for, not just reading off a number.

## What this teaches about vibe coding and AI agents

Groupings say as much about the method as about the data.

**From Cannock's district families to AI groupings**

| In the clustering project | When AI groups or recommends |
|---|---|
| Single linkage made one giant family | A default method can give a useless answer |
| Ward gave balanced families | The right method depends on the purpose |
| Cannock Chase's closest match was Barnsley | Similar on one measure is not similar on all |
| The highest-scoring tree was not the most useful | Scores need judgement to interpret |
| Three districts were outliers | Unusual cases can dominate some methods |

## What is hierarchical clustering, and why does the linkage matter?

It builds a family tree by merging the most similar groups step by step; the linkage decides what "most similar" means.

On England's 296 districts, single linkage produced one family of 293 and three outliers, while Ward's method produced four balanced families. Same data, same distances, very different stories.

Learners who have seen that happen ask which method sits behind any AI grouping before they believe what it says.
