Kansas, United States

Coding and AI classes in Kansas

How many Kansas high schools offer computer science?

About 38%. Code.org (2025) counts 153 of 404 Kansas public high schools offering computer science in 2023 to 2024, and 3,654 high school students enrolled, roughly 2.5%. For a Kansas student whose school has no course, Modern Age Coders teaches live online, from Scratch in the early grades to Python, web projects and AP Computer Science.

Kansas sits well below the national share of 60% that Code.org gives for 2023 to 2024. In the most recent Kansas count, fewer than four in ten public high schools offered a computer science course, and one high school student in forty was enrolled in one. That is not a judgement on Kansas teachers; it reflects the hard arithmetic of small rural schools and a subject that needs a trained teacher. This page sets out the numbers as published, what we could and could not verify about state policy, how a student can still move from a Kansas high school into a computer science degree, and a Python project on City of Lawrence open data that teaches one of the oldest ideas in computing: finding the nearest thing.

State policy

What we could verify about Kansas rules, and what we could not

We go to the state agency itself for standards and requirements. In Kansas that door was shut on the day we checked.

The education department did not respond

On 17 October 2026 the Kansas State Department of Education’s websites answered our requests with a “Request Rejected” page. We did not try to get around that block, so this page makes no statement about Kansas computer science standards, course requirements or graduation rules. Check those directly with the department or your district.

What we could read comes from the Kansas Board of Regents, the governing body for the six state universities. Its pages cover what a Kansas student needs for university admission and how computer science credits travel between Kansas colleges, which matters to any high schooler thinking about the subject as a degree.

Reading the curriculum list

The Kansas Scholars Curriculum is a Regents scholarship curriculum, not a graduation rule. Its five subject lines leave computer science out, so a student building that transcript fits coding in as an elective or outside school.

The transfer route

A Kansas teenager can start a computer science associate degree at a public community college and carry it into a bachelor’s program, according to the Regents. Arriving with real programming experience makes the first year far easier.

What to ask your district

Whether the high school offers a computer science course this year, whether it counts as a math, science or elective credit locally, and whether online courses can earn credit. Answers vary across Kansas districts.

Signature project

Which Lawrence park is closest to a bus stop? A nearest-neighbour search

The City of Lawrence publishes its parks and its transit stops as open data. Students load both, compare every park with every stop, and keep the closest.

Clean before you search

The MUNICIPALITY column spells the city two ways: 76 records say Lawrence and 9 say LAWRENCE. Filter on one spelling and you lose nine parks without any error message. Lower-casing first fixes it.

Brute force first

With 84 parks and 452 stops, a double loop makes 37,968 distance calculations, which Python finishes in well under a second. Students write that version, then learn why a city with millions of points needs a spatial index.

Follow the data’s own note

The city’s description of the stops layer asks users to filter out KU Stop 500. Reading the metadata before the data is a habit professional analysts build early.

What the numbers do not say

Every figure above is ours. We measured from the centre of each park polygon, so a long riverside park can sit far from a stop on paper while its entrance is next to one, and a few parks the file assigns to Lawrence lie well outside the bus network. The city releases the data as is, without warranty, and changes it often, so the counts describe one download on one day.

An extension for stronger students: switch the question around and ask which stop serves the most parks within a quarter mile, or redo the search with the true polygon edge instead of the centre point and compare the two medians. Both teach that the algorithm is only half the answer; the other half is deciding what “closest” should mean.

Class times

Most of Kansas is on Central Time, the far west on Mountain

The IANA time zone database files most of Kansas under US central time and far western Kansas under US mountain time.

Book around your week

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays.

Two clocks, one state

A family near the Colorado line may run an hour behind one in Topeka. We confirm every class in the family’s own zone, Central or Mountain, so nobody turns up an hour early.

Seasonal changes

Both Kansas zones set clocks an hour earlier on November 1, 2026 and an hour later on March 14, 2027. By our arithmetic, India time is 10 hours 30 minutes ahead of Central Daylight Time and 11 hours 30 minutes ahead of Mountain Daylight Time.

A boundary that moved

The tz database records that Kearny County, Kansas was put entirely on central time in October 1990, after being split between Mountain and Central. Students who write a program to label Kansas places by zone discover that the right answer depends on the date, which is exactly why software relies on the tz database rather than a hand-made list.

Coding and AI

Can an AI tool replace a missing computer science class in Kansas?

It can answer questions. It cannot tell a student which question to ask of the data.

Ask a chatbot for the Lawrence park nearest a bus stop and it may answer confidently. But was the distance measured from the park edge or its centre? Were the nine parks spelled LAWRENCE counted? Was the stop the city told users to drop left in? A student who wrote the search knows; one who only read the answer does not.

That is why AI tools pay off most for students who can already code. They speed up typing and suggest fixes, and the student checks every result against the data. In a state where many high schools have no computer science course, learning to code first is how a student gets the full benefit of AI rather than its mistakes.

A Kansas student without a school course loses nothing by starting online, as long as the work is real code they understand line by line. The longer argument is in why learning to code still matters now.

FAQ

Kansas coding and AI questions

Answers for Kansas families, with sources named.

How common is a computer science course in a Kansas high school?

In the Code.org 2025 sheet, 153 of 404 Kansas public high schools offering computer science in 2023 to 2024, or 37.9%. Its source label for Kansas is State Education Agency and School Catalogs.

How many Kansas high school students take computer science?

The same data shows 3,654 students enrolled in 2023 to 2024, set against an NCES count of 146,266 high school students, which we calculate as 2.50%.

Does Kansas require computer science to graduate?

We could not verify it. The Kansas State Department of Education websites rejected our requests on 17 October 2026, so we make no claim either way; please check with the department or your district.

Is computer science in the Kansas Scholars Curriculum?

No. The Kansas Board of Regents lists English, Mathematics, Science, Social Studies and World Language for that curriculum, and computer science is not among them.

Can a Kansas community college student move into a CS degree?

Yes, according to the Regents. The Computer Science Systemwide Transfer Degree links Kansas public community college associate degrees in computer science to Kansas public university bachelor's programs.

Which time zone are Kansas classes in?

Most of Kansas is on Central Time and far western Kansas on Mountain Time, per the tz database. We confirm each class in the zone where the family lives.

How are class times arranged?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays.

Do your classes give Kansas school credit?

Not by themselves. Credit decisions belong to the school or district. Our lessons build skill and projects, and a student's graded school work stays their own.

Do you have a Kansas location?

No. We teach only online, live, from India, and do not claim any Kansas office or classroom.

How much do classes cost?

The fees panel lists three prices: nothing for the first lesson, then a monthly price for a group place or for private teaching. Billing waits until after that first lesson.
