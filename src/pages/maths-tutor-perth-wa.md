# Maths tutor in Perth

Ask someone in Perth how far behind Sydney they are and you will usually hear "two hours". For about half of every year that is wrong. Perth keeps the same clock all year, while Sydney moves forward an hour each October, so the gap jumps from two hours to three and back again. Written as maths, the time difference is a piecewise function of the date, with two breakpoints a year. It is a neat example of something we care about in every lesson: a rule that sounds simple, tested properly against the real thing. Our live online maths tutoring for Perth learners covers primary school, the lower secondary years and the WA ATAR mathematics courses, plus adults returning to maths, all booked on Perth time.

## The gap between Perth and Sydney is a piecewise function

A piecewise function uses different rules on different parts of its domain. Perth's time difference to the eastern states is a real one, with the date as input and the gap in hours as output.

The international time zone database records the rules. New South Wales follows daylight saving from the first Sunday in October to the first Sunday in April. Western Australia does not, although the database shows it tried several times, most recently from 3 December 2006 until March 2009. We asked a computer to compare the clocks at 6:00 pm Perth time on every day of 2027.

**How far ahead of Perth each place is, every day of 2027 at 6:00 pm Perth time (our calculation)**

| Place | Hours ahead | Days in 2027 | When it changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sydney, Melbourne, Hobart | 3 | 183 | Until 4 April and from 3 October |
| Sydney, Melbourne, Hobart | 2 | 182 | From 4 April to 2 October |
| Adelaide | 2.5 or 1.5 | 183 and 182 | Same dates as Sydney |
| Brisbane | 2 | 365 | Never |
| Darwin | 1.5 | 365 | Never |
| India (our teachers) | Perth is 2.5 ahead | 365 | Never |

### Writing it as a function

For 2027, the gap to Sydney is 3 hours for dates before 4 April, 2 hours from 4 April to 2 October, and 3 hours again from 3 October. Two breakpoints, three pieces, one function.

### When the simple rule wins

For Brisbane and Darwin, which keep one clock all year like Perth, "2 hours" and "1.5 hours" are right on all 365 days. There, the constant rule is the whole truth, and a piecewise version would add nothing.

### When it fails

"Two hours behind Sydney" was right on only 182 of 365 days in 2027. A 4:00 pm Perth lesson is 7:00 pm in Sydney on 1 March but 6:00 pm on 1 July.

### A border curiosity

The same database lists a zone for Eucla, near the South Australian border, at 8 hours 45 minutes ahead of UTC, three quarters of an hour from Perth. Students enjoy finding the one place in Australia whose offset is not a whole or half hour.

It is a neat extension: add a third rule and draw the step graph.

### What students practise

Reading rules, defining a function in pieces, drawing step graphs, and checking every boundary case: what happens on the exact morning the clocks change?

Senior students can write the function with set notation and an indicator for each interval.

Source: IANA, [time zone database, australasia file](https://data.iana.org/time-zones/tzdb/australasia), read 13 October 2026. The day counts are our calculation with Python's zoneinfo module.

A function made of different rules on different intervals. The time difference between Perth and Sydney is one: 3 hours in summer and 2 hours in winter, switching on set dates.

No. By our calculation for 2027, Perth is 2 hours behind on 182 days and 3 hours behind on 183 days, because Sydney uses daylight saving and Perth does not.

Because a time-zone gap is a function that students already live with. Turning it into rules, intervals and a step graph makes piecewise functions concrete, and checking the exact changeover dates teaches care at the boundaries.
