AI and Python classes for teens in Gatineau

How does a Gatineau teenager learn Python and AI properly, on Quebec’s school calendar?

Modern Age Coders teaches Gatineau teens aged 13 to 18 live online, in English, in a class of fifteen to twenty or one to one. Most start with Python for Teens, then move to AI and Machine Learning for Teens, problem solving and algorithms, or preparation for the CCC, which CEMC offers in French or English. Lessons are booked in Eastern time, and the first one is free.

Gatineau sits across the Ottawa River from Ontario’s capital, and the two cities share a clock, but a Gatineau teenager studies in a different system. Quebec’s secondary school runs from Secondary I to Secondary V and is followed by CEGEP, and its 2026 digital competency framework now names computer science and artificial intelligence. Ontario course codes that Ottawa friends mention, such as ICS3U, do not apply on this side of the bridge. This page explains where programming and AI sit for a Quebec teen, quoting the government’s own documents, which contests are open and in which language, which AI tools a teenager may use, and a data project we built on the Ville de Gatineau’s published traffic counts, in which the main job is catching the numbers that cannot be right.

Quebec does not publish a secondary computer science course in the documents we read, so programming reaches a Gatineau teen in other ways. The Digital Competency Framework 2026, from the Ministère de l’Éducation, is the clearest sign of direction: one of its dimensions now “also incorporates computer science and concepts related to AI”, one element asks learners to “Develop or adapt a digital tool using one’s knowledge and skills in computer programming.”, and its sample themes include “Machine learning and deep learning”. A framework is not a timetable, and how a school applies it varies. What it tells families is that the province now treats programming and AI as part of a young person’s digital competence, which is exactly the ground our classes cover, with real code rather than descriptions of it.

Problem solving, in Quebec’s words

Quebec’s secondary cross-curricular competencies say: “Problem solving is not a linear process that can be reduced to the application of an algorithm.” We agree, and teach students to define the problem, try, test and start over, before any code is final.

Programming languages are the same in every human language: a Python loop reads the same in a French-language school as in an English one. Ville de Gatineau data project

Which of Gatineau’s traffic counts cannot be right? An anomaly detector in Python

The Ville de Gatineau publishes thousands of 24-hour traffic counts on Données Québec, with French field names such as DEBIT_TOTAL24H and PCENT_V_LOURD. Before training any model on data like this, a careful student hunts for the rows that are impossible or suspicious. Every count of flags in this section is our own result, not the City’s.

Our anomaly checks on the Ville de Gatineau Débits de circulation file (7,384 rows from 2016 to 2023, downloaded from Données Québec on 22 October 2026)
CheckWhat it looks forRows it flags
Validity ruleA heavy-vehicle share (PCENT_V_LOURD) above 100 per cent, which no real count can have25
Validity ruleA 24-hour total of zero644
Missing labelNo sector recorded1,916
City-wide fenceTotals above the upper quartile plus one and a half times the interquartile range (14,100)624
Paired checkTwo rows for the same corner, approach and year where one is over three times the other16 of 1,864 pairs

This is the program, in standard-library Python. It downloads the GeoJSON file from Données Québec, applies three validity rules, builds one fence for the whole city from the quartiles, then compares each row with its partner row at the same corner. On 22 October 2026 it printed every number in the table:import json, urllib.request from collections import defaultdict URL = ("https://www.donneesquebec.ca/recherche/dataset/f1e7f552-278d-47d6-89c9-6aa9d3293bd1/" "resource/388e4ff5-e1f1-4c81-a734-79b907fcf236/download/comptage.json") raw = urllib.request.urlopen(URL).read().decode("utf-8-sig") rows = [f["properties"] for f in json.loads(raw)["features"]] print(len(rows), "counts from", min(r["ANNEE"] for r in rows), "to", max(r["ANNEE"] for r in rows)) # 1. rules a count must obey print("heavy-vehicle share above 100%:", sum((r["PCENT_V_LOURD"] or 0) > 100 for r in rows)) print("24-hour total of zero:", sum(r["DEBIT_TOTAL24H"] == 0 for r in rows)) print("no sector recorded:", sum(not r["SECTEUR"] for r in rows)) # 2. one fence for the whole city: quartiles of the positive totals flows = sorted(r["DEBIT_TOTAL24H"] for r in rows if r["DEBIT_TOTAL24H"] > 0) q1, q3 = flows[len(flows) // 4], flows[3 * len(flows) // 4] fence = q3 + 1.5 * (q3 - q1) print("city-wide fence", fence, "flags", sum(f > fence for f in flows), "rows") # 3. compare each count with its partner: same corner, same approach, same year pairs = defaultdict(list) for r in rows: pairs[(r["NOMRUE"], r["APPROCHE"], r["ANNEE"])].append(r["DEBIT_TOTAL24H"]) twos = [v for v in pairs.values() if len(v) == 2 and min(v) > 0] odd = [v for v in twos if max(v) > 3 * min(v)] print(len(twos), "two-way pairs;", sum(v[0] == v[1] for v in twos), "identical;", len(odd), "where one side is over three times the other") print("most lopsided pair:", max(odd, key=lambda v: max(v) / min(v)))

1. Rules before statistics

Some values are wrong by definition. A share of heavy vehicles cannot pass 100 per cent, yet 25 rows do, the highest at 324.6, all from 2017 to 2019. No statistics are needed to flag those; a single comparison in code does it.

2. A global fence misfires

A fence built from the whole city flags 624 rows, and most of them are simply busy roads. Gatineau mixes quiet residential corners with arterials, so “far from the typical count” mostly means “a main road”, not “an error”.

3. Compare like with like

Comparing each count with its partner at the same corner is far sharper. Of 1,864 pairs, 1,762 match exactly and only 16 differ by more than three times. Those 16 are worth a human look; some will be genuine, such as one-way streets.

Two rows a student should question

At Aylmer / Vanier in 2018, one approach records 127,400 vehicles in 24 hours beside a partner row of 12,700, and 127,400 is the largest value in the whole file, three times the next largest (41,400). At Fournier & Lac Leamy in 2019, a pair reads 243 against 24,300, exactly a factor of one hundred. Both look like slips in entering digits, but the file alone cannot prove it. A flag is a question for the data owner, not a verdict, and a good analyst writes it up that way.

Two patterns are worth noting honestly. In 1,762 of the 1,864 pairs the two directions carry exactly the same total, which suggests the file sometimes repeats one figure on both rows rather than counting each direction separately; we pose that as a question rather than state it. And the sector field is empty on 1,916 rows, including every 2023 row, so any analysis by sector (Aylmer, Buckingham, Gatineau, Hull, Masson-Angers) has to drop the most recent year. The file is published by the Ville de Gatineau on Données Québec under the licence “Attribution (CC-BY 4.0)”; Données Québec explains that this licence lets others “distribuer, remixer, arranger et adapter votre œuvre, même à des fins commerciales”, and the Creative Commons deed says use is allowed “for any purpose, even commercially.” Source: Ville de Gatineau, Débits de circulation, Données Québec. Quebec’s own framework now lists AI literacy among its sample themes, and AI literacy starts with knowing what a tool cannot do. The traffic project makes that concrete. Ask a chatbot to summarise Gatineau’s traffic file and it will happily average numbers that include a heavy-vehicle share of 324.6 per cent and a count ten times its partner. It takes a person who has looked at the rows to say that those values should not be in the average at all. We want Gatineau teens to be that person, so our students write the checks themselves first, and any AI tool, where a parent allows one, comes afterwards as something to be checked.

Parents give permission

OpenAI’s terms require a parent’s or guardian’s permission under 18. We tell families which lessons could involve the tool and keep every lesson complete without it.

Adult-only tools stay out

Under Anthropic’s terms Claude is for users aged 18 and over, so it is never part of a teen lesson, and teens are not placed in our adult agentic coding course.

Work stays the student’s

We explain and review. We do not write graded school or CEGEP work, and we point out when code looks pasted rather than understood.

Doubt before tools

Why a Gatineau teen should learn to distrust data before trusting AI

Because every model is only as good as the rows it was fed, and only a person checks the rows.

The traffic file is a fair sample of real public data: mostly sound, with a few values that are impossible and a few that are merely strange. A model trained on it without checks would quietly learn from both. A student who has written the checks knows the difference between a rule that is broken, a value that is unusual, and a value that is simply large.

So we teach in a fixed order. Describe the question, inspect the data, write the steps, predict the result, run it and argue with it. AI tools come later, inside the age rules, as a reviewer to be questioned. A teen who learns that order can use any tool well; one who skips it is at the tool’s mercy.

The CCC makes the same point on contest day, with no assistant allowed, so the thinking on the page is the student’s own. The longer argument is in a longer case for learning to code in 2026.

FAQ

Questions from Gatineau families

What Gatineau parents and teens ask us most often.

Are your classes in French or English?

In English. Teachers give French terms for the main ideas, and students can still write the CCC in French, since CEMC offers it in both languages.

Do you have a centre in Gatineau?

No. All teaching is live online, in a class of fifteen to twenty or one to one with a private teacher.

My teen is in a Quebec school. Do Ontario courses like ICS3U apply?

No. ICS3U is an Ontario course. A Gatineau student in the Quebec system goes from Secondary I to V and then CEGEP, and we plan around that.

Do your courses give Quebec credits?

No. We teach alongside school and we do not grant provincial credits; secondary and CEGEP results come from the student's own institutions.

Can my 14 year old use ChatGPT?

Only with your permission: OpenAI's terms say users under 18 need a parent's or legal guardian's permission. Our lessons never rely on it.

Is Claude allowed for teenagers?

No. Anthropic's consumer terms require users to be at least 18, so we never suggest Claude to students under 18.

Can AI tools be used during the CCC?

No. The grader rules forbid other internet use, including generative AI such as CoPilot or ChatGPT, so contest practice is done with AI off.

How does a Gatineau student enter the CCC?

Through a school: CEMC says all students must participate through their school. The 2027 CCC is on Wednesday 17 February 2027, and it can be written in French or English.

When are classes for Gatineau families?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Times are confirmed in Eastern time.

Is the traffic data project real?

Yes. It uses the Ville de Gatineau's traffic counts published on Données Québec under CC BY 4.0. Students download the file and write the checks themselves; the City has not endorsed our teaching.
