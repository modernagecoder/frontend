## Saturday is the busiest day: parsing Winnipeg's fall 2026 Leisure Guide

The City of Winnipeg publishes every activity in its LeisureONLINE recreation catalogue as open data, from swimming lessons to pottery. One column packs each activity's weekly timetable into a short piece of text. Our students take the fall 2026 rows apart and count what is inside. All figures are ours, from the file fetched on 22 October 2026.

**Weekly meeting slots by day, counted by us in the City of Winnipeg LeisureONLINE fall 2026 rows (2,440 activities, 2,451 slots, because 11 activities meet twice a week; fetched 22 October 2026)**

| Day | Weekly slots |
|---|---|
| Saturday | 546 |
| Monday | 417 |
| Sunday | 356 |
| Tuesday | 326 |
| Wednesday | 326 |
| Thursday | 308 |
| Friday | 172 |

Each activity's timetable sits in a column called default_pattern_dates, written like Tuesday,08:00 PM,1h30m. Eleven activities meet on two days, and the file joins their two slots with a slash. So the program splits on the slash first, then on the commas, and counts each piece. It is plain Python with the standard library, run in the same folder as the CSV:

```
import csv
from collections import Counter

days = Counter()
start_hours = Counter()
categories = Counter()

with open("leisureonline-2026-fall.csv", encoding="utf-8-sig") as guide:
    for row in csv.DictReader(guide):
        categories[row["category"]] += 1
        for slot in row["default_pattern_dates"].split("/"):
            day, start, length = slot.split(",")
            days[day] += 1
            start_hours[start[:2] + " " + start[-2:]] += 1

print(categories.most_common(4))
print(days.most_common())
print(start_hours.most_common(3))
```

### 1. Split twice

Splitting on commas alone would break on the eleven two-day rows. Splitting on the slash first turns those rows into two slots each, which is why the day counts add to 2,451, not 2,440. We count slots, and say so in the caption.

### 2. Read the clock text

Start times arrive as text like 06:00 PM. Taking the first two characters and the last two gives an hour label. The most common starts are 6 pm, with 481 slots, and 5 pm, with 466, just after school and work.

### 3. Notice the shape

Weekends hold 902 of the 2,451 slots, and Saturday alone 546, while Friday has only 172. Swimming lessons are 1,476 of the 2,440 activities, so the swim timetable shapes the whole week.

**More of our checks on the same City of Winnipeg rows (our results)**

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Activities listed as Open and as Closed | 2,427 and 13 |
| Largest categories | Aquatics - Swimming Lessons 1,476; Learn to Skate 428; Active Living 264 |
| Most common slot lengths | 30 minutes 1,561; 45 minutes 441; one hour 255 |
| Activities typed as French-language | 64 |
| A spelling quirk in the header | The column is named activty_number |

### What the timetable cannot show

A slot count is not attendance. The file says when activities are scheduled, not how many people come, and a 30-minute swim lesson and a three-hour workshop each count once. The misspelled activty_number column is a reminder that real data has typos and code must match them exactly.

## Winnipeg's permanent clock, from 1 November 2026

Manitoba's news release says the clocks stay where they are on 1 November 2026, and the province's Official Time Amendment Act sets Manitoba time at five hours behind UTC. From then on Winnipeg has one offset all year, and so does India, where our teachers work.

**Two Winnipeg example hours shown in India and Toronto time (Manitoba UTC-5 all year from 1 November 2026; Ontario UTC-5 in winter and UTC-4 from 14 March 2027; our arithmetic)**

|---|---|---|---|

### The same hour all year

A Winnipeg class booked for Thursday at 4:30 pm stays at 4:30 pm in Winnipeg, and at 3 am Friday in India, in every month. Only the Toronto column moves, when Ontario changes in March 2027.

### Neighbours on the map

Manitoba's release lists British Columbia, Yukon, the Northwest Territories, Alberta and Saskatchewan as provinces and territories with, or moving toward, a permanent time. Calgary and Regina now sit one hour behind Winnipeg all year.

## AI can parse the Leisure Guide. Who checks that it split the slash?

A person who has written the split themselves.

Ask an AI assistant to count Winnipeg recreation activities by weekday and it may split each timetable on commas and report 2,440 neat results. It will have miscounted the eleven activities that meet twice a week, or crashed on them. The fix is a single extra split, and the only reliable way to know it is needed is to have looked at the data.

We teach students to look first, plan the steps, write the code and test it against a few rows checked by hand. Older students bring AI tools in under the age limits we apply, to check and extend their own work, not to replace the thinking.

Manitoba's framework expects students to keep learning new languages as they appear; the thinking underneath them, and the habit of checking, is what transfers. The longer argument is in [our argument for learning to code even when AI writes code](/blog/is-coding-worth-learning-2026).
