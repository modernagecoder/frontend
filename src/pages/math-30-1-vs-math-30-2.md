# Math 30-1 vs Math 30-2: which Grade 12 math in Alberta?

Families often ask which of Alberta&rsquo;s two Grade 12 diploma math courses is the &ldquo;harder&rdquo; one. Alberta&rsquo;s own Guide to Education answers that the question is framed wrongly: its math sequences &ldquo;are designed based on content rather than level of difficulty.&rdquo; Math 30-1 points toward programs that need calculus; Math 30-2 toward everything else. This page sets the two side by side from Alberta Education&rsquo;s documents and solves the same two problems in the style each course expects. We teach both alongside the school course, and we do not grant provincial credits.

## Two courses, two destinations

Each bulletin opens its course description with the destination, and the destinations differ in one word: calculus. Everything else on this page follows from that.

### What the 30-2 bulletin stresses

The 30-2 description puts modelling at the centre: graphing calculators are used &ldquo;to explore and create patterns, examine relationships, test conjectures, model, and solve problems.&rdquo; It adds a line the 30-1 bulletin does not have, that &ldquo;solutions may vary depending upon how the problem is understood.&rdquo;

Its topics show why. Logical reasoning (sets, Venn diagrams, puzzles) and probability (odds, mutually exclusive and dependent events) carry about half of the exam. Neither appears as a strand in 30-1.

### What the 30-1 bulletin stresses

The 30-1 course is algebra-first: transformations of functions, inverses, logarithms, polynomial, radical and rational functions, then a full trigonometry strand with identities and proofs. Those are the skills a first calculus course assumes on day one.

Counting is shared ground. Both courses meet permutations and combinations, but 30-1 carries them to the binomial theorem, while 30-2 uses them inside probability questions.

## Same morning, same weight, different questions

The two exams share a great deal of their structure. Alberta&rsquo;s 2026 to 2027 schedule puts Mathematics 30-1 and Mathematics 30-2 in the same 9 AM to 12 PM slot on each date: Tuesday 3 November 2026, then 20 January, 8 April, 17 June and 5 August 2027. Both count for 30 per cent of the course mark. Where they part is the written response.

### Written responses in 30-1

Three questions, each with two parts scored on a 2-mark and a 3-mark guide, for five marks a question. The student guide puts it simply: &ldquo;Written-response questions are worth five marks each in Math 30-1 and seven marks each in Math 30-2.&rdquo;

The 30-1 commentary focuses on algebraic process: proving identities, simplifying versus solving, and sketching graphs with every key feature.

### Written responses in 30-2

Two questions, each of four parts: the 30-2 bulletin says each &ldquo;will begin with a 1-mark part followed by three 2-mark parts.&rdquo; Each part goes on its own answer sheet, so the student writes eight separate pieces.

The 30-2 markers write most about communication: labelling odds and probability correctly, using &ldquo;appropriate use of units and appropriate rounding&rdquo;, and relating an answer back to the context.

**Where Math 30-2 students lose marks, from the 2026 to 2027 bulletin commentary (left column quoted; right column our response)**

| Alberta&rsquo;s commentary on 30-2 | How we teach it |
|---|---|
| Odds should be considered &ldquo;part-part&rdquo; and probability &ldquo;part-whole&rdquo; | Every answer is labelled: odds in favour, odds against, or P(event) |
| &ldquo;Some students continue to express probability as a percent&rdquo; | Probabilities are written as fractions or decimals from 0 to 1, never as percentages |

A student moving from one course to the other changes not just topics but the kind of mistake to watch for.

### Content, not difficulty

The clearest sign of how Alberta thinks about these courses is a rule on course challenges. Normally a student cannot challenge a course in a lower sequence after earning credit in a higher one. Mathematics is the exception: &ldquo;High school mathematics course sequences are an exception, as they are designed based on content rather than level of difficulty.&rdquo;

The Guide goes on: &ldquo;A student may challenge Mathematics 20-3 or Mathematics 20-2 after being awarded credits in Mathematics 20-1&rdquo;, and &ldquo;The same exception applies to 30-level mathematics courses.&rdquo; In diploma courses, a challenge covers only the school-awarded mark; the diploma exam still has to be written.

### Who decides a switch

Switching sequences is a school matter. The Guide says &ldquo;The school authority shall have a policy that clearly states the criteria to be met by a student who wishes to change course sequences.&rdquo; So the first conversation is with the school counsellor, not with a tutor.

For the diploma itself, the math requirement is at the 20 level: the Guide lists &ldquo;Mathematics 20-1, Mathematics 20-2 or Mathematics 20-3&rdquo; among the requirements for the 100-credit Alberta High School Diploma. A 30-level math course is a choice made for what comes after.

## Two problems, solved the 30-2 way and the 30-1 way

These are our own problems, not Alberta Education questions. Each one sits inside both courses, and each is solved twice: once the way a 30-2 student is taught to model it, and once the way a 30-1 student is expected to handle it algebraically.

Comparison 1: growth. A town of 8 000 people grows by 3 per cent a year. After how many years will it reach 12 000?

### The 30-2 way: model, then solve

Write the model from the context: P = 8000(1.03)t, where t is in years. The growth factor 1.03 comes from adding 3 per cent to 100 per cent.

Set 8000(1.03)t = 12 000, so (1.03)t = 1.5. Since 1.5 is not a power of 1.03, there is no common base, which is exactly the case Alberta&rsquo;s 30-2 commentary says students find hard. Either graph y = 8000(1.03)x and y = 12 000 and find the intersection, or take logarithms: t = log 1.5 / log 1.03 = 13.717..., so about 13.7 years. Then answer in context: the town passes 12 000 during its fourteenth year.

### The 30-1 way: algebra, then structure

A 30-1 question would ask the student to algebraically determine t, so the graph is a check, not the method: log((1.03)t) = log 1.5, then t log 1.03 = log 1.5 by the power law, then t = 13.717...

It might then push further: write t as a function of P. Solving P = 8000(1.03)t for t gives t = log1.03(P/8000), the inverse of the growth model, with domain P > 0. Inverses and logarithmic functions as objects in their own right are 30-1 territory.

Comparison 2: chance. A bag holds 5 red and 3 blue marbles. Two are drawn without replacement. What is the probability that both are the same colour, and what are the odds in favour?

### The 30-2 way: cases and odds

There are two cases, and the 30-2 commentary warns that students who see more than one case often fail to combine them. Both red: (5/8)(4/7) = 20/56. Both blue: (3/8)(2/7) = 6/56. The cases are mutually exclusive, so add: 26/56 = 13/28, about 0.46.

Odds compare part with part: 13 favourable to 15 unfavourable, so the odds in favour are 13:15. Writing 13:28 here would be the &ldquo;part-whole&rdquo; mix-up the markers describe.

### The 30-1 way: count the selections

A 30-1 student would count instead. Ways to choose two reds: 5C2 = 10. Ways to choose two blues: 3C2 = 3. Ways to choose any two: 8C2 = 28. So 13 of the 28 equally likely selections are same-colour.

The answer is identical, 13/28, and that is the lesson: the counting methods 30-1 builds are the engine underneath the probability 30-2 uses. A student switching courses carries more across than they fear.

## Eight practice questions, labelled by course

All eight are our own. The first five are in the style of Math 30-2, the next two in the style of Math 30-1, and the last belongs to both. None is taken from an Alberta exam or practice test.

1. 30-2. The probability of rain tomorrow is 0.35. State the odds in favour of rain in lowest terms.
2. 30-2. The odds against an event are 5:3. What is the probability that it happens, as a decimal?
3. 30-2. Simplify (x2 &minus; 9) / (x2 + 5x + 6) and state the non-permissible values.
4. 30-2. Solve x + 6/(x &minus; 3) = 2x/(x &minus; 3), and justify any root you reject.
5. 30-2. In a group of 40 students, 22 play soccer, 15 play basketball and 9 play both. How many play neither?
6. 30-1. The point (4, &minus;2) lies on y = f(x). Find its image on y = 3f(&minus;x) &minus; 1.
7. 30-1. Verify that (1 + tan2x)cos2x = 1 for x = &pi;/4. Does this prove the identity?
8. Both. How many different arrangements can be made from all the letters of PRAIRIE?

1. 7:13. The probability 0.35 means 35 parts in favour out of 100, so 65 against: 35:65, which reduces to 7:13.
2. 0.375. Odds against of 5:3 mean 3 favourable outcomes for every 5 unfavourable, out of 8: P = 3/8 = 0.375.
3. (x &minus; 3)/(x + 2), with x &ne; &minus;2 and x &ne; &minus;3. Factor: (x &minus; 3)(x + 3) over (x + 2)(x + 3). The non-permissible values come from the original denominator, so &minus;3 stays excluded after cancelling.
4. x = 2. First note x &ne; 3. Multiply by (x &minus; 3): x(x &minus; 3) + 6 = 2x, so x2 &minus; 5x + 6 = 0 and x = 2 or x = 3. Reject x = 3, because it makes a denominator zero. Check x = 2: 2 + 6/(&minus;1) = &minus;4, and 4/(&minus;1) = &minus;4.
5. 12. Those who play at least one sport: 22 + 15 &minus; 9 = 28, since the 9 were counted twice. Neither: 40 &minus; 28 = 12.
6. (&minus;4, &minus;7). The mapping is (x, y) to (&minus;x, 3y &minus; 1): reflect in the y-axis, stretch vertically by 3, then translate down 1. So x becomes &minus;4 and y becomes 3(&minus;2) &minus; 1 = &minus;7.
7. True at &pi;/4, but not a proof. tan(&pi;/4) = 1 and cos2(&pi;/4) = 1/2, so (1 + 1)(1/2) = 1. Alberta defines verify as checking a particular case; a proof must show the identity for every permissible x, for example by replacing 1 + tan2x with sec2x.
8. 1260. PRAIRIE has 7 letters, with R twice and I twice: 7! / (2! &times; 2!) = 5040 / 4 = 1260.

## How to choose between Math 30-1 and Math 30-2

Our checklist for a family making the choice, usually in Grade 10 or Grade 11 when the 20-level course is picked. It is advice, not a rule: the school&rsquo;s counsellor and the student&rsquo;s intended programs have the final word.

1. Start from the destination. List two or three programs the student might apply to and read each one&rsquo;s admission page for the year of entry. If any of them requires calculus or names Math 30-1, keep the 30-1 route open.
2. Remember the diploma needs less. The Alberta High School Diploma requires a 20-level math course (20-1, 20-2 or 20-3). Grade 12 math is chosen for what comes after school.
3. Look at Math 10C and 20-1 honestly. Not the mark alone, but the algebra: can the student factor, rearrange formulas and work with functions without notes? 30-1 builds on exactly that.
4. Weigh the interests. A student who enjoys logic puzzles, data and chance may find 30-2 a better fit for its content, which is how Alberta describes the difference.
5. Ask the school about switching. The chart shows routes between the sequences, and each school authority must have a policy on the criteria. Find out before the semester starts, not halfway through it.
6. Plan Math 31 early if calculus is likely. Math 31 needs 30-1 as a prerequisite or corequisite, so a student who wants it must be on the 30-1 track in time.
7. Decide, then commit. Whichever course is chosen, the diploma exam is 30 per cent of the mark. The first unit deserves full effort, because it sets up the rest.

### When 30-2 is the right call

For a student heading into a trade, the workforce, or a program that does not ask for calculus, Math 30-2 is not a lesser course: it is the course Alberta built for that destination, with probability, logical reasoning and modelling that many adults use more often than trigonometric identities.

We tutor 30-2 students with the same seriousness as 30-1 students, and we push them on communication, because that is where the 30-2 markers say marks are lost.

### When 30-1 is worth the stretch

For a student with a realistic interest in engineering, the sciences, computer science or anything else built on calculus, 30-1 is the course that keeps those doors open. It asks for steady algebra, and that can be built.

If a student chooses 30-1 and struggles in the first unit, the usual cause is a gap from Math 20-1: factoring, function notation or exponent laws. Those gaps can be closed in a few weeks of focused work, which is often a better plan than switching.

## Math 30-1 vs 30-2: common questions

### What is the difference between Math 30-1 and Math 30-2?

Alberta designs Math 30-1 for post-secondary programs that require calculus, and Math 30-2 for daily life, the workforce and programs that do not. 30-1 centres on functions and trigonometry; 30-2 adds logical reasoning and probability and stresses modelling.

### Is Math 30-2 easier than Math 30-1?

Alberta's Guide to Education says its high school math sequences are designed based on content rather than level of difficulty. The courses differ in what they cover and where they lead, and each has its own hard parts.

### Do both courses have a diploma exam?

Yes. Mathematics 30-1 and Mathematics 30-2 are both diploma exam courses, each exam counts for 30 percent of the final mark, and in 2026 to 2027 both are written in the same 9 AM slot on the same five dates.

### How are the written responses different?

Math 30-1 has 3 written-response questions worth 5 marks each, in two parts. Math 30-2 has 2 written-response questions worth 7 marks each, a 1-mark part followed by three 2-mark parts. Both exams also have 32 machine-scored questions worth 75 percent.

### Can my teen switch from Math 30-1 to Math 30-2?

Alberta's course chart shows routes between the two sequences, and each school authority must have a policy setting out the criteria for changing sequences. Ask the school counsellor about the policy before the semester begins.

### Does the Alberta diploma require Math 30-1 or 30-2?

Neither. The Alberta High School Diploma requires a 20-level math course: Mathematics 20-1, 20-2 or 20-3. A 30-level math course is chosen for post-secondary plans.

### Which course leads to Math 31?

Math 30-1. Alberta's Guide to Education says Mathematics 30-1 is a prerequisite or corequisite for Mathematics 31.

### Do you grant credit for Math 30-1 or 30-2?

No. We teach both courses alongside school and we do not grant provincial credits. Credit and the 70 percent school mark are matters for the student's school.
