## Two City of Regina layers, two answers about playgrounds

The City of Regina publishes its parks as open data in two layers: park areas with yes or blank columns for each amenity, and a separate set of amenity points. Both describe playgrounds. Our students check whether they agree. The counts are ours.

**Our reconciliation of the City of Regina ParksAndAmenities layers, downloaded 22 October 2026 (998 park areas, 977 amenity points), matched on the NAME column**

| Measure | Our count |
|---|---|
| Park areas in layer 0 | 998, under 442 different names |
| Park areas with PLAYGROUND marked Yes | 210, under 200 names |
| Playground points in layer 1 | 254, under 220 names |
| Names in both lists | 175 |
| Names flagged Yes but with no playground point | 25 |
| Names with a playground point but not flagged | 45, of which 35 do not appear in layer 0 at all |

Here is the script. It uses only the json module in Python 3, with both downloaded files in the same folder, and Python sets do the matching:

```
import json

with open("parks-layer0.geojson", encoding="utf-8") as f:
    parks = [feat["properties"] for feat in json.load(f)["features"]]
with open("amenities-layer1.json", encoding="utf-8") as f:
    points = [feat["attributes"] for feat in json.load(f)["features"]]

flagged = {p["NAME"] for p in parks if p["PLAYGROUND"] == "Yes"}
pointed = {a["NAME"] for a in points if a["ASSET_TYPE"] == "PLAYGROUND"}

print("park areas:", len(parks), "distinct names:", len({p["NAME"] for p in parks}))
print("playground points:", sum(1 for a in points if a["ASSET_TYPE"] == "PLAYGROUND"))
print("names flagged Yes:", len(flagged))
print("names with a point:", len(pointed))
print("in both:", len(flagged & pointed))
print("flag only:", len(flagged - pointed))
print("point only:", len(pointed - flagged))
```

### One park, many shapes

The first surprise is that 998 areas carry only 442 names. A large park can be drawn as many polygons, so counting rows counts pieces, not parks. Turning names into a set fixes that before any comparison.

### Set operations

With two sets of names, the questions become one line each: the intersection gives names on both lists, and the two differences give names on only one. A Grade 9 student can follow it, and it is exactly the kind of data structure work Computer Science 30 names.

### Why the key fails

Of the 45 point-only names, 35 never appear among the park areas. That points to names spelled or recorded differently in the two layers rather than missing playgrounds. A text name is a weak key; the stronger next step is a spatial test of which area each point falls inside.

### What a careful student writes

Not &ldquo;the city has 254 playgrounds&rdquo;, and not &ldquo;45 playgrounds are missing from the map&rdquo;. A fair sentence is that the two layers agree on 175 park names and differ on 70, and that most of the differences look like naming mismatches. The file supports that, and nothing stronger.

Regina&rsquo;s winters show up in the same file: the amenity layer lists 34 outdoor rink shelters, 31 unboarded and 24 boarded outdoor rinks, 19 toboggan hills and 7 cross-country ski trails, a good second exercise in grouping categories. For Grades 9, 10 and 11 respectively. CEMC lists them for Wednesday 7 April 2027: four questions mixing answer-only and full-solution parts, in 75 minutes. Writing a clear solution is the skill being tested.

The Canadian Team Mathematics Contest takes teams of six students from any mix of Grades 9 to 12. CEMC lists it at the University of Waterloo on Tuesday 13 April 2027, and in schools on Wednesday 14 April 2027 or later.

## A clock written into Saskatchewan law, and what it means for lessons

Most of Canada changes its clocks on 1 November 2026. Regina does not. Here is what the province&rsquo;s own bill says, and how our class times follow from it.

**Regina class times in India time, all year. Regina is on UTC-6 with no daylight saving (IANA tzdb 2026e); the conversions are ours**

| Regina class time | India time | Same in winter and summer? |
|---|---|---|
| 5:00 pm on a weekday | 4:30 am the next day | Yes |
| 7:00 pm on a weekday | 6:30 am the next day | Yes |
| 11:00 am on a Sunday | 10:30 pm the same day | Yes |

### The Time Act, 2026

Bill No. 58 defines central standard time as UTC-6 and says the time to be observed in Saskatchewan is central standard time, including in Lloydminster. The bill comes into force by order of the Lieutenant Governor in Council; we have not confirmed that order, and nothing on this page depends on it.

### Regina numbers

Saskatchewan numbers start with 306, 639 or 474. The form takes the ten digits; Canada&rsquo;s +1 is already set.

### Booked once, fixed all year

Because neither Regina nor India moves its clocks, a class booked in October is at the same Regina time in February and in May. We still confirm every time in Saskatchewan time.

## Can a Regina student let AI write the Computer Science 30 capstone?

It could write a lot of the code. It could not do the part the course actually assesses.

Ask an AI tool to compare the two Regina layers and it may report 45 missing playgrounds with total confidence. It has no way of knowing that 35 of those names simply never appear in the other layer. A student who wrote the set operations, then went looking at why the names differed, has done the thinking that CS30-CAP2&rsquo;s documentation and reflection are meant to capture.

We teach that thinking: understand the problem, build the solution yourself, then challenge it. Students meet AI assistants as they grow, inside each tool&rsquo;s age rules, as a tutor to question, never as the author of work they submit.

A capstone you cannot explain line by line is not proficiency, and Saskatchewan&rsquo;s outcome asks for proficiency. The longer argument is in [our longer piece on why the reasoning cannot be outsourced](/blog/is-coding-worth-learning-2026).
