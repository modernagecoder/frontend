KS2 SATs maths papers: arithmetic, reasoning and the scaled score

The short answer. Year 6 SATs maths is three papers: Paper 1 arithmetic (40 marks, 30 minutes) and Papers 2 and 3 reasoning (35 marks and 40 minutes each). Raw marks out of 110 are converted to a scaled score from 80 to 120, and 100 or more means the expected standard. In 2027 the papers are on 12 and 13 May.

One arithmetic paper, two reasoning papers.

The tests are taken on paper. The equipment list for Paper 1 is a pen or dark pencil, a ruler and a rubber; for the reasoning papers it adds a sharp pencil for drawing, a protractor and a mirror. No calculator appears on the list.

The two reasoning papers have no significant differences in format, and questions in each paper are placed in an approximate order of difficulty, so the hardest questions tend to come at the end.

Where the marks come from

Across all three papers, number, ratio and algebra carry 83 to 93 of the 110 marks (75 to 85%). Measurement, geometry and statistics carry the other 17 to 27 marks, all on the reasoning papers. Paper 1 is entirely number.

Scaled scores

Why 56 marks out of 110 meant 100 in 2026.

Each child's marks from the three papers are added to make a raw score out of 110. Because the papers change every year and may be slightly easier or harder, the raw score is converted into a scaled score so results can be compared across years.

Scaled scores run from 80 to 120. A score of 100 or more means the child has met the expected standard; 99 or less means they have not yet met it. A child also needs a minimum raw score to get any scaled score at all. The expected standard itself was set by panels of teachers in 2016 and has been held steady since.

The raw score needed for 100 moves a little each year with the difficulty of the papers. If the test is harder, fewer raw marks are needed; if easier, more.

A formal written method, set out so every step can be checked.

Arithmetic style

2/3 + 1/4

2/3 = 8/12
1/4 = 3/12
8/12 + 3/12 = 11/12

A common denominator first. A frequent slip is adding the denominators.

Reasoning style

A school packs 500 pencils into boxes of 45. How many boxes are full, and how many pencils are left over?

500 ÷ 45 = 11 remainder 5
check: 11 × 45 = 495, and 495 + 5 = 500
11 full boxes, 5 pencils left

Divide, then decide what the remainder means in the story: here it is the pencils that do not fill a box.

How to prepare

Four habits that protect marks on the day.

Arithmetic little and often

Paper 1 is 40 marks in 30 minutes. Short daily practice of written methods builds the speed that a single long session cannot.

Show the working

On longer calculations, a clear method shows exactly where a slip happened, which makes it far easier to fix in practice.

Read reasoning questions twice

Underline what is being asked. A common slip is a correct calculation that answers a different question.

Check with the opposite operation

A multiplication checked by division, or a subtraction checked by addition, catches errors before they cost marks.

Common questions

Year 6 SATs maths: what parents ask.

How many maths papers are there in Year 6 SATs?

Three: Paper 1 arithmetic, and Papers 2 and 3 reasoning.

How long is each KS2 maths paper?

Paper 1 arithmetic is 30 minutes. Papers 2 and 3 are 40 minutes each, so the whole maths test is 110 minutes.

How many marks is the KS2 maths test out of?

110 marks: 40 on the arithmetic paper and 35 on each reasoning paper.

What is a good scaled score in KS2 SATs?

A scaled score of 100 or more means a child has met the expected standard. Scores run from 80 to 120.

How many marks do you need for 100 in KS2 maths?

It changes each year. In 2026 a raw score of 56 out of 110 converted to a scaled score of 100.

When are the 2027 KS2 SATs?

From Monday 10 May to Thursday 13 May 2027. Maths Papers 1 and 2 are on Wednesday 12 May and Paper 3 on Thursday 13 May.

Can children use a calculator in KS2 SATs maths?

No calculator is on the equipment list for any of the three papers. The list is a pen or dark pencil, ruler and rubber, plus a protractor and mirror for the reasoning papers.

Are SATs and key stage 2 tests the same thing?

Yes. The government calls them key stage 2 national curriculum tests and notes that some people refer to them as SATs.

What topics come up most in SATs maths?

Number, ratio and algebra carry 75 to 85% of the marks. Measurement, geometry and statistics carry the rest, and only on the reasoning papers.
