Gestel today

Three wijken between the Dommel and the campus

Since Napoleonic times Gestel belonged to the municipality of Gestel en Blaarthem, and it was one of the villages joined to Eindhoven on 1 January 1920. The statistics office now divides it into three wijken, Rozenknopje, Oud-Gestel and Gestelse Ontginning, whose areas add to 953 hectares, 917 of land and 35 of water. The encyclopaedia gives Gestel as 9.53 square kilometres, 9.17 land and 0.35 water, the same figures to the hectare, so the three wijken are the whole stadsdeel. The encyclopaedia calls the third wijk Oud Kasteel where the office says Gestelse Ontginning; the areas show that the two names cover the same ground. The 2026 resident counts add to 28,334, a sum of three published rows rather than a published total.

Rozenknopje, next to the centre

6,934 residents on 111 hectares of land, the most tightly built of the three, in three buurten: Hagenkamp, the Schrijversbuurt and the Oude Spoorbaan. Six in ten households hold one person, 2,455 of 4,120, and 57 percent of the 3,751 dwellings are rented. One secondary establishment here teaches 1,209 pupils.

Oud-Gestel, the old village and the parks

13,340 residents on 561 hectares, in seven buurten from Genderdal and Rapelenburg to Blaarthem, Gennep and the Beemden. Much of the land is the Genneper Parken and the water of the Dommel and the Gender, which is why the density is low for a wijk this full. Two thirds of the 6,958 dwellings are rented, 4,662 of them.

Gestelse Ontginning, owned and older

8,060 residents on 245 hectares, in three buurten: Genderbeemd, Hanevoet and Ooievaarsnest. The owner-occupied share is the highest in Gestel, 2,290 of 3,694 dwellings, and so is the share over sixty-five, 1,845 residents, 23 percent. Four primary establishments teach 954 pupils.

A painted mill, a village church and a research campus

The encyclopaedia entry for Gestel supplies the landmarks. The Sint-Lambertuskerk, with its pastorie, was built in 1910 to a design by Wolter te Riele. The Genneper Watermolen was painted four times on canvas by Vincent van Gogh. The Historisch Openlucht Museum Eindhoven stands in the Genneper Parken, among a cluster of other sports grounds. The Clarissenklooster is on the Claralaan and the convent of the Zusters van Liefde van Schijndel on the Hoogstraat. The small river Gender, which once meandered, has become a drainage channel, while the Dommel still flows south of Gestel. And the High Tech Campus Eindhoven, which began in 1998 as the central research centre of Philips and opened to other innovative companies in 2003, lies in the south of Gestel.

Signature project

Say the gap in points first, then say which ratio you mean

A difference between two percentages can be taken by subtracting or by dividing, from either end, on either side of the complement, or as odds. Only one of those is symmetric. The rest are honest only when they are labelled.

1. Start with the counts and the bases

2,296 of 6,958 and 2,290 of 3,694. Before any comparison, write down both numbers in each fraction, because the counts alone say the wijken are identical and the shares alone hide how different the wijken are in size.

2. Take the difference in points

Subtract the shares: 62.0 minus 33.0 is 29 points. Start from either wijk and the answer is the same, and it is the same for owning and for renting. That symmetry is why it is the safest first figure, and why a percentage point should never be called a percent.

3. Then any ratio, with its base named

88 percent higher, measured from Oud-Gestel. 47 percent lower, measured from Gestelse Ontginning. 76 percent more renting, measured on the other side. Odds 3.3 times. Each is fine with its base stated in the same sentence, and misleading without it.

Subtracting is symmetric, dividing is not, and code has to choose

The reason relative figures run away is arithmetic, not dishonesty. Divide by a small base and every change looks enormous, which is why error rates near zero produce spectacular percentages and why a model that goes from 2 percent wrong to 1 percent wrong can be announced as halving its errors. Divide from the other end and the same gap shrinks, because the base is bigger; that is 88 percent against 47. Switch to the complement and the base changes again, which turns 88 into 76. Odds stretch the scale further still, and an odds ratio of 3.3 is regularly read aloud as three times as likely, which it is not. Programming meets this choice directly. The standard comparison function in Python, math.isclose, takes two tolerances, a relative one and an absolute one, because neither works alone: a relative tolerance is useless when comparing values close to zero, and an absolute one is meaningless when comparing large ones. Tests of numerical code, dashboards of conversion rates and papers reporting model gains all make the same decision, whether or not anyone notices. The discipline is simple and almost never followed. Give the difference in points, or in the raw unit, first; then, if a ratio is useful, give it with its base named in the same sentence; and never let a percentage point be written as a percent. Gestel makes the point with two wijken on one river that own the same number of homes, six apart, and differ by 29 points.

Coding and AI

An AI will say one wijk of Gestel has 88 percent more homeowners. Why should a teenager in Hanevoet ask 88 percent of what?

Because it is also 29 points, 47 percent fewer, odds 3.3 times, and six homes.

Give a language model the two tenure rows and ask how the wijken compare, and it will usually choose a ratio, because ratios sound informative and most of the comparison sentences it learned from use them. It may say owner-occupation is 88 percent higher in Gestelse Ontginning, which is true, or nearly twice as high, which is also true, and it is unlikely to mention that the same data supports 47 percent lower from the other side, or that the two wijken contain the same number of owner-occupied homes. The reply is fluent and correct in every word. The reader has simply been handed one of five framings, chosen by the grammar of the question rather than by anyone deciding what matters.

A learner who has written code that compares results, and has had to pick between a relative and an absolute tolerance to make a test pass for the right reason, knows that every comparison is a decision. Machine learning makes the lesson constant, since every paper, leaderboard and release note reports an improvement, and the difference between one point and fifty percent fewer errors is the difference between an honest release note and a misleading one. Models will go on picking framings that sound good. Asking in points, from which base and on which side stays a human habit.

The case for a child in Gestel learning to code in 2026 is therefore not the research campus at the southern edge of the stadsdeel. It is that a program comparing two numbers has to say how it compares them, and a person who has written one learns to ask every headline the same thing. The longer argument is in why a child should still learn to code in 2026.

What does Gestel include?

How different are Oud-Gestel and Gestelse Ontginning in home ownership?

It depends how the gap is stated, and every version is true. They hold nearly the same number of owner-occupied homes, 2,296 and 2,290. As shares that is 33 percent against 62, a gap of 29 percentage points. What is the difference between a percent and a percentage point?

A percentage point is a subtraction of two percentages: 62 percent minus 33 percent is 29 points. A percent change is a division: 62 over 33 is 88 percent higher. Points give the same answer from either side; percents do not, which is why 88 percent higher and 47 percent lower can describe the same gap.
