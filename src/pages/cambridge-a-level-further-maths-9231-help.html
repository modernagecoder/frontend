Cambridge A level Further Maths 9231 help

Cambridge International AS and A Level Further Mathematics, syllabus 9231, is offered by Cambridge International to schools in its network worldwide. It is examined in four papers, two of pure and one each of mechanics and probability and statistics, combined in fixed ways for AS and for the full A Level. More than most Further Maths qualifications, it is written to sit on top of a particular A Level Maths, Cambridge 9709, paper by paper. This page sets out that structure, the routes and how we teach 9231.

9231 in brief

Cambridge International AS and A Level Further Mathematics (9231) has four papers: Further Pure Mathematics 1 and 2 (each 2 hours, 75 marks), Further Mechanics and Further Probability and Statistics (each 1 hour 30 minutes, 50 marks). AS is Paper 1 with Paper 3 or Paper 4; the A Level is all four. Exams run in June and November, and only a scientific calculator is allowed. Four papers

What are the four Cambridge 9231 Further Maths papers?

Two 2-hour pure papers worth 75 marks, and two 90-minute applied papers worth 50. Every question is compulsory and structured.

The 9231 syllabus for 2026 and 2027 lists the components plainly and says "All AS Level candidates take two written papers" and "All A Level candidates take four written papers." Every paper is "Answer all questions", written and externally assessed.

Complex numbers sit on Paper 2, not Paper 1. That differs from the English A level boards, where the Department for Education places complex numbers in the compulsory core. For 9231 AS candidates, who sit Paper 1 but not Paper 2, complex numbers are therefore not examined at all.

Routes

What are the AS and A Level routes for Cambridge 9231?

Three: AS only, an A Level staged over two years, or the whole A Level in one series. AS always uses Paper 1 plus one applied paper.

The syllabus offers AS candidates two options: "Further Pure Mathematics 1 and Further Mechanics (Paper 1 and Paper 3)" or "Further Pure Mathematics 1 and Further Probability & Statistics (Paper 1 and Paper 4)." Paper 2 is "Not available for AS Level". The A Level "includes all four components".

  2. Pick the applied paper for AS: Paper 3 suits students strong in 9709 mechanics; Paper 4 suits those strong in 9709 statistics. That pairing advice is ours.
  3. For the staged route, schedule Paper 2 and the remaining applied paper for the second year.
  4. Check with the school's exams officer which series, June or November, and which administrative zone timetable applies.
  5. Confirm the syllabus year: the 2026 and 2027 syllabus is current, and Cambridge already lists a 2028 to 2030 syllabus on its 9231 page.

9709 first

Which Cambridge 9709 papers do you need before 9231?

Cambridge expects most of 9709 first, and names the exact 9709 papers each 9231 paper assumes. Pure Mathematics 3 is needed for every one.

The syllabus states: "It is expected that learners will have studied the majority of the Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics (9709) subject content before studying Cambridge International AS & A Level Further Mathematics (9231)." It then gives a table of prior knowledge, which we reproduce below because it is the single most useful planning tool for a 9231 student.

Prior knowledge from 9709 required for each 9231 paper, from section 3 of the 9231 syllabus
9231 paper9709 papers assumed
Paper 1 Further Pure Mathematics 19709 Papers 1 and 3
Paper 2 Further Pure Mathematics 29709 Papers 1 and 3
Paper 3 Further Mechanics9709 Papers 1, 3 and 4
Paper 4 Further Probability and Statistics9709 Papers 1, 3, 5 and 6

Read the table for its consequences. Every 9231 paper needs 9709 Paper 3, Pure Mathematics 3, which is A Level rather than AS content in 9709. Further Probability and Statistics needs both 9709 statistics papers, 5 and 6. So a student cannot sensibly sit 9231 AS in the same series as 9709 AS unless 9709 Pure 3 has already been taught. Cambridge permits both syllabuses in the same series ("candidates can take AS & A Level Mathematics (9709) in the same exam series"), but the teaching order still has to respect the table.

A sequence that works

Year 1: 9709 Papers 1 and 3 early, with a 9709 applied paper; begin 9231 Paper 1 in the second half of the year.

Year 2: finish 9231 Paper 1, then Paper 2 and the applied papers, once 9709 Papers 4, 5 and 6 are in place. This plan is ours, not Cambridge's.

A sequence that struggles

Starting 9231 Paper 4 before 9709 Paper 6. The continuous random variables and inference in Further Statistics rest directly on 9709 statistics.

Cambridge also publishes a support document, Guide to prior learning for Paper 4.

Exam rules

Can you use a graphical calculator in Cambridge 9231?

No. Only a scientific calculator is allowed, and an answer from the calculator with no working earns nothing.

The syllabus is direct: "Computers, graphical calculators and calculators capable of symbolic algebraic manipulation or symbolic differentiation or integration are not permitted." It adds that "no marks will be given for unsupported answers from a calculator." This is a real difference from the English A level boards, several of which allow graphical calculators, and students moving between systems should know it.

Examples, ours

Three 9231 style problems, worked by us

One from Further Pure 1, one from Further Mechanics and one from Further Probability and Statistics. None is a Cambridge question.

Our Paper 1 problem, summation by differences. Find the sum of 1/(r(r + 1)) for r = 1 to n, and hence for n = 20. Write 1/(r(r + 1)) = 1/r − 1/(r + 1). Adding from r = 1 to n, every middle term cancels, leaving 1 − 1/(n + 1) = n/(n + 1). For n = 20 that is 20/21. As n grows the sum tends to 1, which is a typical follow-up part.

Our Paper 3 problem, Hooke's law. A light elastic string of natural length 0.8 m and modulus of elasticity 24 N hangs from a fixed point with a 1.2 kg particle at rest on its end. Take g = 10 m s−2 in this example. In equilibrium the tension equals the weight: 24x ÷ 0.8 = 12, so the extension is x = 0.4 m. The elastic potential energy stored is λx2 ÷ 2l = 24 × 0.16 ÷ 1.6 = 2.4 J.

Our Paper 4 problem, a probability generating function. A random variable X takes the values 0, 1 and 2 with probabilities 0.5, 0.3 and 0.2.

The generating function

G(t) = 0.5 + 0.3t + 0.2t2. Check G(1) = 1, as every probability generating function must.

G'(t) = 0.3 + 0.4t, so E(X) = G'(1) = 0.7.

The variance

G''(t) = 0.4, and Var(X) = G''(1) + G'(1) − [G'(1)]2 = 0.4 + 0.7 − 0.49 = 0.61.

Computing E(X2) directly, 0.3 + 0.8 = 1.1, gives 1.1 − 0.49 = 0.61 too.

9231 questions

What students ask about Cambridge Further Maths 9231

How many papers are in Cambridge A Level Further Maths 9231?

Four for the full A Level: Further Pure Mathematics 1 and 2, Further Mechanics, and Further Probability and Statistics. AS Level uses two of them.

What papers do you take for Cambridge AS Further Maths?

Paper 1, Further Pure Mathematics 1, together with either Paper 3, Further Mechanics, or Paper 4, Further Probability and Statistics. Paper 2 is not available at AS Level.

Do I need 9709 before 9231?

Cambridge expects learners to have studied most of 9709 first and strongly recommends studying it alongside or before 9231. Every 9231 paper assumes 9709 Papers 1 and 3.

Can I take 9709 and 9231 in the same exam series?

Yes. The syllabus says candidates can take AS and A Level Mathematics 9709 in the same series as Further Mathematics 9231.

Is a graphical calculator allowed in 9231?

No. Cambridge permits a scientific calculator only; graphical calculators and those with symbolic algebra or calculus are not allowed.

When are the 9231 exams?

Cambridge offers 9231 in the June and November series. The exact dates depend on the school's administrative zone timetable.

What is MF19?

MF19 is the list of formulae and statistical tables supplied in the exam. It is shared between 9709 and 9231, and any formula in it may be needed.

What is Further Maths 9231 equivalent to?

It is the Cambridge International A Level in Further Mathematics, the same level as an A Level. How a university treats it alongside other qualifications is set by that university, so check its admissions page.

When are your 9231 classes?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. UK families see times in UK time.

Are you connected with Cambridge International?

No. Modern Age Coders is independent of Cambridge International and Cambridge University Press and Assessment. All problems on this page are ours.
