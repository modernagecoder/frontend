Technovation Girls guide

Technovation Girls is the name most families know for a free global program in which girls aged 8 to 18 find a problem in their community, build a working tech solution for it, and pitch it on video. The organiser now runs it as the Technovation Challenge, with a Girls Track and, new for the 2027 season, a Co-ed Track. This guide reads the official 2026-27 pages closely: the divisions, every published date with US time zones, the new rule that any working digital solution counts, what goes in the 4-minute video and the written answers, and a 12-week plan.

In short

Technovation Girls is the Girls Track of the Technovation Challenge, a free program for ages 8 to 18 in which teams of 1 to 5 build a working digital solution to a community problem and submit a video of up to 4 minutes plus written answers. The name

Technovation Girls, the Girls Track and the new Co-ed Track

The program people search for as Technovation Girls is now officially the Technovation Challenge. Here is how the parts fit for 2026-27.

What stays the same for girls

The Girls Track keeps the full award structure: Judges will select 15 Finalist teams from around the world, and the final round is judged at World Summit, where one Grand Prize Winner per division is announced.

The curriculum, the honor code and the submission rules are the same for both tracks, so a girls’ team prepares exactly as before.

Local groups are optional

Technovation runs through local Chapters, Sites, Clubs and Classes, often at school districts, nonprofits and universities. The home page says joining one is not mandatory.

A team of friends can take part virtually on its own, which matters in US towns with no local chapter.

Sources: Competition, Submission Guidelines and Get Involved pages, read 17 October 2026. Modern Age Coders works independently of Technovation; we teach the coding and AI skills.

Divisions

Beginner, Junior or Senior: the 2027 divisions

The division is set automatically by the age of the oldest team member on August 1, 2027. The grade column is our rough US mapping; the rule itself is age.

DivisionAge on August 1, 2027 (official)Typical US grade (our mapping)Curriculum (official)
Beginner8 to 12About grades 3 to 730+ hours: ideation, coding, AI, pitching
Junior13 to 15About grades 8 to 1050+ hours, adds entrepreneurship
Senior16 to 18About grades 11 to 1250+ hours, adds entrepreneurship

The oldest member decides

The guidelines state the size rule simply: "A team can have between 1-5 members." Age is where teams get caught out. A team of three 12-year-olds and one 13-year-old competes in the Junior Division, because the rule is "based on the age of the oldest member of your team". Families mixing ages should check this before forming a team.

Use the age on August 1, 2027, not today’s age. A student who turns 13 in July 2027 is already Junior for this season.

Profiles and consent

Every member completes a profile with name, age on August 1, 2027, location and a parental consent form; a media consent form is optional.

Members under 16 on that date need parental approval on file before any identifiable information, such as a full name, photo or location, goes into the profile or the submission.

The competition is not open to anyone older than 18, but the official Competition page adds that the curriculum is free for learners of all ages and genders, so an older sibling or a parent can follow along.

New for 2027

From apps to any working digital solution

The rule underneath has not changed

The guidelines say the solution must involve coding or AI, be tested with real users, and work. A website that only shows information is not eligible; it must be interactive.

What is new is the choice of format, and the team must now explain why the format it chose fits its users and its problem.

Choosing a format (our advice)

Start with the user. A problem for grandparents who text but never install apps suits an SMS tool; a problem for classmates who live on their phones suits a mobile app.

For a first season, choose the format the team can finish and test. A simple working tool tested by ten real people beats an ambitious one that never runs.

What to submit

The 4-minute video and the written answers

Every team submits a video, its technical work and written components. The rules differ a little by division; these are the shared essentials.

Submission essentials for 2027 (official Submission Guidelines)
ItemRule
Video"One video, maximum of 4 minutes", replacing the old separate pitch and technical videos
HostingYouTube or Vimeo, unlisted or public. "Private videos cannot be judged."
LanguageWritten items in English; the video may be in any language, with English subtitles required if it is not in English
Project descriptionAt most 100 words: the problem and the solution
EthicsTwo or more ethical issues considered, such as privacy, accessibility or bias, in at most 150 words
AccuracyHow information and any AI output were checked, with a specific example, in at most 100 words
Learning reflectionAt most 200 words
Senior DivisionA business canvas, and key business canvas elements in the video
Junior DivisionTwo user adoption answers of at most 300 words each

What the Senior and Junior video must show

Why the problem matters and the research behind it; how the solution works and why that format; evidence that target users gave feedback; what was built and with which tools; and "2+ iterations of your solution with specific examples of what improved".

It must also show the debugging process and the metrics or evidence that the solution actually worked. That is a lot for four minutes, so script it.

The Beginner video

Beginners show why the problem matters to them, how they approached it, one or two key features, how they built it, and the features they plan next.

For an 8 to 12-year-old, a clear screen recording with the team explaining in their own words is exactly what the rules ask for.

Fair play

The 2027 Honor Code: mentors, remixes and AI

Every team agrees to the Honor Code when it submits. Three of its rules shape how a student should get help, including from us.

Key points of the 2027 Technovation Honor Code (official Competition page)
TopicWhat the Honor Code says
Own workThe submission is created by the students on the team; remixing other materials or updating a past project is acceptable only with significant improvement
CitingTutorials, code libraries, templates and datasets are cited in the written components
MentorsMentors help students learn and find resources, but "the mentors do not create, design, develop, or write any aspect of the team's submission deliverables"
AI toolsAny AI use is disclosed: the tools, what they helped with, and how the team built on or checked the output; AI "should not replace the students' own thinking"

We hold our own teachers to the same standard. In class, a teacher explains a concept, such as how a button triggers a database write, using a separate example; the student then writes the code for her own project. We never write, design or edit a team’s app, video or written answers. Technovation’s official mentors are volunteers who register, pass a background check and give 30 to 40 hours across the season; our classes are lessons, not that role.

A worked example

Showing that version 2 is better, with numbers

Judges ask for user feedback, two or more iterations and evidence the solution worked. Here is how a team can turn a simple user test into that evidence, using our own sample ratings.

Our example: a Junior team builds a homework reminder app for their middle school. Six classmates try version 1 and rate it from 1 to 5. The team fixes the three complaints that came up most, then the same six testers rate version 2.

How the numbers go into the submission

In the video, one sentence and a simple chart: "After our second iteration, the average rating rose from 2.8 to 4.0 out of 5, and 5 of our 6 testers preferred it." That covers feedback, iteration and evidence at once.

In the written answers, name the three complaints and the exact change made for each. Specific examples of what improved are what the guidelines ask for.

What the averages hide

One tester did not rate version 2 higher. A strong team asks that person why, and reports the answer honestly; that is a third iteration waiting to happen.

This is the lesson we teach: a number means little without the question you asked, how many people answered, and what you changed because of it.

Questions from families

Technovation Girls: common questions

Is Technovation Girls the same as the Technovation Challenge?

Yes. Technovation Girls is the name many families still use. The organiser now calls the program the Technovation Challenge, with a Girls Track and, new for 2027, a Co-ed Track.

Who can enter Technovation?

Young people aged 8 to 18. The official Get Involved page says the competition is free and open to all youth 8-18, and teams are placed in the Girls or Co-ed track by team composition.

How many people can be on a team?

Between 1 and 5 members. The team's division is set by the age of its oldest member on August 1, 2027.

What are the divisions?

Beginner for ages 8 to 12, Junior for 13 to 15 and Senior for 16 to 18, all measured on August 1, 2027 for the 2026-27 season.

When is the 2027 Technovation deadline?

Submissions are due May 5, 2027 at 5pm PDT, which is 8:00 p.m. Eastern. Registration for students and mentors closes March 11, 2027 at 23:59 UTC.

Does the project have to be a mobile app?

No, not any more. For 2027 any working digital solution counts, including web apps, chatbots, browser extensions, dashboards, SMS tools and robots. Scratch is allowed for the Beginner Division only.

How long is the video?

One video of at most 4 minutes on YouTube or Vimeo, set to unlisted or public. Private videos cannot be judged, and non-English videos need English subtitles.

Can a mentor or tutor help build the project?

They can teach skills and point to resources, but under the Honor Code mentors do not create, design, develop or write any part of the submission. Our teachers follow the same rule.

Can teams use AI tools?

Yes, if every use is disclosed in the written components, including which tools, what they helped with and how the team checked the output. AI should not replace the students' own thinking.

When are your classes?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays.

How does Modern Age Coders help with Technovation?

We teach girls to code and build the kind of solution Technovation asks for, test it with users and explain it clearly. The project, the video and the answers remain the team's own work.
