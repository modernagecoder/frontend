Mississippi, United States

Coding and AI classes in Mississippi

Does Mississippi require schools to teach computer science?

Yes, by law. The Mississippi Computer Science and Cyber Education Equality Act (HB 633, approved in 2021) phased computer science into every public school: middle schools from 2022 to 2023, half of high schools from 2023 to 2024, and all schools from 2024 to 2025. Modern Age Coders adds live online classes on top, from block coding in second grade to Python, cybersecurity and AP.

Mississippi took an unusual route. Instead of starting with a single graduation course, the legislature told every district to build computer science into elementary, middle and high school at once, on a three-year clock. In 2021 lawmakers wrote, as a finding of fact, that “More than half of high schools in Mississippi do not teach a computer science course.” By the 2024 to 2025 school year the published count had moved to 204 of 234 high schools. This page reads the act line by line, sets out what the access and participation figures say and do not say, and shows how a family can add depth with live teaching: a Python project on the state’s own census place list, cybersecurity for teens, and AP preparation timed to the College Board calendar.

The 2021 act

What the Computer Science and Cyber Education Equality Act requires

Read from the enrolled text of House Bill 633, 2021 Regular Session, on the Mississippi Legislature’s bill status site on 18 October 2026. The Department of Education site refused our request with a 403 error, so its own guidance is not quoted here.

The HB 633 phase-in, school year by school year (Mississippi Legislature)
FromWhat each district or charter school must provide
2022 to 2023All middle schools offer instruction in foundations of computer science; half of elementary schools give at least one hour of computer science a week; charter schools serving middle or high school offer a course
2023 to 2024At least fifty percent (50%) of the high schools in each district offer a computer science course; every elementary school gives one hour of exploratory computer science each week
2024 to 2025All schools in every district offer instruction in computer science

Topics written into law

The curriculum rests on the 2018 Mississippi College and Career-Readiness Standards for computer science and lists computational thinking, problem solving, programming, cyber security, data science, robotics, and artificial intelligence and machine learning.

What counts as offering

A course counts if the teacher is in the building, or teaches virtually with a proctor onsite at the school. That clause is how small and rural districts were expected to meet the deadlines.

A university partner

The act names the Center for Cyber Education at Mississippi State University as the department’s partner for curriculum, delivery options and teacher training.

Two further clauses matter to high school families. The department’s strategic plan had to identify approved computer science courses that may fulfill at least four (4) units of academic credit for high school graduation, which is about how courses count, not a requirement that every student take one. And the department must report by December 31 of each year through 2025 with a list of the schools in each district teaching computer science. The bill status record shows the governor approved the act on March 24, 2021, with an effective date of July 1, 2021.

Read the curve with care. A school counts as offering computer science once one course is on its books, so 87.2% says nothing about how many seats there are or who fills them. The participation tab and the count tab also round differently (11.1% against 10.9%), so we print both as published and treat neither as a precise current rate.

Signature project

Is Florence one town or two? Cleaning Mississippi’s place names in Python

MARIS, the state’s geographic information center, publishes the Census Bureau’s incorporated places for 2010 and 2020. Merging the two lists sounds trivial. It is a first-class lesson in cleaning and deduplication.

Mississippi incorporated places by census label (our counts from the MARIS layers, queried 18 October 2026)
Label2010 layer2020 layer
city110112
town169168
village1919
Total rows298299

1. The naive merge

Students join the decades on the full label, such as “Jackson city”. Only 297 labels match, and the combined list claims 300 places, because Florence appears once as a town in 2010 and again as a city in 2020.

2. Clean, then compare

Stripping the trailing word city, town or village leaves a bare name. Now Florence matches itself, and the only true newcomer is Diamondhead, a city in the 2020 file with no 2010 row. The deduplicated list holds 299 places.

3. Prove it with an ID

Each row also carries a census GEOID. Matching on that code gives the same 298 shared places, which is how students learn to trust an identifier over text that people type and retype.

Punctuation makes a second exercise. Bay St. Louis, D’Iberville and D’Lo all contain characters that break careless code: a student who lowercases names and deletes every apostrophe and full stop must then check that no two places collapse into one. In this file none do, and proving that, rather than assuming it, is the point. A stretch task converts the ALAND20 field from square meters: by our arithmetic Jackson has the largest land area, about 111.7 square miles.

What these numbers are, and are not

Every count above is ours, from attribute tables downloaded on one day. MARIS describes the layers as extracted from Census TIGER data and states that it makes no warranties about accuracy or completeness. They are a teaching dataset, not an official tally of Mississippi municipalities.

Class times

Scheduling on Central Time from Biloxi to Tupelo

The IANA time zone database lists Mississippi under US central time, represented by America/Chicago. Our teachers work from India, which keeps one clock all year.

The gap in summer

From March to November, with Mississippi on Central Daylight Time, the teacher in India is 10 hours 30 minutes later in the day than a student in Hattiesburg, which is why the teacher, not the family, adjusts to the gap.

The gap in winter

On Central Standard Time the difference grows to 11 hours 30 minutes. Families do not need to track this: every booking is confirmed in Central Time.

Two dates to note

This school year the switch happens on November 1, 2026 (back one hour) and March 14, 2027 (forward one hour), dates the tzdb derives from its Sun>=1 and Sun>=8 rule lines.

A bug worth making once

Ask a student to write a reminder that fires at 4 p.m. Central every Tuesday, using a fixed offset of minus five hours. In November it fires an hour late. Rewriting it with the America/Chicago zone is a five-minute fix that teaches why real software never hard-codes an offset.

Coding and AI

The act lists AI by name. Why learn to code by hand as well?

Because the hard part of the Florence problem is noticing it, and no tool notices for you.

Give an AI assistant the two census files and ask for a merged list, and it may well return 300 rows without comment. The code is fine; the question was wrong. Only a student who has looked at the labels knows that a town can become a city and still be the same place.

Mississippi’s law groups artificial intelligence and machine learning with data science and cyber security for a reason: each depends on judging inputs. Students who write the cleaning step themselves can then use AI tools to go faster without handing over their judgment.

A school course can introduce these ideas; regular practice on messy, local data is what makes them stick. The longer argument is in our case for learning to code while AI writes code.

FAQ

Mississippi coding and AI questions

Answers drawn from the 2021 act and the published data.

Does Mississippi law require computer science in schools?

Yes. HB 633, the Mississippi Computer Science and Cyber Education Equality Act, was approved on March 24, 2021 and took effect on July 1, 2021. From the 2024 to 2025 school year every school in every district must offer computer science instruction.

Is computer science a graduation requirement in Mississippi?

The act requires schools to offer computer science, not every student to pass a course. It asked the department to identify approved courses that may fulfill at least four units of academic credit toward graduation. Ask your high school how those courses are counted.

What did the law require of middle schools?

From 2022 to 2023, each district had to provide that all of its middle schools offer instruction in foundations of computer science, and charter schools serving middle or high school students had to offer a course.

What about elementary schools?

Half of elementary schools had to give at least one hour of computer science a week from 2022 to 2023, and every elementary school one hour of exploratory computer science a week from 2023 to 2024.

Which standards does Mississippi teach from?

The act bases the curriculum on the 2018 Mississippi College and Career-Readiness Standards for computer science, including programming, cyber security, data science, robotics, and artificial intelligence and machine learning.

How many Mississippi high schools offer computer science?

Code.org reports 204 of 234 Mississippi high schools, or 87.2%, offering computer science in 2024 to 2025, up from 47% in 2018 to 2019. Its count tab shows 14,211 high school students enrolled in CS.

Can online classes count toward the Mississippi requirement?

Not ours. The act lets a school use a virtual teacher with a proctor onsite, but that covers approved school courses. Our live classes sit alongside school work for extra depth and AP preparation.

When are classes held for families in Mississippi?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience. Mississippi is on Central Time, and that is the zone every booking is confirmed in.

Is there a Modern Age Coders location in Jackson or on the coast?

No. Every class is taught live online from India, so a student anywhere in Mississippi joins in the same way with a computer and a steady connection.

What is the census place-name project?

Students merge the 2010 and 2020 lists of Mississippi incorporated places published by MARIS, find why a naive merge counts 300 places instead of 299, and fix it by cleaning names and matching on census IDs.
