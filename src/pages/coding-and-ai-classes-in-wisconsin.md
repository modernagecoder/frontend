# Coding and AI classes in Wisconsin

**How many Wisconsin high schools offer computer science?** In 2023 to 2024, 318 of 585 Wisconsin high schools (54.4%) offered computer science, and 8,377 of 262,627 high school students (3.19%) were enrolled in it, according to Code.org (2025). Both figures sit below the national 60% and 6.4%. Modern Age Coders teaches Wisconsin students live online in Central Time, from computational thinking and Roblox to Python and machine learning.

Every number on this page is a share of something, and the first skill we teach Wisconsin students is to ask of what. Just over half of the state&rsquo;s high schools offered computer science in the latest Code.org year, but a little over three in every hundred high school students were enrolled. Same state, same year, two very different percentages, because one counts buildings and the other counts people. Below are both series as published, a Madison open data project that turns the same idea into a chart a student can defend, and the practical details of live classes for families from Superior to Kenosha.

## Share of schools, share of students: Wisconsin&rsquo;s two series

Left column: buildings. Right column: people. Each value is copied from Code.org&rsquo;s 2025 spreadsheet, and where it holds a dash for Wisconsin we write that nothing was reported.

| Measure | Wisconsin | Comparison |

The two series moved in different directions. Between 2020 to 2021 and 2023 to 2024 the share of schools offering computer science rose a few points, while the share of students enrolled drifted down from 4.9% to 3.19%. A school can add a course without many more students taking it; the sheet records what happened and not why, so we make no claim about the cause.

One more denominator note: the student total of 262,627 is the NCES fall 2022 count, as the Code.org tab states, so 3.19% is an estimate built from two different years. Citation: Code.org (2025). 2025 State of AI + CS Education.

## Most Madison parks are small. Most Madison park land is not

The City of Madison publishes every park parcel as open data, each with a Type and an Acreage. Students compute two sets of percentages from the same 297 records and discover that they tell opposite stories.

**Madison park parcels by Type: share of records and share of summed acreage (our calculations from Parks Land Polygons, 18 October 2026)**

| Type | Records | Share of records | Summed acres | Share of acres |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MINI | 102 | 34.3% | 202.5 | 3.4% |
| NEIGHBORHOOD | 84 | 28.3% | 825.6 | 13.8% |
| COMMUNITY | 33 | 11.1% | 2,168.5 | 36.1% |
| CONSERVATION | 21 | 7.1% | 1,850.1 | 30.8% |
| OPEN SPACE | 21 | 7.1% | 112.3 | 1.9% |
| TRAFFICWAY | 23 | 7.7% | 27.9 | 0.5% |
| SPECIAL | 10 | 3.4% | 786.9 | 13.1% |
| SPORTS COMPLEX and OTHER | 3 | 1.0% | 28.5 | 0.5% |

### Choose the denominator

Mini parks are a third of all records but only 3.4% of the acreage. Community parks are about a ninth of the records and more than a third of the land. Neither percentage is wrong; each answers a different question.

### Pie or bar?

With nine categories, two of them tiny, a pie chart turns into slivers nobody can compare. Students draw both and usually choose a sorted horizontal bar chart, with records and acres side by side.

### Check the parts

Our acreage figures are sums of one field across 297 rows, 6,002.45 acres in all. Rounded shares may not add to exactly 100%, which is a rounding effect and a good thing to say out loud in a write-up.

The code is short and the thinking is not. In pandas, df.groupby("Type")["Acreage"].sum() / df["Acreage"].sum() gives the land shares, and df["Type"].value_counts(normalize=True) gives the record shares. The city&rsquo;s own description adds a twist worth reading: SPECIAL parcels include golf courses, maintenance facilities, a botanical garden and the zoo, so a student must decide whether those belong in a chart about parks at all, and say which choice they made.

### Our sums, not the city&rsquo;s

All counts, acreage sums and percentages above are our calculations from one download. The City of Madison publishes the parcels for informational purposes and states that it does not warrant their completeness or accuracy, so 6,002.45 acres is a property of this file, not an official figure for Madison parkland.

## Wisconsin is on Central Time

The IANA time zone database lists Wisconsin among the Central Time states, kept under the America/Chicago zone.

### After school in Madison

A 5:00 p.m. class in Madison begins at 3:30 a.m. the next day in India during daylight saving time, and at 4:30 a.m. after the November change.

### The size of the gap

India stays at UTC plus 5 hours 30 minutes. Wisconsin is UTC minus 5 in summer and UTC minus 6 in winter, so India is 10 hours 30 minutes or 11 hours 30 minutes ahead.

### Dates for 2026 to 2027

Under the US rule in the IANA data, clocks go back on Sunday, November 1, 2026 and forward on Sunday, March 14, 2027, each at 2 a.m. We restate class times in Central Time before both.

**A percentage of a day** Time zones make a neat percent problem too: an 11 hour 30 minute gap is almost half a day. Students who write a converter with America/Chicago and Asia/Kolkata, instead of typed offsets, see the gap change by itself in November.

## An AI model is 90% accurate. Ninety percent of what?

The question students learn to ask about park percentages is the same one they need for every AI claim.

A claim that a model is 90% accurate sounds clear until you ask what the 90% is a share of: all cases, the easy ones, or a test set the model has already seen. The Madison exercise trains exactly that reflex, because the same 297 parcels give 34.3% or 3.4% for mini parks depending on the denominator.

In our AI course, students build small models themselves and report results with the denominator stated, then use AI assistants to speed up the code while checking every figure they print. That combination, fluent with the tools and sceptical of the numbers, is what we aim for.

Wisconsin students who can say what a percentage is a share of will read both school statistics and AI marketing more clearly than most adults. The longer argument is in [is coding still worth it in 2026](/blog/is-coding-worth-learning-2026).
