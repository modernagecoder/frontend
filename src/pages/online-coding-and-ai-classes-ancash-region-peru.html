Coast and valley

Where do people live in Áncash: Chimbote or Huaraz?

More of them on the coast. INEI's 2025 census counted 1,205,923 people in the department of Áncash, in 20 provinces over 35,877.7 square kilometres, and 500,520 of them in the coastal province of Santa alone. The capital, though, is Huaraz.

The Chimbote area, kept separate

INEI has not published 2025 city totals, and its 2017 sources give no single figure for greater Chimbote either. What they do give is two separate centros poblados: Chimbote itself, 179,909 people at 52 metres above sea level, and Buenos Aires in the district of Nuevo Chimbote, 158,385 people at 40 metres.

They are neighbours, but they are counted as two places, and we keep them that way. Adding them would produce a number INEI never published.

Huaraz and the Callejón de Huaylas

The city of Huaraz had 118,836 people in the 2017 census. Within it, INEI's directory lists the centro poblado of Huaraz with 53,733 people at 3,073 metres, and Centenario, in the district of Independencia, with 60,683 at 3,046.

Up the valley lie Carhuaz, Yungay and Caraz, and across the Cordillera Blanca the provinces of the Conchucos side, such as Huari and Pomabamba. Distances are long and roads climb steeply, which is exactly where a class that needs no travel makes a difference.

In 2025 the census counted 117,586 people aged 6 to 11 and 122,886 aged 12 to 17 in the department, close to the primaria and secundaria age bands. These are people interviewed in person, so they sit a little below what a share of the headline total would suggest; INEI adds an estimate of people missed only to the totals. Two public universities serve the region: INEI's compendium counts 7,684 undergraduates at the Universidad Nacional Santiago Antúnez de Mayolo in Huaraz and 5,557 at the Universidad Nacional del Santa in 2025.

A regional signal

On 21 August 2024 the Dirección Regional de Educación de Áncash reported that a mobile classroom, run with MINEDU, had finished a two-week programme in the region for more than 160 secundaria students in rural areas, closing in the district of Ticapampa. It was a trailer equipped to work with or without connectivity, aimed at digital and entrepreneurial skills.

It shows both the appetite and the problem: digital learning in rural Áncash often has to plan for no connection at all. Our classes need one, so we say so plainly.

What to check before the free class

A steady connection for about an hour of video, a laptop or desktop for typed code, and headphones if the house is busy. Children doing Minecraft or Scratch can manage on a tablet with a keyboard.

If the signal is better at certain hours, tell us and choose those hours. One to one makes it easier to pause when the line drops, and the mentor can send the code afterwards so nothing is lost.

School in Áncash

What do Áncash schools teach about computing?

They follow Peru's national curriculum, the CNEB, which has no programming or computing subject. On the 2025 national assessment Áncash matched the national average in primaria maths and fell a little below it in secundaria.

The curriculum, briefly

Under Law 28044 primaria runs for six years and secundaria for five; a child's grade follows from the age they have reached by 31 March, MINEDU's cut-off. The CNEB treats digital skills as competency 28, a transversal competency that belongs to every subject but has no lessons of its own.

The regular secundaria timetable has 4 weekly hours of Matemática, 3 of Inglés and none of programming. MINEDU's only Python pilot, Fábrica de Programadores, is for a thousand older secundaria pupils in the north of Lima, far from Áncash.

Áncash's schools in numbers

In 2025, Áncash's public schools taught 104.2 thousand pupils in primaria and 89.9 thousand in secundaria, and the region had 25,211 teachers across all levels. Nationally, 6,225,000 students in 53,631 schools began the 2026 school year on 16 March 2026.

The secundaria maths figure, 10.7% satisfactory in 2° de secundaria, is where most families tell us they want help: not more of the same exercises, but a different way into the same ideas, often through code.

Contests are open too. The ONEM, Peru's school maths olympiad, held its 2026 school round on 8 July 2026, a two-hour paper of ten questions, and entry is through the colegio. The OPI, the programming olympiad UTEC organises free for every secundaria student, held its 2026 final on 11 April 2026. We teach with our own problems and have no link with MINEDU, UTEC or either contest. A real Áncash project

How long does the soil above Huaraz stay dry?

INAIGEM, the national institute for glacier and mountain ecosystem research, publishes daily soil moisture readings from Tayacoto, in the district of Independencia above Huaraz. Our students use them to count dry spells, and to meet one of the most common bugs in programming: forgetting the streak that is still going when the data runs out.

The data

The dataset is "Mediciones de humedad del suelo en el sector Tayacoto, distrito de Independencia, Ancash", published by INAIGEM on the Plataforma Nacional de Datos Abiertos under the Open Data Commons Attribution Licence (ODC-By). Its metadata says it covers the wetland area (bofedales) of Carhuacpampa from 2019 to 2023, measured by three small automatic stations: two set among pine plantations (02 and 03) and one on grassland (04).

The file has 3,920 daily rows: stations 02 and 03 run from 15 August 2019 to 31 August 2023 with no missing days, and station 04 stops on 4 April 2022. Each row gives the average volumetric soil moisture at 20, 60 and 100 centimetres down.

The question

How many dry spells did the topsoil at station 02 go through, how long was the longest, and how long was the latest? We have to define "dry", and we do it openly: a day is dry, for this exercise, when the 20 centimetre reading is below 6. That threshold is our choice, not INAIGEM's.

The method is the classic streak counter. Walk through the days in order, add one to a counter on each dry day, and when a wet day arrives, save the counter as a finished spell and reset it to zero.

Six spells, the longest of 119 days, from 26 July to 21 November 2020, and the most recent of 80 days, ending on 28 December 2022. It looks finished. But the record ends on 31 August 2023, in the middle of the Andean dry season, and Version 1 only saves a spell when a wet day arrives. The 48 dry days at the very end never met a wet day, so the counter was holding them when the loop stopped and the program simply threw them away.

The fix is one short block after the loop: if the counter is not zero, the file ended during a spell, so save it. Version 2 adds that block, turns the counting into a function, and runs it on all three stations.

Station 02 now has seven spells, and the most recent is the open one, still running on the last day of the file. The other two stations tell a story of their own. Under the second stand of pines, station 03, the topsoil went through 19 dry spells and was dry for its last 110 days. Under grassland, station 04 never once read below 6 at 20 centimetres, though its record is shorter and ends in April 2022. The data cannot say why on its own, and the metadata gives no explanation; what it shows is that a single sentence about "the soil above Huaraz" would hide a large difference between neighbouring stations.

Spells, dates and the threshold of 6 are ours. A careful reader of the publisher's data dictionary will notice that it labels the moisture columns with the unit "grado Celsius", a temperature unit, while the metadata describes them as volumetric soil moisture; we follow the metadata.

Spell 1 has the mirror-image problem. It begins on 15 August 2019, the first day of the file, so the soil may already have been dry before the station started recording. An honest report says "at least 38 days" for that one, just as it says "at least 48 days, still running" for the last.

  1. Under 9, with a calendar. Colour rainy days blue and dry days yellow for two weeks, then count the longest yellow run. What if the two weeks end on a yellow day?
  2. Scratch, around 9 to 11. Make a list of ten numbers and a counter that grows while numbers are below 6 and resets otherwise. Test it with a list that ends on a small number.
  3. First Python, around 11 to 13. Type in the first program, see it miss the last spell, and write your own repair before looking at Version 2.
  4. Ages 13 and over. Change the threshold from 6 to 5 and to 7. Which conclusions survive and which depend on our choice?
  5. Going further. Repeat the count at 60 and 100 centimetres, and find out whether the deeper soil dries later than the surface at each station.

Taught in English

Are the classes in Spanish?

No. Our classes are live, online and in English only, taught by mentors in India who keep their English simple and adjust to each learner.

Where the region stands

In EF's English Proficiency Index 2025, the Áncash region scores 475, in EF's low band (EF's page spells the region "Ankashu"). Peru as a whole scores 519 and Lima city 555. EF lists no separate score for Chimbote or Huaraz.

That makes the first weeks the important ones. Mentors use short instructions, show rather than tell, and put new words in the chat so the learner can read them as well as hear them.

Why English and code fit together

Programming vocabulary is small and repetitive: if, while, print, list. Learners meet the same words every lesson, which makes code an unusually gentle way to practise English.

Replies in Spanish are fine: the mentor follows them and hands back the English version. For a Spanish explanation to share with grandparents, there is clases de programación para niños en inglés.

What time would classes be in Chimbote or Huaraz?

Whatever time suits you. All of Áncash keeps Peru time, UTC minus five all year round, and mentors work to it.

Peru has stayed on UTC minus 5 without summer time since 1994, as the America/Lima entry of the tz database records; India stays on UTC plus 5:30. So the difference is fixed at 10 hours 30 minutes.

A slot agreed in January stays the same in July, because neither country moves its clocks. You choose times on your own clock when booking and the mentor sees the India equivalent. Families on the coast and in the mountains often prefer the morning, when the Indian evening gives a calm start before colegio.

Online coding and AI classes in Áncash: questions and answers

Are the classes in Spanish?

No. Teaching is in English only, live and online. Mentors keep sentences short, repeat when asked, adapt to the learner's pace and accent, and answer a child who speaks Spanish with the English words.

Can we join from Chimbote and from Huaraz?

Yes, from anywhere in Áncash. Classes are online, so a learner in Chimbote, Nuevo Chimbote, Casma, Huaraz, Carhuaz, Caraz or Huari joins the same way, from home.

Is our connection good enough?

Test it in the free class. INEI found an internet service in 51.5% of Áncash homes in 2025. A lesson needs about an hour of steady video; a laptop or desktop is best for typed code.

How young can a child start?

At six, with Minecraft or Scratch blocks, no typing needed. Typed Python normally comes a few years later, and teenagers and adults can start from nothing.

Do Áncash schools teach programming?

Not as a subject. Peru's national curriculum spreads digital skills across all areas and gives secundaria no computing hours; MINEDU's Python pilot runs only in Lima Norte.

What times are available?

Any day, any time, flexible: choose a batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at the hour that suits your family. India is 10 hours 30 minutes ahead of Áncash all year, so 07:00 in Huaraz is 17:30 for the mentor.

Can university students join?

Yes. Students from UNASAM, the Universidad Nacional del Santa or any other university take adult classes, usually Python, data analysis or automation, timed around their lectures.

Do you help with the ONEM or the OPI?

We build the reasoning those contests reward with problems we write ourselves, never the organisers' papers. Students enter through their colegio or the organiser's own registration. We are not connected with MINEDU, UTEC or either olympiad.

How are fees paid?

Monthly by card, in US dollars, converted to soles by your bank. Someone visiting the page from India sees rupees. The first class is free of charge.

Is there a certificate?

Courses that award one give a Modern Age Coders certificate of completion only. It is not a Peruvian qualification, and MINEDU, the UGEL, universities and schools do not issue or endorse it.

What is the soil moisture project?

An original Python lesson on INAIGEM's open readings from Tayacoto, above Huaraz: a streak counter finds 6 dry spells at one station, then a one-line fix finds the 7th, still running when the record ends on 31 August 2023.
