## Direct answers

**Can students in Oman join live AI and machine learning classes from home?**
Yes. Classes are live video with the same teacher every week. Students in Muscat, Salalah, Sohar,
Nizwa and Duqm join from home with a laptop. Oman is on Gulf Standard Time, UTC+4, one hour and
thirty minutes behind Indian Standard Time, so our teaching hours fall on Omani evenings.

**What makes this different from other AI courses available in Oman?**
Most teach prompting. This teaches model building: preparing data, choosing a model, training it,
measuring the error in real units, and improving it. Prompting is a product skill that changes
with the product. Modelling rests on statistics, linear algebra and code, which do not expire.

about OMR 38.500. The Omani rial has been pegged at 2.6008 US dollars to the rial since 1986, so
those rial figures are stable. First class free, no card.

## The first project: the Omani date yield model

Dates are Oman's primary crop, occupying 49 per cent of cultivated land and 82 per cent of all
fruit grown. More than nine million palms across roughly 62,000 acres produce 400,655 tonnes a
year, placing Oman eighth in the world and second in the Gulf.

Training data, output by governorate:

| Governorate | Output, tonnes |
|---|---|
| Al Dakhiliyah | 70,604 |
| Al Dhahirah | 66,421 |
| Al Batinah South | 58,508 |
| Al Batinah North | 55,487 |

Cultivar shares: Khalas 15 per cent, Naghal 12 per cent, Fardh 9 per cent.

Steps the student works through:

1. Get the data into a shape a model can read, and reconcile national cultivar shares against regional tonnages
2. Plot before modelling
3. Split the data honestly, holding rows back before training
4. Fit a linear regression and interpret the coefficients in tonnes
5. Measure mean absolute error in real units
6. Add the cultivar feature, refit, and argue about whether the improvement is real on four rows
7. Write it up with an honest limitations paragraph and publish it at a link

Tools: pandas, scikit-learn, matplotlib.

## Why this matters in Oman now

On 22 September 2024 the Ministry of Transport, Communications and Information Technology
announced Oman's National Programme for Artificial Intelligence and Advanced Digital Technologies,
running 2024 to the end of 2026, announced by His Excellency Said bin Hamoud Al Maawali. It
includes a National Open Data Platform, a National Centre for AI Research and Development, an AI
Studio, an Omani language model referred to as Oman GPT, a Centre for the Fourth Industrial
Revolution with the World Economic Forum, and quantum computing work.

The digital economy contributed 2 per cent of Omani GDP in 2021, with a target of 10 per cent by
2040 under Oman Vision 2040, and an ambition to enter the top 50 of the Oxford Insights Government
AI Readiness Index. In 2026 Oman moved to establish a dedicated AI economic zone.

Every item on that list is a building project, and staffing it needs people who can train a model,
not only use one.
