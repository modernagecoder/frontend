Defining the word

What makes an online maths tutor the best fit?

No organisation in Peru rates private maths tutors, and reviews mostly measure how pleasant a tutor is. A more useful definition: the tutor who leaves this learner able to work without them. That shows up in five habits a parent can watch for.

  1. They diagnose before they teach. The first session should include questions designed to find the gap, not a lesson on whatever the homework says. Ours begins with a short diagnostic built around the four competencies below.
  2. They make the learner talk. A good tutor asks "why does that step work?" more often than they explain. In our classes the learner explains each step aloud, in English, before moving on.
  3. They cover all of maths, not just sums. Peru's curriculum has four maths competencies, and drilling arithmetic only touches one. Our plans rotate through all four.
  4. They separate checking from proving. Trying examples is how mathematicians start; it is not how they finish. The lesson further down shows why, and we teach it from early secundaria.
  5. They report work, not hours. Ask what the learner can now do that they could not before. Our mentors send short notes on problems solved and ideas understood.

What Peruvian school asks for

What maths does the Peruvian curriculum expect a learner to do?

The Currículo Nacional (CNEB) organises school maths into four competencies, each a kind of problem the pupil should be able to solve. A tutor who knows them can find gaps faster than one working from the textbook's chapter order.

Most learners we meet are uneven: comfortable with quantity, shaky on data, or the reverse. A diagnostic that touches all four in twenty minutes tells a tutor more than a month of homework help. It also matters for older pupils, because university admission exams in Peru lean heavily on maths: PUCP, for example, says its admission tests place "special emphasis" on reading comprehension and mathematics.

School and national tests

How much maths does school in Peru give, and what did ENLA find?

More hours than most families think, and steadier results than the headlines suggest. A tutor should build on school, not repeat it.

Hours at school

The CNEB gives Matemática five periods a week in 1st and 2nd of primaria and four from 3rd to 6th. In secundaria it is four periods under the regular school day and six in Jornada Escolar Completa schools. A period, the CNEB says, is 45 minutes.

Our arithmetic: that is 225 minutes a week in the first two primaria grades, 180 minutes from 3rd of primaria through regular secundaria, and 270 minutes in full-day secundaria. A tutor adding one or two hours a week is a small fraction of that, so the hours have to be spent on what school cannot do: individual diagnosis and explanation.

What ENLA 2025 found

MINEDU's national assessment, ENLA, tested over 200,000 pupils in 8,700 schools between 11 and 13 November 2025, in 4th of primaria and 2nd and 5th of secundaria.

Reporting on 13 May 2026, MINEDU said national results were similar to 2023 and 2024 in reading and maths, that 2nd of secundaria showed gains in maths over 2023 for the first time since school closures, and that Moquegua, Tacna and Arequipa still lead the country.

We are careful with this. MINEDU's press summaries are verified; the detailed percentages by achievement level sit on assessment pages we could not reach, so we do not quote them. What the summaries do show is that a national picture, however steady, tells a family nothing about their own child. That is what a diagnostic is for.

Olympiads

How should a tutor prepare a pupil for ONEM?

By teaching problem solving with original problems, not by drilling past papers. ONEM, the national school maths olympiad, rewards pupils who can reason about something unfamiliar, and the only preparation that builds that is unfamiliar problems.

What ONEM 2026 involved

The school stage was held nationwide on Wednesday 8 July 2026, in three levels: Level 1 for 1st and 2nd of secundaria, Level 2 for 3rd and 4th, Level 3 for 5th. Its paper had ten closed constructed-response items in two hours, so pupils write answers rather than pick them.

Pupils go forward from school to UGEL, then regional, then national stages. El Peruano reports that ONEM 2026 began with more than 1.7 million secundaria pupils and that the national stage, on Sunday 11 October 2026 at Huampaní, brought together 237 finalists.

How we prepare

Our arithmetic: two hours for ten items is twelve minutes each, long enough to need a plan and short enough to punish a wrong first approach. We practise choosing an approach quickly, then checking it.

Entry is through the pupil's school, which registers them; families should ask the school. We never reproduce ONEM problems or solutions. We have no connection with MINEDU, ONEM or its organisers, and we promise no result.

  1. Problem of ours, Level 1 style. The sum of three consecutive whole numbers is 87. What are they, and can the sum of three consecutive whole numbers ever be 100? Explain.
  2. Problem of ours, Level 2 style. How many two-digit numbers have digits that add up to 9? Find a way to count them without listing them all.
  3. Problem of ours, Level 3 style. Show that the product of any four consecutive whole numbers is always one less than a perfect square, and then check your argument with 5 × 6 × 7 × 8.

A real lesson

Is checking lots of examples the same as proving a maths rule?

No, and this is the most important habit a maths tutor can teach after arithmetic. Here is the lesson we use with secundaria pupils, with a little Python to do the checking.

The pattern

Take the expression n × n + n + 41. For n = 0 it gives 41, then 43, 47, 53, 61, and every one is a prime number. A pupil checks the first ten by hand and announces a rule: this always makes primes.

The mentor does not argue. Instead the class writes a short program that checks the first forty values of n at once.

The surprise

All forty are prime. The pattern looks overwhelming. Then the program tries n = 40 and gets 1681, which is 41 × 41: not prime.

Algebra explains it in one line. When n = 40, the expression is 40 × 40 + 40 + 41 = 40 × 41 + 41 = 41 × 41. The failure was built in from the start; forty successful checks just hid it.

This rule is famous, and mathematicians long ago noticed its forty primes. We use it because it fails so late. A pupil who has seen it never again says "it worked for every example I tried, so it is true". In ONEM-style problems and in IB or IGCSE proof questions, that sentence costs marks; in later life it costs more.

  1. Find it by hand. Without the program, explain why n = 41 must also fail. (Hint: every term then has a common factor.)
  2. Prove a true rule. Show that the sum of the first n odd numbers is n × n. Check n = 1 to 5, then give an argument that covers every n.
  3. Spot the gap. A classmate claims every number of the form 2 × 2 × ... × 2 − 1 is prime because 3, 7, 31 and 127 are. Use the program idea to test 2 to the power 11, minus 1.
  4. Say it in English. Write two sentences explaining the difference between evidence and proof.

The teaching language

Can my child learn maths online in English if their school teaches in Spanish?

Usually yes, because maths is the subject where language matters least. Symbols, diagrams and worked steps carry most of the meaning, and the English needed is a short list of words used again and again.

EF's English Proficiency Index 2025 rates Peru 519, in its moderate band, and Lima 555, in its high band. Our mentors, based in India, keep sentences short, write every key word on screen, and accept answers as numbers, drawings or a few words. Children at bilingual and international schools, where maths is often taught in English already, adjust at once; others usually settle within a few lessons. Questions from Peru

Finding an online maths tutor in Peru: questions and answers

Are the classes in Spanish?

No. Our maths classes are live, online and taught only in English. The mentor keeps language simple, writes key words on screen and lets the learner answer with numbers, drawings or short phrases, so the mathematics stays in front.

Who is the best online maths tutor in Peru?

Nobody can honestly name one, because no one measures tutors the same way. Look for a tutor who diagnoses first, makes your child explain, covers all four areas of maths and reports what has been learned. Watch a free class and judge those things yourself.

Do you follow the Peruvian curriculum?

We work alongside it. Our diagnostics and plans use the CNEB's four maths competencies, and mentors help with what the learner meets at school. The school sets and marks its own assessments; we do not award Peruvian grades or qualifications.

Can you help my child prepare for ONEM?

Yes, with problem solving on original problems we write. Entry is through the school, which registers pupils. We never copy ONEM problems, have no link with its organisers, and do not promise any result.

Do you teach IGCSE and IB maths?

Yes. We teach IGCSE Core, Extended and Additional Mathematics and IB Mathematics AA and AI at SL and HL, including help planning the internal assessment. The school and the exam board decide grades.

Is online maths tutoring as good as at home?

For most learners, yes, if the tutor uses a shared whiteboard and asks the learner to write and explain. Online also removes travel, and the learner keeps a record of every worked problem.

When are classes in Peru time?

Any day, any time, flexible: choose a batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at the hour that suits your family. India is 10 hours 30 minutes ahead of Lima on every day of the year, so a 16:00 class in Lima is 02:30 the next morning in India, which our mentors can arrange.

Group or one to one?

A group of fifteen to twenty at the same level suits steady progress and competitions. One to one suits a learner with specific gaps, an exam date, or who needs a slower pace. The diagnostic in the free class helps decide.

What does my child need?

A computer or tablet, a stable connection, paper and a pencil. A headset helps. A drawing tablet is useful but not necessary.

How are fees paid?

By card, monthly, in US dollars; your bank converts to soles. The free first class needs no card.
