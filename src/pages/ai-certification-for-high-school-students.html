AI certification for high school students

A tenth grader who has trained a first model often asks the same thing next: is there a certificate that says I can do this? There is more than one, and they are not alike. One comes from a testing company that sells to schools and test centers, some from a cloud provider for people starting careers, and one by a Python foundation that tests code rather than ideas. This page lays the four realistic options side by side using only what each issuer publishes: who may sit the exam, how long it runs, what it tests, how long the credential lasts, and what a teenager needs a parent to sign.

How to read the table

Three of the four test ideas about AI. Only PCEP tests whether you can write and read code, and it tests Python rather than AI. PCEP makes a natural first exam because it rewards exactly the work a beginner is already doing.

The Microsoft pass mark is a scaled score. Microsoft says a score of 700 "may not equal 70% of the points", because harder question sets need fewer points to pass. Do not convert it to a percentage in your head.

Two details teenagers miss

Microsoft strongly recommends registering with a personal account, warning that with a work or school account "your exam records will be lost if you leave your organization". A student who registers through a school email can lose the record at graduation.

For AWS, "Candidates must be at least 13 years old at the time of scheduling an exam", and the Python Institute says children under 13 are not allowed to test or receive certification even with parental consent. A parent signs for both until the student turns 18.

Exam by exam

What each issuer says about its own exam

The comparison table flattens real differences. Here is the detail behind each row, in the issuers' words where the words matter.

Certiport IT Specialist: AI

Certiport describes candidates as "AI enabled learners" who are prepared for the professional use of AI. It expects approximately 150 hours of instruction and hands-on experience with the topic, and Certiport says the American Council on Education recommends three college credits per IT Specialist exam passed. A recommendation is not a guarantee: each college decides for itself.

Microsoft AI-901

Microsoft updated the English version of this exam on April 15, 2026. It asks for knowledge of Python coding syntax and familiarity with Azure resources, which is more hands-on than the word Fundamentals suggests. The weight sits on building: "Implement AI solutions by using Microsoft Foundry" makes up 55 to 60% of the exam.

AWS AI Practitioner

AWS files this exam under its Foundational category and describes it as a starting point for people exploring AI and machine learning on AWS. It is the longest of the four at 90 minutes. Its credential lasts three years, the shortest stated validity of the four, and AWS lets a later exam renew it.

Honest about value

What a certificate shows, and what it does not

Parents are right to ask what a certification is worth. The answer is narrower than the marketing around exams suggests, and more useful once it is clear.

What it does show

That on one day, under supervised conditions, the student answered enough questions correctly on a published list of objectives. That is real information. The objectives are public, so anyone can see exactly what was measured.

For a younger teenager it is also a finish line. Preparing for a fixed date teaches revision habits that a self-paced tutorial never does.

What it does not show

It does not show that the student can build anything. An exam samples knowledge in a fixed time; a model on GitHub with an honest write-up of where it fails says more about ability than any badge.

It is not an admission ticket or a job offer, and no issuer claims it is. We make no such promise either: we teach the material, the student sits the exam, and the result belongs to them.

Federal policy has also moved toward credentials for teenagers: the April 23, 2025 executive order on AI education asks agencies to encourage dual enrollment so students can earn "industry-recognized AI credentials concurrent with high school education". Neither turns a certificate into a guarantee. Our own view of certificates is in a certificate earned, not given.

  1. Start from the objectives. Download the issuer's objective list and tick what the student can already explain out loud. Under half ticked means the exam is months away, not weeks.
  2. Check the age rule before paying. For AWS and PCEP a parent or legal guardian gives consent for students aged 13 to 17. Do this before buying a voucher.
  3. Use a personal account. Register with the student's own personal email, not a school account, so the record survives graduation.
  4. Book the practice test, then the exam. Every issuer here offers official practice material. Sit it under timed conditions first.
  5. Build one thing alongside. Pair the exam with a small project that uses the same ideas, so the certificate has evidence behind it.
  6. Decide what comes next. PCEP leads to PCAP; the AI exams lead to a real model and a write-up. Plan the next step before exam day.

A worked example

Practice a core AI fundamentals idea: precision versus recall

Judging a model by more than one number is a core idea in AI fundamentals. Here is the idea worked through in code, on a dozen made-up emails. Both scripts are ours; we ran them with Python 3.13 and scikit-learn 1.7.2 on 17 October 2026.

Why the two numbers disagree

Precision asks: when the filter shouts spam, how often is it right? Recall asks: of all the real spam, how much did it catch? scikit-learn defines them as tp / (tp + fp) and tp / (tp + fn), the same sums Script 1 does by hand.

Accuracy hides the trade. A filter that never flags anything would score 7 out of 12 here, about 58%, while catching no spam at all. That is our arithmetic, and it is the kind of trap a well-written question sets.

Why we teach it with code

A student who has typed pairs.count((0, 1)) knows what a false positive is. One who has only read the definition usually mixes up the two error types under exam pressure.

Change one value in the predicted list and run it again. Watching precision move while recall stays put is worth a page of notes.

Six practice questions, written by us

These are ours, written in the spirit of the published objectives. They are not real exam items and no issuer has reviewed them. Answers are in the second column, so cover it first.

An original practice set on AI fundamentals (ours)
QuestionAnswer and reason
1. A model flags 10 photos as cats; 8 are cats. What is its precision?0.8: 8 correct out of 10 flagged.
2. There were 16 cat photos and the model found 8. What is its recall?0.5: it found half of the real cats.
3. A dataset has 95 normal transactions and 5 unusual ones. Why is 95% accuracy unimpressive?Guessing normal every time scores 95% and finds nothing unusual.
4. You turn a column of dates into day of week. Which objective is this?Feature engineering: making a new input the model can use.

The hours, honestly

Certiport expects about 150 hours of instruction and practice. Spread over eight weeks that is 150 / 8 = 18.75 hours a week, our arithmetic, which is too much beside school. So start earlier or take the exam later.

Where we fit

Our classes cover the skills in weeks 2 to 7 as part of a normal course. The official practice test and booking stay with the student and the issuer.

If the score disappoints

Read the score report by objective, not the total. One weak objective is a week of work; three weak ones mean the exam came too soon.

AI certification for teenagers: common questions

Is there an AI certification for high school students?

Yes. Certiport's IT Specialist Artificial Intelligence exam is one, and Certiport says its IT Specialist exams suit ages 14 and up. AWS Certified AI Practitioner is open from 13, with a parent's consent until 18. Microsoft publishes no age rule for AI-901 on the pages we read, so check with Microsoft.

Can a 15 year old take the AWS Certified AI Practitioner exam?

Yes, with consent. AWS says candidates must be at least 13 when they schedule an exam, and candidates aged 13 to 17 may test with the consent of a parent or legal guardian.

How long is the Certiport IT Specialist AI exam?

Certiport lists each IT Specialist exam at 50 minutes, with 45 to 50 questions for Artificial Intelligence.

What score do you need to pass Microsoft AI-901?

Microsoft lists a passing score of 700 on a scale of 1 to 1,000. It is a scaled score, so Microsoft says it may not equal 70% of the points.

Do AI certifications expire?

It depends on the issuer. Microsoft says Fundamentals certifications do not expire, AWS says its AI Practitioner certification is valid for 3 years, the Python Institute lists PCEP as valid for 5 years, and Certiport describes its exams as a one-time issuance.

Is PCEP an AI certification?

No. PCEP tests entry-level Python: data types, loops, functions and exceptions. It pairs well with AI study because courses such as CS50 AI and libraries such as scikit-learn use Python.

Will an AI certification help with college admission?

We cannot promise that, and no issuer claims it. A certification shows that published objectives were met on one test day. Colleges decide for themselves what they value.

Should my teenager register with their school email?

Microsoft strongly recommends a personal account, warning that exam records tied to a school or work account will be lost if the student leaves that organization. Use a personal email for any exam.

Do you run the exams?

No. We teach the skills and the material in live classes. Students book and sit the exam directly with the issuer. We are not connected with Certiport, Microsoft, AWS or the Python Institute.

When are your classes for US students?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays.

What do your classes cost?

The first class is free. After that families pay monthly for teaching, and the fees section of this page shows the figure in US dollars. Exam vouchers are separate and come from the issuer.
