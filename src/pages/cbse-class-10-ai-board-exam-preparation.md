## The theory paper's shape

Fixed by the curriculum: Part A, employability skills, ten marks from five units at two marks each; Part B, forty marks weighted seven (project cycle and ethics), eleven (advanced modelling), ten (evaluating models), four (computer vision) and eight (NLP). Statistical Data and Advance Python carry no theory marks. Variable by session: CBSE's published sample question paper and marking scheme for 417 set the exact question count and shapes. The stable families are one-mark objective questions, two-mark short answers with an example, and longer case-based questions that describe a scenario and ask the student to scope it, build or read a confusion matrix, or choose and justify a metric.

## The weighted revision plan

Advanced Concepts of Modeling, 11 marks, about a quarter of revision: redraw the model tree from memory and place ten described problems on it. Evaluating Models, 10 marks, about a quarter: compute metrics from six confusion matrices by hand and justify the metric each time. Part A, 10 marks, a smaller share: short-answer drills, two marks per unit. NLP, 8 marks: bag of words and TF-IDF by hand on fresh sentences. Project cycle and ethics, 7 marks: scope three unseen problems, write one bioethics case cold. Computer Vision, 4 marks: one evening. Plus three timed mocks, each reviewed question by question. The practical half should be finished before this plan begins.

## A worked four-mark answer

A spam filter tested on 100 emails: 40 spam correctly flagged, 10 genuine wrongly flagged, 5 spam missed, 45 genuine correctly passed. The one-mark answer computes accuracy, 85 percent, and calls the model good. The four-mark answer draws the matrix (TP 40, FP 10, FN 5, TN 45), then accuracy 0.85, precision 40 over 50 = 0.80, recall 40 over 45 = 0.889, F1 = 2 x 0.80 x 0.889 over 1.689 = 0.842, and answers the question's last sentence: a false positive hides a genuine email, so precision is the metric to improve. Matrix first, formula before substitution, always answer the last sentence.

## The two-phase board system

From 2026, a compulsory phase one in February to March (17 February to 9 March in 2026) and an optional phase two in May (15 May to 1 June in 2026) for improving marks in a limited number of subjects, with the better score recorded. CBSE framed the improvement exam around the main subjects, so whether a skill subject like AI can be re-attempted is a question for your school. Plan on one attempt for 417, with the school practical, which precedes the theory paper, banked early.

## The eight-week countdown

Weeks 8 and 7: a diagnostic mock, then the heavy modelling and evaluation units. Weeks 6 and 5: NLP, project cycle and ethics, and the first Part A drill. Week 4: the second mock, every lost mark re-attempted on the spot. Weeks 3 and 2: Computer Vision in one evening, Part A finished, weak question types by name. Week 1: the third mock, then maintenance only, no new material.

## Six errors that cost a grade

Precision and recall swapped; Part A abandoned; Python written in the theory paper; the last sentence of a case question ignored; revising by chapter count; the practical left to January.
