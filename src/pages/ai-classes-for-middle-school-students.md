# AI classes for middle school students

Middle school is when AI stops being a toy and starts being homework help, a search engine and a source of rumors, all at once. It is also, conveniently, the age when students can understand how it works: that a model learns from examples, that the examples can be skewed, and that a confident answer can be wrong. US schools have started teaching exactly this. This page explains what that school teaching now covers in grades 6 to 8, what a good outside class should add, which tools suit an 11 year old versus a 14 year old, and what a first model looks like in a few lines of Python.

## AI in US middle schools: two free curricula worth knowing

Two nonprofits now publish full AI courses for middle school, free for any school to adopt. Knowing what they cover helps a parent judge what an outside class should add. Both are described here from their own pages, read on 17 October 2026.

**Two middle school AI curricula, from the publishers' pages (read 17 October 2026)**

|  | CodeAI AI Discoveries | Day of AI PreK to 12 curriculum |
|---|---|---|
| Who publishes it | CodeAI, the nonprofit formerly presented as Code.org | Day of AI, founded in 2021 through MIT's RAISE initiative |
| Middle school content | A unit on AI and machine learning: how ML works, training data, classification models, bias in AI systems | Grades 5 to 8: predictions, data, machine learning, bias, fairness and privacy |

### AI Discoveries in more detail

CodeAI rebuilt its middle school course around AI. Its page says AI and Machine Learning moves to Unit 2, so AI ideas come early in the year rather than at the end, and AI Discoveries connects directly to AI Foundations, the high school course.

The physical computing unit lets classes choose between Circuit Playground or BBC micro:bit, and an AI Tutor in programming units helps students work through problems without giving away the answer.

### Day of AI in more detail

Day of AI says it became an independent nonprofit in 2024 and has reached over 4 million students. Its new curriculum, announced in a release dated August 19, 2026, is built as a progression from preschool to grade 12.

For grades 5 to 8 the release lists predictions, data, machine learning, bias, fairness, and privacy, and among its broader topics, mandatory fact-checking of AI-generated content. That last one is the habit most worth bringing home.

Sources, read 17 October 2026: [CodeAI AI Discoveries](https://code.org/curriculum/artificial-intelligence-discoveries); the [Day of AI curriculum release](https://dayofai.org/news/day-of-ai-launches-nations-first-free-grade-by-grade-prek-12-ai-literacy-curriculum). We are not connected with CodeAI or Day of AI. Ask your child's teacher which, if either, the school uses.

## What a good AI class for grades 6 to 8 should cover

Our list, built from what the school curricula above emphasize and from the questions middle schoolers bring to class. A class that covers only the first row is a tool tutorial, not an AI class.

**Five things a middle school AI class should teach (ours)**

| Topic | What the student can do after | A question that tests it |
|---|---|---|
| Using AI carefully | Ask a clear question and judge the answer | "How would you check that?" |
| Learning from examples | Explain that a model copies patterns in its training data | "Where did it learn that?" |
| Bias and gaps | Spot when examples leave someone or something out | "Who is missing from the examples?" |
| Wrong but confident | Know that fluent text can be invented | "Is there a source for this?" |
| A first model in code | Train and test a tiny model themselves | "What happens if we add one more example?" |

### Why middle school is the right moment

Sixth graders meet AI in search results, recommendations and voice assistants, often without knowing it, and chatbots are a click away by eighth grade. Learning how these systems work before habits set is easier than unlearning them at 16.

Middle schoolers are also ready for the core idea. A twelve year old can follow why a model trained only on loud songs will call every song loud, and that one example carries most of AI literacy.

### How it differs from a coding class

A coding class teaches a language. An AI class teaches how systems learn from data, using code as one of its tools. The strongest mix is a class where students write the small model themselves.

For general programming at this age, from Scratch to Python, see our page on [coding for 12 year olds](/coding-for-12-year-olds). This page stays on AI.

## Which AI tools suit an 11 year old, and which wait until 13 or later

Grades 6 to 8 straddle an important line: 13 is a common minimum age in online terms (edX, for one, requires users to be at least 13), and some services set a higher age. Read each tool's own terms before a child signs up. These are the points we could check at source.

**Typical US grade and age, and what that means for AI tools (ages typical; tool rules from each provider, read 17 October 2026)**

| Grade | Typical age | What to know |
|---|---|---|
| 6 | 11 to 12 | CodeAI says Web Lab and AI tools are built for students ages 11 and up |
| 7 | 12 to 13 | Machine Learning for Kids asks under-13s to use only a small language model with an adult supervising |
| 8 | 13 to 14 | Some services open at 13, some only with a parent's consent, and some later; check each one's terms |
| Any | Any | Plain Python on the family computer needs no account at all |

### What the providers say

CodeAI states that AI Tutor interactions in AI Discoveries are stored securely and automatically deleted after 90 days, and that student interactions are never used to train third-party AI models. That is the kind of statement to look for in any tool a school uses.

Machine Learning for Kids, a free teaching tool, tells users: "If you are under the age of 13, please only use a small language model with supervision from a trusted adult." It is a sensible rule well beyond that one site.

### How we choose tools in class

For students under 13 we teach the ideas with Scratch, with tools built for children, and with Python that runs offline. Nothing needs the child to create an account on an adult service.

For 13 and 14 year olds we add tools whose terms allow teenagers, with a parent's consent where the terms ask for it. Our [AI tool age guide](/ai-tools-age-guide) lists what popular tools' terms say.

For homework, the line that matters is honesty about what the student did. Our guide to [safe AI tools for homework](/blog/safe-ai-tools-for-kids-homework-2025) goes through it, and [teaching kids AI at home](/blog/how-to-teach-kids-ai-at-home) has activities that need no tool at all.

## A first AI model a middle schooler can write and understand

This is the first model we suggest a middle schooler writes. It uses plain Python, no libraries to install and no accounts, and it learns from eight examples. We wrote it and checked the output under Python 3.13 on 17 October 2026.

### The surprise in the third answer

A song at 100 beats per minute and loudness 6 sounds like it could go either way. The model says chill. Our arithmetic: its distance to the chill example (90, 2) is about 10.8, and to the dance example (118, 7) about 18.0.

Why? Tempo numbers run from 70 to 132, while loudness only runs from 2 to 9. A difference of 18 in tempo outweighs everything loudness can say. The model is not weighing the two features fairly.

### What the student has just learned

That a model only knows its examples, and that the way data is measured changes the answer. Real machine learning engineers fix this with scaling, and a middle schooler has now seen why it matters.

Next steps in class: add examples and watch answers change, invent a song the model gets wrong, then rescale tempo to 1 to 10 and run it again.

## What a middle schooler covers in twelve weeks of AI classes

Our plan for a first term, for students in grades 6 to 8 with or without coding. Students who already code move faster through weeks 1 to 4.

**A first term of AI for grades 6 to 8 (ours)**

| Weeks | Focus | Made by the student |
|---|---|---|
| 1 to 2 | What AI is and is not; where they already meet it | A list of AI they used this week, sorted by how sure they are |
| 3 to 4 | Examples and patterns, in Scratch or on paper | A sorting game a friend can play |
| 5 to 6 | A first model in Python | The dance or chill script, extended with their own songs |
| 7 to 8 | Bias and missing data | A model that fails on purpose, and a fix |
| 9 to 10 | Checking AI answers | Three AI claims, each checked against a real source |
| 11 to 12 | A small project of their choice | A model, a test and a two-minute explanation |

### Grade 6

More discussion and Scratch, shorter Python. The ideas are the same; the code is lighter.

### Grade 7

The full plan as written, with the project in weeks 11 and 12 chosen by the student.

### Grade 8

Faster through the basics, then a second model with a real dataset and an honest test.

Schools are adding AI activities too. CodeAI says the Hour of Code is now the Hour of AI, with 100 one-hour tutorials available in over 45 languages. A one-hour activity is a good taster; a term of classes is where understanding sets in.

## Four stages of a middle schooler learning AI

Where a sixth grader might start, and where a year of good teaching can take them.

**Stages of AI understanding in grades 6 to 8 (ours)**

| Stage | How you can tell |
|---|---|
| 1. User | Uses AI apps; trusts most answers |
| 2. Questioner | Asks where an answer came from, and checks |
| 3. Explainer | Can say how a model learns from examples |
| 4. Maker | Has written a small model and broken it on purpose |

### For parents

Stage 2 is the one that protects your child. Ask "how would you check that?" at dinner and you are teaching it.
Stage 4 is where a middle schooler becomes genuinely ahead: not using more AI, but understanding it.

### For students

When an AI answer surprises you, ask where it learned it. That question is most of AI.
Write your own tiny model. Once you have, every AI app makes a bit more sense.

## AI classes for middle schoolers: common questions

### What should an AI class for middle school students teach?

How models learn from examples, where they go wrong through bias and invented answers, how to check AI output, and a first small model in code. Using AI tools well is part of it, not all of it.

### Do US middle schools teach AI?

Some do, and free curricula make it easier. CodeAI publishes a free middle school course, AI Discoveries, with a unit on AI and machine learning, and Day of AI publishes a free grade-by-grade curriculum that covers grades 5 to 8. Ask your school which it uses.

### Is AI safe for an 11 year old?

With the right tools and supervision, yes. CodeAI says its Web Lab and AI tools are built for students ages 11 and up, and Machine Learning for Kids asks under-13s to use only a small language model with a trusted adult supervising.

### Can a middle schooler use ChatGPT?

Check the tool's own terms first, because age rules differ between services and change over time. For students under 13 we teach the ideas without adult chatbot accounts, using child-suitable tools and offline Python.

### Does my child need to code before an AI class?

No. A good class starts with ideas and examples, then introduces a small amount of Python. Our first model on this page is about 20 lines.

### What is the difference between AI classes and coding classes?

Coding classes teach a programming language. AI classes teach how systems learn from data, using code as one tool. Doing both, in that order or side by side, works well.

### What happens to my child's data in school AI tools?

It depends on the tool, so ask the school. CodeAI says AI Tutor interactions in AI Discoveries are automatically deleted after 90 days and never used to train third-party AI models.

### Which course should an eighth grader take?

An eighth grader who is 13 or older can usually join our teen Python course or AI tools course for ages 13 and up. Younger or newer students start with AI literacy for ages 8 to 14.

### When are classes for US middle schoolers?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays.
