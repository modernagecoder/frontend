For Aachen parents

Modern Age Coders teaches coding to Aachen children aged 6 to 12 in live online classes in English. Younger children solve puzzles and build Scratch games; from about 9 they type real Python and use real data, such as the city's own map of glass containers. One regular mentor adjusts the speed and the English to suit your child, in a group of 15 to 20 children of a similar age or one to one, after school or on weekend mornings.

Ages 6 to 8: hands and stories

Young children learn sequences with their bodies first. In class they direct the mentor, or a sprite, one square at a time, "two steps right, one step up", and discover that order matters. Short lessons, lots of praise, no long reading.

By eight, most children can make a Scratch character walk to a point on the stage, which is already the first idea of a coordinate.

Ages 9 to 12: typing and data

Around nine, typing stops being a barrier and Python becomes fun. Children learn that the computer is strict about spelling and that an error message is a clue, not a telling-off.

By eleven or twelve, they can open a real file from Stadt Aachen and answer a real question with it. That moment, when code answers something about their own town, is what keeps children coding.

Aachen schools

When do children in Aachen meet Informatik at school?

In NRW, at the start of secondary school. Before that, at primary school, coding is not a subject of its own.

Aachen children, like children in most of Germany, attend Grundschule from grade 1 to grade 4, according to the Kultusministerkonferenz, and there is no leaving exam at the end of it. They then move to a secondary school. There, the Gesellschaft für Informatik notes in its Informatik-Monitor 2026/27, every general school type in Nordrhein-Westfalen teaches Informatik as a compulsory subject in grades 5 and 6.

That gives Aachen families a clear window. Between about 6 and 10, a child who enjoys puzzles has four primary years in which coding is not on the timetable. A child who uses those years for Scratch and a little Python starts grade 5 already comfortable with loops and conditions, and Informatik becomes a subject they look forward to rather than one they catch up in. Our classes sit alongside school and never replace it.

Live, in English

Can my child follow a coding class in English?

Almost always yes. Our classes are only ever taught in English, so the mentors are practised at making that easy.

Only English, no German

We say it early because it matters: our mentors, based in India, teach every class in English. There is no German-language option. For Aachen families near the Belgian and Dutch borders, English is often the language the children share anyway.

Matched to the child

A mentor speaks slowly with a six year old and faster with a confident eleven year old, shows rather than tells, and gets used to each child's accent. Children who mix in a German word are helped, never corrected sharply.

Screens do half the talking

In Scratch and Python, much of a lesson is pointing at a block or a line and trying it. A child with simple school English can follow, and their English grows with the coding.

A real Aachen project

How far is the nearest bottle bank from Kaiserplatz?

Stadt Aachen publishes the location of every public glass container as open data that anyone may reuse. In our classes the eleven and twelve year olds turn it into a lesson on one of the oldest ideas in mathematics, and younger children play the same game on paper.

The map in the file

The city's file has 190 glass containers. Each row has a street, an Ortsteil and a position in metres east and north on the map grid. Mitte has 117 of them; Brand and Laurensberg have 15 each, Eilendorf 11.

For a starting point we use another file from the same city: the list of traffic lights, where the junction numbered K1005 is called simply "Kaiserplatz".

The idea

To get from Kaiserplatz to a container you go some metres east and some metres north. Those two moves are the short sides of a right-angled triangle, and the straight line between the two points is the long side.

Square the east steps, square the north steps, add them, take the square root. Children who have never heard the name Pythagoras use it happily once they have drawn it.

The nearest of the 190 is at Rudolfstraße, on the corner of Wenzelstraße, 228 metres from the Kaiserplatz junction as the crow flies. The next four are 335, 460, 560 and 586 metres away. Children then ask the obvious question: is 228 metres the walk? It is not. Streets bend and crossings take time, so a real walk is longer. A straight-line answer is a lower limit, and saying so is part of the answer.

Data: Stadt Aachen, Glascontainer Stadt Aachen and Lichtsignalanlagen (LSA) Stadt Aachen, via Open.NRW and GovData, licence Datenlizenz Deutschland Namensnennung 2.0, downloaded 3 November 2026. Distances are ours, measured in a straight line on the UTM map grid from junction K1005, Kaiserplatz.

  1. Ages 6 to 7, grid paper. Put a star on one square and a bottle on another. Count the squares right and the squares up. Who can find the shortest path along the lines?
  2. Ages 7 to 9, Scratch. Place a sprite at x 0, y 0 and a bottle at x 120, y 90. Glide there in two moves, then in one. Which looks shorter? Use the "distance to" block to check: it says 150.
  3. Python, from about 10. Type in Version 1 yourself, then change the start to your own school's nearest junction from the traffic-light file.
  4. Challenge. Which Ortsteil has the most containers for its size? Before you answer, write down what extra data you would need.

Aachen parents ask

Coding classes for kids in Aachen: questions

Are the classes in German?

No. We teach only in English, and every class is live with a mentor based in India. Mentors keep their English simple for young children, adjust to each child's pace and accent, and show things on screen, so children with basic school English cope well.

At what age can my child start?

At 6. The youngest children begin with puzzles and very simple blocks, in short and playful lessons. Most move to full Scratch at 7 or 8 and to typed Python at about 9.

Is coding taught at Aachen primary schools?

Not as a separate subject. Grundschule runs from grade 1 to 4, and in NRW Informatik becomes a compulsory subject for every pupil in grades 5 and 6 at secondary school, according to the Gesellschaft für Informatik.

When are the classes?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. In Aachen most children come after school, from about 15:00, or on a weekend morning.

What does my child need at home?

A laptop or desktop computer with a microphone and a reliable internet connection. A tablet is enough for the first Scratch weeks. Everything we use is free and runs in the browser or installs in minutes.

Can my child enter the Informatik-Biber?

Yes, if the school registers. The Biber 2026 runs from 9 to 20 November 2026, is free and needs no prior knowledge; pupils from grade 3 upward take part during a school lesson. Ask the class teacher.

How many children are in a class?

Group classes have 15 to 20 children of a similar age and stage. If your child would rather work alone with a mentor, the one-to-one plan gives two private classes each week.

Which currency do we pay in?

The card is charged in US dollars. On this page the monthly fee is shown in euro using the ECB reference rate so you can see what to expect, and your bank does the conversion. The first class is free.

What is the bottle-bank project?

An original project of ours on Stadt Aachen's open data. Children find the glass container nearest to the Kaiserplatz traffic light, 228 metres away in a straight line, using east and north steps and Pythagoras, and learn why the real walk is longer.
