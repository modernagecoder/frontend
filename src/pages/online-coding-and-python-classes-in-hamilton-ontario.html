Online coding and Python classes in Hamilton, Ontario

Can a Hamilton student take coding and Python classes live online?

Yes. Modern Age Coders runs live online coding and Python classes for Hamilton, Ontario students aged 6 to adult. A teacher sees the student's own code on a shared screen while it is written, in a group of fifteen to twenty or a private class. Children begin with Python and AI for Kids, teens with Python for Teens, and the first class is free.

Hamilton's open data includes a waterfall inventory: the City lists 103 of them, from cascades a few metres high to drops of forty metres. We use that file on this page the way we use real data in class, as something a student loads, sorts and questions in Python. Before that, the page answers the practical question parents ask first, which is what a live online class actually looks like on the screen in front of their child, and what separates it from a video course. Then comes the route we follow in Python, from short programs to a finished project, and the school and contest facts a Hamilton student should know for 2026 to 2027.

On the screen

What a Hamilton student sees during a live Python class

Parents often picture a lecture on video. The screen in our classes is mostly the student's own code. This is how it is laid out and who controls each part.

The parts of a live online class and who controls each (our description of how we teach)
On screenWho controls itWhy it is there
The teacher's camera and voiceThe teacherSo the student is taught by a person who can be asked anything, at the moment it matters

The student keeps the keyboard. When a program fails, the teacher does not take control and fix it; they ask the student to read the error message out loud, point to the line it names, and say what they expected that line to do. That habit, slowing down at the error rather than guessing, is most of what makes a programmer.

A group of fifteen to twenty

Every student in a group works at the same stage. Students are named and asked to share, so nobody spends a class hidden behind a muted microphone.

Private lessons

One teacher for one student. The whole screen is that student's code, and the pace follows them, which suits ICS4U projects and CCC practice.

Equipment

A laptop or desktop, a headset or microphone, and Python installed in the first class with the teacher's help. A tablet works for blocks, not for typed Python.

Hamilton data project

Which are the tallest falls in Hamilton's open data, and what does a mean hide?

The City of Hamilton publishes City Waterfalls, a file it describes as publicly accessible waterfalls in Hamilton, on both public and private property. We downloaded it on 22 October 2026 and analysed it with the program below. The figures are our counts, not the City's.

Our summary of the City of Hamilton City Waterfalls file (103 rows, downloaded 22 October 2026), by the COMMUNITY column
COMMUNITY in the fileRowsMean height (m)Tallest (m)
Hamilton4110.839.5
Flamborough239.941
Stoney Creek1711.137
Ancaster158.021
Dundas65.49
Burlington125.025

Two small functions do most of the work: one loads the file, one takes a mean. The sort uses a key function, which tells sorted() to order whole records by a single field. This program, run on the saved file, printed every number on this page:import json def load_falls(path): with open(path, encoding="utf-8") as f: return [feature["attributes"] for feature in json.load(f)["features"]] def mean(values): return sum(values) / len(values) falls = load_falls("waterfalls.json") tallest = sorted(falls, key=lambda fall: fall["HEIGHT_IN_M"], reverse=True) for fall in tallest[:5]: print(fall["NAME"], fall["HEIGHT_IN_M"], "m", fall["COMMUNITY"]) for kind in ("Waterfall", "Cascade"): heights = [fall["HEIGHT_IN_M"] for fall in falls if fall["TYPE"] == kind] print(kind, len(heights), round(mean(heights), 1)) by_community = {} for fall in falls: by_community.setdefault(fall["COMMUNITY"], []).append(fall["HEIGHT_IN_M"]) for place, heights in sorted(by_community.items()): print(place, len(heights), round(mean(heights), 1), max(heights))

The five tallest

Sorted by HEIGHT_IN_M, the file puts Tew's Falls first at 41 m, then Lower Princess Falls at 39.5 m, Devil's Punchbowl Falls at 37 m, Ferguson Falls at 30 m and Snake Falls at 25 m.

Waterfall or cascade

The TYPE column splits the rows into 47 waterfalls with a mean height of 12.5 m and 56 cascades with a mean of 8.1 m. Students then ask whether the tallest few are pulling the waterfall mean up.

A one-row community

Snake Falls is the only row whose COMMUNITY is Burlington, so Burlington's mean of 25.0 m rests on a single record. A student who prints that mean without the count of 1 beside it has made a misleading table.

Mean against median

Across all 103 rows the median height is 7 m and 11 rows sit at the minimum of 3 m, while every community mean in the table is higher than 7 m except Dundas. A few tall drops lift a mean; the median shows the typical row. Writing both, with the counts, is the honest report.

Some columns are not explained in the file itself. RANKING holds A on 18 rows, B on 36 and C on 49, and OWNERSHIP includes a value written public-I on 28 rows. We teach students to say they do not know what a code means rather than invent a meaning, and to look for the publisher's documentation first. The data is used under the City of Hamilton Open Data Licence: contains public sector Data made available under the City of Hamilton's Open Data Licence (licence). The City does not endorse this page or these classes, and a file of accessible falls is not advice about visiting them.

Python and AI

Why write Python yourself when an AI will write it for you?

Because the hard part of the waterfalls project was never the typing.

An AI assistant will produce a script that sorts the Hamilton file in seconds. It will not stop to notice that one community has a single row, that a mean of 25.0 m is built on it, or that RANKING has no published meaning in the file. A student who has written the sort and the mean by hand notices all three.

We therefore teach the reasoning first: understand the problem, write the code, read the output critically. Older students later use AI tools within the age rules we follow, and they treat the answer as a draft to test, not a solution to hand in.

A Hamilton student who can explain why the median says more than the mean about these falls has learned something no code generator does for them. The longer argument is in why we still teach students to write the loop.

FAQ

Questions from Hamilton families

Asked by Hamilton parents and students before the first lesson.

How is a live online class different from a coding video course?

A teacher is present at a fixed time, reads the student's own code on a shared screen and asks questions while it is written. A video cannot see the student's mistakes.

Do you teach coding in person in Hamilton?

No. There is no Hamilton classroom. Every lesson is live online, so students in Dundas, Ancaster, Stoney Creek, Waterdown or on the Mountain join from home.

How large are the groups?

Fifteen to twenty students at the same stage. Families who want one teacher for one student choose private lessons instead, with the same syllabus.

What age should a child start Python in Hamilton?

Usually about 9. From age 6 we start with blocks, then move to typed Python once the child can read and type comfortably.

Does Ontario school already teach coding?

Yes. Ontario math includes coding from Grade 1, Grade 6 science uses it in investigations, and MTH1W in Grade 9 asks students to read and change code. Our classes go deeper.

Can Hamilton students write the CCC in Python?

Yes. CEMC accepts Python 3 for the CCC on Wednesday 17 February 2027, entered through a school. We prepare students with original practice problems.

Will these classes give my teen an Ontario credit?

No. We do not grant Ontario credits. Credits such as ICS3U and ICS4U come only from the school; our companion course is extra practice beside them.

What times do Hamilton classes run?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. We confirm in Eastern time.

Is there a Python course for adults in Hamilton?

Yes. Adults start from zero if they need to and follow the same milestones at their own pace, in a group or privately.

Do 905 and 289 numbers work for booking?

They do, along with 365 and 742. Key in the ten-digit number; the +1 for Canada is already set.

Where are the prices?

In the fees section of this page: the free first lesson, the monthly group fee and the monthly private fee, each in your own currency. You pay only after the free lesson.
