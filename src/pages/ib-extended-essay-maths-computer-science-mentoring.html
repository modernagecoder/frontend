IB Extended Essay in maths or computer science: what help is allowed

The extended essay is the IB Diploma's independent research task: a 4,000-word academic essay on a topic the student chooses, written with a school supervisor and assessed by the IB. Students can write it in mathematics, in computer science, or across two subjects. The IB's guide for first assessment in 2027 is unusually firm about outside help: students may not receive assistance with "any aspect of the research, writing or proofreading of the essay beyond that which is permitted through their supervisor." So Modern Age Coders does not mentor extended essays. What we do teach, live online, is the mathematics and programming a student may want to understand, and the research habits the guide describes, practised on problems of our own. This page sets out the rules from the guide and where that line falls.

Can a tutor help with an IB Extended Essay in maths or computer science?

Not with the essay itself. The IB's Extended essay guide for first assessment 2027 says that, because the essay is an independent task and a formal assessment component, "students are not allowed to receive assistance with any aspect of the research, writing or proofreading of the essay beyond that which is permitted through their supervisor." The essay is up to 4,000 words, takes about 40 hours, and is supported by a school supervisor through three reflection sessions; the supervisor may comment on one draft only. Modern Age Coders therefore teaches IB students mathematics, programming and research method live online, in a group or 1-on-1, on practice problems of our own, and never works on a student's essay.

The essay

What is the IB Extended Essay?

The guide calls it "a piece of independent research and writing on a topic chosen by the student in consultation with a designated supervisor in the school." It is compulsory for Diploma students and optional for course students.

Key features of the extended essay, from the IB Extended essay guide, first assessment 2027
FeatureWhat the guide says
LengthA formal academic essay of no more than 4,000 words
Reflective statementNo more than 500 words, included on the reflection and progress form (RPF)
Student timeApproximately 40 hours of work
SupervisionRecommended at three to five hours, including three mandatory reflection sessions
Final sessionThe viva voce, a short concluding interview with the supervisor
PathwaysSubject-focused (one DP subject) or interdisciplinary (two DP subjects)
AssessmentExternal; with theory of knowledge it contributes up to three points to the diploma
MinimumA D grade or higher is needed for the diploma to be awarded

Source: International Baccalaureate, Extended essay guide, first assessment 2027 (an unaltered copy hosted by an IB school; ibo.org refused our requests). The guide is for students submitting from May 2027 onwards; your school's coordinator will confirm which guide applies to you. Modern Age Coders is independent of the IB.

In mathematics

The guide describes mainly theoretical essays, exploring an area of mathematics outside the syllabus, and mainly practical ones, using mathematics to investigate an issue or a data set. Many essays mix both. Either way, "it is mandatory to include secondary research, i.e. a review of the literature."

It also warns that "mathematics uses a lot of algebra, so the actual final word count for your extended essay is likely to fall short of 4,000 words." Fewer words is normal, not a weakness.

In computer science

Computer science sits with the sciences in the guide. A science essay "offers the opportunity to investigate and collect primary or secondary data, based on a focused research question", using an accepted, subject-specific method and leading to a valid, evidence-based conclusion.

The guide also lists mathematics and computer science as a possible pairing for the interdisciplinary pathway, for a topic that genuinely needs both lenses.

How it is marked

What are the IB Extended Essay criteria A to E?

Five criteria. A to D apply to the essay as a whole; the guide says "Criterion E is applied only to the reflective statement on the student's RPF."

Extended essay assessment criteria, first assessment 2027, from the IB guide
CriterionGuiding question in the guideMark levels
A: Framework for the essayDo the research question, research methods and structural conventions provide an effective framework?0, then 1 to 2, 3 to 4, 5 to 6
B: Knowledge and understandingDoes the student demonstrate knowledge and understanding of the subject matter used?0, then 1 to 2, 3 to 4, 5 to 6
C: Analysis and line of argumentDoes the student analyse the information and produce a coherent line of argument?0, then 1 to 2, 3 to 4, 5 to 6
D: Discussion and evaluationDoes the student discuss the findings and evaluate the essay?0, then 1 to 2, 3 to 4, 5 to 6, 7 to 8
E: ReflectionDoes the student evaluate the effect of the learning experience on them as a learner?0, then 1, 2, 3 or 4

What the maths guidance stresses

On Criterion B the guide is direct: "Showing your knowledge and clear understanding of the mathematics you use is arguably the most important criterion in a mathematics essay." It also cautions that "an extended essay is not a research paper revealing that you have discovered something profound and new".

On Criterion A it asks for an equation editor and warns against "using inappropriate symbols like * for multiplication and ^ for powers". On research questions it says: "Be careful with research questions that compare two different approaches to a topic."

What this means for learning, not for the essay

If understanding the mathematics is what matters most, the most useful thing a student can do before or alongside the essay is to learn mathematics well. That is ordinary teaching, and it is what we do.

The same goes for writing mathematics properly: line-by-line algebra, correct notation, a typeset equation. These are skills we practise in lessons on our own problems, which a student can then use independently.

The line

What outside help does the IB allow on the Extended Essay?

Very little, and only through the school. The guide sets the supervisor as the channel for permitted support, limits feedback to one draft, and treats help beyond that as a possible case of academic misconduct.

Help and the extended essay, as the 2027 guide sets it out
QuestionWhat the guide says
Who supports the student?A supervisor allocated by the school, who runs the three reflection sessions and signs the RPF
How much draft feedback?The supervisor may "read and comment on one draft only of the essay (no direct editing is permitted)"
Proofreading?"You must do your own proofreading", including spelling, grammar, formatting and the word count
Other help?Students may not receive assistance with any aspect of the research, writing or proofreading beyond what the supervisor permits
External mentors?Only "with the agreement of the school", with a signed external mentor form, and a school supervisor still allocated

What we do not do

We do not suggest, refine or approve a topic or research question. We do not recommend sources, collect or analyse data, write or debug code for an essay, read drafts, comment on structure, proofread, or prepare a student for the viva voce.

We do not act as an external mentor, even where a school might agree to one. That role belongs to institutions the school selects for research access, under the IB's form.

What we do

We teach mathematics, statistics and programming as subjects, at and beyond the Diploma syllabus, in ordinary lessons. We teach how mathematical writing works and how to read a research article, using material that has nothing to do with the student's essay.

If a student tells us their essay topic, we will not teach to it. We ask students to keep the essay out of our lessons, so that their supervisor and the IB can be confident the work is theirs.

Our lesson family

How can a student practise research skills without touching the essay?

By running small investigations of their own on problems that are not, and will not become, their extended essay. The two below are ours, deliberately short and far from typical essay territory; they exist only to practise method.

  1. Ask a narrow question. Our practice question: in how many ways can a 2 by n strip be tiled with 1 by 2 dominoes? Narrow is good; the guide warns against taking on too much.
  2. Gather cases honestly. Draw the tilings for n = 1, 2, 3, 4: there are 1, 2, 3 and 5 ways. Count each twice.
  3. Read before you claim. Look the pattern up in a textbook or a reputable reference and note where you found it. Practising a short literature note is practising the review of literature the maths guidance makes mandatory.
  4. Explain, do not just state. The last domino column is either one vertical domino, leaving a 2 by (n minus 1) strip, or two horizontal ones, leaving a 2 by (n minus 2) strip. So the count for n is the sum of the counts for n minus 1 and n minus 2: the Fibonacci rule.
  5. Write it properly. Typeset the rule with an equation editor, one step per line, with no * or ^ symbols, and a sentence saying what each symbol means.
  6. Evaluate. Say what the argument covers and what it does not: it counts tilings of a 2-wide strip only. What changes for a 3-wide strip?

What the computing practice teaches

Controlled comparison: the same lists and targets for both methods, many repetitions, and only the input size changed. That mirrors what the guide asks of science essays, controlled variables and a standardised method, on a problem too small to be an essay.

Interpretation: linear search time grows roughly in proportion to n, binary search far more slowly. The student should explain why from the code, not only report the numbers.

Why practise on someone else's problem

Because the guide is clear that help with the essay's research must come through the supervisor. Practising method on unrelated problems builds the skill without importing anyone else's thinking into the student's essay.

It also keeps the student honest with themselves: if they can run a small investigation alone, they can run their own essay alone.

Extended essay questions

What students ask about a maths or computer science EE

Can a tutor help with my IB Extended Essay?

The IB's 2027 guide says students may not receive assistance with any aspect of the research, writing or proofreading beyond what their supervisor permits. We therefore teach subjects and method on our own problems and never work on an essay.

How long is the IB Extended Essay?

No more than 4,000 words, with a reflective statement of no more than 500 words on the reflection and progress form. The guide estimates about 40 hours of student work.

What are the extended essay criteria?

A, framework for the essay; B, knowledge and understanding; C, analysis and line of argument; D, discussion and evaluation; and E, reflection, which applies only to the reflective statement.

Is a maths extended essay shorter than 4,000 words?

Often. The guide says mathematics uses a lot of algebra, so the final word count is likely to fall short of 4,000 words.

Does a maths extended essay need a literature review?

Yes. The guide says it is mandatory to include secondary research, meaning a review of the literature, whether the essay is mainly theoretical or mainly practical.

Can I write an interdisciplinary extended essay in maths and computer science?

The guide lists mathematics and computer science among its example pairings for the interdisciplinary pathway. Check with your school, and note that interdisciplinary essays must currently be in English, French or Spanish.

How many times can my supervisor read my draft?

Once. The guide says the supervisor may read and comment on one draft only, with no direct editing, after the interim reflection session and before the final one.

Is AI allowed in the IB extended essay?

The guide says using AI to write an essay presented as your own is dishonest. AI-generated material can be used as a resource if it is checked and properly cited.

What grade do I need on the extended essay?

The guide says a student must achieve a D grade or higher on the extended essay to be awarded the diploma. With theory of knowledge it contributes up to three points.

When are your lessons?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. UK students see UK times when booking.
