Massachusetts, United States

Coding and AI classes in Massachusetts

Is computer science required to graduate in Massachusetts?

No statewide course is required. A Massachusetts diploma rests on the Competency Determination plus local district rules, and the class of 2027 coursework list names English, maths, science and U.S. history only. But MassCore, the state-recommended program, lets an approved computer science course replace a maths or lab science unit. Modern Age Coders teaches those courses live online, from first Scratch projects to AP Computer Science Principles.

Massachusetts took a different road from the states that legislated a computer science course for every graduate. Its Department of Elementary and Secondary Education (DESE) wrote one of the earlier standalone K to 12 frameworks, the Digital Literacy and Computer Science (DLCS) framework of 2016, and then built incentives around it rather than a mandate: a teacher license, a reviewed curriculum guide, and a rule inside MassCore that treats a rigorous computer science course as a maths or science unit. DESE is also unusually candid about the gap that remains. Its own resource page says 82% of public high schools offer computer science while only 5.9% of public high school students take a foundational course. This page sets out those rules and numbers as DESE publishes them, adds the Code.org and College Board figures for the state, and finishes with a small Python project on Cambridge open data that any Bay State teenager can run.

MassCore, the recommended path

The Board adopted MassCore in 2007 and amended it in 2018. It asks for four units of English, four of maths, three of lab science, three of history, two of one world language, one of the arts and five more core courses.

The substitution rule

DESE says a computer science course with rigorous mathematical or scientific content, aligned to the 2016 framework, can stand in for either a maths course or a laboratory science course within MassCore.

Four approved course codes

The DLCS pages list 10011 Computer Science Principles, 10012 Exploring Computer Science, 10019 AP Computer Science Principles and 10090 Computational Thinking and Problem Solving.

So the honest summary for a Massachusetts family is this: a student can graduate without computer science, but a student who takes an approved course can count it toward MassCore without giving up anything else. Whether a particular high school offers one of the four listed courses, and how the district records it, is a question for the school counselor; we do not issue school credit, and our classes sit alongside the school program rather than inside it.

DESE publishes quick reference guides by span. The early spans lean on digital citizenship and simple algorithms, and DESE adds suggested standards for each elementary grade.

Who teaches it in school

Massachusetts licenses DLCS teachers for grades 5 to 12 through the MTEL test, and DESE’s computer science page announces that a DLCS PreK-6 License is now available. A 2025 Massachusetts Curriculum Guide, reviewed for DESE by STEM Learning Design, sorts curricula into core and supplemental options for each grade span.

Why do 7.91% and 5.9% both appear? They measure different things. Signature project

Which T station is closest to each Cambridge library? Nearest neighbour in Python

Two public datasets, one formula. Students load Cambridge’s library points and the MassGIS rapid transit stops, then find the nearest station to every branch.

O’Connell Branch LibraryLechmere (Green)0.70 km, about 0.43 mi

1. Two layers

The library layer holds 7 points with a site name and address. The stops layer holds 170 records across Green, Silver, Red, Orange and Blue, with 157 distinct station names because a few stations appear once per line.

2. The haversine formula

Latitude and longitude are angles on a sphere, so students cannot subtract them like grid coordinates. They write a haversine function, test it on two known points, then loop over every library and keep the minimum.

3. Read it critically

Collins Branch sits more than 2 km from its nearest stop, and two of the seven answers are Green Line stations rather than Red. A straight line is also not a walking route: the river, rail yards and one-way streets all lengthen the real trip.

A line count worth checking

Grouping the 170 stop records by LINE gives Green 66, Silver 45, Red 27, Orange 16 and Blue 10, plus six records that carry two lines, such as GREEN/ORANGE. A student who sums the single-line counts and gets 164 has found the shared records; deciding how to count them is the real exercise.

Our numbers, their data

The distances and counts above are ours, computed from one download on 17 October 2026. The City of Cambridge publishes its library layer under the Open Data Commons Public Domain Dedication and License v1.0; MassGIS describes its MBTA service as a public resource that anyone may use, and accepts no liability for errors. Neither agency checked our results, and the stop list can change.

Coding and AI

Why learn the haversine formula when an AI tool can find the nearest station?

Because the tool can be confidently wrong, and only someone who understands the method can tell.

Ask a chatbot for the closest T stop to a Cambridge branch library and it may answer in a second. It may also measure from the wrong address, ignore a station on another line, or treat degrees of longitude as if they were miles. A student who has written the distance function, and checked it against a known pair of points, can spot each of those errors.

This is what the Computing and Society and Computational Thinking strands ask for together: understand the algorithm, then judge the tool that runs it. Students who can do both use AI assistants to move faster on the parts they already understand, and slow down on the parts they do not.

Massachusetts left computer science as a choice; the students who choose it well are the ones who can question an answer as easily as they can produce one. The longer argument is in is coding still worth learning in 2026.

FAQ

Massachusetts coding and AI questions

Answers drawn from DESE, College Board and Code.org pages, read in October 2026.

Does Massachusetts require computer science to graduate?

No statewide course. DESE says a diploma requires the Competency Determination plus local graduation requirements, and the class of 2027 coursework list covers English, maths, science and U.S. history only. A district can add its own rules.

Can a computer science course count toward MassCore?

Yes. DESE says a computer science course with rigorous mathematical or scientific concepts, aligned to the 2016 DLCS framework, can substitute for either a mathematics course or a laboratory science course in MassCore.

Which courses are on the MassCore substitute list?

DESE lists 10011 Computer Science Principles, 10012 Exploring Computer Science, 10019 AP Computer Science Principles and 10090 Computational Thinking and Problem Solving.

What are the Massachusetts DLCS standards?

The Digital Literacy and Computer Science framework of 2016, organised in four strands (Computing and Society, Digital Tools and Collaboration, Computing Systems, Computational Thinking) across the spans K-2, 3-5, 6-8 and 9-12.

How many Massachusetts high schools offer computer science?

DESE puts it at 82% of public high schools. The Code.org 2025 data shows 325 of 400 high schools, or 81.25%, for 2023 to 2024, the latest Massachusetts year in that sheet.

How many students actually take it?

DESE reports 5.9% of public high school students in a foundational course. Code.org counts 23,127 of 292,428 high school students, or 7.91%, using a different definition and denominator.

Do your classes give Massachusetts school credit?

No. Credit and MassCore substitutions are decided by the school district. Our classes add depth and project work alongside a school course, and anything a school grades stays the student's own work.

What time are classes for Massachusetts students?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience. Times are quoted in Eastern Time, which in the IANA database is America/New_York.

When do clocks change for Massachusetts this school year?

US clocks fall back on November 1, 2026 and spring forward on March 14, 2027. India does not change, so the gap moves from 9 hours 30 minutes to 10 hours 30 minutes and back.

Is there a Modern Age Coders classroom in Boston?

No. We have no premises anywhere in Massachusetts; every class is live online, taught from India, and a student joins from home with a computer and a reliable connection.
