Why Chatswood's monthly percentages add up to the wrong answer

Transport for NSW publishes Opal entries for every station each month. On our location check three stations fall inside the Willoughby boundary, and their 23 months make a clean lesson in growth arithmetic.

Why percentages drift

A fall of 9.12% followed by a rise of 13.02% looks like +3.9 in total, but the rise is measured from a smaller base. Over 22 months these small errors pile up in one direction.

Why logs behave

The log difference ln(new / old) for each month adds exactly: the 22 Chatswood values sum to 0.16369, the same as ln(949,108 / 805,797).

Back to a percentage

exp(0.16369) minus 1 is 0.1779, so the log route returns the true 17.79% growth with no correction needed.

The St Leonards trap

Entries at St Leonards fell from 263,333 to 257,822, a drop of 2.09%. Yet the monthly percentage changes sum to +10.65%. A report built by adding them would say the station grew when it shrank. Summing log differences gives -0.02115, which converts back to the correct -2.09%.

The same idea explains averages: Chatswood's simple mean monthly change is 0.989%, while the steady monthly rate that reproduces the real growth is 0.747%. Entries are Opal gate taps rather than people, Chatswood appears in the file with the station type "Metro Shared", and every percentage and log value here is our own calculation from the published counts.

Coding and AI

Can a Willoughby learner trust an AI to calculate growth?

Only after learning to check it, which is what this page's project trains.

Ask a chatbot for the total growth across a table of monthly percentages and it may add them, exactly the mistake that turns St Leonards' fall into a rise. The answer arrives with confident wording and tidy formatting, and nothing in it flags the error.

A learner who has run the Chatswood comparison knows the sum should match ln(last / first), and can test any AI answer in one line of Python. That habit of checking a result against a simple identity is what separates using AI from being misled by it.

In 2026 AI can produce the report; a Willoughby learner who knows why percentages do not add is the one who spots when the report is wrong. The longer argument is in why teenagers should still learn to code in 2026.

How many people live in the City of Willoughby?

The 2021 Census QuickStats for the Willoughby local government area counted 75,613 residents, with a median age of 39 and 20,701 families.

Why can monthly percentage changes not be added?

Each percentage is measured from a different starting value, so adding them double-counts or misses growth. Log differences avoid this: they add exactly, and the total converts back to the true percentage change.

What does the Chatswood project find?

From October 2024 to August 2026 Chatswood entries rose 17.79%, but the 22 monthly percentage changes sum to 21.75%. At St Leonards the summed percentages show growth of 10.65% although entries fell 2.09%.
