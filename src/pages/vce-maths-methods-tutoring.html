In short

VCE Mathematical Methods Units 3 and 4 cover four areas of study: functions, relations and graphs; algebra, number and structure; calculus; and data analysis, probability and statistics. School-assessed Coursework counts 40 per cent across three tasks, Examination 1 (1 hour, no technology) counts 20 per cent, and Examination 2 (2 hours, with approved technology) counts 40 per cent.

Inside the study design

Four areas of study, three outcomes

Drawn from the VCE Mathematics Study Design, which covers Foundation, General, Mathematical Methods and Specialist Mathematics, read on 7 October 2026. Units 1 to 4 are accredited from 1 January 2023.

Mathematical Methods Units 3 and 4: areas of study and typical placement
Area of studyWhat it coversTypical unit
Functions, relations and graphsPower, exponential, logarithmic and circular functions, their key features, and the effect of transformationsUnit 3, finished in Unit 4
Algebra, number and structureComposition and inverse functions, and solving equations of these function typesUnit 3, finished in Unit 4
CalculusDerivatives and their applications, antidifferentiation, and the definite integralBegun in Unit 3, extended in Unit 4
Data analysis, probability and statisticsDiscrete and continuous random variables, the binomial and normal distributions, and sample proportionsUnit 4

The study design describes Unit 3 as typically covering functions, algebra and the early calculus of stationary points and graph features, with Unit 4 completing the calculus and adding random variables, probability distributions and the distribution of sample proportions. Schools order content themselves, so a student's timetable may differ.

Three outcomes run through both units. The first is concepts and routines; the second is applying mathematics in non-routine contexts; the third is to "apply computational thinking" and use the numerical, graphical, symbolic and statistical functions of technology to develop ideas and carry out analysis.

That third outcome is why a coding school teaches maths well. A student who has written a small program to sample a binomial distribution, or to check a derivative numerically, understands what the CAS calculator is doing instead of pressing buttons in hope.

Methods is also the assumed knowledge for Specialist Mathematics Units 3 and 4, which the study design says requires concurrent study or previous completion of Methods Units 3 and 4. Students taking both need Methods secure early.

How the study score is built

One exam without technology, one exam with it

Every weight and condition below is copied from the study design. The VCAA timetable supplied the 2026 sessions when we looked on 7 October 2026.

Where a Methods study score comes from
Piece of assessmentShareFormat
Unit 3 coursework (SAC)One fifthAn application task: four to six hours, spread over one or two weeks
Unit 4 coursework (SACs)One fifthTwo modelling or problem-solving tasks of two to three hours across a week each; one has to be on data analysis, probability and statistics
Examination 1One fifthSixty minutes with no calculators, software or notes; a sheet of formulas is provided
Examination 2Two fifthsTwo hours of multiple-choice and extended-answer questions, with approved technology and a bound reference

In 2026 Mathematical Methods Examination 1 sits on Thursday 5 November in the 9:00 am to 10:15 am session, and Examination 2 on Friday 6 November from 11:45 am to 2:00 pm, as the VCAA timetable prints them. Two papers on consecutive days leave very little room for catch-up revision between them.

Examination 1 rewards a particular kind of fluency: exact values, clean algebra and short calculus done by hand. It is only an hour, so a student who has to reconstruct a method under pressure runs out of time, not knowledge.

Examination 2 assumes access to technology with numerical, graphical, symbolic and statistical functionality, and allows one bound reference. Students who rely on the calculator without understanding often lose marks on interpretation: the device solves the equation, but the student has to say which solution fits the context.

The coursework tasks are completed mainly in class within a limited timeframe, as the study design requires. Good preparation is the underlying skill, not rehearsing a particular task, because tasks change from school to school.

A worked lesson

Dilate then translate, or translate then dilate?

The study design lists the effect of transformations on graphs and the general transformation of a function. Students who know each transformation individually still lose marks when two are combined, because the order changes the answer. Both examples below are ours.

The setup

Start with y = x squared, vertex at the origin. Apply two transformations: a dilation by factor 3 from the y-axis, and a translation 2 units right.

Dilation first

Stretching first moves the vertex nowhere, then the translation carries it to x = 2. The rule is y = ((x - 2)/3) squared. The point (1, 1) lands on (5, 1).

Translation first

Moving first puts the vertex at x = 2, then the stretch from the y-axis multiplies that x-value by 3. The vertex ends at x = 6, with rule y = ((x - 6)/3) squared. The point (1, 1) lands on (9, 1).

Two orders, two different graphs (our worked examples)
OriginalTransformationsOrderImage ruleKey feature
y = x squaredDilation factor 3 from the y-axis; translation 2 rightDilate, then translatey = ((x - 2)/3) squaredVertex at (2, 0)
y = x squaredThe same twoTranslate, then dilatey = ((x - 6)/3) squaredVertex at (6, 0)
y = 2 to the power xDilation factor 2 from the x-axis; translation 3 upDilate, then translatey = 2 times 2 to the power x, plus 3Asymptote y = 3
y = 2 to the power xThe same twoTranslate, then dilatey = 2 times 2 to the power x, plus 6Asymptote y = 6

The rule that saves marks is to follow a single point through each transformation in the stated order. Track (1, 1) for the parabola, or the asymptote for the exponential, and the image rule writes itself. Students who instead edit the equation in their head usually apply the steps in the order they appear in the rule, which is often the reverse of the order stated in the question.

This is exactly the kind of slip Examination 1 punishes, because there is no technology to sketch the graph and reveal the mistake. Practising with point tracking makes the check routine.

It is also where a little code helps. Plotting both images in a few lines of Python shows the two vertices sitting 4 units apart, and testing a dozen points confirms which rule matches which order. That is the "computational thinking" outcome in action, and it builds a habit students then carry into Examination 2.

The same reasoning extends to the inverse transformation the study design also lists: undoing a sequence means undoing the steps in reverse order, the way you take off shoes before socks.

If Examination 1 is the worry

Timed, by-hand practice in short sets is the cure: ten minutes, five questions, exact answers, every week. Speed comes from fluency, not from rushing.

Most lost marks in technology-free work are algebra, not calculus. Repairing algebra repairs the whole paper.

Questions about Methods

Methods questions we hear most

What is VCE Mathematical Methods?

It is a VCE mathematics study covering functions, algebra, calculus, and probability and statistics. Units 1 to 4 are accredited from 1 January 2023, and Units 3 and 4 are assessed by coursework and two end-of-year examinations.

How is the Methods study score made up?

School-assessed Coursework contributes 40 per cent (20 per cent in each unit), Examination 1 contributes 20 per cent and Examination 2 contributes 40 per cent.

Can I use a calculator in Examination 1?

No. The study design says no technology (calculators or software) or notes of any kind are permitted. A sheet of formulas is provided. Examination 2 allows approved technology and one bound reference text.

When are the 2026 Methods exams?

The VCAA timetable lists Examination 1 on Thursday 5 November 2026 (9:00 am to 10:15 am session) and Examination 2 on Friday 6 November (11:45 am to 2:00 pm session).

What are the Methods SACs?

Unit 3 has an application task of 4 to 6 hours over 1 to 2 weeks. Unit 4 has two modelling or problem-solving tasks of 2 to 3 hours over a week each, one of them on data analysis, probability and statistics.

What is a dilation from the y-axis?

A stretch parallel to the x-axis, away from the y-axis, by a given factor. A dilation by factor 3 from the y-axis turns the point (1, 1) into (3, 1) and replaces x with x divided by 3 in the rule.

Do I need Methods for Specialist Mathematics?

The study design says Specialist Mathematics Units 3 and 4 assumes concurrent study or previous completion of Mathematical Methods Units 3 and 4.

Will you help with my SAC?

We teach the mathematics with our own problems. We never complete, check or edit any part of a SAC, because the VCAA accepts coursework only as the student's own work.

Is online maths tutoring effective for Methods?

For most students, yes. Working on a shared screen, writing every step and getting immediate correction is close to sitting beside a tutor, provided the student does the writing rather than watching.

What time do lessons run for Victorian students?

Late afternoon in Victoria works for most, and it falls during our tutors' working day. The weekly time is agreed after the free class.
