# Coding and AI classes in North Carolina

**Do North Carolina students have to take computer science to graduate?** Yes. Under G.S. 115C-12(9d), signed into law on September 29, 2023, every North Carolina public school unit must offer computer science courses that meet a new high school graduation requirement, in full from the 2026 to 2027 school year, and an introductory middle school elective from 2025 to 2026. We teach the same five core concepts live online, from Scratch to AP Computer Science.

North Carolina has done something it had not done in a hundred years: added a new content area to what every student needs to graduate. In the words of the state&rsquo;s own deputy superintendent in 2023, &ldquo;We haven&rsquo;t added a content area to the graduation requirements for North Carolina students in a century.&rdquo; This page explains the requirement as the Department of Public Instruction publishes it, the five core concepts the state standards are built on, what the published figures say about how many high schools offer computer science and how many students take it, and how our live online classes fit around school, from a first Scratch game in elementary school to AP Computer Science in eleventh grade.

## What North Carolina&rsquo;s computer science law requires

The requirements as the North Carolina Department of Public Instruction (NC DPI) sets them out for G.S. 115C-12(9d), read on 17 October 2026.

**G.S. 115C-12(9d), Computer Science, as summarized by NC DPI**

| Requirement | What NC DPI says |
|---|---|
| When it became law | Adopted by the legislature on September 21, 2023 and signed into law on September 29, 2023 |
| High school | Every public school unit must offer computer science course(s) that fulfill the graduation requirement by the 2026 to 2027 school year, and as early as 2024 to 2025 |
| Middle school | Every public school unit must offer an elective introductory computer science course in middle school by the 2025 to 2026 school year |
| Middle school credit | Courses that meet the graduation requirement may be offered to middle school students and would fulfill it |
| Waivers | Local delay waivers were available only for 2024 to 2025 and 2025 to 2026; none are permitted for full implementation from 2026 to 2027 |
| Delivery | Courses in person when practicable, with remote enrollment options allowed under state law |

### Who it applies to

NC DPI&rsquo;s 2023 announcement says the requirement applies to all students entering high school in the 2026 to 2027 school year, so the incoming ninth graders of fall 2026 are the first full class.

### Two exemptions

Students who enter a public high school after completing eleventh grade are exempt, and students on an IEP may be exempted if their disability would prevent them from completing the requirement.

### Which courses count

NC DPI reviewed courses against the state&rsquo;s core concepts: a course was aligned if at least 80% of its content focused on three or more of them. The 2026 to 2027 review was broadened to the CSTA framework.

At the time the bill passed, NC DPI wrote that North Carolina would be the eighth state to require a computer science course to graduate. That was the count in 2023; other states have changed their rules since, so the number is quoted here as the department stated it then, not as a current ranking.

## The five core concepts North Carolina teaches, grade band by grade band

NC DPI says the K-12 computer science standards were derived from the national CSTA standards and are organized by grade band in K-8 and by course in high school.

**The five core concepts of the NC K-12 Computer Science Standards, and how our classes cover each (the right-hand column is ours)**

| Core concept | What it means in practice | Where it shows up in our classes |
|---|---|---|
| Computing Systems | Devices, hardware and software, and how they work together | Kids classes start with how a computer runs a program; teens debug on real machines |
| Networks and the Internet | How data moves between computers, and how it is kept safe | Web projects, APIs and the cybersecurity course |
| Data and Analysis | Collecting, cleaning and drawing conclusions from data | The Raleigh greenway project below, then data science |
| Algorithms and Programming | Writing and testing step-by-step solutions in code | Every course, from Scratch to AP Computer Science A |
| Impacts of Computing | How computing affects people, society and careers | Discussed in every AI unit: who a model helps and who it misses |

### K-2 and 3-5

The standards group elementary grades into two bands. At this age the concepts are taught with blocks, unplugged puzzles and short programs a child can explain out loud.

### 6-8

The middle school band lines up with the new introductory elective. Students move from blocks to typed code and start working with real data.

### High school

High school standards are written by course, starting with Introduction to Computer Science (ICS) and continuing into discipline-specific courses.

### What parents told the state

The standards page reports that 93% of parents in North Carolina want schools to teach computer science. The requirement turns that wish into a course every student takes; outside classes are for students who want more depth, more speed or more projects than one course can hold.

## Which Raleigh greenway trail is longest? A data project on city open data

The City of Raleigh publishes its greenway trail inventory as open data. Students download it, group it and sum it in Python, then learn why their answer is not the official one.

**Raleigh greenway trails by summed map miles (our calculation from the city&rsquo;s Greenway Trails data, downloaded 17 October 2026)**

| Trail name | Our sum of MAP_MILES |
|---|---|
| Neuse River Trail | 33.61 |
| Walnut Creek Trail | 20.27 |
| Crabtree Creek Trail | 18.63 |
| Mine Creek Trail | 6.54 |

### 1. Read the data

The city&rsquo;s feature service held 1,266 records on the day we downloaded it: 701 trail segments, 365 structures such as boardwalks and bridges, and 114 crossings, among other types.

### 2. Group and sum

Students group the records by TRAIL_NAME and add up MAP_MILES for each of the 26 named trails, the first real use of a dictionary or a pandas groupby.

### 3. Question the answer

Then the hard part: does a summed map length equal the trail&rsquo;s official length? Not necessarily. Segments can overlap, structures may carry their own mileage, and the city may measure differently.

### Why our numbers are not official

The sums above are ours, from segment lengths in one download. They are a teaching result, not the City of Raleigh&rsquo;s published trail lengths, and the city says it makes no warranty about the data. Learning to say exactly what a number is, and what it is not, is the most valuable lesson in the project.

The surface types make a second exercise: 756 records are asphalt, 230 wood decking and 142 concrete. A student who charts those counts, then asks which surfaces are structures rather than trail, has practised the Data and Analysis concept from the state standards on a dataset from their own state.

## If AI can write code, why does North Carolina now require computer science?

Because judging code, data and AI output is a skill, and it is learned by building.

An AI assistant can produce a working Python script in seconds. What it will not do on its own is ask whether the answer means what it seems to mean. In the Raleigh project, the summed miles look like trail lengths, and a student who has not built the calculation has no way of knowing they are not quite that.

That is the Impacts of Computing and Data and Analysis concepts at work. Students who write the code themselves learn to ask what each number is, where it came from and what it leaves out, and they can then use AI tools as helpers rather than as oracles.

The new requirement gives every North Carolina student one course; the students who go further are the ones who learn to question a result, not just produce one. The longer argument is in [is coding still worth learning in 2026](/blog/is-coding-worth-learning-2026).
