# KS1 maths tuition online

Many parents of six and seven year olds have heard that "KS1 SATs" no longer happen, and wonder whether Year 2 maths still matters. The tests did change: since the Standards and Testing Agency made them optional, a school may or may not use them. What did not change is the national curriculum. By the end of Year 2, the Department for Education still expects children to know their number bonds to 20, to understand tens and ones, and to recall the 2, 5 and 10 times tables, and every later year of primary maths is built on those facts. This page sets out what Years 1 and 2 cover, how the optional tests work in 2027, simple games families can play at home, and how our live KS1 classes teach it.

## What should a child know in maths by the end of Year 2?

The national curriculum sets out what children should be taught in each year. These are the statutory expectations, summarised from the programme of study.

The Department for Education states the purpose plainly: "The principal focus of mathematics teaching in key stage 1 is to ensure that pupils develop confidence and mental fluency with whole numbers, counting and place value." It sets one clear goal for the end of the stage: "By the end of year 2, pupils should know the number bonds to 20 and be precise in using and understanding place value. An emphasis on practice at this early stage will aid fluency." Everything else in KS1 grows from those two ideas.

**Key Stage 1 maths, year by year (from the national curriculum programmes of study)**

| Area | Year 1 | Year 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Counting and place value | Count to and across 100, forwards and backwards; read and write numbers 1 to 20 in numerals and words | Count in steps of 2, 3 and 5 from 0 and in tens from any number; tens and ones in two-digit numbers; compare with <, > and = |
| Adding and subtracting | Number bonds and subtraction facts within 20; add and subtract one-digit and two-digit numbers to 20 | Facts to 20 recalled fluently; add and subtract a two-digit number and ones, and two two-digit numbers |
| Multiplying and dividing | One-step problems with objects, pictures and arrays | Recall the 2, 5 and 10 times tables; know multiplication can be done in any order and division cannot |
| Fractions | Recognise a half and a quarter | Find one third, one quarter, two quarters and three quarters of a shape, set or quantity |
| Time | Tell the time to the hour and half past | Tell the time to five minutes; minutes in an hour and hours in a day |
| Money | Recognise coins and notes | Use the pound and pence symbols; give change |
| Shape and data | Name common 2-D and 3-D shapes; whole, half and quarter turns | Sides, edges, faces and vertices; pictograms, tally charts and block diagrams |

The curriculum's notes describe how this should feel in practice. Children should "partition numbers in different ways (for example, 23 = 20 + 3 and 23 = 10 + 13) to support subtraction", and in Year 2 they meet three quarters as "the first example of a non-unit fraction". In geometry, the notes even suggest "programming robots using instructions given in right angles", which is where early maths and early coding meet.

Source: Department for Education, [Mathematics programmes of study: key stages 1 and 2](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/national-curriculum-in-england-mathematics-programmes-of-study), read on 31 October 2026. The table summarises the statutory requirements; the full document has more detail.

## Are KS1 SATs still happening in 2027?

Not as statutory tests. The end of KS1 tests are optional, and a school decides whether to use them. The Standards and Testing Agency recommends that schools that do use them give them in May 2027.

The Standards and Testing Agency says it directly: "National curriculum tests at the end of KS1 are optional." Schools can use them "to support their own measurement of pupil attainment", and although the results can help when reporting to parents, the agency adds that "there is no requirement for schools to do so". Some things are still statutory in Year 2. In the agency's words, "the following remain statutory: the phonics screening check annual reports for parents about each pupil's general progress in year 2", along with trialling for a small number of selected schools.

**The optional KS1 maths test, 2026 to 2027 (STA guidance and scaled score notice)**

| Part | What it is | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Paper 1: arithmetic | A single test paper of calculations | Approximately 20 minutes |
| Paper 2: reasoning | A single paper, with an aural practice question and 5 aural questions read aloud | Approximately 35 minutes |
| Timing | "The optional KS1 tests are not designed to be strictly timed." | Recommended during May 2027 |
| Scaled scores | 85 is the lowest scaled score; 100 to 115 means the expected standard was met | Converted from the raw score |

### What this means for families

Your child's school may give the optional tests in May 2027, or may not. Either way, the school still reports each child's general progress in Year 2.

Ask the class teacher what the school uses to check maths at the end of Year 2, and what it shows. That conversation is more useful than any practice paper.

### What is under review

The agency notes that "future assessment arrangements are under review" following the Curriculum and Assessment Review, published with the government's response in November 2025.

For this school year it is clear that "Assessment arrangements for the academic year 2026 to 2027 will remain unchanged."

The agency also says something parents rarely hear: its test frameworks are written for test developers, and "Teachers should not use the test frameworks to guide teaching and learning." The curriculum is the guide. That is the approach we take: teach the Year 1 and Year 2 content well, and any test that a school chooses to use will look after itself.

Read on 31 October 2026.

## What KS1 maths games can we play at home?

Five games of our own that need nothing more than a pack of cards, some coins or a clock. Each one practises a statutory KS1 skill.

**Our kitchen-table games for Years 1 and 2**

| Game (ours) | What you need | What it practises |
|---|---|---|
| Make Ten | Cards 1 to 9 | Turn two cards; keep them if they make 10. Later, make 20 with three cards. Number bonds to 10 and 20. |
| Tens and Ones Shop | Ten-pence and one-penny coins | Pay for items priced up to 99p using the fewest coins. Place value, and the pence symbol. |
| Clock Detective | A real analogue clock | One player sets a time to five minutes, the other reads it aloud. Year 2 time. |
| Share Fairly | Twelve small snacks | Share them into 2, 3 and 4 equal groups and name each fraction. Halves, thirds and quarters. |
| Robot Steps | A floor and a toy | Give instructions: forward 3, quarter turn right. Position, direction and turns. |

Little and often works better than long sessions at this age. The curriculum's own advice is that "An emphasis on practice at this early stage will aid fluency", and in our experience five or ten minutes a day of a game a child enjoys does more for number bonds than a long weekend worksheet. Stop while it is still fun.

## Four KS1 examples we use, step by step

Our own examples, written for a parent sitting beside a child. Each follows the curriculum's order: objects first, then pictures, then numbers.

### 1. Subtraction by partitioning (Year 2)

**Question (ours).** 34 &minus; 18.

**With objects.** Make 34 from three ten-sticks and four ones. To take away 18, we need eight ones, but there are only four, so swap one ten-stick for ten ones: now 2 tens and 14 ones.

**Take away.** 14 ones minus 8 ones leaves 6 ones. 2 tens minus 1 ten leaves 1 ten.

**Answer.** 1 ten and 6 ones is 16. This is the curriculum's idea that 34 can be seen as 20 + 14 as well as 30 + 4.

### 2. Number bonds through 10 (Year 1)

**Question (ours).** 8 + 5.

**Picture.** Draw a ten-frame with 8 dots. Two more fill it. That uses 2 of the 5, leaving 3.

**Numbers.** 8 + 2 = 10, then 10 + 3 = 13.

**Why it matters.** Bonds to 10 turn every sum up to 20 into two easy steps, which is why the curriculum wants them known by heart.

### 3. The 5 times table on a clock (Year 2)

**Question (ours).** The minute hand points to the 7. How many minutes past the hour is it?

**Count in fives.** Each number on the clock is 5 minutes: 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35.

**Answer.** 35 minutes past, which is 7 × 5. The curriculum links the 5 times table to "the divisions on the clock face" for exactly this reason.

### 4. Three quarters of a set (Year 2)

**Question (ours).** What is three quarters of 12 strawberries?

**Share.** Put the 12 into 4 equal groups: 3 in each. One quarter is 3.

**Three quarters.** Three of the groups: 3 + 3 + 3 = 9.

**Answer.** 9 strawberries. Sharing first makes it clear why three quarters is more than a half (6) but less than the whole.

**Practice set (ours, with answers).** (1) What must be added to 13 to make 20? (2) Write 47 as tens and ones, then as 30 and something. (3) 6 × 5. (4) Half of 18. (5) Which is more, 3 tens or 28 ones? (6) A lesson starts at quarter past 2 and lasts 30 minutes; when does it end? **Answers:** (1) 7; (2) 4 tens and 7 ones, and 30 + 17; (3) 30; (4) 9; (5) 3 tens, because 3 tens is 30; (6) quarter to 3.

## How does online KS1 maths tuition work for a six year old?

Short, lively and practical. Young children learn number with their hands and eyes before symbols, and online lessons can do that well when they are designed for it.

1. **A gentle first class.** The teacher plays a few number games with the child to see where they are with counting, bonds and place value. It is free and there is nothing to prepare.
2. **Things to hand.** We ask families to keep a small kit by the screen: counters or buttons, a few coins, and later a clock face. The child moves real objects while the teacher shows the same thing on screen.
3. **Objects, pictures, then numbers.** Each idea is met with objects, drawn as a picture, then written as a number sentence, the order the curriculum describes.
4. **Short practice between classes.** A five-minute game or a handful of questions for the days in between, never a long worksheet.
5. **A note for parents.** After each block of lessons, a short message on what the child can now do and what comes next.

**What a KS1 child should be able to do**

## What parents ask about KS1 maths

### What maths should my child know by the end of Year 2?

The national curriculum expects number bonds to 20, a precise understanding of tens and ones, and recall of the 2, 5 and 10 times tables. Children should also tell the time to five minutes and find simple fractions such as three quarters.

### Are KS1 SATs still happening?

The end of KS1 tests are optional. Schools can choose to use them, and the Standards and Testing Agency recommends May 2027 for those that do.

### What is in the optional KS1 maths test?

Two papers: arithmetic, about 20 minutes, and reasoning, about 35 minutes, including five questions read aloud. The agency says the tests are not designed to be strictly timed.

### What do KS1 scaled scores mean?

The lowest scaled score is 85. A score between 100 and 115 means the child met the expected standard on that test.

### Should my child practise KS1 past papers?

We would not drill them. The tests are optional, and the agency itself says its frameworks should not guide teaching. Games and steady practice of the curriculum facts help far more.

### What KS1 maths games can we play at home?

Simple card and coin games work well: making 10 or 20 from playing cards, paying for things with ten-pence and penny coins, and reading a real clock to five minutes. Five or ten minutes a day is plenty.

### Is online maths tuition suitable for a six year old?

It can be, when lessons are short, practical and designed for young children. We ask families to keep counters and coins by the screen so the child handles real objects.

### When are classes?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. All times are UK time.

### Which course should a Year 1 child start with?

Usually Elementary Maths. For a child still building counting, Early Maths Foundations comes first; the free first class helps decide.

### Are you connected with my child's school or the STA?

No. We are an independent online school, separate from schools, the Department for Education and the Standards and Testing Agency.
