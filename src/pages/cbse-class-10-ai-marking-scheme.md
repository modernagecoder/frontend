> The class 10 AI marking scheme for CBSE 417 gives examiners value points, not full answers: any correct wording earns the mark if the point is there. In the 2025 and 2026 schemes, objective items carry 1 mark each with no negative marking, 2-mark answers usually split into two 1-mark points or a point plus two half-mark examples, and 4-mark answers into named parts such as 1 per step or half a mark per formula and per substitution. ## How the class 10 AI marking scheme is laid out

The 417 theory paper is 50 marks in two hours: 24 objective marks in Section A and 26 written marks in Section B. The 2025 and 2026 board schemes and the 2025-26 sample paper scheme all use this shape.

- Part of the paper | Choice | Marks each | Total | How the scheme marks it

- Section A, employability skills | any 4 of 6 | 1 | 4 | Right or wrong; often an option letter or its text is accepted

- Section A, AI items (four questions) | any 5 of 6 in each | 1 | 20 | Same; no negative marking

- Section B, employability skills | any 3 of 5, 20 to 30 words | 2 | 6 | Usually 1 per relevant point

- Section B, AI short answers | any 4 of 6, 20 to 30 words | 2 | 8 | Two points, or a point plus examples

- Section B, AI long answers | any 3 of 5, 50 to 80 words | 4 | 12 | Split into named parts, steps or calculations

### The rules on the front pages, in plain words

- Value points are a guide. The scheme says its answers are not complete answers; a student's own wording earns the marks if the point is correct.

- Competency questions are read generously. If an answer shows the skill being tested, examiners are told to credit it even when it is not the scheme's wording.

- One error, one penalty. A mistake carried into later parts is not punished again.

- Extra questions. If you answer more than the choice allows, the higher-scoring answer is kept.

- Full marks are allowed. Examiners are told to use the whole scale, so a complete answer gets everything.

### What this means for the way you write

Examiners look for the points, so make each point easy to find: one idea per line, the key term written exactly (precision, data acquisition, justice), and the example labelled "Example:". An answer that is right but buried in a paragraph risks a point being missed.

A note on sources: we read set 4 of the 2025 and 2026 papers and the sample paper scheme. Other sets have other questions but the same marking style. The 2025 paper still carried some older topics, such as AI bias and data science applications; 2026 follows the current units. Unit-by-unit weightage is on [the exam date and paper pattern page](/cbse-class-10-ai-exam-date-and-paper-pattern).

## How AI answers are marked in Section A

Every objective item is one mark, all or nothing, with no negative marking. The schemes accept either the option letter or the option text, and some items list more than one acceptable answer.

### Letter or words
The 2026 scheme writes answers as "(C) OR the option's text". Writing both is safest: a letter alone can be misread, and the words show you knew it.

### More than one right answer
In 2026, a two-statement item about overfitting accepted two options, because each statement was true on its own. In 2025, a chatbot item accepted several names for a smart bot.

### Half marks can appear
The 2025 chatbot item gave half a mark when a student named only an example product instead of the type. So even a one-mark item can be partly credited when the scheme allows it.

### Three original one-mark items, with how they would be marked

- A gate camera reads car number plates. Which AI domain is this?
Marking Computer Vision [1]. "CV" also earns it. "Image processing" alone is a narrower idea, and a scheme may not list it.

- Assertion: Precision is the share of predicted positives that were correct. Reason: Precision uses TP and FN.
Marking Assertion true, reason false [1]. Precision uses TP and FP. No working is needed or marked.

- Fill in the blank: the stage of the AI project cycle that puts a model into real use is ______.
Marking Deployment [1]. Naming any other stage, such as evaluation, earns nothing.

## Two-mark answers: where each mark sits

A 2-mark answer is almost always worth two separate points. The schemes use four splits again and again, and knowing which one a question uses tells you what to write.

- Split | Where we saw it | What to write

- 1 + 1, two points | Two characteristics of a classification model (2026); two stages explained (2025-26 SQP) | Two separate lines, each a complete point

- 1 for a difference + &frac12; + &frac12; for examples | Sector-based against value-based frameworks (2026) | One clear difference, then one example of each, labelled

- 1 for a difference + &frac12; + &frac12; for applying it | Stemming against lemmatisation on a plural word (2026) | The difference, then the stem and the lemma of the given word

- Graded by how many points | The 7 Cs of communication (2026): all seven for 2, fewer for 1&frac12;, 1 or &frac12; | Every item in the list, even if short of time

### Q. Differentiate classification and regression, with one example of each. (2)

Full-mark answer. Classification predicts a label or category from fixed classes, while regression predicts a continuous number [1]. Example of classification: sorting emails into spam or not spam [&frac12;]. Example of regression: predicting tomorrow's temperature in degrees [&frac12;].

Answer that gets 1. "Classification puts things in groups and regression is for numbers, e.g. house price." The difference earns its mark; there is only one example, and it is not labelled as regression, so at most half more is likely.

### Q. What is model evaluation, and why is it needed? (2)

Full-mark answer. Model evaluation is checking how well a trained model performs, using metrics such as accuracy, precision and recall on data it has not seen [1]. It is needed because the scores show the model's weak points, so it can be improved before people rely on it [1].

Answer that gets 1. "Evaluation is the fifth stage of the AI project cycle where we test the model." That places it, but gives no reason it is needed, so the second point is missing. This "define, then explain" split is the one the 2025-26 sample paper used for its evaluation item.

Every two-mark difference pair the units offer is answered on [CBSE Class 10 AI difference between questions](/cbse-class-10-ai-difference-between-questions).

## Step marking in 417: four-mark answers, annotated

Yes, in effect. The four-mark items are split into parts, steps or calculations, each with its own mark, so a student who gets the final value wrong can still earn most of the marks for the matrix, the formulas and the substitution.

Q. A library tests a model that predicts whether a borrowed book will be returned late, on 400 loans. It predicted "late" for 80 loans, of which 72 were late. Of the 320 it predicted "on time", 28 were late. (a) Draw the confusion matrix. (2) (b) Calculate precision and recall. (2)

```
(a)            Reality: Late  Reality: On time
   Pred: Late     TP = 72        FP = 8
   Pred: On time  FN = 28        TN = 292   [2]

(b) Precision = TP / (TP + FP)              [&frac12;]
              = 72 / (72 + 8) = 72 / 80     [&frac12;]
              = 0.90
    Recall    = TP / (TP + FN)              [&frac12;]
              = 72 / (72 + 28) = 72 / 100   [&frac12;]
              = 0.72
```

- Slip | Likely cost

- Matrix drawn with all four cells right | 2 of 2

- Matrix with only two cells right | about 1&frac12;

- Matrix frame with no values | &frac12;

- FP and FN swapped, so "precision" = 0.72 | loses cell marks; formula marks can survive, and the error is penalised once

- Formula only, no substitution | &frac12; per part

- Right formula, arithmetic slip in the last line | usually little or nothing

The costs above follow the 2026 and 2025 schemes for their matrix items: two marks for a fully correct matrix with partial credit below that, and half marks for each formula and its substitution. Both schemes add a note that the calculations themselves can be ignored, which puts the weight on the method. Still write the final value: accuracy here is 364 / 400 = 0.91, and F1 is 2 x 0.90 x 0.72 / 1.62 = 0.80, not the plain average 0.81.

### Bag of Words: one mark per step

Q. Apply the four steps of Bag of Words to: D1 "Robots help farmers." D2 "Farmers teach robots and robots help." (4)

Step 1, normalise [1]: D1 = robots, help, farmers; D2 = farmers, teach, robots, and, robots, help.Step 2, dictionary [1]: robots, help, farmers, teach, and.Step 3, vector for D1 [1]: [1, 1, 1, 0, 0].Step 4, table for all [1]: D1 [1, 1, 1, 0, 0]; D2 [2, 1, 1, 1, 1].

The 2026 scheme gave one mark per step and said steps 3 and 4 may be shown together. A table without the step names risks the marks for the steps you skipped. Fully worked versions are on [Bag of Words and TFIDF for Class 10 AI](/bag-of-words-tfidf-class-10-ai).

### Other four-mark splits we saw

- AI, ML and DL with a Venn diagram (2026): 1 for each term, 1 for the labelled diagram.

- Two scenarios, name the domain (2026): 1 for each domain, 1 for each justification.

- Biased selection case (2025-26 sample): 1 for each of two reasons, then half for each principle named and half for each explanation.

- Matrix, accuracy and wrong predictions (2025-26 sample): half per correct cell, then half for each formula and half for each answer.

## Where 417 answers lose marks, and the habit that keeps them

Most lost marks in this paper are not lost on hard topics. They go on answers that miss one value point the scheme was looking for. These are the patterns we see most when marking mocks.

- What happens | Which split it breaks | The habit that fixes it

- A difference written, but no examples | Difference plus two half-mark examples | Write "Example:" twice whenever a question says "with an example"

- Principle described but not named | Half for the name, half for the explanation | Start each line with the principle's name: "Justice: ..."

- Only the final number in a numerical | Half per formula and per substitution | Formula line, substitution line, answer line, every time

- Bag of Words table with no steps | One mark per named step | Head each part "Step 1", "Step 2" and so on

- Two points merged into one sentence | 1 + 1 | One point per line; never let a "because" hide the second point

- Long answers past the word limit | Not a rule for marks, but time lost | 20 to 30 words for 2 marks, 50 to 80 for 4; stop when the points are made

- Extra questions attempted "just in case" | Only the higher answer counts | Choose early, spend the time checking

The marking scheme covers the 50 theory marks only. The other 50, the practical file, practical exam, viva and project, are assessed in school; that half is on [the project and practical file page](/cbse-class-10-ai-project-and-practical-file). Past papers to practise these splits on are described on [CBSE Class 10 AI previous year question papers](/cbse-class-10-ai-previous-year-question-papers).

## How the live batch marks answers the CBSE way

Knowing the splits is half the work; the other half is writing to them under time until it is automatic. That only happens when someone marks your answers the way the board will and shows you the half marks you dropped.

### Written to the word limit
On Mondays students write two-mark and four-mark answers against the clock, to the 20 to 30 and 50 to 80 word limits the paper sets.

### Marked on screen
On Wednesdays the teacher marks a few answers on the shared screen, writing the half marks in the margin, so everyone sees which line earned what and which point was missing.

### Rewritten, then mocked
Each student rewrites one answer that lost marks. From December, full timed mocks are marked against the value points and returned with a mark per question.

The mock calendar is on [the sample paper and mock tests page](/cbse-class-10-ai-sample-paper-and-mock-tests). Students can pause any marking session to argue for a half mark; working out why the scheme would or would not give it is often where the lesson lands.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the class 10 AI marking scheme?** It is the document CBSE gives examiners for each paper, listing the value points an answer should contain and how many marks each point earns. For 417 it covers the 50-mark theory paper. CBSE publishes main exam schemes on its website and a scheme with each sample paper, which is how students can see how answers are marked.

**Where can I find the class 10 AI marking scheme 2026?** CBSE posts main exam marking schemes on cbse.gov.in, by year and subject code, and sample paper schemes on cbseacademic.nic.in. Each main exam has several sets with different questions; we read set 4 of the 2026 paper, so other sets differ in questions but not in marking style.

**Is there step marking in CBSE Class 10 AI?** In effect, yes. Four-mark numericals and Bag of Words items are split into parts: marks for the confusion matrix, half marks for each formula and each substitution, or one mark per Bag of Words step. The 2025 and 2026 schemes also note that calculations can be ignored, so the method carries most of the marks.

**Is there negative marking in the class 10 AI paper?** No. The 2025 and 2026 papers and the 2025-26 sample paper all state there is no negative marking in Section A. Attempt every objective item you choose, and use elimination when unsure.

**How are 2 mark questions marked in class 10 AI?** Usually as two separate points of one mark each, or one mark for a difference plus half a mark for each of two examples. Some list-based answers are graded by how many items you give. Write each point on its own line so the examiner can find it.

**Do I have to write the exact words from the marking scheme?** No. The scheme says its value points are guidelines and that a student's own expression earns the marks if it is correct. Key terms still matter: write precision, data acquisition or justice exactly, because those words are what the examiner looks for.

**What happens if I answer an extra question?** The examiner marks the extra answer too, but only the higher-scoring one is counted and the other is struck out. It rarely helps, because the time is better spent checking the answers you chose.

**How many words should a 4 mark AI answer have?** The 417 paper asks for 50 to 80 words for four-mark answers and 20 to 30 words for two-mark answers. The marks come from the value points, not the length, so aim to cover every point clearly within the limit.

**Will the 2027 marking scheme be the same?** No one can say until CBSE publishes the 2026-27 sample paper for 417, and the one listed so far is the 2025-26 paper. The 2025-26 sample paper and the 2026 board paper used the same structure and splits, so practising to them is the sensible preparation.

**Is marked answer practice included in your classes?** Yes.
