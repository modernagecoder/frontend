## A Unit 3 evaluation numerical, worked in full

Unit 3, Evaluating Models, carries 10 theory marks, and confusion matrix numericals are its most predictable questions. This one is ours, set on a Pune monsoon, with every figure checked in Python before it went on the page.

### Question (4 marks)

A model predicts each morning whether it will rain in Pune that afternoon. Over 120 days it gave: rain predicted and it rained, 30; rain predicted but it stayed dry, 10; no rain predicted but it rained, 6; no rain predicted and it stayed dry, 74. (a) Draw the confusion matrix. (b) Find accuracy, precision, recall and F1 score. (c) A school uses the forecast to decide whether to hold sports day outside. Which matters more, precision or recall? Give a reason.

- | Actually rained | Actually dry

- Predicted rain | TP = 30 | FP = 10

- Predicted no rain | FN = 6 | TN = 74

-
**Accuracy** (TP + TN) / total = (30 + 74) / 120 = 104 / 120 = 0.87 (0.8667). The model is wrong on 16 days.

-
**Precision** TP / (TP + FP) = 30 / 40 = 0.75. When it says rain, it is right three times in four.

-
**Recall** TP / (TP + FN) = 30 / 36 = 0.83 (0.8333). Of the 36 rainy days, it caught 30.

-
**F1 score** 2 x P x R / (P + R) = 2 x 0.75 x 0.8333 / 1.5833 = 0.79 (0.7895). The plain average of P and R, 0.7917, happens to round to the same 0.79 here, but on other numbers it does not, so always use the formula.

-
**(c) Precision or recall?** Recall. A missed rainy day (FN) means children and equipment caught in a downpour; a false alarm (FP) only moves the event indoors. A good answer names the costly error and links it to the metric.

One more line we always add in class: a lazy model that says "no rain" every day would be right on the 84 dry days, an accuracy of
0.70, with a recall of zero. That is why accuracy alone is never the whole answer. More numericals of this kind are on
[the confusion matrix numericals page](/confusion-matrix-class-10-ai-numericals), and the ideas behind
them on [the evaluating models page](/cbse-class-10-ai-evaluating-models-questions).

## Inside one 9 PM class, step by step

Every class follows the same shape, so a Pune student knows what is coming and nothing is wasted: doubts first, one idea taught, tried, marked and checked before the class ends.

-
**Open with doubts** Questions left over from the worksheet or the school lesson come first, answered on the shared screen so the whole group benefits.

-
**Teach one thing properly** On a Monday it might be precision and recall; on a Wednesday, a NumPy program or an Orange workflow. One idea, with examples, rather than a whole chapter rushed.

-
**Everyone attempts it** Students write the answer or the code themselves, in their own notebook or Jupyter, under a short time limit. Nobody copies from the teacher's screen.

-
**Mark it where everyone can see** Two or three attempts are shared and marked against value points. Students see exactly which line earned a mark and which did not, then fix their own.

-
**Close with one exit question** A single quick question that shows whether the idea landed. If many miss it, the next class starts there. The worksheet for the week follows; there are more on [the worksheets page](/cbse-class-10-ai-worksheets).

## The 417 project and its viva: 15 marks your school awards

The project carries 10 marks and the project viva 5, and the school assesses both, usually close to the practical exam. We help the student choose a project that is small enough to finish and their own enough to explain.

### Wet or dry waste, from photos
The student photographs household waste, labels each picture wet or dry and trains an image classifier in Orange. Clear data, a real local use, and an easy viva story about why some photos fool the model.

### Will it rain this afternoon?
The student keeps a simple log of morning conditions and whether it rained, then reasons about the model using the confusion matrix above. Good for a student who enjoys the Unit 3 numericals.

### How did people feel about it?
The student writes or collects short reviews of a school event and runs sentiment analysis, linking the project to Unit 6. Ideal for a student who prefers text to images.

-
**Pick early, in class** The idea is fixed in the first weeks so it can grow alongside the units, one project-cycle stage at a time.

-
**Write it up as you go** Each stage, from problem scoping to evaluation, is recorded the week it is done, so there is no write-up panic at the end.

-
**Explain it out loud, twice** Each student presents their project to the class and answers follow-up questions, then does it again a fortnight later. More ideas are on [the project ideas page](/cbse-class-10-ai-project-ideas), and the oral questions on [the viva questions page](/cbse-class-10-ai-viva-questions).
