# AQA vs Edexcel vs OCR GCSE maths: what actually differs?

**The short answer.** Less than most people think. All three boards teach the same Department for Education content, grade 9 to 1 on two tiers, and set three 1 hour 30 minute papers, one without a calculator. AQA and Edexcel papers carry 80 marks each and OCR papers 100. The school chooses the board, so prepare for the papers you will actually sit.

What is the same everywhere

## The content, the topic balance and the skills are fixed nationally.

The Department for Education publishes one list of GCSE maths content, and every board in England must teach and assess all of it. A student who moves from an AQA school to an OCR school is not learning a different subject. They are meeting the same topics in a different paper layout.

The balance of topics is fixed too. AQA's specification states that the weighting of topic areas "has been prescribed by Ofqual and is common to all exam boards". So the share of marks for number, algebra or geometry is the same whichever board sets the paper.

The assessment objectives match as well: on Foundation, about 50% of marks test standard techniques and 25% each test reasoning and problem solving; on Higher the split is about 40%, 30% and 30%.

What really differs

## Paper format, numbering and grade boundaries.

### Marks per paper

OCR papers are out of 100 marks; AQA and Edexcel papers are out of 80. The time is the same, so an OCR paper usually holds more questions or more parts. Practise with the right paper length so pacing feels normal.

### Which paper has no calculator

On AQA and Edexcel the first paper is the non-calculator one. OCR numbers its Higher papers 4 to 6, and its non-calculator papers are Paper 2 on Foundation and Paper 5 on Higher. Students who move board sometimes walk in expecting the wrong one.

### Grade boundaries

Ofqual says grade boundaries "typically differ between exam boards, because grading reflects the level of difficulty of each particular paper". A higher boundary means that paper was easier, not that the grade was harder to reach.

Question styles also vary between boards in small ways, which Ofqual describes as "small differences between the type of questions they ask or the balance of different types of questions". The honest way to see them is to sit a past paper from each, which our tutors do with students who change schools.

Read your marks properly

## Why a percentage on one board does not tell you the grade on another.

Two students, two boards

**Asha scores 54 on an AQA paper. Ben scores 67 on an OCR paper. Who did better?**

Asha: 54 ÷ 80 = 0.675 = 67.5%Ben: 67 ÷ 100 = 0.67 = 67%The percentages are almost equal.

Raw marks mislead because the papers have different totals. Even the percentages only compare fairly when the papers were equally hard, which is exactly what boundaries adjust for.

The whole qualification

**What total is the grade based on?**

AQA or Edexcel: 3 × 80 = 240 marksOCR: 3 × 100 = 300 marksThe grade comes from the total across all three papers.

That is why one weak paper is not the end: a strong calculator paper can carry a shaky non-calculator one. We track all three when we plan revision.

Common questions

## AQA, Edexcel and OCR: what students and parents ask.

Which GCSE maths exam board is easiest?

None, by design. All three boards assess the same Department for Education content with the same topic weightings, and grade boundaries are set so that a grade reflects the same standard of work. Ofqual says a higher boundary on one board means that paper was easier, not that the grade was harder to get.

Can I choose my GCSE maths exam board?

In school, the maths department chooses the board for the whole year group. What you can choose is how you prepare: practise with the papers and mark schemes of the board you will sit.

How many papers are there in GCSE maths?

Three on every board, each 1 hour 30 minutes, all at the same tier and in the same exam series.

How many marks is each GCSE maths paper?

AQA and Edexcel papers are out of 80 marks each. OCR papers are out of 100 marks each.

Which GCSE maths paper is non-calculator?

Paper 1 for AQA and Edexcel. For OCR, Paper 2 on Foundation tier and Paper 5 on Higher tier.

Are the grade boundaries the same for every board?

No. Boundaries are set separately for each paper after marking, and they usually differ between boards and between years because they reflect how hard each paper turned out to be.

Is the content different between AQA, Edexcel and OCR?

No. Every board covers the same national subject content. The differences are in paper layout, numbering, marks per paper and question style.

Can I resit GCSE maths in November with any board?

All three boards run a November series for GCSE maths, but only for students who were at least 16 on the relevant 31 August. See our [GCSE maths resit guide](/gcse-maths-resit-explained).

Do all boards give a formulae sheet?

Yes. Ofqual requires a formulae sheet in GCSE maths exams on every board, and decided in 2026 that this continues for the remaining lifetime of the current specifications.
