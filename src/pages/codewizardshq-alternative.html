CodeWizardsHQ alternative: what each one offers

CodeWizardsHQ is a US online coding school built around one idea: a structured, multi-year track where each 12-week course leads to the next. Many families who search for an alternative are not unhappy with it; they want to check whether a different structure, schedule or group size suits their child better. This page sets out what CodeWizardsHQ publishes about itself, read on its own site on 17 October 2026, places our own offer beside it in plain terms, and leaves the decision with you.

What a fixed sequence gives a child

A child always knows the next step, and each course assumes the one before. CodeWizardsHQ describes its approach as a complete path rather than disconnected courses, and its middle school track moves from Python to JavaScript, HTML and CSS, then databases and APIs.

Students with experience are not forced to start at the bottom: the FAQs describe a placement exam for matching to the right course.

What a fixed sequence asks of a family

Commitment. The FAQs say a Wizard Level of four courses, including the Capstone, takes approximately one year. That suits families planning years ahead and fits less well with a child who wants one topic now, such as Roblox or an AP exam in May.

For short, single-topic goals CodeWizardsHQ also lists summer camps and three-week accelerated tracks.

What the figure leaves out

The 44 hours count scheduled class time only. CodeWizardsHQ also offers daily homework help, recordings and, in advanced courses, office hours, so a motivated student can spend far more time with an instructor than the timetable shows.

It also leaves out homework itself, which the FAQs describe as part of each course.

Why do the sum at all

Because "a year-long level" and "44 hours of class" describe the same thing differently, and it helps to see both before comparing any provider. Ask every school you shortlist, including us, for the live minutes per month.

The arithmetic is also a good habit to teach a child: turn a marketing phrase into units you can compare.

Families who often prefer a fixed track

Those who want the decisions made for them for several years, with the same small group moving together, and who value US-based teachers.

CodeWizardsHQ’s recorded classes and drop-in homework help also suit a child who likes to review at their own pace between sessions.

Families who often look elsewhere

Those with one clear goal this term, such as an AP exam, a Roblox game or catching up in maths, or who want coding and maths taught by one school.

Also families who want month-to-month flexibility, or a private teacher whose times move with the family calendar.

One difference deserves a plain word. CodeWizardsHQ states that every one of its instructors is based in the United States; our teachers are in India and teach US students live over video, with class times set in your local time zone. Some families care a great deal about this and some not at all. The only reliable test is a lesson: listen to how clearly the teacher explains, how quickly they spot a mistake in your child’s code, and whether your child leaves the class wanting to build something else. Group size is the other real difference. Four to six students in every class is a defining feature of CodeWizardsHQ, while our default batches are larger, with a mini batch or a private teacher available when a child needs closer attention.

Choosing

A placement walk-through: a 12-year-old with some Scratch

Our worked example of how a family might test both options for the same child, using each school’s published route.

  1. Check the starting course. CodeWizardsHQ’s middle school track begins with Intro to Programming with Python; its FAQ offers a placement exam for experienced students.
  2. Decide whether Scratch counts as experience. CodeWizardsHQ’s FAQ describes its Scratch course as structured and different from casual use, so a child may still start there or at Python.
  3. Book comparable trials. The first four CodeWizardsHQ sessions fall under its money-back guarantee; our first lesson is free. Test the same topic, Python, in both.
  4. Compare the hour, not the brochure. After each, ask your child what they built, how often they asked for help and whether they want to go back.
  5. Check the calendar fit. A 12-week course and a monthly plan suit different families; write down your term dates before deciding.

Questions from families

CodeWizardsHQ and alternatives: common questions

What is CodeWizardsHQ?

A US online coding school for ages 7 to 18 that teaches live classes of four to six students in 12-week courses, grouped into Wizard Levels, according to its own website read on 17 October 2026.

How long is a CodeWizardsHQ course?

Its FAQs say each regular course is 12 weeks long with 55-minute sessions, and that a Wizard Level of four courses takes approximately one year. Accelerated versions run over six or three weeks.

Does CodeWizardsHQ offer a free trial?

Its site says it offers a money-back guarantee over the first four sessions instead of a free trial class, and that the guarantee does not apply to summer camps.

Are CodeWizardsHQ classes recorded?

Yes. Its site says all classes are recorded and the video and slides are made available after each session.

How is Modern Age Coders different?

We teach ages 6 to 67, in batches of 15 to 20, mini batches of up to 6 or 1-on-1, with monthly billing, teachers working live from India, and maths as well as coding. CodeWizardsHQ uses smaller fixed groups, US-based instructors and multi-course tracks.

Which is better for my child?

That depends on your child and your calendar, not on either school. A child who thrives in a small, steady group on a long track may prefer one; a child with a single goal or a moving schedule may prefer the other. Try both.

Can my child switch between the two?

Yes, families do. The easiest time is at the end of a course. Bring your child's recent projects so either school can place them quickly.

When are your classes?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays.

Is Modern Age Coders part of CodeWizardsHQ?

No. We are a separate, independent school. This page reports CodeWizardsHQ's published information and links to its site.
