Coding and AI classes in Calgary

Can a Calgary student learn coding and AI live with a real teacher, online?

Yes. Modern Age Coders runs live online coding, Python and AI classes for Calgary learners from age 6 to adult, taught one to one or in a class of fifteen to twenty. Young children usually begin with block coding, teenagers with Python for Teens, and Grade 12 students add our Pre-calculus 12 and Math 30-1 course. The first class is free.

For a Calgary student in a Grade 12 diploma course, the course ends at a desk with an Alberta diploma exam, and in Mathematics 30-1 that one sitting is worth 30% of the final mark. Long before then, Alberta's newer elementary math curriculum, brought in for kindergarten to Grade 3 in September 2022 and for Grades 4 to 6 in the 2023 to 2024 school year, has been asking children to reason step by step. Coding is one of the clearest ways to practise that, because a program does exactly what it is told, and a student learns to count, check and explain before trusting an answer. This page sets out the Alberta dates a Calgary family will meet in 2026 to 2027, the CEMC contests open to Calgary students, a project our students run on the City of Calgary playground equipment file, and why Calgary's single all-year clock makes live classes easy to schedule.

Calgary schools follow Alberta's curriculum and its provincial exams. We read the government pages and bulletins below on 22 October 2026; short quotations are Alberta's, the rest is our summary.

Seventy and thirty

Alberta says diploma exam weighting returned to 30% from 1 September 2023: 70% of the final mark comes from course work and 30% from the exam. To pass the course, the combined mark has to reach at least 50%.

January 2026 was cancelled

Alberta cancelled its January 2026 diploma exams and Grade 9 Provincial Achievement Tests. A Calgary student planning around a January sitting should check the current bulletin rather than last year's habits.

On a screen, not on paper

Alberta now has students write diploma exams and achievement tests digitally rather than with pencil and paper. Typing, reading carefully on a screen and checking work there are skills a coding class practises every week.

Below Grade 12, the math curriculum itself has changed. Alberta's renewal timeline shows the new elementary math arriving in two steps: kindergarten to Grade 3 in September 2022, then Grades 4 to 6 in the 2023 to 2024 school year. A Calgary child in elementary school today is learning from that newer program. Our guide to Alberta's new math curriculum sets out what changed, and families with a Grade 12 student can read about Math 30-1 diploma preparation and choosing between Math 30-1 and 30-2.

Calgary data project

Rows are not swings: counting Calgary's playground equipment properly

The City of Calgary publishes a list of playground equipment that Calgary Parks maintains or that sits on land in its care, updated weekly. Our students use it to learn the difference between counting rows and counting things. All figures below are ours, worked out from the copy we downloaded on 22 October 2026.

A child asked how many swings Calgary has will read 1,225 off that table. That is the number of swing rows, and a swing frame can hold several seats. The file has columns for that, COUNT_SR_SWING for standard seats and COUNT_BABY_SWING for baby seats, and adding them up gives a different and more interesting answer. This short program counts the types, sums the seats and runs one more check. Nothing beyond the standard library is needed, and it looks for the CSV in its own folder:import csv from collections import Counter by_label = Counter() seats = Counter() blank_baby = 0 labels_for_code = {} with open("playground-equipment.csv", encoding="utf-8-sig") as equipment: for row in csv.DictReader(equipment): label = row["TYPE_DESCRIPTION"] by_label[label] += 1 labels_for_code.setdefault(row["ASSET_TYPE"], set()).add(label) if label == "SWING": for column in ("COUNT_SR_SWING", "COUNT_BABY_SWING"): if row[column].strip(): seats[column] += int(row[column]) if not row["COUNT_BABY_SWING"].strip(): blank_baby += 1 for label, n in by_label.most_common(8): print(label, n) print(dict(seats), "| SWING rows with no baby-seat count:", blank_baby) for code in sorted(labels_for_code, key=int): if len(labels_for_code[code]) != 1: print("code", code, "has labels", sorted(labels_for_code[code]))

1. Count rows

A Counter tallies TYPE_DESCRIPTION once per row. It answers how many pieces of each type the city lists, which is a fair question with a clear answer: 1,225 swings, 1,116 multi play complexes, 684 spring toys.

2. Sum quantities

On the swing rows, the seat columns add up to 1,356 standard seats and 1,162 baby seats. But 176 swing rows leave the baby-seat count blank, so 1,162 is a floor, not a total: the true number can only be higher.

3. Check codes against labels

Each type also has a numeric code in ASSET_TYPE. Code 1 should always mean SWING, yet one code-1 row says CLIMBER; code 5 carries SPINNING TOY, KIDS_TABLE and one SPRING TOY. Those few rows are worth a polite email to the city, not a guess.

Where the file stops

The file lists equipment, not playgrounds, and it does not tell us how many separate playgrounds there are, how old each piece is or what state it is in. SENIOR and JUNIOR are the city's own age classes for equipment; we do not guess the ages behind them. Saying exactly what a count means is the part of the lesson that lasts.

Ask the school

CEMC does not take individual registrations: in its words, all students must participate through their school. A Calgary student whose school does not order a contest can ask another local school to let them write there.

Younger writers welcome

The Gauss contest is set for Grades 7 and 8, but CEMC adds that motivated students in lower grades are welcome. A strong Grade 6 coder often enjoys it as a first taste.

Free practice

CEMC posts ten years of past papers free, with no account needed. We send students to CEMC's archive to practise and teach with problems of our own, never CEMC's.

The CCC Junior ladder starts where the playground project starts: a loop over input, a condition, a count. By the later Junior questions CEMC expects counting combined with loops and conditions, then recursion and two-dimensional arrays. Our CCC preparation page describes how we build up to it, and the Waterloo math contest page covers Gauss and the high school contests. CEMC and the University of Waterloo have no link with us.

Class times

One clock all year: Calgary time and India time

Alberta's Official Time Act fixes official time at 6 hours behind Coordinated Universal Time, with no spring or autumn change. That makes Calgary one of the easiest cities to schedule from India, where our teachers are.

No change in November

Eastern Canada changes its clocks on 1 November 2026 and again in mid-March 2027; Calgary does not. A Calgary class keeps the same Calgary hour all year, and only its Eastern equivalent shifts.

Neighbours on the map

Saskatchewan also keeps UTC-6 all year, so Regina and Calgary read the same time. British Columbia now sits on UTC-7 all year, one hour behind Calgary in every month.

Coding and AI

An AI tool will count the swings. Will it say the count is a floor?

Not unless someone asks the right question.

Hand an AI assistant the Calgary playground file and ask how many swings there are, and it may report 1,225, the number of rows. Ask for seats and it may add the columns and report 1,162 baby seats as a total, without noticing the 176 blanks that make it a minimum. Both answers look confident. Only a student who has written the sum themselves knows which one to believe.

So we teach students to think through a problem before typing: what is being counted, what could be missing, and how to check. Older students use AI tools too, inside the age limits we keep to, as a way to test and extend their own reasoning, never as a replacement for it.

Alberta's diploma exams reward the same habit: read the question exactly, show the working, and check the answer against what was asked. The longer argument is in why we still think every student should learn to write code.

FAQ

Calgary coding and AI questions

Questions Calgary parents and students ask us most.

Are there coding classes for kids in Calgary with a live teacher?

Yes, online. From age 6, children learn with a live teacher over video, either in a class of fifteen to twenty or privately. There is no Calgary building to visit.

What does Alberta school expect in math before Grade 12?

Alberta brought in new elementary math in two steps, kindergarten to Grade 3 in September 2022 and Grades 4 to 6 in 2023 to 2024. In Grade 12, Mathematics 30-1 ends with a diploma exam worth 30% of the final mark, with course work making up the other 70%.

When are the 2026 to 2027 Math 30-1 diploma exams?

Alberta lists Tuesday 3 November 2026, Wednesday 20 January 2027, Thursday 8 April 2027 and Thursday 17 June 2027, each from 9 am to noon. Check the current bulletin before planning.

Do your classes count toward Alberta credits?

No. We do not grant Alberta credits or diploma marks. Our Math 30-1 and coding courses run alongside school, and all credits are earned there.

Do you have robotics classes in Calgary?

We teach the coding behind robotics, not the hardware: Python, logic and working with sensor data. A student who wants to build robots should join a school or community team as well.

Are there Python classes for Calgary teens?

Yes. Python for Teens starts at a student's first line of code and works up to reading files, writing functions and handling data; our AI and machine learning course follows on from it.

Which AI tools will my Calgary teen use in class?

Only tools suitable for their age. Tools with an adult-only rating, Claude among them, are never used with under-18 students. If a lesson involves ChatGPT, a teenager needs a parent's permission, and we get it before that lesson.

Does Calgary change its clocks for class times?

No. Alberta keeps UTC-6 all year under its Official Time Act, so a Calgary class stays at the same Calgary hour in every month.

When do classes run for Calgary students?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. We write every confirmed time in Calgary time.

Will a 403 or 587 number work in your form?

Yes. Calgary numbers with 403 and Alberta numbers with 587, 825 or 368 are all accepted; type the ten digits that follow the +1.

Where are the fees shown?

In the fees section further down: first the free lesson, then what a group place and private lessons cost per month, with the currency code printed beside each. Billing starts only after the free lesson.
