## Which Surrey off-leash park is the roundest? Area and perimeter from city coordinates

The City of Surrey maps its dog off-leash areas as polygons in its open data. Foundations 11 students compute each area and perimeter from the corner coordinates, then compare shapes with one ratio. All the numbers below are our calculations.

The layer is called Park Dog Off Leash Areas, and its summary reads simply: this dataset includes dog off-leash areas in Surrey. We queried it on 23 October 2026 and received 22 polygons, every one marked as operating. Each polygon is a list of corner points in metres, east and north, in the city&rsquo;s map projection, which is exactly what a student needs to work out area and perimeter from first principles.

**Selected Surrey off-leash areas: our shoelace areas and perimeters from the polygon corners, and our compactness ratio 4&pi;A/P&sup2; (1 for a circle, about 0.785 for a square). City of Surrey open data, queried 23 October 2026**

|---|---|---|---|
| Dogwood Park | 85,586.6 | 1,272.1 | 0.665 |
| Tynehead Park | 24,012.2 | 1,703.1 | 0.104 |
| East View Trail Park | 13,404.1 | 676.5 | 0.368 |
| Clayton Dog-Off-Leash Park | 12,222.9 | 454.4 | 0.744 |
| Bolivar Park | 5,592.2 | 289.2 | 0.840 |
| Nicomekl Off Leash Park, main area | 3,538.1 | 224.4 | 0.883 |
| Joe Brown Park, large dogs | 3,186.6 | 268.2 | 0.557 |
| Joe Brown Park, small dogs | 1,243.8 | 173.6 | 0.519 |

Across all 22 polygons our areas run from 622.2 to 85,586.6 square metres, with a median of 5,392.5, and they add up to 213,749 square metres, about 21.4 hectares. That total is our own sum of our own areas, not a City figure. Our shoelace results agree with the area the City stores for each polygon to within 0.0002 percent, a good check that the method and the units are right.

The shoelace formula adds up cross products of neighbouring corners and halves the result. This program applies it to every ring of every polygon and prints the area, perimeter and compactness:

```
import json, math

def signed_area(ring):
    total = 0
    for (x1, y1), (x2, y2) in zip(ring, ring[1:]):
        total += x1 * y2 - x2 * y1
    return total / 2

def perimeter(ring):
    return sum(math.dist(a, b) for a, b in zip(ring, ring[1:]))

with open("offleash_26910.json", encoding="utf-8") as f:
    areas = json.load(f)["features"]

for park in areas:
    rings = park["geometry"]["rings"]
    area = abs(sum(signed_area(r) for r in rings))
    edge = sum(perimeter(r) for r in rings)
    ratio = 4 * math.pi * area / edge ** 2
    print(park["attributes"]["DESCRIPTION"], round(area, 1), round(edge, 1), round(ratio, 3))
```

### Long and thin

Tynehead&rsquo;s area is a strip along the park, and its ratio of 0.104 says so. Its perimeter of 1,703.1 m is 2.75 times the perimeter of a square with the same area, which would need only 619.8 m of fence.

### Holes subtract

East View Trail Park is stored as three rings: an outer boundary of 14,383.4 square metres and two holes of 357.2 and 622.1. The signs in the shoelace sum handle this automatically, and the area left is 13,404.1. Students should explain why.

### Two fields, one park

Joe Brown Park has separate polygons for large dogs and small dogs, 3,186.6 and 1,243.8 square metres. Counting parks and counting polygons give different answers, and a good report says which one it means.

### What compactness does and does not say

A ratio near 1 means the shape encloses a lot of area for its edge; a ratio near 0 means a lot of edge. Nicomekl&rsquo;s main area, at 0.883, is rounder than a square; Tynehead, at 0.104, is a corridor. The ratio says nothing about how pleasant a park is for a dog. It answers one geometric question, and students are expected to keep it to that question.

## Worked problem: the most area from 240 metres of fence

Our own problem, at Foundations 11 level, answering the question the off-leash table raises: for a fixed amount of fence, which shape encloses most?

**Question.** A city crew has 240 m of fencing for a new off-leash area. (a) If the area is a rectangle, which width gives the largest area? (b) How much more would a circle enclose? (c) Bolivar Park&rsquo;s off-leash polygon has a perimeter of 289.2 m and an area of 5,592.2 square metres. Is it more or less efficient than a square with the same perimeter?

**Step 1, write the area as a function.** If the width is w, the length is 120 minus w, because two widths and two lengths make 240. The area is A(w) = w(120 &minus; w), a quadratic that opens downward with zeros at 0 and 120.

**Step 2, find the maximum.** The vertex sits halfway between the zeros, at w = 60, so the best rectangle is a 60 by 60 square with area 3,600 square metres. A table shows the symmetry: widths 20, 40, 60, 80 and 100 give 2,000, 3,200, 3,600, 3,200 and 2,000.

**Step 3, try a circle.** A circumference of 240 m gives a radius of 240 divided by 2&pi;, about 38.197 m, and an area of about 4,583.7 square metres, roughly 27 percent more than the square. Of all shapes with a given perimeter the circle encloses the most, which is why the compactness ratio is built so that a circle scores exactly 1.

**Step 4, judge Bolivar.** A square with a 289.2 m perimeter has sides of 72.3 m and an area of about 5,227.3 square metres. Bolivar&rsquo;s polygon encloses 5,592.2, more than that square, which matches its compactness of 0.840 being above a square&rsquo;s 0.785. A circle with the same perimeter would enclose about 6,655.6.

**Try it yourself.** (1) If one side of the rectangle is a river and needs no fence, what dimensions maximise the area now? (2) Find the compactness of a 30 by 120 rectangle. (3) Explain in two sentences why a long, thin park can still be a sensible design. We take answers in the free lesson.

## If an AI can compute the area, what is left for a Surrey student?

Choosing the right formula, checking the units and deciding what the number means.

An AI assistant will happily compute an area from a list of coordinates. Give it the East View Trail Park polygon and it may add the holes instead of subtracting them, or treat degrees as metres, and print a confident wrong answer. Only someone who understands why the shoelace formula carries signs will notice.

Our teachers ask Surrey students to estimate first, compute second and explain third. AI tools come in only for older students, within the age rules we follow, and only to check work the student has already done. School assignments that are marked stay entirely the student&rsquo;s work.

Foundations 11 lists counter-examples and proof among its first topics, which is a good description of what a student must do to an AI answer. The longer argument is in [why thinking skills matter in an AI world](/blog/is-coding-worth-learning-2026).
