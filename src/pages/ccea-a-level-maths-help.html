CCEA A level Maths help

In Northern Ireland, A level Mathematics is usually CCEA's GCE Mathematics, taken across Years 13 and 14 as four units. Two are sat at AS and count for 40 per cent; two follow at A2. The most practical advice about it comes from CCEA's own examiners, who reported in 2025 that many students revised from recent past papers and were caught out by topics not asked lately. This page sets out the units, the rules and that report, and how we teach CCEA maths.

At a glance

CCEA GCE Mathematics (subject code 2210) is the Northern Ireland A level in maths, first taught in September 2018. It has four externally examined units: AS 1 Pure Mathematics and AS 2 Applied Mathematics, together 40 per cent, and A2 1 Pure Mathematics and A2 2 Applied Mathematics, 60 per cent. Each applied unit is half mechanics, half statistics. Modern Age Coders teaches CCEA maths live online in the A Level Maths course, in a batch of 15 to 20 or 1-on-1, from a free first lesson.

The four units

What are the four CCEA A level Maths units?

AS 1 and A2 1 are pure; AS 2 and A2 2 split evenly between mechanics and statistics. Students answer every question on every paper.

CCEA describes the structure simply: "We have divided this course into four units: two units at AS level and two units at A2." It adds that "The full Advanced GCE award is based on students' marks from the AS (40 percent) and the A2 (60 percent)." The specification "assumes knowledge of Higher Tier GCSE Mathematics", and sets guided learning at 180 hours for AS and 360 for the A level.

What the applied units expect

The statistics in both applied units "should be taught through the use and interrogation of a large data set." CCEA does not name one in the specification; schools choose how to meet the requirement.

CCEA also says it "will not set questions that directly test students' ability to construct such tables and diagrams and calculate such measures", meaning box plots, stem-and-leaf and averages, but will expect students "to interpret and draw inferences from them."

What the A2 units add

A2 units include synoptic assessment, building on AS material and "bringing together and making connections" across the course.

CCEA also promises "more demanding unstructured questions" at A2. A2 1 is the longest paper, at 150 raw marks in two and a half hours.

2025 report

What did CCEA examiners say about A level Maths in 2025?

That students were well prepared in many ways, but leaned too heavily on recent past papers, under-showed working when using calculators, and missed topics not asked lately.

CCEA publishes a Chief Examiner's report after each summer series. The 2025 report for GCE Mathematics opens warmly, commending "diligent preparation", and then names its concerns. In the subject overview it says: "There was evidence of an over-reliance in using past examination papers for preparation for these examinations. This was particularly evident in questions requiring proofs and graphs." On A2 1 it is more specific still: many candidates "continue to heavily focus their revision on recent past papers", and "a significant number of candidates demonstrated little understanding in areas of the specification that have not been asked in recent years such as sketching reciprocal trigonometric functions and dealing with improper partial fractions."

Read together, these comments describe one habit and its cost. Past papers show what was asked; they do not show what can be asked. The specification is the list of what can be asked, so our scheme of work is built from its learning outcomes, and past papers are used for timing and style once the content is secure.

Resits and A*

Can you resit CCEA A level Maths units, and how is A* awarded?

Each AS and A2 unit can be resat once, with the better result counting. An A* needs an overall A and 90 per cent of the A2 uniform marks.

The specification says: "It is possible to resit individual AS and A2 assessment units once and count the better result for each unit towards an AS or A level qualification." Every unit is available "in summer each year", and unit results "can count towards a qualification until we withdraw the specification." Results are reported on a uniform mark scale reflecting each unit's weighting, and grades come from adding the uniform marks.

On the top grade CCEA states: "To be awarded an A*, candidates need to achieve a grade A on their full A level qualification and at least 90 percent of the maximum uniform marks available for the A2 units." AS is graded A to E; A level is graded A* to E, with U below E. As with any unitised A level, the A* condition sits on the A2 units, so a strong AS year is necessary for the A but cannot by itself deliver the A*.

  1. After AS results in August, compare each unit result with the grade you need for the A level as a whole.
  2. If a resit makes sense, remember it is the only one: each unit can be resat once.
  3. Plan the resit for the following summer alongside A2 1 and A2 2, which together carry 60 per cent.
  4. Give A2 1 the most time. At 36 per cent it is the single heaviest unit, and A* depends on it.
  5. Check subject combinations early if you are also taking Further Mathematics, as CCEA warns that universities may treat overlapping qualifications as one.

Calculator

Which calculator is allowed in CCEA A level Maths?

One with an iterative function and statistical distributions, and without a computer algebra system.

CCEA requires that calculators include "an iterative function" and "the ability to compute summary statistics and access probabilities from standard statistical distributions", and states: "Students must not have access to technology with a computer algebra system function during examinations." The assessment objectives are weighted the same on every unit: 50 per cent AO1 technique, 25 per cent AO2 reasoning, 25 per cent AO3 problem solving, each within a tolerance of plus or minus 3 per cent.

The 2025 report shows why the calculator rule and the working rule must be taught together. A calculator that solves a quadratic or evaluates a definite integral is allowed, but the front cover of every paper tells candidates to show the full development of their answers, and examiners noted many did not. Our rule in class is simple: if the calculator did a step, the line it replaced is still written down.

Worked examples, ours

Worked examples on the topics CCEA examiners flagged

A disguised quadratic, an improper partial fraction and a lift, three of the areas named in the 2025 report. All three problems are ours.

Our AS 1 style problem: a disguised quadratic. Solve 4x − 6(2x) + 8 = 0.

Spot the substitution

Write 4x as (2x)2 and let y = 2x. The equation becomes y2 − 6y + 8 = 0, which factorises as (y − 2)(y − 4) = 0.

Return to x

2x = 2 gives x = 1, and 2x = 4 gives x = 2. Taking logarithms of the original equation first leads nowhere, because the log of a sum does not split, which is the trap the examiners described.

Our A2 1 style problem: an improper partial fraction. Express (x2 + 3x + 5) ÷ ((x + 1)(x + 2)) in partial fractions. The numerator and denominator are both degree 2, so divide first: (x + 1)(x + 2) = x2 + 3x + 2, leaving 1 + 3 ÷ ((x + 1)(x + 2)). Then 3 ÷ ((x + 1)(x + 2)) = A/(x + 1) + B/(x + 2) with A = 3 and B = −3. The answer is 1 + 3/(x + 1) − 3/(x + 2). Forgetting the leading 1 is the usual error.

Our AS 2 style problem: a lift. A lift of mass 400 kg accelerates upwards at 0.5 m s−2. Take g = 9.8 m s−2. Draw the lift with tension T up and weight 400g down. Newton's second law upwards gives T − 400g = 400 × 0.5, so T = 400 × 10.3 = 4120 N. If a 60 kg passenger stands inside while the lift accelerates downwards at 1.2 m s−2, the floor's reaction is 60(9.8 − 1.2) = 516 N. The diagram, not the formula, is what makes an unfamiliar setting manageable.

CCEA maths questions

What students in Northern Ireland ask about CCEA A level Maths

How many papers are in CCEA A level Maths?

Four, one per unit: AS 1 Pure, AS 2 Applied, A2 1 Pure and A2 2 Applied. AS 2 and A2 2 each have a mechanics section and a statistics section.

How much is AS worth in CCEA A level Maths?

The AS units count for 40 per cent of the A level: AS 1 is 24 per cent and AS 2 is 16 per cent. A2 1 is 36 per cent and A2 2 is 24 per cent.

How long is the CCEA A2 1 Pure exam?

Two hours and thirty minutes, with 7 to 12 questions worth 150 raw marks. It is the longest and heaviest of the four units.

Can I resit a CCEA A level Maths unit?

Yes, once. CCEA says each AS and A2 unit can be resat once, and the better result counts towards the qualification.

How do you get an A* in CCEA A level Maths?

You need an overall grade A and at least 90 per cent of the maximum uniform marks for the two A2 units. The A2 condition is separate from the overall total.

Can I use past papers to revise for CCEA maths?

Yes, but not on their own. CCEA's 2025 examiners reported an over-reliance on past papers and weak answers on topics not asked recently, so revise from the specification's learning outcomes first.

Is a graphical calculator allowed for CCEA A level Maths?

CCEA requires an iterative function and statistical distributions and bans any technology with a computer algebra system function. Check the specific model against current exam regulations with your school.

Does CCEA A level Maths have a large data set?

The specification says the statistics in both applied units should be taught through a large data set. Questions test interpretation and inference rather than constructing tables or diagrams.

When are your CCEA maths classes?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. All lesson times are UK time.

Are you connected with CCEA?

No. Modern Age Coders is independent and has no link with CCEA. The problems and practice questions on this page are ours.
