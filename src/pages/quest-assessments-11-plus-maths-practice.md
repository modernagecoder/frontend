## Who are Quest Assessments?

A test company that designs entrance assessments to order for grammar schools and independent schools, and also sells classroom progress tests. It is part of Atom Learning, which runs a home-learning platform.

Quest's parent guidance page describes two sides to its admissions work. "Grammar schools use Quest Admissions assessments to identify pupils who are naturally suited to a grammar school environment", and "Quest Admissions is an exam provider used by UK independent schools." For both, the warning is the same: "Every school will use different papers and follow different structures for their assessments." The admissions page aimed at schools lists what Quest offers its customers, including tests that are "Bespoke and branded to your school", delivered on "Paper and online", for "Main entry and in-year" admissions and "For ages 7+ to 16+". It says Quest is "Trusted by 130+ schools".

### How a Quest test is made

Quest publishes its own five steps: it meets the school to discuss entry requirements, designs an assessment suite for the school to review, works with the school on the candidate experience, runs the test and processes results, and returns full reports on each candidate.

So when a family asks "what is on the Quest paper", the honest answer is "whatever your area agreed with Quest". The local page is the only reliable guide.

### Who to ask about a Quest test

Not Quest. Bexley Council states that "Quest Assessments cannot provide information directly to parents or respond to individual concerns"; questions go to the council's School Admissions Team.

The same pattern holds elsewhere: the school or consortium runs registration and results, and Quest supplies the papers.

## How do you fill in a Quest OMR answer sheet?

With one horizontal pencil line through the box beside the chosen answer, nothing else. Quest spells the rules out because a correct answer marked the wrong way may not be read.

Quest's guidance for parents explains that "Most 11+ papers use OMR (Optical Mark Recognition) answer sheets", so a child "won't write out answers" but will "draw a line through a box beside the answer they've chosen". It then lists what not to do: "Do not shade in the whole rectangle", "Do not tick the box" and "Do not put a cross through the box". Mistakes are rubbed out, and "Each question number must match the number on the answer sheet." The maths familiarisation booklet shows rows labelled A to E, the five-option layout children should expect.

**OMR habits we practise, from Quest's published rules (rules: Quest; drills: ours)**

| Quest's rule | The usual mistake | Our drill |
|---|---|---|
| A single horizontal line through the box | Shading the box fully, as on some school worksheets | Ten rows marked against a timer, checked for line shape, before any maths |
| Rub out and re-mark if you change your mind | Two lines on one row, so neither counts | Practise erasing cleanly with a soft eraser, not crossing out |
| Question number must match the sheet | Skipping a hard question in the booklet but not on the sheet | Leave a small dot on the row you skip; check numbers every five questions |
| Some questions ask for more than one answer | Marking the first correct option and moving on | Read the stem twice; count how many answers it asks for before marking |

### "Select more than one" questions

Quest's parents' guide warns that children "might be asked to select more than one answer - remind your child to always double check what the question asks them to do". It is the single biggest difference between a Quest paper and the older one-answer formats.

In Quest's own maths familiarisation booklet, one of the 20 sample questions has four correct options in the answer key. We do not reproduce it; the booklet is free from Quest.

### Use the free booklets first

Quest publishes free familiarisation booklets for English, maths, verbal reasoning and non-verbal reasoning, each with a sample OMR sheet. The maths booklet says of its questions: "These questions will not appear in your real assessment."

Children from families who qualify for the government's extra school funding for disadvantaged pupils can also ask for a free Atom Home account through Quest's parent guidance page, once their primary school confirms eligibility.

## How should a child tackle a Quest-style maths question?

Three original Year 5 problems, two of them in a select-every-correct-option style. None comes from a Quest booklet or paper. Watch the checking, not just the answer.

### 1. Every option, every time

Which of these are multiples of both 3 and 4: 12, 18, 24, 30, 36? A number divisible by both 3 and 4 is divisible by 12, so test each against 12. 12 yes, 18 no, 24 yes, 30 no, 36 yes. Three answers: 12, 24 and 36. A child who marks 12 and moves on loses the question, because the stem never said "which one".

### 2. Equivalent, or only close?

Which of these equal 0.75: three quarters, six eighths, nine twelfths, seven ninths, 75 hundredths? Simplify each. Six eighths and nine twelfths both reduce to three quarters; 75 hundredths is 0.75 by place value. Seven ninths is 0.777..., close but not equal. Four answers. Simplifying first is quicker than turning every fraction into a decimal.

### 3. Same units first

A ribbon 3.6 m long is cut into pieces of 45 cm. How many pieces? Convert first: 3.6 m is 360 cm. Then 360 divided by 45: 45 times 8 is 360, so 8 pieces. Dividing 3.6 by 45 gives nonsense, and that wrong number often appears among the options. Units before arithmetic is the rule.

**A practice set written by Modern Age Coders, at Year 5 level**

| Question (ours) | Answer | The reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Which of these are prime: 9, 11, 15, 17, 21, 23? | 11, 17 and 23 | Check each for a factor other than 1 and itself: 9 is 3 times 3, 15 is 3 times 5, 21 is 3 times 7. Three primes, so three marks on the sheet. |
| Write MCMXC as a number. | 1990 | M is 1000, CM is 900, XC is 90. Roman numerals to 1000 are on the Year 5 curriculum and easy marks if practised. |
| Round 46,350 to the nearest thousand. | 46,000 | Look only at the hundreds digit, 3, which is under 5, so round down. The tens and ones do not matter. |
| An L-shape is a 10 cm by 6 cm rectangle with a 4 cm by 3 cm corner removed. What is its area? | 48 square centimetres | Whole rectangle 60, minus the missing 12. Subtracting a missing piece is faster than splitting the L into two rectangles. |
| Which of these are square numbers: 16, 24, 36, 50, 64? | 16, 36 and 64 | 4 squared, 6 squared and 8 squared. 24 and 50 sit between squares. Another multi-answer row. |
| A bag of flour holds 1.5 kg. How many 125 g scoops does it hold? | 12 | 1.5 kg is 1500 g, and 125 goes into 1000 eight times and into 500 four times. |

Half of this practice set has more than one correct option. That is deliberate: in our experience the costly error on a multi-answer paper is not a wrong sum but a stopped search. Children learn to count how many answers a stem allows, check every option against the condition, and only then draw their lines on the sheet.

## Does Quest say a child needs tutoring?

Quest's own guidance puts school learning first and warns against "excessive tutoring". We agree, and keep our offer to the maths and the format.

### In Quest's words

The parents' guide says: "The best preparation is steady, meaningful learning over time" and "not last-minute cramming or excessive tutoring." It suggests practising maths "in fun, everyday ways", such as cooking and shopping, with "small, regular sessions rather than long hours".

Area pages written with Quest repeat the theme: a strong grounding in school learning, wide reading, and the ability to apply knowledge to new contexts.

### Where teaching still earns its place

A child who reaches the summer of Year 5 unsure of fractions, measures or multi-step problems is missing things school expects, test or no test. Fixing that is useful for Year 7 as much as for September.

So we teach Year 5 maths properly, add the OMR and multi-answer habits, and stop. We do not claim knowledge of any Quest paper, and we do not sell "Quest papers".

1. Find your area's or school's Quest page and note the papers, timings and which paper holds the maths.
2. Download Quest's free maths familiarisation booklet and the OMR sample, and work through them slowly, once.
3. Secure the Year 5 curriculum: place value to a million, Roman numerals to 1000, fractions and decimals, area and perimeter, unit conversion.
4. Practise multi-answer questions and line-marking on short sets of ten, not full papers.
5. Only in the final weeks, sit timed sections in your area's exact layout.

## What parents ask about the Quest 11 plus

### What is the Quest 11 plus exam?

It is an entrance test written by Quest Assessments for a particular grammar school area or independent school. Quest says every school uses different papers and structures, so the Quest test in Bexley is not the same as the one in Dorset.

### Who owns Quest Assessments?

Quest says it is part of the education company Atom Learning. It provides entrance tests for grammar and independent schools and separate progress tests for classrooms.

### What maths is in a Quest 11 plus?

Quest's parents' guide says the test covers what children have learned in school up to the end of Year 5, with maths that may include problem solving, arithmetic and reasoning. There is no need to learn Year 6 topics.

### How long is the Quest maths paper?

It depends on the area. Dorset's consortium sets three 40-minute papers with maths first; Bexley and Wirral set two papers of about 50 minutes, with maths sharing paper two with non-verbal reasoning.

### How do you fill in a Quest answer sheet?

Draw one horizontal pencil line through the box beside your answer. Quest says not to shade the whole rectangle, tick it or cross it, and to rub out cleanly if you change your mind.

### Can a Quest question have more than one right answer?

Yes. Quest's guidance says children might be asked to select more than one answer, so they should check what each question asks before marking the sheet.

### Where can I find Quest practice papers?

Quest publishes free familiarisation booklets for maths, English, verbal and non-verbal reasoning on its parent guidance page, with a sample OMR sheet. It says those questions will not appear in the real test.

### Is Quest the same as FSCE?

No. FSCE, Future Stories Community Enterprise, is a separate provider whose tests are written by grammar schools for grammar schools. Gloucestershire's grammar schools will use FSCE for the 2028 intake.

### When can my child have lessons?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Every time is confirmed in UK time.

### Do you have real Quest papers?

No, and nobody outside the schools should. We teach Year 5 maths and the Quest answer format using questions we write ourselves, and we never promise a score or a place.
