Every market we teach in gets a first project built on its own data. In Muscat that project is the airport. Muscat International sits in the Wilayat of Al Seeb, the same wilayat the Royal Decree chose for the AI zone, and in 2025 it handled 13,157,559 of Oman's 14,939,209 air passengers, 88.1 per cent of the national total. ### The finding the model has to surface

International flights at Muscat fell 4.5 per cent, from 86,797 to 82,913, while international passengers rose 1.3 per cent, from 11,681,790 to 11,838,223. Passengers per flight therefore moved from 134.59 to 142.78. The headline says growth. The ratio says consolidation: fewer aircraft, fuller aircraft. A student who reads only the passenger column reports the wrong story, and reports it confidently.

The same derived column ranks the whole 2025 frame: Salalah domestic 164.37, Muscat international 142.78, Salalah international 138.88, Muscat domestic 137.34, Sohar domestic 121.65, Duqm domestic 98.53, Sohar international 3.55. That last figure is 390 passengers on 110 flights, which is not a commercial schedule, and any regression fitted with the row left in is dragged by something that does not describe scheduled air travel at all.

The same article prints a first quarter total of 2,857,817 against 2,929,495 a year earlier, a fall of 2.4 per cent. The three monthly figures as printed add to 2,858,447, which is 630 more. We do not tell students where the gap comes from, because we do not know. We tell them what the page prints and make them write the difference down instead of quietly choosing the number they prefer.

Three rows cannot carry a regression, and the class is told so plainly. What three rows can carry is the first habit of anyone who works with data for a living: add the parts, compare them with the whole, report the difference.

1. #### Prove the dataset before trusting it

  Sum the seven 2025 rows and confirm 14,939,209 passengers and 104,510 flights against the published totals. Then recover the three 2024 cells that are printed nowhere: Sohar domestic passengers of 18,247 are described as a fall of 21.8 per cent, giving 23,331, which is exactly the residual against 14,537,674. Nothing is modelled until the recovery closes to the digit.
2. #### Plot before fitting

  A scatter of flights against passengers, coloured by airport, with segment as the marker shape. Six points suggest a line. Sohar international lands nowhere near it. The outlier is seen before a model quietly averages it away.
3. #### Add the derived column, then decide

  Passengers per flight is one line of pandas and it changes the story. The student then decides, in writing, whether Sohar international belongs in the training set, and defends the decision either way. Both answers are acceptable. Not deciding is not.
4. #### Split honestly, and find out why that is hard here

  Rows are held back before anything is fitted. Fourteen rows makes this uncomfortable, and the discomfort is the lesson: one random split of eighty and twenty would be meaningless at this size, so leave one out cross validation is the honest choice.
5. #### Fit the regression and read the coefficient out loud

  A linear regression of passengers on flights, with airport and segment one hot encoded. The flights coefficient is stated in extra passengers per extra flight. Then the hard question: can the model reproduce what happened at Muscat, where flights fell and passengers rose?
6. #### Measure the error in passengers, not in R squared

  Mean absolute error in passengers is a number a parent who flies out of Muscat can judge. Report it in passengers and as a percentage of its own row, then refit without the Sohar international row to see how much of the error was one bad row.
7. #### Write the limitations paragraph and publish it

  Fourteen annual rows. One airport carrying 88.1 per cent of the traffic. A 2026 shock the model never saw. A 630 passenger discrepancy in the source. All of it goes in the write up, published at a link with arithmetic anyone in Muscat can re-run, which is worth more in an application than a certificate.

One question is left deliberately open. In May 2026 Indian nationals were the largest passenger group at Oman's airports, 175,022 movements made of 76,515 arrivals and 98,507 departures. Does that departure surplus line up with schools closing on 31 May, with Eid travel, or with none of them? Published reporting attributes the 2026 decline to regional tension in aviation and says nothing about schools. The student has to argue it from the data and then state what the data cannot settle, which is the hardest sentence in the course to write.

The distinction

## Operating a model and creating one are not the same lesson

We teach both, in that order, and we tell parents which of the two holds its value.

#### Using AI tools

- Type a prompt, read the reply, reword it, try again
- Learn the menus of whichever product is fashionable this year
- Success means an answer that looks plausible on screen
- Nothing transfers when the tool is retired or repriced
- Worth a few weeks of anyone's time, and no more
- We teach it properly, including how to catch a confident wrong answer

#### Building AI

- Find the data, prove it adds up, defend every choice made about it
- Pick a model, and know what it assumes on your behalf
- Train it, then state the error in units a non specialist can check
- Work out whether the failure is the data, the features or the model
- Put it in front of real inputs and watch it degrade
- Built on statistics, linear algebra and code, which stay put

The distinction is sharper in Muscat than in most cities, because the governorate is building AI capacity rather than only buying it. What follows is a decree zoning land, seven data centres, a research centre, an open data platform and a national language model. Not one of them is staffed by a generation trained only to prompt.

## A Royal Decree put an AI zone in Al Seeb, and the rest of the city is already wired for it

Royal Decree No. 50/2026, issued on 30 April 2026 by His Majesty Sultan Haitham bin Tarik, establishes an Artificial Intelligence Special Zone in the Governorate of Muscat, in the Wilayat of Al Seeb, on approximately 104,000 square metres. The Board of Directors of the Public Authority for Special Economic Zones and Free Zones appoints the party that will manage, operate and develop it, in coordination with the Ministry of Transport, Communications and Information Technology. Reported target industries are semiconductors, robotics, integrated AI systems and software development, with special economic zone incentives, exemptions and a lighter regulatory setup. It sits inside the Eleventh Five Year Development Plan for 2026 to 2030 and Oman Vision 2040. The National reported combined chip sector investment of RO 14.63 million, USD 38 million, involving GSME, Lumotive and Movandi.

Above it sits the national frame. On 22 September 2024 His Excellency Said bin Hamoud al Maawali, Minister of Transport, Communications and Information Technology, announced the National Programme for Artificial Intelligence and Advanced Digital Technologies, running to the end of 2026: a National Open Data Platform, a National Centre for AI Research and Development, an AI Studio, an Omani national language model, a Centre for the Fourth Industrial Revolution with the Ministry of Economy and the World Economic Forum, and quantum computing work. The stated targets are to raise the digital economy from 2 per cent of GDP in 2021 to 10 per cent by 2040, and to enter the top 50 of the Oxford Insights Government AI Readiness Index.

The skills pipeline underneath it is measured rather than promised. At the five year Makeen forum on 27 April 2026 the ministry reported more than 11,000 trainees since September 2022, 139 programmes across more than 30 technology fields, a 50 per cent employment rate in the technology bootcamp track, 2,790 Omanis supported into jobs, IT sector Omanisation raised to 45.5 per cent, and 150 or more Omani semiconductor engineers behind five start-ups. The next phase targets 10,000 jobseekers over five years, with new programmes in AI agents and quantum computing.

The work is already here, and it is infrastructure rather than prompting. Otech, the technology arm of Omantel launched on 11 February 2026 and reported as the first company in the Middle East accredited by Amazon Web Services for sovereign cloud, runs seven Tier 3 certified data centres with a combined 12 megawatts across Muscat and Duqm, serving about 1,200 customers. Its GPU workloads answer the question a teenager should be asking, which is what would I actually do here: healthcare imaging, computer vision and seismic analysis. Knowledge Oasis Muscat at Al Rusayl is a one million square metre free zone whose tenants include Ericsson, Oracle, Microsoft and Huawei, with Middle East College on the same campus. Byanat, founded in Muscat in April 2022 by Ahmed Alghadani and Dr Ahmed Albadi, builds AI observability for telecom operators and data centres. Bank Muscat and Bank Dhofar are reported to analyse suspicious transactions with algorithms in real time.

Read that as a parent in Bousher and one pattern stands out: every item is a building project. A child who is twelve today is twenty two in 2036, four years short of the Vision 2040 horizon, applying for exactly these roles. That is why we will not sell a prompt engineering course as an AI education.

## Timed to a Sunday to Thursday week, which is the part most providers get wrong

Muscat runs on Gulf Standard Time, UTC+4, with no daylight saving at any point in the year, exactly one hour and thirty minutes behind India. The clock is the easy half. The week is the half that catches people out: the Omani working and school week runs Sunday to Thursday and the weekend is Friday and Saturday, so a Muscat student's free evenings are Sunday through Thursday and their free mornings are Friday and Saturday.

### What a session actually is

Ninety minutes on live video with one teacher who already knows where your child stopped last week. Screens are shared both ways, so the teacher watches the code being written and interrupts at the moment the mistake happens rather than marking it three days later. Batches are five to eight students, or one to one.

The week in between carries a build, not a worksheet. Make it, break it, bring it back. Questions during the week go to the same teacher. Timings shift around Ramadan and the Eid holidays rather than running through them.

### The Muscat school year, and when to start

Muscat's Indian schools run an April to March year rather than August to June. Indian School Darsait's Calendar of Events for 2026 to 2027 opens on 6 April 2026, begins summer vacation on 31 May, and reopens on 12 July for Classes IX to XII and 20 July for the younger classes under a Board circular issued 31 December 2025. Renaissance Day falls on 23 July, half yearly examinations begin 20 and 21 September, the second term opens 6 October, National Day holidays fall on 25 and 26 November, winter break runs 20 to 31 December, school reopens 3 January 2027, annual examinations run through February, and the next academic year begins 4 April 2027. Several examination windows are printed as tentative in the calendar itself.

Two moments in that year are the natural ones to start something new: early April and the middle of July. Two are best avoided: the June travel exodus and the annual examinations. Outside those, admission is rolling.
