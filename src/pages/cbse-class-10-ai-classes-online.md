## The 417 marks ledger for 2026-27 (from the CBSE curriculum)

Theory, 50 marks: Part A employability skills, five units at 2 marks each (10); Part B Unit 1 Revisiting AI Project Cycle and Ethical Frameworks for AI (7); Unit 2 Advanced Concepts of Modeling in AI (11); Unit 3 Evaluating Models (10); Unit 5 Computer Vision (4); Unit 6 Natural Language Processing (8); Units 4 Statistical Data and 7 Advance Python carry practical hours and no theory marks.

Practical, 50 marks: practical file with a minimum of 15 programs (15); practical examination on Units 4 to 7 (15); viva voce (5); project work, field visit or student portfolio (10); viva voce on the project (5).

Three consequences shape the batch: half the marks are things a student must do in Python in front of an examiner; the modelling and evaluation units carry 21 theory marks and are easiest to understand and hardest to memorise; and Part A is ten marks nobody revises.

## What the examiner tests in each unit

Project cycle and ethics: scoping an unfamiliar problem and spotting the ethical trap. Modeling: supervised versus unsupervised, classification versus regression, a model as a rule learned from examples. Evaluating models: precision, recall, accuracy, the confusion matrix and which one matters when a false alarm costs less than a miss. Statistical data: reading, cleaning, describing and plotting a dataset in Python. Computer vision: images as numbers, pixels, channels, what a convolution does. NLP: tokens, stop words, bag of words. Advance Python: Jupyter, packages, the Python that carries the practical marks. Part A: ten short answers worth two marks each.

## Why understanding beats memorising in the practical exam

Asked for the mean, median and mode of a marks list, the memorised answer copies the file program and freezes when the examiner says "now for marks above 70 only". The understood answer adds one list comprehension, answers "and below 70" by changing one symbol, and explains why the mode is now undefined, earning the viva mark. Same syllabus, same library, one extra line.
