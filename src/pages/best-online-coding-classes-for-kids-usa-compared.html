What the table already tells you

The six are not really the same product. Two are built around small live groups, one around private lessons and summer camps, one around self-paced video, one mixes online with in-person classes, and one around larger live batches with smaller and private options.

So the first question is not "which is best" but "which format suits my child": a group to learn alongside, a private teacher, or the freedom to work at any hour.

What it cannot tell you

Group size on paper is not attention in practice. A class of five with a distracted teacher can give less help than a class of fifteen with a sharp one, and the reverse is just as possible.

Use the table to shortlist, then use a trial lesson to judge. The trials section below sets out what each provider publishes about trying first.

Live teaching

Five of the six are mainly live. CodeWizardsHQ says students spend 90% of class time coding, and that its classes are not pre-recorded videos or self-paced tutorials. Coding with Kids says its instruction is live only.

A live class gives a child someone to ask at the moment they are stuck, and a fixed time that keeps the habit going. The cost is less flexibility: the class happens when it happens.

Self-paced learning

CodaKid’s main program is the opposite model: more than 115 self-study courses with video lessons, 24/7 AI-powered learning support and scheduled sessions with a real teacher. A child can work at midnight or for ten minutes before school.

Self-paced suits a self-starting child and a busy calendar. It asks more of the child’s own motivation, which is worth being honest about before you subscribe.

A note on teachers: three providers stress that their instructors are based in the United States. Ours are not; our teachers work live from India and schedule around US school days and time zones. Whether that matters is a family choice, and a free first lesson is the simplest way to judge a teacher for yourself.

  1. Book the trial in the format you would actually buy. A free group intro tells you little about private lessons, and the other way round.
  2. Watch, do not just listen. Sit nearby for the first ten minutes and notice whether your child is writing code or watching someone else do it.
  3. Count the questions. After the trial, ask your child how many times they asked for help and what happened.
  4. Ask what happens next. What level would they start at, how is progress reported, and what if a class is missed?
  5. Read the renewal terms. Note when a card is first charged and how to cancel, before you give it.

Score it yourself

A weighted score in Python, on made-up options

Our worked example of a simple decision method. It uses three invented options, not real companies, because the weights are yours to set, not ours.

Why the method works

Each criterion is multiplied by how much you care about it, then added up. Something an option offers but you do not value adds nothing, which stops a long feature list from looking better than it is.

It is the same idea as a weighted grade: homework worth 20%, tests worth 50%. Children who have met weighted averages in school can follow it line by line.

Try it with your own facts

Copy the facts from the tables above into the options dictionary, set your own weights, and run it. Then change one weight and see whether the answer moves.

If the top two are close, the method has done its job: it has told you to let the trial lessons decide.

Where your child is

Match the format to the stage, not to the brand

The same child often needs different formats at different stages.

For parents

Switching format is not failure. Many children start in a group and move to private lessons for one hard term, or the other way round.

Keep the projects your child makes; they travel between providers.

For kids

Tell your parents honestly whether you liked the class or just the game you made in it. Both answers are useful.

If you are stuck more than you are building, ask for a different format.

Comparing online coding classes: common questions

What is the best online coding class for kids in the USA?

It depends on the format your child needs. Small live groups, private lessons, self-paced courses and in-person classes all have strong providers. Shortlist by format, then use trial lessons to choose.

Which providers teach live rather than by video?

From their own sites, CodeWizardsHQ, Create & Learn, Coding with Kids and Modern Age Coders teach live classes, and iD Tech teaches live lessons and camps. CodaKid's main program is self-paced video, with live private lessons and camps as well.

Which has the smallest groups?

Of the providers that publish a number, Create & Learn states three to five students and CodeWizardsHQ four to six. Modern Age Coders offers mini batches of up to six as well as larger batches and 1-on-1.

Which providers offer 1-on-1 lessons?

Five list private lessons on their sites: iD Tech, CodaKid, Create & Learn, Coding with Kids and Modern Age Coders. CodeWizardsHQ offers 1-on-1 office hours with the instructor within its advanced courses rather than a separate private-lesson program.

Are the teachers based in the US?

CodeWizardsHQ, Create & Learn and iD Tech say their instructors are US-based. Modern Age Coders teachers work live from India. The others do not state a location on the pages we read.

Why does this page not compare prices?

Prices change often and differ by format, so we link to each provider instead. Our own fees are shown in US dollars in the fees section.

Is there an in-person option?

Coding with Kids lists in-person classes in several US regions, and iD Tech runs summer camps on university campuses. The others compared here are online.

When are your classes?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays.

Are you connected with any of these companies?

No. Modern Age Coders is independent of CodeWizardsHQ, Create & Learn, iD Tech, CodaKid and Coding with Kids. We read their public pages on 17 October 2026 and link to them.

How current is this comparison?

Every fact was read on the providers' sites on 17 October 2026. Programs change, so confirm details on each site before you enroll.
