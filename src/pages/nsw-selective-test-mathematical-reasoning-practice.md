Students answer 35 multiple-choice questions, each with five possible answers, in 40 minutes on a department computer. No calculator is allowed in any section. The department says questions draw on the NSW curriculum up to Year 6 and that coaching is not necessary.

## 35 questions, 40 minutes, five options each

Every detail comes from the NSW Department of Education, read on 7 October 2026.

| Section | Questions | Time | Type |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reading | 17 (3 questions have multiple parts) | 45 minutes | Multiple choice |
| Mathematical Reasoning | 35 | 40 minutes | Multiple choice, five possible answers |
| Thinking Skills | 40 | 40 minutes | Multiple choice, four possible answers |
| Writing | 1 | 30 minutes | Open response |

The test is computer-based and sat on computers the department provides, at test centres that are mostly local public high schools. Students can do working out on paper; the department's video for students says they "can use paper for working out" in Mathematical Reasoning.

Calculators are on the list of items not allowed, and the practice page states: "Students cannot use a calculator during this, or any other, test."

For Year 7 entry in 2027, the department lists the test on Friday 1 to Saturday 2 May 2026, with a make-up test on Friday 22 May 2026. Each student sits on one day only. From 2026 the placement tests are held only in New South Wales.

The department describes the section as testing "mathematical reasoning skills" in which a child "will use different concepts from maths to solve problems", and notes that the concepts are based on what the child already learns at school.

Problems we wrote

## Tape diagrams for multi-step problems

A tape diagram turns a word problem into equal units drawn as strips. Once the picture is right, the arithmetic is small. All problems below use only upper-primary content and need no calculator.

### Draw equal units

If one amount is four times another, draw one strip of 1 unit and one strip of 4 units. Equal-sized boxes are the whole idea.

### Label what you know

Write the total, or the difference, against the strips it belongs to. The question usually tells you one of these.

### Find one unit

Divide to find the value of a single unit, then build every answer from it. Check by putting the numbers back into the story.

| Problem | The diagram | One unit | Answer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Three fifths of a class are girls. There are 12 more girls than boys. How many students are in the class? | Girls 3 units, boys 2 units; the difference is 1 unit | 12 | 60 students |
| Sam has 4 times as many cards as Lee. Together they have 85. How many does Sam have? | Lee 1 unit, Sam 4 units: 5 units altogether | 17 | 68 cards |
| Red and blue beads are in the ratio 2 to 3. There are 10 more blue beads than red. How many beads in total? | Red 2 units, blue 3 units; the difference is 1 unit | 10 | 50 beads |
| A tank is two fifths full. Adding 18 litres makes it seven tenths full. What does the tank hold? | Tenths: 4 shaded, then 7 shaded; 3 tenths added | 6 litres per tenth | 60 litres |
| Ana uses a quarter of her stickers, then a third of what is left. She has 30 left. How many did she start with? | Quarters: 1 used, 3 left; a third of those 3 used, 2 quarters left | 15 per quarter | 60 stickers |
| Three consecutive whole numbers add to 72. What is the largest? | Three equal strips plus 0, 1 and 2 extra | 23 | 25 |

Notice how the ratio and fraction problems collapse into the same move: find the gap or total in units, divide once, then rebuild. The sticker problem looks like two separate fraction steps, but on a diagram the second step simply halves what is left, and the whole question becomes "2 quarters are 30".

Walk through the first problem slowly. The class is split into fifths, so draw five equal boxes: three for girls, two for boys. The girls have one box more than the boys, and the story says that difference is 12 students, so one box is 12. Five boxes make 60. To check, three fifths of 60 is 36 girls and two fifths is 24 boys, and 36 is indeed 12 more than 24. Every problem in the table can be read back into its story the same way.

With five options per question, a tape diagram also helps eliminate answers. If the class must be a multiple of 5 units, any option that is not a multiple of 5 can be crossed out before any calculation.

Pacing

## About 68 seconds per question

Our arithmetic: 40 minutes divided by 35 questions is roughly 68.6 seconds each.

### Habits that help

Read the last sentence of a question first, so you know what is being asked before reading the story.

Draw small and fast. A tape diagram on scrap paper takes five seconds and saves a muddled calculation.

Use the five options as a check, not a shortcut. If your answer is not among them, the diagram needs another look.

### Habits to avoid

Spending three minutes on one problem. A question left for later is better than five questions never reached.

Guessing from the look of the numbers. Options are often built from common mistakes.

Practising under time pressure every day. Calm, accurate reasoning first; speed follows.

## Four stages across Years 4 to 6

Most capable students move through these over two or three years of ordinary good teaching.

| Stage | What the student does | Secure when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Part and whole | Draws a total split into parts | Solves "together they have 85" problems |
| 2. Comparison | Draws two strips and marks the difference | Solves "12 more girls than boys" problems |
| 3. Fractions of a whole | Divides a strip into fifths or tenths | Solves the tank problem without converting |
| 4. Two-step changes | Redraws the strip after each change | Solves the sticker problem in one diagram |

### Keeping it healthy

The department warns that too much coaching can harm wellbeing, and we agree. An hour a week of enjoyable maths is plenty for most children.

If your child is stressed, stop practising tests and go back to puzzles they choose themselves.

Questions about Mathematical Reasoning

## What NSW families ask us

### How many questions are in the selective test Mathematical Reasoning section?

35 multiple-choice questions, each with five possible answers, in 40 minutes, according to the NSW Department of Education.

### Can students use a calculator?

No. The department says students cannot use a calculator during Mathematical Reasoning or any other section, though they can work out on paper.

### Is the selective test on a computer?

Yes. It is computer-based and sat on computers provided by the department at test centres, mostly local public high schools.

### What maths does it cover?

The department says questions draw on concepts from the NSW curriculum up to Year 6, and that children do not need to study extra concepts.

### Does my child need coaching?

The department says coaching or tutoring for the placement test is not necessary and that it does not recommend or endorse coaching. Its free practice tests are the place to start.

### What is a tape diagram?

A drawing of a word problem as equal-sized strips or boxes, so that totals, differences and fractions can be seen as units before any calculation.

### When is the test?

For Year 7 entry in 2027 the department listed Friday 1 to Saturday 2 May 2026, with a make-up test on Friday 22 May 2026. Check the department's site for later years.

### Can you advise on which school to choose?

No. We do not give school-choice, placement or appeals advice. Those questions belong with the department and the schools.

### Will lessons improve my child's chances?

We make no promise about scores or placement. We teach mathematics that is worth knowing regardless of the test.

### When would lessons run?

We agree one weekly time after the free trial, usually after school. During daylight saving Sydney is five and a half hours ahead of India time.
