State math tests by state: what each US state uses

There is no national math test for American schoolchildren. Each state picks, names and schedules its own, which is why a parent in Austin talks about STAAR and a parent in Buffalo about the Regents. This index puts the ten states we have researched into one table, every row taken from the state’s own documents, then points you to each state’s page.

States this table leaves out

Forty states are missing, and we would rather say so than guess. The education department sites of Florida and Virginia refused our requests, Georgia and Tennessee did not answer, and Maryland, Louisiana, Mississippi, Missouri and Ohio blocked us in October 2026. We describe no test without reading the state’s own pages.

If your state is missing, the four questions further down still work with your own department’s assessment page.

Why Smarter Balanced has its own row

Smarter Balanced is a design, not a state. Its content specifications describe a summative assessment of the Common Core State Standards for Mathematics, and member states give it under their own names. California is the one member whose portal we could read, so it is the only one named.

The consortium’s own site refused our request, so we print no member list.

State tests, grades 3 to 8

Which grades 3 to 8 state math test does my child take?

The one your public school’s state gives, in your child’s enrolled grade. Every page below covers one state test in depth: its question types, calculator rules, score report and an original practice set.

California. CAASPP math practice is for California families decoding a score in the 2,000s: the untimed adaptive section, the separate performance task and the state’s scale bands.

Any Smarter Balanced state. Smarter Balanced math practice reads the shared consortium blueprint rather than one state’s manual, so it suits families outside California whose state uses the same design under another name. Its focus is the four claims and the reasoning questions most families never practice.

Colorado. Colorado CMAS math practice covers the four subclaims Colorado reports, with particular attention to the written reasoning and modeling tasks. It is the page for a child whose report shows a Met or Approached level and a weak reasoning score.

Illinois. Illinois IAR math practice deals with the strictly timed 60-minute units and the state’s scoring expectations for a written answer, where a bare correct number does not earn the top point.

Massachusetts. MCAS math prep spans grades 3 to 8 and the grade 10 test, and is built around the constructed-response question and the two-session structure in which the calculator arrives only in grade 7.

North Carolina. NC EOG math practice is about the calculator-inactive part of the End-of-Grade test, so it leans on mental arithmetic and fraction sense, and it explains the end-of-year window.

New Jersey. NJSLA math practice explains what changed when New Jersey’s test became adaptive in 2026: why the first answers matter more, and why going back later is limited to the same session.

New York. NYS math test prep for grades 3 to 8 concentrates on partial credit in the two- and three-credit questions of Session 2, and on the two-day testing pattern New York schools follow.

Pennsylvania. PSSA math practice scores sample open-ended answers against the state’s four-point guideline, which is the clearest way to see what separates a 2 from a 4.

Texas. STAAR math tutoring reads the six grade-level blueprints, the no-calculator rule up to grade 7, and the switch to the Student Success Tool from 2027 to 2028.

High school end-of-course and graduation tests

After grade 8, most states we cover test by course instead of by grade: an Algebra I test comes at the end of Algebra I, whenever that is. Some count toward graduation, others toward a course grade.

One page for every state. Algebra 1 EOC prep is the starting point when a family knows only that an “Algebra 1 end-of-course test” is coming. It compares the Algebra I tests of four states side by side and teaches the linear-against-exponential reasoning that every one of them asks for.

Texas. STAAR Algebra I EOC prep is for a Texas student who must pass this test to graduate. It covers the five reporting categories, the three windows a year for retakes, and the draft blueprint for the replacement test from spring 2028.

Pennsylvania. Keystone Algebra I prep works through the two modules anchor by anchor, using Pennsylvania’s Eligible Content list, and explains which winter or spring wave suits a given course schedule.

North Carolina. NC Math 1 EOC prep takes both of the state’s math end-of-course tests, NC Math 1 and NC Math 3, and links them through one technique, completing the square.

New Jersey. NJGPA math prep is for grade 11 students facing the graduation test, and lays out the three routes New Jersey offers if a student is Not Yet Graduation Ready on the first try.

New York. Three pages share the Regents, each with a different emphasis. Algebra I Regents prep is about the long Part IV modeling question and the reference sheet. Geometry Regents prep is about proof, compass-and-straightedge construction and the rule on pi. Algebra II Regents prep covers the newest of the three exams, first given in June 2026, and its functions and regression work.

Graduation test or course test?

Texas, Pennsylvania and New Jersey tie a math test to the diploma, each with alternatives, and New York does for now through the Regents. North Carolina folds the result into the course grade, at no less than a fifth of it.

Massachusetts changed its rule after a November 2024 ballot question. For the class of 2027 and later, the math part of the Competency Determination is coursework: a year each of Algebra I and Geometry, or of Integrated Math I and II.

Middle schoolers who take high school math

An advanced grade 8 student may sit the course test instead of the grade test. North Carolina does not give such students the grade 8 math EOG, and New York lets schools exempt students who will sit a math Regents in June.

A worked example (ours, not a real student). A grade 7 student moves from Texas to New Jersey, then to New York a year later. The Texas report said Approaches Grade Level, which TEA counts as a pass. In New Jersey the same child lands in Approaching Expectations, Level 3 of five, one below Met. In New York a Level 2 would mean partially proficient. Three reports, three vocabularies, one honest summary: close to grade level, with a few topics to fix. Read the reporting categories on each report, not the raw number, to find them.

Find your test

How do I find out which math test my child takes?

Four questions settle it, answered by your state’s assessment page or the last score report.

  1. Which state, and which kind of school? State tests are written for public schools; Texas, for instance, requires them in public and open-enrollment charter schools. Private and home schooling follow other rules.
  2. Grade test or course test? Grades 3 to 8 sit the grade test; once in Algebra I or Geometry, use the high school table.
  3. What is it called this year? Match the name on the last report to the table; New Jersey added “-A” in 2026.
  4. When is the window? The state sets it and your school picks the days. Then open that state’s page above.

State math tests: common questions

What math test does each state use?

Each state picks its own: among those we cover, Texas uses STAAR, California CAASPP (Smarter Balanced), Massachusetts MCAS, Pennsylvania the PSSA, New Jersey the NJSLA-A, Illinois the IAR, Colorado CMAS, North Carolina the EOG and New York its own grades 3 to 8 tests.

Is there a national standardized math test for every state?

No. Each state runs its own yearly math test for grades 3 to 8. Some share a design, such as Smarter Balanced, but still set their own name, calendar and labels.

Which grades take state math tests?

In every state on this page, grades 3 to 8 take a yearly math test. In high school, most switch to course tests such as Algebra I, while California tests grade 11, Massachusetts grade 10 and New Jersey grade 11.

When are state math tests in 2027?

Most fall between March and May 2027. For example, Illinois runs online testing March 1 to April 16, Texas April 19 to April 30, and Pennsylvania May 3 to 7, while North Carolina tests in the last ten days of the school year.

Is STAAR being replaced in Texas?

Yes. Under House Bill 8, passed in September 2025, TEA says STAAR will be replaced from the 2027 to 2028 school year by the Student Success Tool, with tests at the start, middle and end of the year. Spring 2027 is still a STAAR window.

Are the New York Regents exams ending?

No. NYSED says it is not eliminating the Regents Examinations. It has proposed that passing them stop being a diploma requirement from fall 2027, but the Board of Regents must approve that, and until then the current rules apply.

Can I compare my child's math score with another state's?

Not directly, since each state sets its own scale and cut scores. Compare the level, such as Proficient, in each state's own words.

What if my state is not on this page?

Open your state education department's assessment page and find the test name, grades, window and practice site. We left out states whose sites blocked our research rather than describe them second-hand.

When are your classes?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Times are set in your US time zone.

Are you connected with any state or test company?

No. Modern Age Coders is an independent online school with no tie to any state education department, district, the Smarter Balanced consortium or any test contractor. We quote and link the states' public documents.
