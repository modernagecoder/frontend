## What does every A level Computer Science course cover?

The same government list, whichever board a school chose. The DfE subject content sets out what is "common to all AS and A level specifications in computer science".

**The DfE subject content for A level Computer Science (April 2014), in its own terms**

| Area | What the content requires |
|---|---|
| Principles | "abstraction, decomposition, logic, algorithms and data representation" |
| Programming | Fundamentals of programming; data types from primitive to complex data structures |
| Algorithms | Following and writing algorithms; at A level, "the importance of the efficiency of an algorithm" and "standard algorithms" |
| Data | Capturing, selecting, exchanging and managing data; at A level, databases including "database programming and producing a data model" |
| Systems | Systems software; processors, storage, input, output and connectivity; networks and protocols |
| Paradigms | At A level, "the need for and characteristics of a variety of programming paradigms" |
| Skills | "design, write and test programs"; arguing for correctness and efficiency; modular structure; writing specifications |
| Wider issues | The moral, ethical, legal and cultural opportunities and risks of digital technology |

### Why the core matters to a tutor

Because most of the subject is shared, good teaching of the core serves a student on either board. Recursion, a hash table, a two's complement number or a SQL join is the same idea at AQA and at OCR.

What changes between boards is how the idea is examined: on screen or on paper, in a real language or in pseudocode, inside a pre-released program or from a blank page.

### What the core leaves to boards

Paper structure, timings, weightings, project rules and languages. Those come from each specification, and the board doors linked below go through them one board at a time.

Outside England, check the local list. OCR's specification says H446 "is available in England" and points Wales and Northern Ireland to their own qualification lists.

Source: [Department for Education, GCE AS and A level subject content for computer science](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/gce-as-and-a-level-for-computer-science), April 2014, reference DFE-00359-2014. Read 31 October 2026.

## What is the difference between AQA and OCR A level Computer Science?

Both put 80 per cent in exams and 20 per cent in a project. They part company on where code is written in the exam, and on who can enter at all.

**AQA 7517 and OCR H446 compared, from each board's current specification**

| Feature | AQA 7517 (v1.6, July 2023) | OCR H446 (v3.0, April 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| Exam papers | Paper 1 on screen, 2 hours 30 minutes, 40 per cent; Paper 2 written, 2 hours 30 minutes, 40 per cent | Components 01 and 02, both written, 2 hours 30 minutes each, 40 per cent each |
| Programming in the exam | In an Electronic Answer Document, working with a pre-released Skeleton Program | On paper; any pseudocode style whose meaning "could be reasonably inferred by a competent programmer" |
| Calculators | Not stated in the summary we read | "(no calculators allowed)" on both written papers |
| Project | Non-exam assessment, 75 marks, 20 per cent | Programming project, 70 marks, 20 per cent |
| Languages | C#, Java, Python (not Python 2), VB.Net | Project in Python, C family, Java, Visual Basic, PHP or Delphi, with "a suitable graphical interface" |
| Private candidates | "These specifications are not available to private candidates." | "Private candidates may enter for OCR assessments." |
| Teaching time | Not stated in the pages we read | Total qualification time and guided learning time of 360 hours |

## Do you need maths for A level Computer Science?

Some, and the amount has a legal floor. The DfE content says every accredited specification "must contain a minimum of 10% mathematics", and names three topics.

### Boolean algebra

Expressions in AND, OR and NOT, simplified with laws such as De Morgan's. It is the algebra of logic gates and of every if statement with more than one condition.

### Complexity of algorithms

Comparing how running time or memory grows with input size, written in Big O notation. The DfE marks this topic as A level only "due to its advanced nature".

### Number bases

Binary, hexadecimal and decimal, signed and unsigned integers, fixed and floating point. The DfE calls these Level 2 concepts applied in a Level 3 context.

### A level Maths is not required by the boards

Neither specification we read requires A level Maths to take A level Computer Science. The maths both boards assess is the list above, which OCR lists again as topics "counted as Level 2 (or higher) mathematics", while saying candidates will be "expected to apply the skills in a Level 3 context".

Schools and universities set their own entry requirements, so check those separately.

### Where students actually struggle

In our view, the hard part is rarely the arithmetic. It is the reasoning: why a binary search halves the work, why a two's complement byte can be negative, why two Boolean expressions are equal. Those are the habits the worked examples below practise.

Students taking [A level Maths](/a-level-maths-tuition-online) alongside will meet logarithms there, which is where the log in O(n log n) comes from.

Sources: DfE subject content, annex on mathematical skills; OCR H446 specification, section 5c, Mathematical skills.

## What does A level Computer Science reasoning look like?

Three original problems, one from each of the DfE's maths topics, worked in full. None is from an AQA or OCR paper.

### 1. One byte, two meanings

What is 10110110 in decimal? As an unsigned byte: 128 + 32 + 16 + 4 + 2 = 182, which is B6 in hex. As 8-bit two's complement the top bit is worth minus 128, so the value is minus 128 + 54 = minus 74. Check: 182 minus 256 is minus 74. The bits never changed; only the agreed meaning did.

### 2. Simplify the condition

Simplify (A AND B) OR (A AND NOT B). Factor out A: A AND (B OR NOT B). B OR NOT B is always true, so the whole expression is just A. In code, if (a and b) or (a and not b) can become if a. A truth table with four rows confirms it.

### 3. How many guesses?

At most how many comparisons does binary search need on 1,000,000 sorted items? Each comparison halves what is left. Two to the power 19 is 524,288, too small; two to the power 20 is 1,048,576, enough. So at most 20, against up to 1,000,000 for a linear search. That gap is O(log n) against O(n).

**Example 3 in Python (ours)** (Python)
```python
def binary_search(items, target):
    """Return the index of target in sorted items, or -1.
    Also return how many comparisons were made."""
    low, high = 0, len(items) - 1
    comparisons = 0
    while low <= high:
        mid = (low + high) // 2
        comparisons += 1
        if items[mid] == target:
            return mid, comparisons
        if items[mid] < target:
            low = mid + 1
        else:
            high = mid - 1
    return -1, comparisons

data = list(range(0, 2_000_000, 2))   # 1,000,000 sorted even numbers
print(binary_search(data, 1_999_998))  # last item: found in at most 20 comparisons
```
Run it and the comparison count comes back at or under 20. Change the list to ten million items and the bound rises to 24, not to ten times as many. Students learn more from changing the input and predicting the count than from reading the function.

## What should I look for in an A level Computer Science tutor?

Our checklist, which applies to us as much as anyone. The first question is the board; the last is the project.

1. Ask which board and specification code the tutor will teach to: 7517 and H446 examine the same core in different ways.
2. For AQA, ask whether they will work with the Preliminary Material and Skeleton Program in the board's language your school uses.
3. For OCR, ask how they will practise writing code on paper, in pseudocode a marker can follow.
4. Ask how they teach the maths topics, Boolean algebra, complexity and number bases, not only programming.
5. Ask where their line is on the project. The answer should be that they teach method and never write, edit or debug the student's own project code.
6. Ask for a free first lesson on a topic the student finds hard, and judge the reasoning, not the slides.

## What students and parents ask

### What do you study in A level Computer Science?

The DfE core shared by every board: programming, data structures, data representation, algorithms and their efficiency, databases, systems software, architecture, networks, programming paradigms and the wider issues of digital technology, with at least 10 per cent mathematics.

### Is AQA or OCR A level Computer Science harder?

Neither board publishes a comparison and we will not invent one. AQA sets its programming paper on screen with a pre-released Skeleton Program; OCR sets two written papers with code in pseudocode. Some students prefer one format to the other.

### Do I need A level Maths for A level Computer Science?

The AQA and OCR specifications we read do not require it. Every specification must contain at least 10 per cent maths: Boolean algebra, complexity of algorithms and number bases, applied at A level standard.

### Can I take A level Computer Science as a private candidate?

With OCR, yes, if you are based in the UK and an approved centre agrees to host you; the centre may charge. AQA says its 7517 specification is not available to private candidates.

### How is AQA A level Computer Science assessed?

Paper 1 is a 2 hour 30 minute on-screen exam worth 40 per cent, Paper 2 a 2 hour 30 minute written exam worth 40 per cent, and the non-exam assessment is 75 marks and 20 per cent.

### How is OCR A level Computer Science assessed?

Two written papers, Computer systems and Algorithms and programming, each 2 hours 30 minutes and 40 per cent with no calculators, plus a 70-mark programming project worth 20 per cent.

### Which programming language should I use?

The one your school uses for the exam. AQA supports C#, Java, Python and VB.Net; OCR accepts several languages for the project. We teach in Python and introduce each board's conventions.

We teach project method on our own examples. We never write, edit, debug or review any part of a student's assessed project.
