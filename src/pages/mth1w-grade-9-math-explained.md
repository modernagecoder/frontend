# MTH1W explained: Ontario&rsquo;s Grade 9 math course

MTH1W is the one Grade 9 math course every Ontario student now takes. ## What de-streamed means for Grade 9 math

Before 2021, Ontario students entering Grade 9 chose between an academic and an applied math course. MTH1W ended that choice. The ministry&rsquo;s parent information puts it simply: &ldquo;Starting September 2021, there will be one new Grade 9 math course for all students.&rdquo;

### Why the ministry made the change

The parent information lists several benefits it expects from de-streaming, among them &ldquo;setting high academic standards for all students&rdquo;, &ldquo;enabling students with a range of skills and interests to learn together&rdquo; and &ldquo;keeping future options open for all students (for example, going to college, university or apprenticeship)&rdquo;.

The practical effect for a family is that no Grade 8 student has to pick a math track before they know what they want to study. Everyone takes MTH1W, and the choices start in Grade 10.

### What it means in the classroom

One course for everyone means one classroom with a wider range of starting points. A student who found Grade 8 math easy sits beside one who found it hard, and the teacher plans for both.

In our first classes we see both kinds of student. The confident ones sometimes coast and then meet linear relations unprepared; the anxious ones often know more than they think. Either way, the remedy is the same: secure fractions, integers and the meaning of a variable early in the term.

MTH1W has seven strands in all. The first two, social-emotional learning skills and Mathematical Thinking and Making Connections, run through the course; the other five carry the content. The course description sums up the approach: &ldquo;Students will use mathematical processes, mathematical modelling, and coding to make sense of the mathematics they are learning&rdquo;.

### Strand C is the centre of the course

Algebra in MTH1W covers expressions and equations (C1), coding (C2), applications of relations (C3) and characteristics of relations (C4). Linear relations appear in three of the four, and coding is expected to support all of them.

The jump from Grade 8 is real. In Grade 8 a student finds a value; in MTH1W they compare whole relations, their rates of change and initial values, and explain what a graph means in context.

### Where coding fits

The MTH1W document says students &ldquo;will extend and apply coding skills to dynamically represent situations, analyse mathematics concepts, and solve problems in various contexts.&rdquo; The C2 overall expectation places that work &ldquo;in algebra and across the other strands&rdquo;.

So the coding in MTH1W is not a separate unit to get through. A good teacher uses it whenever a pattern, a relation or a large data set is easier to see by running code than by drawing by hand.

## The MTH1W coding expectations, C2.1 to C2.3

Strand C sets one overall expectation for coding: students will &ldquo;apply coding skills to represent mathematical concepts and relationships dynamically, and to solve problems, in algebra and across the other strands&rdquo;. Three specific expectations sit under it.

**MTH1W coding expectations, quoted (MTH1W curriculum, 2021, read 22 October 2026); the right column is ours**

| Expectation | In the curriculum&rsquo;s words | In plain language (ours) |
|---|---|---|
| C2.1 | &ldquo;use coding to demonstrate an understanding of algebraic concepts including variables, parameters, equations, and inequalities&rdquo; | Show you understand algebra by using it in code: store values, hold a constant, test an equation, test a range |
| C2.2 | &ldquo;create code by decomposing situations into computational steps in order to represent mathematical concepts and relationships, and to solve problems&rdquo; | Break a situation into steps a computer can follow, then write them |
| C2.3 | &ldquo;read code to predict its outcome, and alter code to adjust constraints, parameters, and outcomes to represent a similar or new mathematical situation&rdquo; | Read someone&rsquo;s code, say what it will do, then change it for a new case |

### What C2.1 means by its four words

The curriculum&rsquo;s examples spell out each idea. Variables are used to &ldquo;store data temporarily&rdquo; and to access and alter it. A parameter acts as a placeholder &ldquo;to represent a quantity that influences the output of a mathematical object but is viewed as being held constant&rdquo;; in y = ax, the a.

Equations in code assign values and test equality; inequalities test a range. In our worked example below, the start height and burn rate are parameters, the height at each hour is a variable, the equal heights are an equation and &ldquo;taller&rdquo; is an inequality.

### Coding arrives with history

MTH1W students are not starting from zero. The teacher notes tell teachers to &ldquo;make connections to prior learning in coding from the elementary grades, such as using loops, conditional statements, and subprograms&rdquo;, since Ontario students have had coding in math since Grade 1.

In practice that prior learning varies widely. Some Grade 9 students have written Python for years; others have only dragged blocks once or twice. Neither the curriculum nor the course record names a coding language or tool.

## A worked MTH1W example: two linear relations in Python

A problem we made up for this guide, pitched at MTH1W Strand C. It touches C2.1 (variables, parameters, an equation, inequalities), C2.3 (alter a parameter and predict) and C3.3 (the meaning of a point of intersection). The program ran in Python 3 and the output is printed below it.

The problem. Candle A is 30 cm tall and burns down 2 cm every hour. Candle B is 20 cm tall and burns 1 cm an hour. Both are lit at the same moment. When are they the same height, and what does that moment mean?

1. Name the relations. Height of A: y = 30 &minus; 2x. Height of B: y = 20 &minus; x, where x is hours since lighting. Both are linear, with negative rates of change.
2. Pick out the parameters. The start heights (30 and 20) and the burn rates (2 and 1) stay fixed for each candle. In code they become arguments to one function, so the same function serves both candles.
3. Decompose the situation. For each hour: work out both heights, stop if either candle is gone, then compare. That is C2.2 in four lines.
4. Solve it by algebra too. 30 &minus; 2x = 20 &minus; x gives x = 10, and both heights are then 10 cm. The code should agree; if it does not, one of them is wrong.
5. Interpret the intersection. Before hour 10, A is taller; after it, B is taller. The point (10, 10) is the moment the order changes, which is what C3.3 asks students to put into words.

**Python · two candles (ours)** (python)
```python
# Two candles, two linear relations: when are they the same height? (ours)
def height(start, rate, hours):
    """Height in cm of a candle that starts at start cm and burns rate cm per hour."""
    return start - rate * hours

a_start, a_rate = 30, 2      # parameters for candle A
b_start, b_rate = 20, 1      # parameters for candle B

for t in range(0, 16):
    a = height(a_start, a_rate, t)
    b = height(b_start, b_rate, t)
    if a < 0 or b < 0:
        break
    if a == b:
        print("Hour", t, ": both", a, "cm  <- same height")
    elif a > b:
        print("Hour", t, ": A taller,", a, "vs", b)
    else:
        print("Hour", t, ": B taller,", a, "vs", b)
```
The run prints one line per hour, from Hour 0 : A taller, 30 vs 20 through Hour 9 : A taller, 12 vs 11, then Hour 10 : both 10 cm <- same height, then Hour 11 : B taller, 8 vs 9 and on to Hour 15 : B taller, 0 vs 5. The algebra and the program agree.

### Alter a parameter (C2.3)

Change a_rate from 2 to 3 and predict before running. Algebra: 30 &minus; 3x = 20 &minus; x gives x = 5, height 15. The program agrees: Hour 5 : both 15 cm. A faster burn brings the crossing earlier.

Then try a change that breaks the story. Set a_rate to 1, the same as B. The lines are now parallel; A is always 10 cm taller and the candles never match. Ask the student what that means for the equation: 30 &minus; x = 20 &minus; x has no solution.

### Linear or not? (C2.2)

One of the situations the curriculum lists for decomposing into steps is &ldquo;determining whether a relationship is linear or non-linear&rdquo;. The short program below does it our way, by checking whether every first difference matches the first one.

The seats around tables in a row grow by 4 each time, so they are linear. The areas of squares grow by 3, 5, 7 and 9, so they are not. The same test, run on a pattern from class, gives a quick check before any graph is drawn.

**Python · linear or non-linear (ours)** (python)
```python
# Linear or non-linear? Check the first differences (ours)
def is_linear(values):
    first = values[1] - values[0]
    for i in range(2, len(values)):
        if values[i] - values[i - 1] != first:
            return False
    return True

chairs = [6, 10, 14, 18, 22]      # seats around 1 to 5 tables in a row
squares = [1, 4, 9, 16, 25]       # areas of squares with sides 1 to 5
print("chairs:", is_linear(chairs))
print("squares:", is_linear(squares))
```
Output: chairs: True and squares: False. The function stops at the first difference that does not match, which is the efficient move: one counterexample is enough.

## Five MTH1W coding-for-algebra questions

Our own questions, built on the two programs above. They are not taken from EQAO, a school or a textbook. Answers follow.

1. Predict. Using the height function, what is height(25, 1.5, 4)? What does each argument mean?
2. Alter. Keep candle B as it is. What burn rate for candle A makes the two candles the same height at hour 5? Check with algebra.
3. Linear or not. What does is_linear([2, 6, 18, 54]) return? Describe the pattern in words.
4. Read the relation. For candle B, y = 20 &minus; x. What are the rate of change and the initial value, and what does each mean for the candle?
5. Inequality. For which whole numbers of hours is candle A taller than candle B? Write and solve the inequality, then say how the program&rsquo;s output shows the same answer.

1. 19.0 cm. Start 25, rate 1.5, hours 4: 25 &minus; 1.5 &times; 4 = 19. Python prints 19.0 because a decimal rate makes the result a decimal.
2. 3 cm per hour. 30 &minus; 5r = 20 &minus; 5 gives 5r = 15, so r = 3. Both candles are then 15 cm tall at hour 5, matching the altered run above.
3. False. The differences are 4, 12 and 36, not equal, so the relation is non-linear. Each term is 3 times the one before, which students meet again as exponential growth.
4. Rate &minus;1, initial value 20. The candle starts at 20 cm (the y-intercept) and loses 1 cm each hour (the slope). It burns out after 20 hours.
5. Hours 0 to 9. 30 &minus; 2x > 20 &minus; x gives 10 > x, so x < 10. In the output, every line before hour 10 says A is taller, and hour 10 is the equality.

| Stage | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 1. Solver | Solves a one-step equation and can check the answer by substituting it back |

## MTH1W: common questions

### What is MTH1W?

MTH1W is the Ontario Grade 9 mathematics course, issued in 2021 and listed as de-streamed. It is the one Grade 9 math course for all students, covering number, algebra, data, geometry and measurement, and financial literacy, with coding and mathematical modelling used throughout.

### What does de-streamed mean?

It means Grade 8 students no longer choose between an applied and an academic Grade 9 math course. Ontario's parent information says there is strong evidence for not having Grade 8 students make that choice, and that this is called de-streaming. Since September 2021 everyone takes MTH1W.

### Can my teen earn the MTH1W credit with you?

No. Our classes run beside the school course, and we do not grant Ontario credits. The credit is earned at the student's own school, a board summer program or an Ontario credit provider, while our lessons supply extra teaching and practice.

### What coding is in MTH1W?

Expectation C2 asks students to apply coding skills to represent math concepts and relationships and to solve problems, in algebra and across the course. C2.1 asks them to use code to show they understand variables, parameters, equations and inequalities.

### Which programming language does MTH1W use?

The curriculum does not name one. Classes use different tools, from block-based editors to Python and spreadsheets. We teach in Python unless the class uses something else, and the algebra ideas carry across.

### Is MTH1W hard?

It is a step up from Grade 8, mainly because students compare whole linear relations and explain their meaning, rather than finding single values. Students who are secure with fractions, negative numbers and the idea of a variable usually manage well. No one can promise a mark in advance.

### What replaced the academic and applied Grade 9 courses?

MTH1W did. The MTH1W curriculum says the course supersedes the two Grade 9 courses in the 2005 Ontario mathematics curriculum for Grades 9 and 10.

### Does the EQAO Grade 9 math assessment count?

It does. EQAO states that the result becomes part of the final MTH1W course mark under the ministry's assessment policy, without giving a weight. Its next window runs from January 12 to February 5, 2027.

### What comes after MTH1W?

The MTH1W course table lists it as a prerequisite option for the Grade 10 academic and applied courses, MPM2D and MFM2P, and for the Grade 11 workplace course MEL3E. Students who started Grade 9 in 2024 or later need three math credits for the diploma.

### When can my teen have classes?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. We fix lesson times in your local clock and check them again after Ontario changes its clocks on 1 November 2026.

### How are lessons paid for?

We charge nothing for the first lesson. Families then pay monthly for eight live lessons, without a semester contract; the price box higher up gives the current group and private figures.
