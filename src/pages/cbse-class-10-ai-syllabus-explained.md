> The CBSE Class 10 Artificial Intelligence syllabus for 2026-27 (code 417) is worth 100 marks: a 50-mark theory paper and a 50-mark practical. Theory is Part A, five employability skills units at two marks each, plus Part B, seven AI units of which five carry theory marks: Revisiting the AI Project Cycle and Ethical Frameworks (7), Advanced Concepts of Modeling (11), Evaluating Models (10), Computer Vision (4) and Natural Language Processing (8). Statistical Data and Advance Python are practical-only. Part C is a practical file of at least 15 programs (15), a practical examination on Units 4 to 7 (15), a viva (5), a project related to the Sustainable Development Goals (10) and a project viva (5). The board names Orange Data Mining, Teachable Machine, Lobe and Jupyter Notebook as tools. Modern Age Coders teaches all of it live, Monday and Wednesday at 9 PM IST.

## The unit ledger (hours and marks, from the CBSE curriculum for session 2026-27)

- Part A, five employability units (Communication, Self-Management, ICT, Entrepreneurial, Green Skills): 50 hours, 10 marks.
- Unit 1, Revisiting AI Project Cycle and Ethical Frameworks for AI: 11 theory hours, 4 practical hours, 7 marks.
- Unit 2, Advanced Concepts of Modeling in AI: 18 theory, 7 practical, 11 marks.
- Unit 3, Evaluating Models: 21 theory, 4 practical, 10 marks.
- Unit 4, Statistical Data: 28 practical hours, assessed through practicals, no theory marks.
- Unit 5, Computer Vision: 10 theory, 20 practical, 4 marks.
- Unit 6, Natural Language Processing: 20 theory, 7 practical, 8 marks.
- Unit 7, Advance Python: 10 practical hours, assessed through practicals, no theory marks.
- Theory total 50 marks; grand total 210 hours for 100 marks.

Modeling and Evaluating Models together are 21 of the 50 theory marks. Computer Vision has 20 practical hours and four theory marks: a unit to do rather than revise. The two zero-mark units are where the 15-mark practical examination and the 15-mark file are earned.

## What is inside each unit

Unit 1: the project cycle returns as the spine, problem scoping to evaluation and back; the three domains of AI; ethical frameworks, their types, and bioethics with a healthcare case study. Feeds the case-based question.

Unit 2: AI versus ML versus DL; rule-based versus learning-based models; supervised, unsupervised and reinforcement learning; classification and regression; clustering and association; artificial and convolutional neural networks; Teachable Machine, Google's drum-machine experiment and the TensorFlow playground as activities.

Unit 3: why evaluate; train-test split; accuracy and error; the confusion matrix with precision, recall and F1; activities to build a confusion matrix from scratch, calculate accuracy, and choose the right metric; bias, transparency and accuracy as ethical concerns.

Unit 4: deliberately no-code. Data science and no-code and low-code AI; Orange Data Mining and a spreadsheet; important concepts in statistics; the AI project cycle rebuilt in Orange with the Palmer penguins case study.

Unit 5: computer vision and its applications; pixels, resolution, pixel values, grayscale and RGB; feature extraction, detection and segmentation. Practical: Lobe and Teachable Machine classifiers, a Smart Sorter, a coral bleaching model in Orange, the convolution operator, and CNN kernels and layers.

Unit 6: why language is hard for machines; applications; the stages of NLP, lexicon, syntax, semantics, logical analysis; script bots versus smart bots; text normalisation, bag of words and TF-IDF; a sentiment-analysis case walkthrough in Orange.

Unit 7: Jupyter Notebook, virtual environments, installing packages; Python fundamentals and built-in functions and libraries. The unit that makes the practical file possible.

## A worked bag-of-words example

Two documents, "Aman likes to play cricket" and "Riya likes to play football", are lowercased, stripped of punctuation, tokenised and cleared of the stop word "to". The vocabulary is aman, likes, play, cricket, riya, football. The table counts each word per document: Document 1 has aman 1, likes 1, play 1, cricket 1; Document 2 has likes 1, play 1, riya 1, football 1. Words in both documents say little about which is which; cricket and football carry the meaning, which is what TF-IDF formalises next. Marks are lost in normalisation, not in the table.

## Part C, line by line

Practical file with a minimum of 15 programs (15). Practical examination on Units 4, 5, 6 and 7 (15). Viva voce (5). Project work, field visit or student portfolio, any one, related to the Sustainable Development Goals (10). Viva voce related to the project (5). CBSE's suggested programs: add the elements of two lists; mean, median and mode using NumPy; a line chart from (2,5) to (9,10); a scatter chart for (2,5), (9,10), (8,3), (5,7), (6,18); read a CSV and display 10 rows; read a CSV and display its information; read and display an image; read an image and identify its shape. Sample project: predicting Palmer penguin species from statistical data.

## Three 2026 facts

Class 10 continues on 417 for 2026-27 on the earlier scheme. Code 417 was discontinued for Class 9 from 2026-27; Class 9 moves to Computational Thinking and AI, compulsory with an exam from 2027-28. From 2026, CBSE Class 10 boards run in two phases, a compulsory February to March exam and an optional May improvement exam for a limited number of subjects with the better score counting; the improvement exam is framed around the main subjects, so ask your school whether a skill subject can be re-attempted.

## A school-year sequence

April to June: Units 1 and 2, and the practical file begins. July to September: Unit 3, Unit 4 in Orange, and the project chosen. October to November: Units 5 and 6 with their practicals; the file passes fifteen. December to January: file complete, project finished, first full mocks. February: revision weighted by marks, then the board practical and the paper.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is 417 the same as the new Computational Thinking and AI?** No. 417 is the existing Class 10 skill subject, published for 2026-27 on the earlier scheme; CT and AI is the new curriculum for Classes 3 to 8, compulsory in Class 9 from 2027-28.

**Which units carry theory marks?** Part A (10) and Units 1, 2, 3, 5 and 6 (7, 11, 10, 4, 8). Units 4 and 7 are practical-only.

**Is Python needed for the theory paper?** No; Python lives in Part C, where the file, the practical exam and all of CBSE's suggested programs are.

**What is Orange?** A free, open-source, no-code data science tool named by CBSE for Statistical Data and the practical sides of Computer Vision and NLP; set up in the first practical session.

**How many programs in the file?** A minimum of 15, worth 15 marks; a good file goes past it.

**What must the project be about?** Something related to the Sustainable Development Goals; CBSE's sample is Palmer penguin species prediction.
