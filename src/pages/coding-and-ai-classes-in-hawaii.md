# Coding and AI classes in Hawaii

**Is computer science part of a Hawaii high school diploma?** Not as a named requirement. Hawaii Board of Education Policy 102-15 sets a 24.0-credit diploma in English, social studies, math, science, languages, arts or CTE, PE, health and a transition plan, with 6.0 elective credits where computer science can fit. Modern Age Coders teaches coding, AI and AP Computer Science live online for Hawaii students on any island, in batches or 1-on-1.

Hawaii's published computer science numbers tell an uncomfortable story. The share of high schools offering a course rose to 77% in 2021 to 2022 and has fallen in each year since, to 66.7% in 2024 to 2025, while the share of high school students enrolled dropped to 2.53%. Distance between islands, small schools and teacher supply all plausibly play a part, though the data alone cannot say which. This page sets out the Board of Education's diploma rule as written, the access and enrollment figures, a data project on the state library network that only needs coordinates, and why Hawaii class times are the easiest in the country to plan.

## What Board Policy 102-15 requires for a Hawaii diploma

From the Hawaii State Board of Education's Policy 102-15, High School Graduation Requirements and Commencement, last amended on 02/12/2026 and read on 18 October 2026.

**Minimum course and credit requirements for the Hawaii High School Diploma (Board Policy 102-15)**

| Area | Credits |
|---|---|
| English | 4.0 |
| Social Studies | 4.0 |
| Mathematics, including Algebra 1 and Geometry | 3.0 |
| Science, including Biology | 3.0 |
| World Language, Fine Arts or Career and Technical Education | 2.0 |
| Physical Education | 1.0 |
| Health, and Personal/Transition Plan | 0.5 each |
| Electives | 6.0 |
| Total | 24.0 |

### Where computer science fits

Computer science is not named in the policy's course areas. A school computer science course would normally count as an elective, or within the Career and Technical Education option, depending on how the course is authorized.

### Proficiency counts

The policy lets students meet requirements through proficiency based equivalents, under a process the Department of Education sets up, alongside traditional courses.

### Same rule for every student

Policy 102-15 states that the graduation requirement for students with disabilities is the same as for students without disabilities.

We tried to read the Hawaii Department of Education's own computer science pages and the Hawaii Revised Statutes on the legislature's site, but both returned security blocks to our requests in October 2026. So this page does not describe Hawaii's computer science standards, any law on course offerings, or department guidance; it relies on the Board policy above, which we could read in full.

### A small denominator

With 69 high schools in the count, a single school dropping its course moves the share by about 1.4 points, by our arithmetic. Small totals swing; read the trend, not one year.

### Two sources blended

The sheet names the Hawaii basis as "State Education Agency and School Catalogs", combining an official file with course listings.

### AP held steady

The College Board counts 76 Hawaii schools giving AP exams in 2023 and 75 in 2024, with 47 public schools in both years.

The enrollment figure divides 1,317 students by an NCES count of 51,961 high school students for fall 2022, so it is Code.org's approximation rather than a census of 2024 to 2025 classrooms. Even so, about one high schooler in forty taking computer science is low, and it is the main reason families on the neighbor islands look for classes outside school. ## Which island is each library on? Sorting points with bounding boxes

The Hawaii Statewide GIS Program publishes the 51 branches of the Hawaii State Public Library System as public-domain open data, each with latitude, longitude and an island label. Students ignore the label, place every point with coordinates alone, then check themselves against it.

**State public libraries by island (our counts from the Statewide GIS layer, downloaded 18 October 2026)**

| Island | Branches | Listed with computer classes |
|---|---|---|
| Oahu | 25 | 11 |
| Hawaii Island | 12 | 4 |
| Kauai | 6 | 2 |
| Maui | 6 | 2 |
| Molokai | 1 | 1 |
| Lanai | 1 | 0 |
| All islands | 51 | 20 |

### 1. Draw six boxes

For each island, students choose a minimum and maximum latitude and longitude, our own rough rectangles, then test every library with four comparisons: inside if lat_min <= y <= lat_max and lon_min <= x <= lon_max.

### 2. Check against the label

Our six boxes put all 51 branches on the island the layer names, 51 of 51. That agreement is the evidence the boxes work, and a student should report it before trusting any box-based count.

### 3. Find the weak spots

Rectangles are crude. Maui, Molokai and Lanai sit close together, so a careless box catches the wrong coast; students tighten edges and learn why real tools use polygon outlines instead.

### Ours, from public-domain data

The boxes and counts are our teaching results from one download. The state publishes the layer as public domain with no warranty of accuracy, and opening hours change, so the 28 branches listed as open on Saturday and the 3 open on Sunday are the layer's values on that day, not a current timetable.

The coordinates span latitudes 19.06 to 22.21 and longitudes -159.67 to -154.94, which is the first sanity check: any point outside that frame is a data error. A strong extension measures how far each library is from the next nearest one on the same island, which turns a filtering exercise into a distance one and says something real about access on the neighbor islands.

## Hawaii Standard Time, unchanged since 1947

Hawaii keeps one clock all year with no daylight saving, which makes scheduling with India unusually simple.

### No clock changes

In the IANA database, Hawaii's zone has been plain HST, UTC minus 10, since June 8, 1947, with no daylight saving rule attached. Your class time never moves.

### A fixed gap to India

By our arithmetic India is always 15 hours 30 minutes ahead of Hawaii, so a 4 pm class in Honolulu is 7:30 the next morning for the teacher, all year.

### The mainland moves instead

Each fall and spring, when the mainland moves its clocks (next on the first Sunday of November 2026 and the second Sunday of March 2027), Hawaii stays still. Your gap to the West Coast shifts by an hour, but your class time stays put.

**History in the database** Before 1947 the islands kept a half-hour offset, UTC minus 10:30, and the database records a brief stretch of daylight time in 1933, from April 30 to May 21. Students who convert old Hawaii time stamps learn why historical data needs the zone name, not a fixed offset.

## Ask an AI which island a coordinate is on, then check it

A language model can guess an island from a latitude and longitude. A student with a bounding box can prove it.

AI assistants are often right about well-known places and quietly wrong about edge cases, such as a point near the channel between Maui and Molokai. A guess delivered fluently is still a guess, and there is no way to tell from the answer alone.

The bounding-box project gives students a method they can verify: four comparisons, a check against a labeled list, and a count of disagreements. Once they can do that, an AI tool becomes something to test rather than something to trust.

With fewer Hawaii students taking computer science each year in the published data, the ones who learn to verify an answer stand out. The longer argument is in [our view on learning to code with AI around](/blog/is-coding-worth-learning-2026).
