USACO for Canadian students

The USA Computing Olympiad runs free online programming contests that anyone can enter from home, and many Canadian students who write the Canadian Computing Competition use it as extra practice through the winter. It is a different kind of contest from the CCC: several online rounds instead of one sitting at school, divisions you climb by promotion instead of a level you choose, and a national team path that is reserved for students in the USA. This page is built from usaco.org's own pages, fetched on 22 October 2026, and sets out what is open to a Canadian, how the two contests compare, and how to use both in one school year without either getting in the way.

In short

USACO, the USA Computing Olympiad, holds free online algorithm contests open to all, so Canadian students can compete from home in Bronze, Silver, Gold and Platinum divisions; only students in the USA can be selected as finalists for its camp. For Canada's IOI team the route is the CCC. Modern Age Coders coaches both live online, in a batch or one to one, in its CCC Junior and Senior Preparation course.

The 2026 to 2027 dates are not out yet

On 22 October 2026, usaco.org still showed its 2025-2026 schedule: contests on 9 to 12 January 2026, 30 January to 2 February 2026 and 20 to 23 February 2026, then a US Open on 28 March 2026, marked proctored. It had not posted dates for the 2026 to 2027 season, so we print none.

Its details page still describes the older pattern of four monthly contests. Check usaco.org before planning.

Four divisions, climbed by promotion

USACO says "All participants start in the bronze division", and that strong scores bring promotion. Bronze is for students who "have recently learned to program"; Silver introduces "fundamental problem-solving techniques (e.g., recursive search, greedy algorithms)"; Gold brings "shortest paths, dynamic programming"; Platinum is for students "well grounded in algorithmic problem-solving techniques".

A perfect score earns an in-contest promotion, with a fresh full clock for the next division in the same weekend. Others wait for the results and the contest's cutoff.

Open to Canadians

What a Canadian student can and cannot do in USACO

The contests themselves are open to everyone. The national team path is not. Both points are stated plainly on usaco.org.

Certified results in Gold and Platinum

USACO describes a "certified contest result" for Gold and Platinum. Problems in those divisions are released at "12:00pm (noon) eastern time (ET)" on the Saturday, and a student who starts "between 12:00pm and 12:15pm ET on that day will receive a certified score".

USACO adds that "A certified result is required for promotion from gold to platinum." Bronze and Silver problems are open across the whole weekend, with no certified start.

The rules that apply to everyone

USACO's integrity rules include working alone and this line: "Use of generative AI (e.g., services like copilot or ChatGPT) is prohibited." Code must be written from scratch, consulting only references for the language's syntax and libraries.

Under 18, ChatGPT needs a parent's permission anyway. For contest practice we ask students to work exactly as the rules require, with only official language documentation open.

Sources: USACO Contest Instructions and Rules, USACO Details and FAQ and USACO Training, read 22 October 2026.

What the comparison means for a Canadian (ours)

For a student in Canada, the CCC is the contest that counts for Canada's IOI team, and it happens once. USACO can be entered again and again, which makes it valuable practice: four hours of real contest pressure, several times a winter, from the kitchen table.

USACO's divisions also give a clear, external measure of progress. A student who reaches Silver has met recursive search and greedy methods under contest conditions; Gold means shortest paths and dynamic programming, which is roughly the ground of CCC Senior problems 3 and 4 in our experience.

Where the two differ in practice

The CCC rewards a single good afternoon and partial marks on subtasks. USACO scores each problem by how many test cases pass within the time limit, and promotion depends on each contest's cutoff.

Python is workable at both lower levels: USACO says it generally tries to ensure "that all bronze-level problems are also fully-solvable in Python", while warning that higher divisions may not be. CEMC gives a similar warning for the CCC. C++ is the safe choice for anyone aiming high in either.

A practice problem

An original Bronze-style problem: Ferry Queue

We wrote this problem for our students; it is ours, not a USACO or CEMC problem. It is the kind of simulation that Bronze rounds and early CCC problems share, with a twist that rewards thinking about efficiency. The solution was run on its sample, and checked against a slow brute force on 500 random cases with no differences.

Ferry Queue. A ferry leaves the dock at times T, 2T, 3T and so on (in minutes) and carries at most C cars each sailing. N cars arrive at the given times, in order; a car arriving exactly at a departure time still makes that sailing. Cars board first come, first served. Print the departure time of the sailing that carries the last car. Input: N, C and T on the first line (1 ≤ N ≤ 100,000; 1 ≤ C, T ≤ 1,000), then the N arrival times on one line, each from 0 to 1,000,000,000. Output: one integer.

Why this problem suits both contests

Simulation with an efficiency twist appears at the start of both ladders. In USACO it is Bronze material; at the CCC it sits around Junior problem 5 or the early Senior problems.

The lesson is the same in both: write the obvious simulation first, then ask which steps change nothing and can be skipped.

Practising it properly

Write the brute force, then the fast version, and run both on many small random inputs until they always agree. Only then trust the fast version on large input.

Then try your own variant: what if the ferry may leave early once it is full? Changing one rule and re-solving is how students learn to read rules carefully.

One season

Using USACO and the CCC in the same school year

USACO's rounds have usually fallen between December and early spring, and the 2027 CCC is on 17 February 2027. Our plan for a Canadian student who wants both, assuming USACO keeps a similar pattern for 2026 to 2027, which it had not yet confirmed.

  1. Now, October and November 2026: create a usaco.org account, try three or four historical Bronze problems from the archive, and confirm with the school that it will order the CCC.
  2. December 2026 and January 2027: write each USACO round that is posted, as a four-hour practice under the real rules. Review every problem the following week, whatever the score.
  3. Early February 2027: protect the CCC. If a USACO weekend falls within a few days of 17 February 2027, treat it as light practice or skip it, and spend the time on CCC past papers.
  4. 17 February 2027: write the CCC at school, Junior or Senior. This is the contest that counts for Canada.
  5. After the CCC: return to USACO for the remaining rounds of the season, aiming for the next promotion.
  6. Spring and summer 2027: keep working through the historical archive at the student's division, ready for the next season.

Which to prioritise

For a student in Canada, the CCC. It is the contest that leads to the CCO and Canada's IOI team, and it is written once. USACO is excellent preparation for it and a lasting measure of progress.

For a student who simply loves algorithms, both, all year. The problems are different, the habits are the same.

What we will not claim

We do not promise promotions, cutoffs or invitations in either contest; both organisers decide those. We teach the algorithms and habits that both contests reward.

Questions from Canadian families

USACO for Canadian students: common questions

Can Canadian students compete in USACO?

Yes. usaco.org says all are welcome to take part in its online contests and training, and that participation is free and open to all. A student in Canada needs only a free account on usaco.org.

Can a Canadian student become a USACO finalist?

No, for a student living in Canada. USACO says only pre-college students in the USA are eligible for selection as finalists, and that a finalist must be a US citizen or a resident of the USA during the first half of the school year.

How are Canadian students ranked in USACO?

usaco.org says pre-college or USA students are typically ranked separately from observers, meaning all others participating. Check each contest's results page for how entries are listed.

Is USACO free?

Yes. USACO's rules say there is never any fee for participation, and its training pages and historical problems with solutions are free online.

USACO vs CCC: which should a Canadian student do?

The CCC, if only one. It is CEMC's national contest, written at school once a year, and the route to the Canadian Computing Olympiad and Canada's IOI team. USACO is excellent extra practice from home across the winter.

When are the 2026 to 2027 USACO contests?

usaco.org had not posted them when we checked on 22 October 2026; it still showed the 2025-2026 schedule. Its details page says contests usually run from December through early spring. The CCC is on 17 February 2027.

What time do USACO contests start in Canada?

Bronze and Silver can be started any time in the Friday-to-Monday window. For a certified Gold or Platinum result, problems open at noon Eastern on the Saturday, which is 10:00 a.m. in British Columbia in winter and 1:30 p.m. in Newfoundland.

Which language should a Canadian student use for USACO?

USACO accepts C, C++, Java and Python, and calls C++ the clear answer at higher levels because it is the only language supported at the IOI. It aims to make all Bronze problems fully solvable in Python. The CCO also requires C++.

Can students use AI in USACO?

No. USACO prohibits generative AI such as ChatGPT during contests, and the CCC rules forbid it too. Outside contests, ChatGPT needs a parent's permission for anyone under 18.

When are your classes for Canadian families?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. We keep weekend slots clear of USACO weekends where a student is competing.
