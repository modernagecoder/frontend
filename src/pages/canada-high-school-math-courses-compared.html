Grade 12 math by province, compared

A student in Toronto takes MHF4U, one in Vancouver takes Pre-calculus 12, one in Calgary takes Mathematics 30-1, and one in Montreal finishes secondary school a year earlier with Secondary V math in one of three options. Parents moving between provinces, and students applying to a university in another one, keep asking whether these are "the same course". They overlap a great deal and they are not identical. This page sets the courses side by side using the names each ministry prints, shows what two Ontario universities ask for, and marks plainly the courses we could not verify. We teach alongside these school courses; we do not grant credits, in Ontario or in any other province.

In short

Grade 12 university-prep math is named differently in each province: MHF4U and MCV4U in Ontario, Pre-calculus 12 in BC, Mathematics 30-1 in Alberta, Pre-Calculus 30 in Saskatchewan. They overlap without being identical; each university sets its own requirements. Modern Age Coders teaches them live online, in a batch or one to one, through its Ontario Grade 12 University Math course.

Ontario

Ontario: the MCR3U, MHF4U, MCV4U chain

Ontario's senior math courses go by five-character codes such as MHF4U. The university route runs through three of them in order, and the ministry's own prerequisite lines fix that order.

Ontario's university-preparation math chain, from the ministry's course descriptions (read 22 October 2026)

What two universities ask Ontario applicants for

The University of Waterloo's Computer Science page asks Ontario students for "Six Grade 12 U and/or M courses including" Advanced Functions and Calculus and Vectors, "Any Grade 12 U English" and "One other 4U course".

The University of Toronto starts from an "Ontario Secondary School Diploma (OSSD) with six subjects at the 4U/M level, including English", then varies by campus: its St. George Computer Science listing names MCV4U, Mississauga names MHF4U and MCV4U with MDM4U "Recommended", and Scarborough names MCV4U and MHF4U.

Why this matters outside Ontario

Neither page assumes every applicant is from Ontario. Waterloo's asks "Not studying in Ontario?" and points to a search of its requirements; Toronto's offers a "Change School System" choice listing every province and territory.

That is the practical answer to "is my course equivalent?": the university has already decided, program by program, and publishes it under your own province's name. Read that page rather than a comparison chart, including this one.

Sources: Ontario Ministry of Education curriculum service, senior mathematics course descriptions; University of Waterloo, Computer Science; University of Toronto, Computer Science; all read 22 October 2026. Requirements change each admission cycle, so check the university's page in the autumn you apply.

BC, Alberta, Saskatchewan, Manitoba

Western Canada: pre-calculus first, calculus as its own course

The four western provinces share a broad shape: a pre-calculus course in the final year, often a separate calculus course beside or after it, and parallel courses for students headed elsewhere. The names and the exams differ.

British Columbia

BC's Mathematics curriculum lists Pre-calculus 11 and 12, Calculus 12, Foundations of Mathematics 11 and 12, Workplace Mathematics 10 and Statistics 12. Calculus 12 lists "parent functions from Pre-Calculus 12" in its content, and its big ideas include "Differentiation and integration are defined using limits." and "The fundamental theorem of calculus describes the relationship between integrals and antiderivatives."

Alberta

Alberta's diploma exam list includes Mathematics 30-1 and Mathematics 30-2. The province says "70% of the final mark comes from course work. The remaining 30% comes from the diploma exam.", a weighting that "returned to 30% effective September 1, 2023". We could not read a primary page for Mathematics 31 or the Grade 10 and 11 sequence, so we leave them out.

Saskatchewan and Manitoba

Saskatchewan's curriculum site lists Pre-Calculus 20 and 30, Calculus 30, Foundations of Mathematics 20 and 30, and Workplace and Apprenticeship Mathematics 10 to 30. Manitoba's Grade 12 page lists Pre-Calculus Mathematics, Introduction to Calculus, Applied Mathematics, Essential Mathematics and Advanced Mathematics I and II.

The Alberta exam is part of the mark

Alberta's government publishes a fixed exam share of the Grade 12 math mark: 30 percent from the diploma exam, 70 from course work. A student moving into Alberta for Grade 12 should know that a single sitting carries almost a third of the result.

That changes how to prepare. Course work rewards steady effort; a diploma exam rewards being able to do it alone, on time, without notes. We practise both, separately.

The Northwest Territories

The NWT is adopting BC's curriculum. Its education department lists, as completed, "Determining the minimum Grade 12 graduation requirements for students using BC courses", so NWT students in the senior grades are moving onto BC's course names.

For a family in Yellowknife comparing courses with a university in the south, the BC row of the table above is the one to start from, with the NWT's own graduation rules on top.

Sources: BC Calculus 12; Alberta diploma exams; Saskatchewan Pre-Calculus 30; Manitoba Grade 12 math; all read 22 October 2026.

Quebec

Quebec: three options at Secondary V, then CEGEP

Quebec does not fit a Grade 12 column. Secondary school ends at Secondary V, and students who aim for university first complete a pre-university program at a CEGEP. The math choice that matters is made earlier, among three options in Secondary Cycle Two.

The three options

The Québec Education Program's math document for Secondary Cycle Two says: "They are the Cultural, Social and Technical option, the Technical and Scientific option and the Science option." Of the Science option it says students "are required to perform more complex algebraic operations".

The same document adds that "All of these options allow students to enroll in a preuniversity program." That is the general rule for entering CEGEP.

Where the option starts to matter

Individual CEGEP programs can add conditions. The ministry's Science program (200.B0) lists as special conditions for admission Secondary V Chemistry, Secondary V Physics and "Secondary V Mathematics: Technical and Scientific option or Science option".

So both statements are true together: every option opens the door to some pre-university program, while science programs ask for one of two options in particular. Our online math tutor page for Canada covers math help across the provinces more broadly.

A Quebec student earns a subject's credits only with a final result of 60 percent or more: Québec.ca says "To successfully complete a subject and earn its related credits, the student must obtain a final result of 60% or higher." We help with the learning behind that mark; the credit, as in every province, comes from the school system.

Sources: Québec Education Program, Mathematics, Secondary Cycle Two; Québec.ca pre-university programs (Science 200.B0); final results in Secondary IV and V; all read 22 October 2026.

Two patterns stand out. First, Ontario splits the material differently from BC: functions and the start of rates of change sit in MHF4U, and derivatives plus vectors in MCV4U, while BC places limits, derivatives and integrals together in Calculus 12. Second, integration appears in the BC Calculus 12 description and not in the MCV4U description we read. A student moving from BC to Ontario, or the other way, should expect some topics to arrive earlier or later than at the old school.

What does not change across provinces

Every course in the table leans on the same base: fluent algebra, comfort with function notation, and the habit of checking an answer by a second route. A student short on any of the three struggles in every province.

That is why our first class in Grade 12 math is mostly diagnosis. The course name tells us the syllabus; the student's algebra tells us where to start.

What does change

Order, depth and the exam. Alberta's diploma exam is part of the Grade 12 mark; Quebec sets a ministerial math exam in Secondary IV; Ontario's Grade 12 marks come from the school.

And the name on the transcript. A university reading an out-of-province transcript compares content, which is why it publishes its own table for each system.

A worked example

One problem every Grade 12 university-prep course can reach

A problem that sits in the overlap of Advanced Functions, Pre-calculus 12 and Mathematics 30-1 (exponential and logarithmic equations), with a second part for students in a calculus course. The problem and solution are ours.

The problem (ours). A sourdough starter doubles in size every 6 hours, so its size after t hours is A(t) = A0 x 2^(t/6), where A0 is the starting size. (a) After how many hours is it ten times its starting size? (b) For a calculus course: how fast is it growing at that moment, in starting sizes per hour?

  1. Set up the equation. Ten times the start means A(t) = 10 x A0, so 2^(t/6) = 10. The A0 cancels, which is why the answer does not depend on how big the starter was.
  2. Take logarithms of both sides. (t/6) x log 2 = log 10, so t = 6 x log 10 / log 2. Any base works, as long as it is the same on both sides.
  3. Evaluate. log 10 / log 2 is about 3.3219, so t is about 19.93 hours: a little under 20 hours.
  4. Check the sense of it. Three doublings (18 hours) give 8 times; four (24 hours) give 16 times. Ten times must fall between 18 and 24 hours, nearer 18. It does.
  5. Part (b), the derivative. A'(t) = A0 x (ln 2 / 6) x 2^(t/6). At the moment the starter is ten times its size, 2^(t/6) = 10, so A'(t) = 10 x ln 2 / 6, about 1.155 starting sizes per hour.

Where it sits by course, from the descriptions on this page: part (a) is the logarithmic work MHF4U names, and the exponential equations Pre-calculus 12 and Mathematics 30-1 students meet; part (b) is the derivative of an exponential function that MCV4U names and that BC's Calculus 12 lists among transcendental functions. If you want to rehearse the algebra behind step 1, our guide to solving simultaneous equations uses the same habit of checking a result by substitution.

Changing provinces

Moving provinces, or applying out of province: five steps

Our checklist for families, in order. None of it replaces the school guidance office, which makes the actual placement decision.

  1. Write down the exact course names and codes from the current transcript, with the mark and the year. "Grade 12 math" is not enough for anyone to place a student.
  2. Find the matching row in the new province in the table above, then ask the receiving school how it places students arriving from your province. Placement is the school's decision.
  3. Open each university program's own requirement page and switch it to your school system. Toronto's page offers a "Change School System" option for exactly this.
  4. Compare topics, not titles. Use the topic map above: if the new course assumes something the old one had not reached, such as limits or vectors, plan for it before the term starts.
  5. Check the exam rules. Moving into Alberta adds a diploma exam worth 30 percent of the final mark; moving within Quebec into Secondary IV means a ministerial math exam. Know the date before the first week.

If the student is a year ahead

A student who has finished Secondary V has completed secondary school in Quebec. Where that student continues, and at what level, is a conversation with the receiving school or college, not a chart.

Our part is the same in every case: make sure the mathematics itself is solid, so the placement decision is the only open question.

If the student is a year behind

A gap of one course, say Calculus 12 never taken before moving to a program that wants MCV4U, is usually closed in a term of steady work, not a summer of cramming.

We plan that term with the student, starting from the topics the new course assumes and the date its first test falls.

Questions from Canadian families

Grade 12 math across Canada: common questions

What is the equivalent of MHF4U in other provinces?

There is no official one-to-one equivalent. The closest courses by name and level are Pre-calculus 12 in BC, Mathematics 30-1 in Alberta, Pre-Calculus 30 in Saskatchewan and Grade 12 Pre-Calculus Mathematics in Manitoba. Universities decide for themselves which course meets each of their requirements.

Is Pre-calculus 12 the same as Math 30-1?

They play a similar role in BC and Alberta, as the main university-preparation math course of the final year, but they are separate provincial courses. Alberta adds a diploma exam that counts for 30 percent of the final mark. Check each university program's requirement page for your province.

What is MCV4U?

MCV4U is Ontario's Grade 12 Calculus and Vectors course. Its description covers derivatives of polynomial, sinusoidal, exponential, rational and radical functions, and vectors with lines and planes in three dimensions. MHF4U must be taken before or at the same time.

Does BC have a calculus course in high school?

Yes. BC's curriculum lists Calculus 12, which defines differentiation and integration using limits and includes the fundamental theorem of calculus. It builds on functions from Pre-calculus 12.

What is the Grade 12 math in Quebec?

Quebec secondary school ends at Secondary V. Students take Secondary V math in one of three options: Cultural, Social and Technical; Technical and Scientific; or Science. CEGEP Science programs ask for Secondary V math in the Technical and Scientific option or the Science option.

Which math do universities need for computer science?

It varies by university and program. For Ontario applicants, the University of Waterloo lists Advanced Functions and Calculus and Vectors among six Grade 12 U or M courses, and the University of Toronto lists MCV4U, or MHF4U and MCV4U, depending on the campus. Applicants from other provinces should switch the university page to their own school system.

What about Alberta Math 31?

We could not read a primary Alberta page for Mathematics 31 on 22 October 2026, so we do not describe it here. The Alberta course we could verify for university-preparation math is Mathematics 30-1, with Mathematics 30-2 as the other diploma course.

Do your classes give high school math credit?

No. Our classes run beside the school course, and we do not grant credits in Ontario or anywhere else. Credits, marks and diploma exams stay with the school system.

When are classes for students across Canada?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. We set the time in your own province's clock.

Can AI tools do Grade 12 math homework?

They can produce answers, and that is the problem: an answer without the reasoning does not survive a test or a diploma exam. We teach the reasoning. ChatGPT needs a parent's permission for anyone under 18.
