Alberta Math 30-1 diploma exam prep, 2026 to 2027

The Mathematics 30-1 diploma exam is one sitting of three hours that decides 30 per cent of a Grade 12 student’s final mark in Alberta’s calculus-track course. It is written on a computer, except for the written responses, which go on paper and are marked by Alberta teachers against a published scoring guide. This page collects what Alberta Education and Childcare has published for 2026 to 2027, works two written responses the way the scoring guide rewards, and lays out an eight-week plan. Reading the schedule

Under each month Alberta adds the same line: “All students are provided with up to double the official time noted above if they require it.” So the 9 AM to 12 PM slot is the designed time, and a student may keep working past noon. The June page also notes “No exams scheduled on June 21, National Indigenous Peoples Day.”

January and June are the large administrations. The bulletin says some subjects “may have two distinct forms” then, both equated to the same baseline, and that each school receives one form per subject.

Who registers whom

Alberta’s writing page is clear on registration: “School staff will register students enrolled in diploma exams courses for the first time.” A student repeating the exam is different: “Students who retake courses will not automatically be registered”, and they register online through myPass.

The student guide confirms the five writing sessions for Math 30-1 (November, January, April, June and August), so a student who misses one sitting or wants another attempt has several choices in the same school year.

What happened in January 2026. Alberta’s overview page records that “Alberta’s government has cancelled the January 2026 diploma examinations and Grade 9 Provincial Achievement Tests (PATs).” Students could write the missed exam in April or June 2026 instead; those who chose not to “will receive their school-awarded mark as their final official course mark”, and “A notation will be added to transcripts confirming that no diploma exam was written.” The 2026 to 2027 schedule above restores a January sitting, on 20 January 2027.

The 70 and 30

How the diploma exam changes the final Math 30-1 mark

Alberta states the rule in two sentences: “70% of the final mark comes from course work. The remaining 30% comes from the diploma exam.” It adds that the weighting “returned to 30% effective September 1, 2023”, and that “To pass a diploma exam course, you must earn a final combined mark of at least 50%.”

The 70 and 30 rule applied to sample marks (our arithmetic; Alberta’s own example is the first row)

What the arithmetic tells a student

Each point on the diploma exam moves the final mark by 0.3 of a point; each point of school mark moves it by 0.7. So the exam cannot be ignored, but neither can the term. A student who coasts through the units and hopes to rescue the year in June is working against a 70 per cent weight.

The table is arithmetic, not a forecast. Nobody can honestly promise a diploma mark in advance, and we do not. What we can do is make sure the student walks in having practised the format, the timing and the written responses until none of them is a surprise.

Rescores and rewrites

A student who doubts a result can ask for a rescore through myPass, but Alberta warns that “The mark resulting from rescoring becomes your final exam mark, even if it is lower than the original mark.” For Math 30-1 only the written responses are remarked; the machine-scored part is not rescored.

A rewrite is the other route: “You may also rewrite a diploma exam without repeating the diploma course.” Alberta’s math fact sheet adds that students rewriting Math 30-1 “must rewrite the entire exam”, and the transcript keeps the highest diploma mark and highest school-awarded mark.

Numerical responses have their own rules

For a numerical-response question the student types a number, rounded “to the nearest tenth or nearest hundredth, as specified in the question.” Two instructions trip people up. First, for a value between 0 and 1, “record the 0 before the decimal place”. Second, “Students should retain all decimals throughout the question, and rounding should occur only in the final answer.”

We drill both with every calculator question in class: the student says the rounding instruction aloud before pressing a key, and stores intermediate results in calculator memory instead of copying a rounded value back in.

The calculator rules for 2026 to 2027

Alberta’s August 2026 calculator document lists approved graphing models, among them the TI-84 Plus CE, the TI-Nspire CX II and the Casio fx-9750 GIII “(must be in Examination Mode)”; the TI-Nspire CX II CAS is allowed only with “CAS must be disabled”.

Prohibited properties include “symbolic manipulation capabilities”, the “ability to provide trigonometric calculation exact values” and the “ability to simplify radicals and rationalize denominators”. In other words, the calculator will not hand a student the exact value of cos(π/6). That has to come from the unit circle.

Partial marks are real marks

The bulletin tells teachers to encourage students “to try to solve the problems in both parts because an attempt at a solution may be worth partial marks.” On a 5-mark question, a correct setup that stalls can still earn 1 or 1.5 on each part. A blank earns nothing.

Alberta’s math fact sheet puts the other side plainly: “Students will not be able to obtain full credit for a response without supporting work.” A correct final number with no work beside it is not a full-mark response.

The words markers keep writing about

In the commentary, markers say many students did not show they know “the directing word algebraically requires students to demonstrate a complete algebraic process or procedure”, or that compare needs an explicit statement of similarities and differences. Determine means every step.

We teach the directing words the way a language teacher teaches verbs: each one with a short example response, then a question where the student has to say which word is in bold before writing anything.

Worked responses, ours

Two written responses, set out the way the scoring guide rewards

Both questions are ours, written for this page in the diploma’s two-part shape. They are not Alberta Education questions, and they do not adapt any released or secured item. Each targets a weakness Alberta’s commentary names: specific terms of a binomial with non-linear terms, and counting with several constraints.

Response 1. Part 1 (3 marks): determine the term containing x7 in the expansion of (x2 − 2/x)8. Part 2 (2 marks): justify whether the expansion has a constant term.

  1. Write the general term. tk+1 = 8Ck (x2)8−k (−2/x)k. Keep the minus sign inside the second term; dropping it is the commonest sign error.
  2. Collect the powers of x. (x2)8−k gives x16−2k, and (1/x)k gives x−k, so the term is 8Ck (−2)k x16−3k.
  3. Solve for k. 16 − 3k = 7, so k = 3. The term wanted is t4, the fourth term, because the general term is numbered k + 1.
  4. Evaluate. 8C3 = 56 and (−2)3 = −8, so the term is −448x7. That completes part 1.
  5. Part 2: state the position, then the reason. There is no constant term. A constant term needs 16 − 3k = 0, which gives k = 16/3, and k must be a whole number from 0 to 8. Since no whole number works, no term is free of x.

Response 2. Part 1 (3 marks): using the digits 1 to 7 with no repetition, determine how many four-digit even numbers greater than 5000 can be formed. Part 2 (2 marks): explain why the count cannot be found with one product of choices.

  1. List the constraints. There are three: the first digit must be 5, 6 or 7 (greater than 5000), the last digit must be 2, 4 or 6 (even), and no digit repeats.
  2. Spot the clash. The digit 6 appears in both lists. If 6 is used first, it cannot also be last, so the number of choices for the last digit depends on the first. That is the signal to split into cases.
  3. Case A: the first digit is 6. The last digit is 2 or 4: 2 choices. The middle two places use any 2 of the 5 remaining digits in order: 5P2 = 20. Case A gives 1 × 20 × 2 = 40.
  4. Case B: the first digit is 5 or 7. The last digit is 2, 4 or 6: 3 choices. The middle again has 5P2 = 20 arrangements. Case B gives 2 × 20 × 3 = 120.
  5. Add the cases. 40 + 120 = 160 numbers. (We confirmed the count by listing every arrangement in a short program.)
  6. Part 2. A single product would treat the choices for the first and last digits as independent, giving 3 × 3 × 20 = 180, which counts numbers such as 6 _ _ 6 that repeat a digit. The cases remove those 20 impossible numbers.

Notice the shape of both responses: a position or formula first, one step per line, and a final sentence that answers the question in words. That shape is what Alberta’s written-response instructions ask for when they say responses “must be presented in a well-organized manner” and “must address all aspects of the question.”

A short mock, ours

Eight questions in the diploma format, with answers

Seven numerical-response questions and one written response, all ours and none taken from Alberta Education. Give yourself 25 minutes, follow the rounding instructions exactly, and use a calculator only where the question allows it.

  1. Numerical response, no calculator. Evaluate log248 − log23.
  2. Numerical response, calculator. Solve 52x = 40. Record x to the nearest hundredth.
  3. Numerical response. The graph of y = x3 is stretched vertically by a factor of 2. How many invariant points are there?
  4. Numerical response. Record the period of y = 4cos(3x) − 1, in radians, to the nearest hundredth.
  5. Numerical response. A committee of 3 is chosen from 7 students, and one named student must be on it. How many committees are possible?
  6. Numerical response. Record cos(π/3) to the nearest hundredth.
  7. Numerical response. In the expansion of (a + b)9, how many terms are there?
  8. Written response. Part 1: algebraically determine the zeros of P(x) = 2x3 + x2 − 13x + 6. Part 2: sketch y = P(x), labelling the intercepts.
  1. 4. The quotient law gives log2(48/3) = log216 = 4.
  2. 1.15. 2x = log 40 / log 5, so x = 1.1460..., which rounds to 1.15. Rounding log 40 to 1.6 too early gives a different answer.
  3. 1. A vertical stretch leaves only points with y = 0 unchanged, and y = x3 crosses the x-axis once, at the origin.
  4. 2.09. The period is 2π/3 = 2.0943..., or 2.09.
  5. 15. With the named student placed, choose 2 more from the other 6: 6C2 = 15.
  6. 0.50. cos(π/3) is exactly 1/2. To the nearest hundredth it is recorded as 0.50, with the 0 before the decimal place, as Alberta instructs.
  7. 10. An expansion to the power n has n + 1 terms.
  8. Zeros 2, 1/2 and −3; y-intercept 6. P(2) = 16 + 4 − 26 + 6 = 0, so x − 2 is a factor. Dividing gives 2x2 + 5x − 3 = (2x − 1)(x + 3). For the sketch: positive leading coefficient and odd degree, so the graph falls to the left and rises to the right, crossing at x = −3, 1/2 and 2, and meeting the y-axis at 6.

Exam-day checklist

The night before: calculator charged or fresh batteries, HB pencils, pens and photo ID packed. Check the calculator is an approved model, because a student may bring only one, and the supervisor clears and configures it before and after.

In the room: read every written-response question before starting the machine-scored ones, so the brain works on them in the background. Flag hard questions and come back. Answer every machine-scored question; there is no reason to leave one blank.

Where the practice material lives

We never copy Alberta’s items into our classes. Students sit the official practice on Alberta’s own platform; our lessons use original questions written to the same outcomes, so the official material stays fresh for timed rehearsal.

Our part, and the clock

What we do, what the school does, and Alberta time

Families sometimes ask whether a tutor can stand in for the course. For Math 30-1 the answer is no, and the reason is structural.

Who does what

The school teaches the course, awards the 70 per cent and grants the credit. Alberta sets, administers and marks the diploma exam that supplies the other 30 per cent. We do neither: we teach alongside the student’s Math 30-1 class and we do not grant provincial credits.

What we add is time with one teacher who knows the format: worked responses marked against the guide, practice timed to the sitting, and a student who arrives knowing exactly what each directing word asks for.

Alberta time and India time

Alberta does not change its clocks: it sits at UTC-6 through the whole year (IANA tz release 2026e), so a class booked for a Saturday morning in Calgary or Edmonton keeps the same local time in November and in June.

Our teachers keep India time, which runs 11 hours 30 minutes ahead of Alberta in every month. A morning class in Alberta is evening in India, which is why weekend mornings are easy to schedule.

Questions about the diploma

Math 30-1 diploma exam: common questions

When is the Math 30-1 diploma exam in 2027?

Alberta's 2026 to 2027 schedule lists Wednesday 20 January, Thursday 8 April, Thursday 17 June and Thursday 5 August 2027, each from 9 AM to 12 PM, after a first sitting on Tuesday 3 November 2026.

How long is the Math 30-1 diploma exam?

It is designed to be completed in 3 hours, and every student may take 3 more hours if needed, for a total of 6 hours at most.

How much is the diploma exam worth?

Thirty percent of the final course mark. Alberta says the weighting returned to 30 percent on 1 September 2023, with the other 70 percent coming from the school-awarded mark. The combined mark must be at least 50 percent to pass the course.

How many questions are on the Math 30-1 diploma?

For 2026 to 2027 there are 32 machine-scored questions worth 75 percent of the exam and 3 written-response questions worth 25 percent. Each written response is worth 5 marks and has two parts.

Is the Math 30-1 diploma exam written on a computer?

Mostly. Alberta now runs diploma exams on a digital platform, so the machine-scored questions are answered on screen. The written responses are written on printed answer sheets that the school scans and uploads after the exam.

Which calculator can I use on the Math 30-1 diploma?

One approved graphing calculator, cleared and configured by the supervisor before and after. Alberta's 2026 to 2027 list includes the TI-84 Plus CE and the Casio fx-9750 GIII in Examination Mode. Calculators that give exact trigonometric values or simplify radicals are not allowed.

Can I rewrite the Math 30-1 diploma exam?

Yes. Alberta lets students rewrite a diploma exam without repeating the course, the whole exam must be rewritten, and the transcript keeps the highest diploma mark. Students who retake must register themselves through myPass.

Should I ask for a rescore?

Think carefully first. Only the written responses are remarked, and Alberta says the rescored mark becomes the final exam mark even if it is lower than the original.

Do you grant credit for Math 30-1?

No. Our lessons run alongside the school's course, and we do not grant provincial credits. The school grants the credit and Alberta marks the diploma exam.

When can my teen take classes from Alberta?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Alberta stays on UTC-6 all year, so the local time of a class does not shift.

What does diploma preparation with you cost?

The first class is free. Families who carry on pay a monthly fee for eight live classes and can leave at the end of a month. The Fees part of this page shows the live figures.
