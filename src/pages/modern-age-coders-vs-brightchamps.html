Modern Age Coders vs BrightCHAMPS for Canadian families

Unlike most comparisons a Canadian parent meets, this one is between two schools that work the same basic way. BrightCHAMPS and Modern Age Coders both teach coding live, online, with a teacher on screen, in groups or one to one. So the useful question is not live against recorded, or centre against home. It is how each school packages a child's learning, and where that learning can lead. BrightCHAMPS, on its Canada pages, offers coding as grade-band courses beside subjects such as financial literacy, robotics and communication. We teach coding and math as one continuing path that runs from age 6 into Canadian school computer science, contests and adult study. This page sets out both, from BrightCHAMPS' own pages as read on 22 October 2026.

Where the two are genuinely alike

Live teaching, a teacher on screen, groups or one to one, blocks for young beginners and typed languages later: on all of these, BrightCHAMPS' Canada page and ours describe much the same thing. A family who wants live online classes rather than videos or a centre can be well served by either.

That is worth saying plainly, because it means the choice is a matter of fit, not of one school doing the job and the other not.

Where they part

The differences are structural. BrightCHAMPS builds its coding offer as a set of courses for each grade band, inside a wider menu of children's subjects. We build one long coding and math path whose later stages are Canadian school computer science, contest preparation and courses for adults.

Neither shape is right for every child. The sections below show what each looks like and who it tends to suit.

What a grade band gives a family

A grade band is easy to understand: a parent finds their child's grade, picks a level, and sees a defined course with a set of projects. BrightCHAMPS says it offers "20 structured online coding courses" for Canada, and its pages list each course's projects in advance.

Its course pages also show what a session is like: "Teachers give a short run-through, then kids try it themselves." Recordings are not shared, and families get project files or notes instead.

What one continuing path gives a family

Our courses are not boxed by grade. A twelve-year-old who has outgrown blocks moves to Python in the same school, and a fifteen-year-old taking ICS3U at school can join a companion course built around that Ontario course.

The same school then carries on past Grade 12: university-level programming, data structures, and courses for working adults. A student can stay with the path for years, and a parent can start their own course in the same place.

Why this matters for a Canadian family

By Grade 11 many Ontario students meet computer science as a credit course with marks attached. A teacher who already knows the student's code from earlier years can help with that course from the first week.

Contest students benefit in the same way. A student who has written typed code with us since age 11 reaches CCC Junior with years of habit already in place.

When a grade band is enough

Not every child wants a path. Many want one good course this year, to try coding and see whether it sticks. For them, a defined grade-band course is a sensible unit, and BrightCHAMPS offers exactly that.

A family can also mix: a short course elsewhere to test interest, and a longer path later once the interest is clear.

A worked example

How we teach debugging: say what you expect, then make the code show its work

This walkthrough is ours, written for our teen classes; it is not from BrightCHAMPS or any other course. The bug is a common one, the kind that turns up in a first school computer science assignment. What matters is the order of the steps, which we repeat until students do it without being asked.

  1. State the expectation. Before touching the code, the student works it out by hand: 7 + 9 + 6 + 10 = 32, and 32 / 4 = 8.0. Now there is a definite gap between 8.0 and 6.25 to explain.
  2. Make the loop show its work. Instead of guessing, the student adds one print line inside the loop so the program reports every value it adds.
  3. Read the evidence. The output shows indexes 1, 2 and 3 only. The first score, 7, is never added: 9 + 6 + 10 = 25, and 25 / 4 = 6.25. The bug is now found, not guessed.
  4. Fix the cause, not the symptom. range(1, len(scores)) starts at 1. The cleanest fix loops over the scores themselves, so there is no index to get wrong.
  5. Write a test that would have caught it. A list whose first score is the only large one, [100, 0, 0, 0], must average 25.0. The old code would print 0.0 for it, so the test proves the fix.

Why the order matters

Students who skip step 1 cannot tell whether a change helped, because they never said what right looks like. Students who skip step 2 change lines at random until something works, and learn nothing about why.

The habit transfers to any language. In Java the print line becomes System.out.println, and the steps stay the same.

Where this sits in a Canadian course

Loops over a list of marks are standard early material in Ontario Grade 11 computer science, and an off-by-one start is one of the most common slips. A companion class turns the slip into a lesson rather than a lost mark.

For contest students, step 5 is the important one: the CCC grader tests code on inputs the student never sees, so writing your own awkward test is part of preparation.

Deciding

Four checks before you choose a live online school

These apply to any online coding school, including the two on this page.

  1. Ask who teaches. Will your child have the same teacher each week? Continuity is what lets a teacher spot a recurring slip.
  2. Ask what happens after this course. When the course or grade band ends, where does your child go next, and does the school know Canadian school courses and contests?
  3. Ask about class times in your province. A class set in one time zone can land awkwardly in British Columbia or Newfoundland. Check the time in your own clock, including after the clocks change.
  4. Take the free first class at both. Afterwards, ask your child to explain one thing they made. The class that left them able to explain more is usually the right one.

Families comparing more than two schools may find our coding classes in Canada hub useful, and our Canadian time zones page covers class times for every province.

Questions from Canadian parents

BrightCHAMPS or Modern Age Coders: questions parents ask

Is Modern Age Coders part of BrightCHAMPS?

No. They are separate companies, and Modern Age Coders has no partnership or affiliation with BrightCHAMPS. The details about BrightCHAMPS on this page come from its own Canada pages, read on 22 October 2026.

Does BrightCHAMPS teach coding in Canada?

Yes. BrightCHAMPS has Canada pages at brightchamps.com/en-ca, including one titled Online Coding Classes for Kids in Canada. It describes live, teacher-led sessions and lists courses by grade band from Grade 1 to Grade 12.

Are BrightCHAMPS classes live or recorded?

BrightCHAMPS says its classes are taught live, that sessions are live only, and that recordings are not shared to protect privacy. Our classes are also live with a teacher.

What is the main difference between BrightCHAMPS and Modern Age Coders?

Both teach live online. BrightCHAMPS offers coding as grade-band courses beside other children's subjects such as financial literacy and robotics. We teach coding and math as one path from age 6 to adult, including companion classes for Ontario computer science and CCC preparation.

Is there a BrightCHAMPS alternative for Canadian teens?

Modern Age Coders teaches teens live online in Python, Java and C++, with a companion course for students taking ICS3U or ICS4U at school and a course for the Canadian Computing Competition. We do not grant Ontario credits; the school grants the credit.

How long is a BrightCHAMPS session?

The BrightCHAMPS Canada course pages we read give 60 minutes per session. We run two live classes a week, eight a month.

Does BrightCHAMPS publish its prices for Canada?

Yes, its Canada course pages show prices, marked taxes not included. We do not repeat them here. Our own monthly fee is listed on our pricing page in your region's currency.

Can my child switch from BrightCHAMPS to Modern Age Coders?

Yes. Book a free first class and the teacher will place your child by what they can do, whatever course they took before. Many students who have finished project-led courses start with us on typed problem solving.

What times are your classes for families in Canada?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. The time is agreed in your province's own time zone.

Do you teach subjects other than coding?

We teach coding and mathematics only, from age 6 to adult. If you want several other children's subjects such as communication or financial literacy from one provider, BrightCHAMPS lists courses in those.
