The six "No" rows

The District of Columbia, Maine, Minnesota, Oregon, South Dakota and Vermont show no adopted K-12 CS standards in the 2025 data, and Nebraska shows standards in progress. Schools in those places still teach computer science; they simply lack a statewide set of grade-band expectations under the report’s rubric.

South Dakota is a useful reminder that one column rarely tells the whole story. Its state page says AI concepts are already in its elementary and middle school CS standards and that "high school standards development is in progress".

Standards are not requirements

Having standards does not mean every student must take computer science. Standards describe what a CS course or unit should cover; a graduation requirement decides who must take one. A state can have detailed standards and no requirement at all.

For a parent, standards are most useful as a checklist: if your child’s school offers CS, the state standards tell you what a good course in that grade band should include.

The shared framework

Why most state standards look alike: the five core concepts

Many states wrote their standards from the K-12 Computer Science Framework, a set of conceptual guidelines developed by national organizations with state representatives. It is not itself a set of standards, which is why each state’s version differs in detail.

The K-12 Computer Science Framework: core concepts and core practices (k12cs.org, read 17 October 2026)

How the framework is organized

The framework sets expectations for each concept at four grade band endpoints: the end of grades 2, 5, 8 and 12. The practices are written as a narrative of growth rather than split by grade.

Its FAQ says plainly that "The framework is not a set of standards." States turn it into measurable performance expectations, and that is where their versions diverge.

One state as an example

North Carolina’s standards are "derived originally from the CSTA standards" and built around the same five concepts. They are organized by grade band from kindergarten to grade 8 (K-2, 3-5 and 6-8) and by course in high school, starting with "Introduction to Computer Science (ICS)".

Expect a similar shape in most states: grade bands through middle school, then course-based standards in high school.

Sources: K-12 Computer Science Framework and its FAQ; NC Department of Public Instruction, NC Computer Science Standards. Read 17 October 2026.

Reading your state&rsquo;s standards

How to read a state CS standard in five minutes

Our short guide for parents opening a state standards document for the first time.

  1. Find your child’s grade band. Most states group kindergarten to grade 8 into bands such as K-2, 3-5 and 6-8.
  2. Look for the concept label on each line, often an abbreviation of one of the five concepts, such as Algorithms and Programming.
  3. Read the verb. "Create", "debug" and "analyze" ask far more than "identify" or "describe".
  4. Check for AI. In a few states it is a named strand; in most it appears, if at all, inside Data and Analysis or Impacts of Computing.
  5. Compare with the school course. Ask which standards the course covers and how student work is assessed against them.

Revision cycles matter

CodeAI’s newer rubric, revised on September 24, 2026, treats standards as historic if they "have not been revised within the past 5 years". Computing changes quickly, so the age of a document is worth noticing.

That newer rubric was published after the 2025 data, so the table on this page does not use its tiers.

What standards leave out

Standards describe outcomes, not lessons. They will not tell you which language a school uses, how many hours a week CS gets, or whether a teacher is trained in it.

Those questions belong to the school. The standards simply give you the vocabulary to ask them.

For grades 3 to 5

Do the same five tasks out loud or on paper, with block code for the loop. The concepts are the same; the tools are simpler.

For grades 6 to 8

Write the loop and the data task in real code, and write two sentences for the impact task.

For high school

Extend each one: a packet simulation, a full data report, and a written argument with sources.

Data and Analysis in code

A Python example for the data task

The data task from the practice set, in a few lines of Python. It uses only the standard library, so it runs anywhere Python 3 is installed.

Which standards this touches

Data and Analysis, through collecting and summarising data. Algorithms and Programming, through variables, lists and built-in functions.

The extension question "why might the mean and median differ?" moves the task from following steps to analysing, which is what higher-level standards ask for.

Make it harder

Ask the student to read the temperatures from a file instead of typing them, then to chart them. Both steps appear in high school standards in many states.

Then ask what would change with thirty days instead of seven. Larger samples are where summary statistics start to earn their keep.

Computer science standards: common questions

How many states have K-12 computer science standards?

Reading the state pages of Code.org (2025), 2025 State of AI + CS Education, we count 44 states with adopted standards and Nebraska in progress. DC, Maine, Minnesota, Oregon, South Dakota and Vermont were marked as not having them.

Which states include AI in their computer science standards?

Four, according to the 2025 report: Colorado, North Dakota, Ohio and Virginia. Iowa, Kentucky, Oregon, South Dakota, Tennessee and Wisconsin were marked as in progress.

What are the five core concepts?

Computing Systems; Networks and the Internet; Data and Analysis; Algorithms and Programming; and Impacts of Computing. They come from the K-12 Computer Science Framework, which many states used to write their standards.

Are standards the same as a graduation requirement?

No. Standards describe what a CS course should cover in each grade band. A graduation requirement decides whether every student must take one. A state can have one without the other.

What does it mean when a state is in progress?

In the report, it means the state had publicly begun creating or revising standards but had not published them when the data were finalized on December 1, 2025.

Is the K-12 CS Framework a set of standards?

No. Its own FAQ says the framework is not a set of standards. States use it to write measurable standards of their own.

My state has no standards. Does that matter for my child?

It mainly means schools have less shared guidance. Many still teach strong CS. The five core concepts are a sensible checklist for any course, wherever you live.

Do your classes follow a particular state's standards?

Our courses cover the five core concepts that most state standards share, and in 1-on-1 classes the teacher can align topics to your state's grade band.

When can my child take classes?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays.

Are you connected with Code.org or a state department?

Not at all. Modern Age Coders runs its own classes; this page only reports what those organisations publish, with links.
