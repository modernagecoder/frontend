# Coding and AI Classes for Girls in Oman

> At Oman's 2025 national collegiate programming contest, 194 contestants competed and the split was exactly 97 male and 97 female. Modern Age Coders teaches girls in Oman the same five-rung Python and AI track it teaches everybody: hand-written Python, real data, machine learning with a measured error, AI agents, and a published GitHub project. Batches are mixed and grouped by ability, one to one is available for families who prefer it, and there is no separate or lighter syllabus. Group teaching is USD 100 a month, private USD 150, and the first class is free.

**What this does show.** At the level of Oman's national collegiate programming contest, women were not a minority presence, a special category or a diversity target. They were half the field. Whatever a family might assume about who competes in this subject in the Gulf, the ministry's own participation figures do not support it. That matters because a great many decisions about what a child should study rest on an assumption about who does that subject, and assumptions of that kind are usually inherited rather than checked. This one is checkable and published.

**What it does not show.** One contest, at university level, in one year, says nothing definitive about what happens in schools, about who continues into work afterwards, or about any individual student's experience. Nor does it tell us anything about how anybody learns. **We make no claim anywhere that girls learn this subject differently, need different material, or need it delivered more gently.** We do not believe that, we have seen no evidence for it, and no part of our teaching is built on it.

## How the classes are actually arranged

**Batches are mixed.** We do not run girls-only batches and will not pretend otherwise. Groups of five to eight are assembled on demonstrated ability from an intake across twenty-five countries, which is the only basis that reliably produces a class at the right level.

**One to one is available.** For families who would prefer private teaching, for any reason at all, that option exists at USD 150 a month and requires no explanation to us.

**The syllabus is identical.** The same five rungs, the same courses, the same projects, the same standards. A student is placed by what they can do in the free first class and by nothing else.

**Teachers and cameras.** Sessions are live video with screen sharing. If a family has preferences about cameras or about who teaches, raise it on the free call.

**Nothing is published without you.** The GitHub work carries firm rules for every student: no personal information, no photographs, no real names of other people, and for a learner under eighteen a parent knows the account exists.

**Adults too.** Ages six to sixty-seven. Women returning to technical work, or moving into it from another field, are a routine part of the intake.

A lighter version of a subject is not a kindness; it is a decision made on somebody's behalf about how far they will go, taken before anybody has watched them work.

## The obvious follow-up question

**Before university:** we have not found published Omani figures broken down by sex for school-level participation in computing, and we are not going to estimate them. What is published is the scale of the visible stage: the 2025/26 National Olympiad drew more than 250 students and 108 supervising teachers, with 70 projects across four fields.

**After university:** participation in a contest is not the same as entering a profession, and we have no Omani employment data to offer.

What a student controls is what they can demonstrate. A learner who arrives with three finished, documented, publicly readable projects is arguing from evidence rather than from a category. That is why rung five exists in this curriculum. A repository is read as commits, tests and reasoning; what it does not show, unless somebody goes looking, is anything about who wrote it.
