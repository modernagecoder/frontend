Here is a puzzle made from Glen Waverley itself. In the 2021 Census, the number of residents at primary school and the number at secondary school add up to 6,923, and the primary figure is 97 larger. Simultaneous equations are where algebra stops being exercises and starts answering questions, and that is how we teach maths to Glen Waverley learners, from Foundation number to VCE Specialist Mathematics.

A Glen Waverley worked example

Two numbers from a sum and a difference

The ABS publishes how many Glen Waverley residents attend each kind of educational institution. We hid two of the counts and kept only their sum and their difference.

Let p be the number at primary school and s the number at secondary school. We know p + s = 6,923 and p − s = 97. Adding the two equations gives 2p = 7,020, so p = 3,510. Subtracting gives 2s = 6,826, so s = 3,413. Both match the 2021 QuickStats counts for Glen Waverley.

The simple method wins

For a sum and a difference, halving (sum + difference) gives the larger number at once. Substitution, elimination or a matrix all reach the same place with more writing.

Why it always works

Adding p + s and p - s cancels s and doubles p. Once students see the cancellation, the shortcut is a consequence, not a trick to memorise.

A sum we made, not a total

The sums here are our own arithmetic. The ABS adjusts small counts for privacy, so we never present added-up parts as an official total.

What students practise

Writing two equations from words, eliminating a variable, checking the answer in both equations, and noticing when a quicker route exists.

Years 9 and 10 students meet this as linear simultaneous equations; VCE students later solve larger systems with matrices and see the same idea.

When the shortcut stops

If the second equation is not a plain difference, for example 2p - s = 3,607, halving no longer works and elimination is the honest tool.

Knowing which situation you are in is the real skill.

The river-current version

A boat going downstream moves at its own speed plus the current; upstream, its speed minus the current. Those two measured speeds are a sum and a difference, so the speed of the boat itself is their average and the current is half their difference.

It is the same two-line method, and Year 10 students are often delighted to see a physics question fall to it.

Checking, every time

Students substitute both answers back into both original equations before writing a conclusion. It takes ten seconds and catches most slips.

In VCE, the same discipline applies to much longer systems, where a single sign error can undo a page of work.

Questions about a maths tutor in Glen Waverley

Do you tutor maths for Glen Waverley students?

Yes, live online in Melbourne time, from Foundation to Year 12 and for adults, privately or in level-matched classes.

Which VCE maths studies do you teach?

Foundation Mathematics, General Mathematics, Mathematical Methods and Specialist Mathematics, across Units 1 to 4.

Can a student take Specialist without Methods?

The VCAA study design says enrolment in Specialist Mathematics Units 3 and 4 assumes current enrolment in, or completion of, Mathematical Methods Units 3 and 4.

How do you find two numbers from their sum and difference?

Add the sum and difference and halve to get the larger; subtract and halve to get the smaller. With a sum of 6,923 and a difference of 97, the numbers are 3,510 and 3,413.

Will tutoring guarantee a study score or ATAR?

No. We teach thoroughly and report honestly, but scores depend on the student and the exams.

Do you help with selective entry maths?

Yes, on a separate page for the Victorian selective entry test; this page covers everyday maths at every level.

What time are lessons?

At fixed Melbourne times, after school or in the evening; the daylight saving changes are handled on our side.

Can adults learn maths with you?

Yes, in adult classes or privately.

Is maths through coding useful for Glen Waverley students?

For many, yes. Writing a short Python program to test a guess, such as checking every pair of numbers with a given sum, makes algebra feel less abstract, and it suits students aged 10 to 15.

Do you teach Foundation Mathematics as well as Methods?

Yes. Foundation and General Mathematics students get the same care as Methods and Specialist students, with examples chosen for practical, everyday contexts.

How much are lessons?

The first lesson is free. Monthly fees for classes and private lessons appear in the fees panel on this page.
