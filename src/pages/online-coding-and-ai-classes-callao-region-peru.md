# Online coding and AI classes in the Callao region

Callao sits beside Lima but is not part of it: it is Peru's Constitutional Province, with its own seven districts, its own regional government and its own education office. It is also the best connected region on our list. ### Next to Lima, counted apart

INEI treats Callao as one of Peru's 26 department-level units, alongside Lima Metropolitana and Región Lima. In its 2017 table of capital cities, though, it counts Callao inside the built-up area it calls Lima Metropolitana, which is why Callao has no separate "city" figure.

For families this is mostly a matter of administration: Callao's schools answer to the Dirección Regional de Educación del Callao, not to Lima's office. For an online class it makes no difference at all, because the class reaches Ventanilla or La Punta as easily as Miraflores.

### Children and teenagers in Callao

The 2025 census counted 96,501 people aged 6 to 11 and 105,284 aged 12 to 17 in Callao, the ages of primaria and secundaria.

Those are people the census interviewed; the headline 1,094,209 also includes INEI's estimate of people it could not count. In 2025 Callao's public schools taught 68.3 thousand primaria pupils and 55.9 thousand secundaria students, with 14,738 teachers across all levels and sectors.

Most of Callao's people live in two districts: Callao itself, around the port and the old town, and Ventanilla to the north. Bellavista, La Perla and Carmen de la Legua Reynoso sit between the port and Lima, Mi Perú lies beyond Ventanilla, and La Punta, the smallest district, occupies the tip of the peninsula. Lima families reading this may prefer our page on [English-medium coding classes in Lima](/english-medium-coding-classes-lima); the classes are the same.

### What that means in practice

Most Callao families who ask about classes already have what a live lesson needs: a connection that holds a video call, a laptop or desktop for typed code, and a corner quiet enough to hear the mentor. That makes Callao one of the easiest places in Peru to start online.

Fewer than half of households have a computer, though, so a family may need to share one between siblings. Separate time slots, which our flexible scheduling allows, usually solve that.

### Why online instead of an academy

From Ventanilla or Mi Perú, an evening class in Miraflores or San Isidro means a long trip each way. Online, the time goes on learning, and nobody crosses the city after dark.

Online also means the class keeps going through holidays and changes of school, because it follows the family's calendar rather than any building's opening hours.

### The curriculum and the gap

Peru's curriculum, the CNEB, makes digital skills a transversal competency, number 28, to be used in every area. No area teaches programming. The regular secundaria week has 4 hours of Matemática and 3 of Inglés, and that is where English and maths stop.

MINEDU's Fábrica de Programadores pilot, launched in July 2025, teaches Python to 1,000 pupils of 4° and 5° de secundaria in Lima Norte, preparing them for the PCEP exam. It is the closest thing to school coding nearby, and it does not cover Callao.

### Maths contests in Callao

The DRE Callao reported on 27 June 2024 that the twentieth ONEM had begun "en todo el Callao", with a second, afternoon session from 1 p.m. to 3 p.m. In 2026 the national school stage was on Wednesday 8 July 2026: ten questions in two hours, entered through the student's school.

For programmers there is the OPI, run free by UTEC for every secundaria student; its 2025 rules allowed only C++ in the final round and banned generative AI. We prepare students for both kinds of thinking with our own problems and have no link with MINEDU, the DREC, UTEC or either contest.

Under Law 28044, primaria lasts six years and secundaria five, and MINEDU fixes a child's grade by their age on 31 March. Across Peru, 6,225,000 students in 53,631 schools started the 2026 school year on 16 March 2026. Our classes sit alongside that system: the colegio keeps marks and promotion, and we add regular, hands-on practice. ## When is the Universidad Nacional del Callao at its busiest?

UNAC publishes its undergraduate timetable as open data: every section, its enrolment and its class times. Our students use it to learn how to count things that last a while. Counting the classes that start at a given time is easy; counting the classes that are going on at that time is the real question, and the answers differ.

### The data

The dataset is UNAC's "Programación Académica", published by the Universidad Nacional del Callao on the Plataforma Nacional de Datos Abiertos under the Open Data Commons Attribution Licence (ODC-By). We used its resource for the 2025B undergraduate term, downloaded on 9 November 2026.

The file has 2,385 section rows across 17 schools, from Administración to Matemática and Ingeniería de Sistemas. Each row gives the number enrolled and a HORARIO text such as "(T) Jue 11:20 a 13:00 (P) Mar 08:00 a 11:20": a theory session on Thursday and a practical on Tuesday. In total there are 3,505 sessions, most of them 100 minutes long, built on 50-minute blocks.

### The question

At what moment of the week are the most enrolled seats in use? A first program reads each session, notes its day and start time, and adds the enrolment to that slot. The slot with the biggest total wins.

One caution before we start: a student enrolled in five courses appears five times, so these are seats, not people. The enrolment column adds up to 58,799 seat registrations; UNAC's own headcount, 9,746 undergraduates in 2025 according to INEI's compendium, is far smaller.

**Version 1: add up enrolment by start time** (Python)
```python
import csv
import re

SESSION = re.compile(r"\((\w)\)\s*(\w+)\s+(\d\d:\d\d)\s+a\s+(\d\d:\d\d)")

starting = {}
sessions = 0
with open("Programacion-Academica-2025B-Pregrado.csv", encoding="utf-8-sig") as timetable:
    for section in csv.DictReader(timetable):
        seats = int(section["N° MATRICULADOS"])
        for kind, day, start, end in SESSION.findall(section["HORARIO"]):
            sessions += 1
            key = (day, start)
            starting[key] = starting.get(key, 0) + seats

busiest = max(starting, key=starting.get)
print("Sessions read:", sessions)
print("Busiest start:", busiest, starting[busiest], "seats")
```
It prints Sessions read: 3505 and Busiest start: ('Jue', '08:00') 3397 seats.

Thursday at 08:00 is when the most seats begin a class. But a class that starts at 08:00 and runs to 09:40 is still going at 08:50, and one that started at 08:00 for a three-hour practical is still going at 10:30. Version 1 counts each class once, at its first minute, so a long class weighs no more than a short one, and a class that started earlier is invisible a block later.

The fix is to expand every session into the 50-minute blocks it covers and add its enrolment to each of them. That is a simple sweep over time, and it turns "how many start now?" into "how many are in progress now?"

**Version 2: count the seats in use in every 50-minute block** (Python)
```python
import csv
import re

SESSION = re.compile(r"\((\w)\)\s*(\w+)\s+(\d\d:\d\d)\s+a\s+(\d\d:\d\d)")

def minutes(clock):
    h, m = clock.split(":")
    return int(h) * 60 + int(m)

def clock(mins):
    return f"{mins // 60:02d}:{mins % 60:02d}"

in_class = {}
with open("Programacion-Academica-2025B-Pregrado.csv", encoding="utf-8-sig") as timetable:
    for section in csv.DictReader(timetable):
        seats = int(section["N° MATRICULADOS"])
        for kind, day, start, end in SESSION.findall(section["HORARIO"]):
            t = minutes(start)
            while t < minutes(end):              # every 50-minute block the class covers
                key = (day, clock(t))
                in_class[key] = in_class.get(key, 0) + seats
                t += 50

busiest = max(in_class, key=in_class.get)
print("Blocks with a class:", len(in_class))
print("Busiest block:", busiest, in_class[busiest], "seats in use")
for day in ("Lun", "Mar", "Mié", "Jue", "Vie", "Sáb"):
    top = max((k for k in in_class if k[0] == day), key=in_class.get)
    print(day, top[1], in_class[top])
```
It prints Blocks with a class: 102, Busiest block: ('Jue', '10:30') 3832 seats in use, then for each day Lun 08:50 3726, Mar 09:40 3639, Mié 09:40 3665, Jue 10:30 3832, Vie 10:30 3139 and Sáb 10:30 1311.

The busiest moment moves from Thursday 08:00 to Thursday 10:30, when 3,832 seats are in use, and every weekday peaks in the middle of the morning rather than at the first bell. Saturday has classes too, but its busiest block holds about a third as many seats. Nothing in the data was wrong in Version 1; the program asked the wrong question of it.

Seats are section enrolments, so one student can be counted in several sessions at different times but never twice in the same block unless the file lists two overlapping sessions for them. Counts are ours. The pattern of the HORARIO text is parsed with a regular expression; every one of the 2,385 rows matched it.

The timetable also shows what Callao's own engineering students study. In Ingeniería de Sistemas, the 2025B list runs from Programación Estructurada and two terms of object-oriented programming to Programación Web, databases, Sistemas Operativos and Teoría de Lenguajes y Compiladores. Our [Java course for university students in Peru](/curso-de-java-para-universitarios-peru) is written for exactly that sequence.

1. **Under 9, with a drawing.** Draw three bars on a clock face for three lessons. At which hour do most bars overlap? Is that the hour when most lessons start?
2. **Scratch, around 9 to 11.** Give three sprites a start and an end time, step a clock forward, and count how many sprites are "in class" at each step.
3. **First Python, around 11 to 13.** Run Version 1 and Version 2, then find one session that Version 1 counts at 08:00 but Version 2 also counts at 10:30.
4. **Ages 13 and over.** Repeat the sweep separately for theory (T), practical (P) and lab (L) sessions. Which kind fills the late morning?
5. **Going further.** Replace the 50-minute steps with a sorted list of start and end events, and count in one pass. Check that the busiest block is unchanged.

At hours you choose. Callao keeps Peru time, UTC minus five, all year round, and mentors plan their day around yours.

**Callao times and the matching India times (illustrations only, no fixed slots)**

| Callao | India | India date |
|---|---|---|

Peru: UTC minus 5 throughout the year; its last change of clocks was in 1994 (tz database, zone America/Lima). India: UTC plus 5:30, with no seasonal change. India is therefore 10 hours 30 minutes ahead of Callao on every date.

Since neither country changes its clocks, a time chosen in the summer holidays still works in the school year. You book in Callao time, and the mentor sees India time. ## Online coding and AI classes in Callao: questions and answers

### Are the classes in Spanish?

No. Every class is in English only, live and online. Mentors speak clearly, follow the learner's pace and accent, and answer Spanish with the English words.

### Is Callao covered, or only Lima?

All seven districts of Callao are covered: Callao, Ventanilla, Bellavista, La Perla, Mi Perú, Carmen de la Legua Reynoso and La Punta. Classes are online, so district makes no difference.

### From what age can my child start?

Six, with game-making in blocks. Typed Python, JavaScript or Luau usually follows from about nine. Teenagers and adults can start with no experience.

### Do Callao schools teach programming?

Not as a subject. Peru's curriculum spreads digital skills across all areas, and MINEDU's Python pilot, Fábrica de Programadores, runs only in Lima Norte.

Any day, any time, flexible: choose a batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at the hour that suits your family. India is 10 hours 30 minutes ahead of Callao all year, so 09:00 in Callao is 19:30 for the mentor.

Probably. In 2025, 76.5% of Callao households had an internet service, one of the highest shares in Peru. A lesson needs a steady video call for about an hour and ideally a laptop or desktop.

### Can UNAC students join?

Yes. Students from the Universidad Nacional del Callao and any other university join adult groups, often for Java, full stack or algorithms, at hours around their timetable.

We train the reasoning both reward, with problems we write ourselves and never the organisers' papers. The ONEM is entered through the school, the OPI through UTEC. We are not connected with MINEDU, the DRE Callao, UTEC or either olympiad.

Monthly by card in US dollars; your bank converts them to soles. Visitors from India see rupees. The first class is free.

### Do you give certificates?
