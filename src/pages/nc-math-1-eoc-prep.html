NC Math 1 EOC prep, and NC Math 3 after it

North Carolina has two math end-of-course tests, and they behave quite differently. NC Math 1 includes a section with the calculator switched off; NC Math 3 has none at all and leans much harder on geometry. Both have 60 items, both count for at least 20 percent of the course grade, and for many students the second one is the state’s high school accountability test. Knowing which test your teenager faces, and what each one weights, is the start of preparing well.

In short

The NC Math 1 and NC Math 3 End-of-Course (EOC) tests are NCDPI’s state exams for those two courses, taken online at the end of the course. Each has 60 items, 50 of them scored, with a graphing calculator; NC Math 1 also has 15 calculator-inactive items. Results count for at least 20 percent of the final course grade. Our Algebra Foundations course covers NC Math 1 live online, with group batches and 1-on-1 options, and our high school course carries on to NC Math 3.

Side by side

What is the difference between the NC Math 1 and NC Math 3 EOC?

Same length, same scoring, different calculator rules and different content weights. NCDPI writes one specification for both, which makes the comparison exact.

NC Math 1: the calculator-free fifteen

Fifteen of the 50 scored NC Math 1 items are calculator inactive. That is where simplifying expressions, solving linear equations, working with exponents and factoring are tested without the graphing calculator to check the answer.

Students who rely on Desmos all year often lose points here first. We teach every NC Math 1 skill by hand before we show the calculator shortcut.

NC Math 3: the calculator never leaves

NCDPI’s specification is plain: “The EOC NC Math 3 Test will include only calculator active items.” Every one of the 50 scored items allows the graphing calculator.

That changes the skill: not avoiding the calculator but using it well, to graph a polynomial, find intersections and check a trigonometric answer quickly, so time is left for the reasoning items.

Source: the NCDPI EOC NC Math 1 and NC Math 3 specifications dated April 2026, which we downloaded on 31 October 2026. We are an independent school with no connection to NCDPI.

Domain weights

Reading the NC Math 1 weights

Algebra and functions together dominate NC Math 1. Statistics and Probability, at 18 to 20 percent, is larger than Geometry, at 8 to 12 percent, which surprises families who think of geometry as part of every math course.

In practice that means scatter plots, lines of best fit, residuals and two-way tables deserve real lesson time in NC Math 1.

Reading the NC Math 3 weights

Geometry rises to 20 to 24 percent in NC Math 3, while statistics falls to 8 to 12. Circles, their equations and their arcs and angles become a central topic.

The Depth of Knowledge profile is identical for both tests, with 60 to 65 percent at DOK 2. Most items ask for a decision or two linked steps, not recall.

Why it counts

Does the NC Math 1 EOC count toward your grade?

Yes. Under State Board policy TEST-003, schools use results from all EOC assessments as at least twenty percent of the student’s final grade for the course.

What the EOC decides, in NCDPI’s and the State Board’s words (NCDPI State Tests page and the coordinators’ handbook for this school year)

A fifth of the grade is a lot

A student averaging a B in class who struggles on a test worth at least 20 percent of the final grade can watch the report card grade drop. The reverse is also true: a strong EOC can lift a borderline grade.

The exact conversion from EOC score to course grade is set locally. Ask the school how it applies the 20 percent minimum.

The accelerated path

For an eighth grader enrolled in NC Math 1, the coordinators’ handbook quotes policy ACCT-021: these students take the NC Math 1 EOC at the end of the course and will take the NC Math 3 EOC for federal accountability in high school.

So a student who took NC Math 1 early faces two EOCs several years apart. The NC Math 3 test then matters for the school’s accountability as well as for the course grade.

Worked examples

How do we teach the hardest topics on each test?

One calculator-inactive NC Math 1 problem and one NC Math 3 circle problem, both ours. They share a thread: rewriting an expression until its structure shows.

  1. Our NC Math 1 problem, calculator off. A school sells adult tickets and student tickets. 3 adult and 5 student tickets cost 41; 3 adult and 2 student tickets cost 29. Find each price.
  2. The reasoning, by elimination. Write 3a + 5s = 41 and 3a + 2s = 29. Both have 3a, so subtract the second from the first: 3s = 12, so s = 4. Substitute into the second: 3a + 8 = 29, so 3a = 21 and a = 7. Check in the first: 21 + 20 = 41.
  3. The habit we teach with it. If elimination gives an awkward fraction for a ticket price, reread the numbers before going on. On a calculator-off item there is no screen to catch a copying slip, so the context itself becomes the check.
  4. Our NC Math 3 problem. Find the center and radius of the circle x² + y² - 6x + 4y - 12 = 0.
  5. The reasoning, by completing the square. Group: (x² - 6x) + (y² + 4y) = 12. Half of -6 is -3, squared 9; half of 4 is 2, squared 4. Add both to each side: (x² - 6x + 9) + (y² + 4y + 4) = 25. So (x - 3)² + (y + 2)² = 25: center (3, -2), radius 5.
  6. The thread. Completing the square first appears with quadratics, to find a vertex. In NC Math 3 the same move finds a circle’s center. Students who learned it as a trick in NC Math 1 relearn it in NC Math 3; students who understood it once keep it.

Both problems are ours. NCDPI’s released NC Math 1 and NC Math 3 forms are free for students on the End-of-Course page and in NCTest, but the department reserves them from commercial use, so they stay out of our paid lessons; we point families to them for practice at home.

When and how

When is the NC Math 1 EOC taken?

At the end of the course: in the last five days of a semester, 4x4 block or summer course, or the last ten days of a traditional yearlong course. Your school’s calendar sets the dates.

How and when the two math EOCs are given (NCDPI EOC specifications and the coordinators’ handbook)

Traditional yearlong courseLast ten days of the instructional period

LanguageEnglish only

Materials on requestGraph paper, scratch paper and a writing utensil

Block schedules test in January too

A student in a fall semester or 4x4 NC Math 1 course takes the EOC in the last five instructional days of that semester, whenever that term ends on the school’s calendar. Yearlong students test in the last ten days of the school year.

The statute quoted in the handbook sets the same rule: the final ten instructional days for yearlong courses and the final five for semester courses.

Practise on the real calculator

NCDPI links a North Carolina version of the Desmos graphing calculator for grade 8 and high school. It is the calculator in NCTest, so it is the one we teach with.

NCDPI also posts an example gridded response space for both tests. Typing a fraction or a negative number correctly into it is worth one rehearsal.

Two plans

How to study for the NC Math 1 or NC Math 3 EOC

Our plans for the last eight weeks of the course, one per test, counted back from the school’s EOC days.

  1. NC Math 1, weeks 1 and 2: calculator-off algebra. Linear equations, inequalities, exponent rules and factoring by hand, because those are the 15 inactive items.
  2. NC Math 1, weeks 3 and 4: functions. Linear, exponential and quadratic functions, from tables, graphs and context.
  3. NC Math 1, week 5: statistics. Scatter plots, lines of fit, residuals and two-way tables, which together can reach a fifth of the test.
  4. NC Math 1, weeks 6 to 8: mixed rehearsal. The released form at home, our timed sets in class, and one full sitting of about three hours.
  5. NC Math 3, weeks 1 to 3: functions and algebra. Polynomials, rational expressions, inverses, exponentials and logarithms, with the graphing calculator from day one.
  6. NC Math 3, weeks 4 and 5: geometry. Circle equations, arcs, sectors and angle relationships for the 20 to 24 percent share.
  7. NC Math 3, weeks 6 to 8: speed with the calculator. Graph-and-read routines so the reasoning items get time, then a full rehearsal.

NC Math 1 and NC Math 3 EOC: questions from parents

How many items does the NC Math 1 EOC have?

NCDPI's April 2026 specifications list 60 items: 50 operational items that count and 10 field-test items that do not. NC Math 3 has the same 60.

Can students use a calculator on NC Math 1?

Yes, a graphing calculator, but 15 of the 50 scored items are calculator inactive. NC Math 3 has no calculator-inactive items; all 50 scored items allow the graphing calculator.

How long is the NC Math 1 EOC?

Plan on roughly three hours; NCDPI caps it at four, and only an accommodation such as Scheduled Extended Time goes beyond that. NC Math 3 runs to the same clock.

Does the NC Math 1 EOC count toward my grade?

Yes. Under State Board policy TEST-003, schools use EOC results as at least 20 percent of the final grade for the course. Your school decides exactly how it applies that minimum.

What is on the NC Math 1 EOC?

Number and Quantity and Algebra at 36 to 40 percent, Functions at 32 to 36 percent, Statistics and Probability at 18 to 20 percent and Geometry at 8 to 12 percent, according to NCDPI.

What is on the NC Math 3 EOC?

Number and Quantity and Algebra at 32 to 36 percent, Functions at 32 to 36 percent, Geometry at 20 to 24 percent and Statistics and Probability at 8 to 12 percent.

When do you take the NC Math 1 EOC?

In the last five days of a semester, 4x4 or summer course, or the last ten days of a traditional yearlong course. Your school calendar sets the actual days.

My 8th grader is in NC Math 1. Which math test do they sit?

Only the NC Math 1 EOC that year. State Board policy ACCT-021 keeps them off the grade 8 EOG, and later, in high school, the NC Math 3 EOC becomes their federal accountability test.

Where can I find an NC Math 1 EOC practice test?

NCDPI posts released forms for NC Math 1 and NC Math 3 on its End-of-Course page and in NCTest, with graph paper and the graphing calculator. Our own practice questions are on this page.

When are your classes?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Lessons are scheduled on Eastern Time.

Are you affiliated with NCDPI?

No. We are a tutoring company working independently; neither the state department nor any North Carolina high school is involved in our classes. What we report about the tests is drawn from documents NCDPI publishes for everyone.
