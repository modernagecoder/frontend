# S1 to S3 maths tutor online in Scotland

The three years from S1 to S3 have no external maths exam, which makes them easy to underestimate. Yet they end with a decision that shapes the rest of secondary school: which maths course a pupil takes in S4. Education Scotland's Benchmarks are plain about the order. Pupils "should have achieved relevant Fourth level Experiences and Outcomes before embarking on the National 5 qualifications", and schools are told to take careful account of this when S4 options are agreed. So the Broad General Education is not a waiting room before National 5. It is where Third level is secured and Fourth level is reached, and where any gap in algebra, rounding or fractions becomes visible before it costs anything.

## Three courses for lower secondary maths in Scotland

Middle School Mathematics covers the Third and Fourth level ground. The National 5 course is the next step once Fourth level is secure, and High School Mathematics suits a pupil racing ahead.

- [Middle School Mathematics](/courses/comprehensive-middle-school-mathematics-mastery): Integers, primes, fractions, ratio, equations, sequences, angles and data: Third level made solid, Fourth level reached.
- [National 5 and Higher Maths](/courses/national-5-and-higher-maths-course): For an S3 pupil with Fourth level secure who is ready to start the National 5 skills.
- [High School Mathematics](/courses/complete-high-school-mathematics-mastery): Algebra and functions in more depth, for pupils working beyond Fourth level.

## What maths do pupils learn in S1 to S3?

Third and Fourth level, organised under the same headings as primary: number, money and measure; shape, position and movement; and information handling. The Benchmarks list what achieving each level looks like.

Education Scotland describes its Experiences and Outcomes as a single journey, letting families see "the curriculum from the early years to the end of S3 as a whole". Most pupils arrive in S1 having achieved Second level and spend S1 and S2 securing Third level. Fourth level is the stretch, overlapping in difficulty with the start of National 5, and many schools teach it through S3.

**Selected Benchmark statements for S1 to S3 maths (Education Scotland, June 2017)**

| Organiser | Third level, in part | Fourth level, in part |
|---|---|---|
| Number | Tables facts to the 12th table; integers in all four operations; decimals to three places | Order of operations with brackets; multi-step problems; scientific notation |
| Multiples and powers | Lowest common multiple, highest common factor, primes to 100, a number as a product of prime factors; powers such as 2 to the 4th | Square roots with positive and negative values; roots as the inverse of powers |
| Fractions and percentages | Converting between forms; adding fractions with different denominators; ratio and simplifying it | Percentage increase and decrease; fractions and mixed numbers in calculations; proportion |
| Algebra | Collecting like terms; solving equations of the form ax &plusmn; b = c; sequences from a rule such as T = 4n + 6 | Expanding brackets; equations of the form ax &plusmn; b = cx &plusmn; d; inequalities; the nth term of a linear sequence; gradient |
| Shape and measure | Corresponding, alternate and vertically opposite angles; bearings and scale drawings; compound areas and volumes | Pythagoras; trigonometry in right-angled triangles; circumference and area of a circle; prisms and cylinders |
| Data and chance | Probability on a scale of 0 to 1; judging whether information is misleading; pie charts and compound bar graphs | Mean, median, mode and range; correlation; choosing and justifying a diagram |

The Fourth level column will look familiar to anyone who has seen a National 5 paper: Pythagoras, trigonometry, gradient and the order of operations all reappear there. That is the point of the Benchmarks' advice. A pupil who reaches S4 with Fourth level secure meets National 5 as a deepening of known ideas; a pupil who reaches S4 at Third level meets most of it for the first time, in a single year with an exam at the end.

The Third and Fourth level pages of Education Scotland's [numeracy and mathematics Benchmarks](https://education.gov.scot/media/s5edgtvx/numeracyandmathematicsbenchmarks.pdf), read on 31 October 2026. The statements are selected and shortened; the document lists more under each organiser, including financial statements we have left out here.

## Why does S3 maths decide the S4 course?

Because the Benchmarks link presentation for National 5 to Fourth level, and schools use teacher judgement of the level, gathered across S1 to S3, when S4 options are agreed. There is no S3 exam to pass; there is a body of evidence that builds up over three years.

The relevant passage in the Benchmarks sits in its guidance for secondary teachers. Benchmarks "will help teachers ensure that learners make appropriate choices and are presented at an appropriate level for National Qualifications in the senior phase", which "can help avoid excessive workload for teachers and unnecessary assessments for learners". It then gives the example that matters most for maths: pupils "should have achieved relevant Fourth level Experiences and Outcomes before embarking on the National 5 qualifications", adding that "Schools should take careful account of this when options for S4 are being agreed."

**S3 numeracy judgements for the 2024 to 2025 school year (gov.scot statistics, 9 December 2025)**

| Measure | Per cent of S3 pupils |
|---|---|
| Third level or better in numeracy | 91 |
| Fourth level in numeracy | 68 |

The statistics publication notes that the share of S3 pupils at Fourth level was "at their highest ever across all organisers" and had "increased markedly over the last three years", and adds a caution in its own words: "This increase may be partially due to improvements in assessment and recording at level four." On the figures as published, roughly one S3 pupil in three had not been judged at Fourth level in numeracy in 2024 to 2025.

## What is the difference between Third and Fourth level maths?

Fourth level asks for the same ideas with fewer supports: rounding to significant figures rather than decimal places, unknowns on both sides of an equation, a formula for a sequence rather than a rule to follow, and right-angled triangles solved with Pythagoras and trigonometry.

**The same organiser at two levels (our summary of the Benchmarks)**

| Idea | Third level asks a pupil to | Fourth level asks a pupil to |
|---|---|---|
| Rounding | Round decimals to three places and estimate routinely | Round to a stated number of significant figures and show the effect of rounding too early |
| Equations | Solve ax &plusmn; b = c | Solve ax &plusmn; b = cx &plusmn; d, with brackets, and solve inequalities |
| Sequences | Generate terms from a given rule | Find the nth term formula and use it |
| Triangles | Find missing angles with angle facts | Find sides with Pythagoras, and sides and angles with trigonometry |
| Percentages | Convert and calculate with and without a calculator | Find percentage increase or decrease and choose the most efficient form |
| Accuracy | Choose appropriate units | Work within a tolerance such as 235 mm &plusmn; 1 mm |

### Rounding becomes a skill

The Fourth level statement about "the impact of rounding an answer before the final step in a multi-step calculation" is the same habit National 5 markers look for. We teach it in S2.

### Algebra carries the load

Unknowns on both sides, brackets and inequalities are where the gap between levels is widest. We spend the most time here.

### Triangles open up

Pythagoras and trigonometry arrive at Fourth level. A calculator is needed, so we also teach which buttons, in which order, and how to check.

## Four S1 to S3 maths examples, step by step

All four are ours, written for Third and Fourth level. They show working the way a teacher in Scotland would want to read it.

### 1. Product of prime factors (Third level)

**Question (ours).** Write 360 as a product of its prime factors.

**Divide by primes.** 360 &divide; 2 = 180, &divide; 2 = 90, &divide; 2 = 45, &divide; 3 = 15, &divide; 3 = 5, and 5 is prime.

**Collect.** 360 = 2 &times; 2 &times; 2 &times; 3 &times; 3 &times; 5 = 23 &times; 32 &times; 5.

**Check.** 8 &times; 9 = 72 and 72 &times; 5 = 360. This also prepares the powers notation that Third level introduces.

### 2. Unknowns on both sides (Fourth level)

**Question (ours).** Solve 5x &minus; 7 = 2x + 11.

**Gather x.** Subtract 2x from both sides: 3x &minus; 7 = 11.

**Gather numbers.** Add 7 to both sides: 3x = 18.

**Solve and check.** x = 6. Left side 30 &minus; 7 = 23, right side 12 + 11 = 23. Equal, so the solution is right.

### 3. Premature rounding (Fourth level)

**Question (ours).** A cylinder has radius 2.36 cm and height 5 cm. Find its volume to 3 significant figures.

**Keep full accuracy.** V = &pi; &times; 2.362 &times; 5 = 87.487&hellip; so V = 87.5 cm3.

**See the error.** Rounding the radius to 2.4 first gives about 90.5 cm3, three whole units out.

**The lesson.** Round once, at the end, exactly as the Fourth level Benchmark on truncation and premature rounding asks.

### 4. Pythagoras and trigonometry (Fourth level)

**Question (ours).** A right-angled triangle has shorter sides 7 cm and 9 cm. Find the longest side and the smaller acute angle.

**Pythagoras.** 72 + 92 = 49 + 81 = 130, so the hypotenuse is &radic;130 = 11.4 cm to 3 significant figures.

**Trigonometry.** The smaller angle is opposite the 7 cm side: tan &theta; = 7 &divide; 9, so &theta; = 37.9&deg; to 1 decimal place.

**Reasonableness.** The hypotenuse is longer than 9 and shorter than 7 + 9. The angle is under 45&deg; because 7 is less than 9.

**An S3 practice set (ours, with answers).** (1) Find the highest common factor and lowest common multiple of 18 and 24. (2) Simplify 3a + 5b &minus; a + 2b. (3) Expand and simplify 4(2x &minus; 3) &minus; 5x. (4) Find the nth term of 7, 11, 15, 19. (5) Increase 240 by 15 per cent. (6) Write 0.00052 in scientific notation. (7) Find the mean and range of 4, 9, 6, 11, 5. (8) Solve 2x + 3 < 15. **Answers:** (1) 6 and 72; (2) 2a + 7b; (3) 3x &minus; 12; (4) 4n + 3; (5) 276; (6) 5.2 &times; 10&minus;4; (7) mean 7, range 7; (8) x < 6.

None of these comes from an assessment; there are no S3 past papers in Scotland. Questions 3, 4, 6 and 8 sit at Fourth level and are a fair, if rough, signal of readiness for the National 5 course.

## What families ask about S1 to S3 maths

### Which CfE level is expected by the end of S3?

The Scottish Government reports S3 pupils at Third level or better and at Fourth level. For 2024 to 2025 it put 91 per cent of S3 pupils at Third level or better in numeracy, and 68 per cent at Fourth.

### Do you need Fourth level to do National 5 Maths?

Education Scotland's Benchmarks say pupils should have achieved relevant Fourth level outcomes before starting National 5, and that schools should take careful account of this when S4 options are agreed. The final decision rests with the school.

### Are there S3 maths past papers?

No national ones. There is no S3 maths exam in Scotland. Teachers judge the level from work across the year, and the S3 standardised assessment has no pass or fail.

### What is the difference between Third and Fourth level maths?

Fourth level adds significant figures, equations with unknowns on both sides, the nth term, gradient, Pythagoras and trigonometry. Third level covers primes, powers, ratio, simpler equations and angle facts.

### What is the Broad General Education?

It is the phase of Curriculum for Excellence from the early years to the end of S3. In maths, S1 to S3 is where Third and Fourth level are taught before the senior phase.

### How long is the S3 numeracy assessment?

It is untimed. According to the assessment website, S3 learners on average finish inside 40 minutes, and no score counts as a fail.

### Is S1 to S3 maths the same as KS3?

The content overlaps a great deal, but Scotland describes it by level rather than by year, and the end of S3 feeds the choice of S4 course rather than a GCSE tier.

### When are lessons held?

### Which course should an S2 pupil start?

Usually Middle School Mathematics, at Third or Fourth level depending on the free first class. A pupil with Fourth level secure may move straight on to the National 5 course.
