# Year 7 maths tutor online

In primary school, the national curriculum told schools what to teach in each year. In Year 7 that stops. Key Stage 3 maths is a single programme of study for Years 7, 8 and 9 together, and each secondary school decides what goes in which year. At the same time the maths itself changes character: letters replace boxes, negative numbers are calculated with rather than only counted, ratio gets its own notation, and fractions are worked with in every form. There is no national test until GCSE. This page explains what that shift means for a Year 7 student, what the school already knows on day one, and how we tutor the year, with four worked examples of our own.

## How is Year 7 maths different from Year 6?

Three things change at once: the curriculum stops being split by year, the content becomes more abstract, and national testing pauses. The table puts Year 6 and Key Stage 3 side by side from the two DfE programmes.

**Year 6 and Key Stage 3 compared (statements from the DfE KS1 and 2 and KS3 programmes of study; the comparison is ours)**

|  | Year 6 (primary) | Key Stage 3 (Years 7 to 9) |
|---|---|---|
| How it is written | A separate statutory list for Year 6 | One list for all three years; the school sets the order |
| Number | Numbers to 10 000 000; rounding, but not to significant figures | Place value for integers of any size; powers and roots; standard form; decimal places and significant figures |
| Algebra | Simple formulae and missing numbers | Notation such as ab and 3y; simplifying, expanding, rearranging; solving linear equations; straight-line graphs |
| Ratio | Informal sharing; pupils "might use the notation a:b" | Ratio notation, simplest form, part to part and part to whole; direct and inverse proportion |
| Statistics and probability | Pie charts, line graphs and the mean | Mean, mode, median and range; the 0 to 1 probability scale; scatter graphs |
| National testing | KS2 tests in May | None in KS3; schools set their own assessments |

The DfE describes the Key Stage 3 programme as "organised into apparently distinct domains, but pupils should build on key stage 2 and connections across mathematical ideas". The word apparently is doing a lot of work. A Year 7 lesson on algebra is really a lesson on the structure of arithmetic, which is why the programme lists the aim to "use algebra to generalise the structure of arithmetic".

The programme also tells schools how to pace students: "Decisions about progression should be based on the security of pupils' understanding and their readiness to progress to the next stage." In Year 7 that usually means more consolidation of primary number than parents expect. It is not a sign that the school is going slowly; it is the curriculum working as written.

From the DfE's [mathematics programmes of study](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/national-curriculum-in-england-mathematics-programmes-of-study) for key stages 1 and 2 and for key stage 3, read on 31 October 2026.

## What do students usually learn in Year 7 maths?

Because the order is the school's choice, there is no official Year 7 list. In our experience most Year 7 schemes start with the Key Stage 3 statements that extend primary number, plus first algebra. These are the ones we see most.

The first working-mathematically aim in the programme is to "consolidate their numerical and mathematical capability from key stage 2 and extend their understanding of the number system and place value to include decimals, fractions, powers and roots". That sentence is a fair summary of most Year 7 autumn terms.

### Number, extended

Negative numbers in all four operations: the programme asks for the four operations "applied to integers, decimals, proper and improper fractions, and mixed numbers, all both positive and negative".

Primes, factors and multiples with a new tool, prime factorisation, "including using product notation and the unique factorisation property". Inequality symbols, including "≠", "≤" and "≥", appear too.

### Algebra, begun properly

Notation first: "ab in place of a × b" and "3y in place of y + y + y and 3 × y". Then collecting like terms, substituting values and simple equations.

Percentages are redefined with more reach. Students learn to "work with percentages greater than 100%", which is new after primary school.

Some schools also start geometry in Year 7 with angle facts and the properties of shapes, and statistics with averages and charts. If you can, ask the school for its Key Stage 3 scheme of work or topic list; most share one. A tutor who knows the order can teach alongside it instead of against it.

Statutory content quoted from the DfE key stage 3 programme of study (DFE-00179-2013). The typical Year 7 ordering is our own observation, not a DfE requirement.

## What does a secondary school know about my child's maths in Year 7?

More than many parents realise. The Curriculum and Assessment Review notes that secondary schools can see incoming pupils' detailed key stage 2 results, and the government expects schools to assess from the start of Year 7.

The Review, published in November 2025, says: "Primary schools can access pupils' detailed results, including their responses to each question, as can secondary schools for incoming Year 7 students." The STA's 2027 arrangements make results available on 6 July to "key stage 3 ( KS3 ) schools with a year 7 intake", the same day primary schools see them.

### Assessment after Year 6

There is no national test in Key Stage 3. As the Review puts it, "Since Key Stage 3 assessments were cancelled in 2008, schools have developed and administered their own assessments."

The government's response adds that it expects every secondary school to identify gaps "as part of their ongoing formative assessment from the beginning of Year 7 onwards".

### Why transition is bumpy

The Review lists three Key Stage 3 problems, including "poor curriculum continuity from Key Stage 2", and says it heard that "the breadth of topics at Key Stage 2 has resulted in significant challenges for primary to secondary transition".

In other words, a wobbly first term in Year 7 maths is common, and it is recognised at national level.

How each school uses this information, including whether and when it sets students for maths, is the school's own decision, and we make no claim about any school's practice. The government also plans a revised national curriculum for first teaching from September 2028, and says it expects schools to assess progress in maths in Year 8; a child in Year 7 now will meet that next year. Until the revised programme is published, Year 7 follows the 2013 programme described here.

Sources: [Curriculum and Assessment Review final report](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/curriculum-and-assessment-review-final-report) and [government response](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/curriculum-and-assessment-review-final-report-government-response) (November 2025); STA, [2027 KS2 assessment and reporting arrangements](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/key-stage-2-assessment-and-reporting-arrangements-ara).

## Four Year 7 problems we wrote, solved in full

Each uses Key Stage 3 content that Year 7 schemes commonly teach. The questions are ours, written for this page.

### 1. Prime factors, HCF and LCM

**Question (ours).** One bus leaves every 84 minutes and another every 90 minutes. They leave together at 6 am. When do they next leave together?

**Prime factorise.** 84 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 7 and 90 = 2 × 3 × 3 × 5.

**Lowest common multiple.** Take each prime the most times it appears in either: 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 × 5 × 7 = 1260 minutes.

**Answer.** 1260 minutes is 21 hours, so at 3 am the next day. The highest common factor, 2 × 3 = 6, answers a different question: the largest equal groups both numbers split into.

### 2. Subtracting a negative

**Question (ours).** −7 − (−12).

**Read it as a difference.** How far is it from −12 up to −7 on a number line? That distance is the answer.

**Count.** From −12 to −7 is 5 steps to the right.

**Answer.** 5. Rewriting as −7 + 12 gives the same result, and seeing why the two match is worth more than the rule "two minuses make a plus".

### 3. Simplify, then substitute

**Question (ours).** Simplify 3a + 5b − a + 2b, then find its value when a = 4 and b = −1.

**Collect like terms.** 3a − a = 2a and 5b + 2b = 7b, so the expression is 2a + 7b.

**Substitute.** 2 × 4 + 7 × (−1) = 8 − 7 = 1.

**Check with the original.** 12 + (−5) − 4 + (−2) = 1. Both forms agree, which is what simplifying means: the same value, written more simply.

### 4. A percentage over 100

**Question (ours).** A seedling was 40 cm tall in May and 52 cm tall in July. Write the July height as a percentage of the May height.

**Fraction first.** 52 out of 40 is 52/40 = 1.3.

**Convert.** 1.3 × 100 = 130%.

**Interpret.** 130% of the May height, or an increase of 30%. A percentage above 100 simply means more than the whole you started from.

**Practice set (ours, answers below).** (1) Write 360 as a product of prime factors. (2) Put in order, smallest first: −0.5, −2, 0.3, negative one quarter. (3) Two thirds plus three quarters minus one sixth. (4) 4 × (−6) − (−10). (5) Simplify 5(x + 3) − 2x. (6) Write five eighths as a decimal. (7) Write 45 as a percentage of 36. **Answers:** (1) 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 × 5; (2) −2, −0.5, negative one quarter, 0.3; (3) one and one quarter; (4) −14; (5) 3x + 15; (6) 0.625; (7) 125%.

## How does a Year 7 maths tutor help with the move to secondary?

By finding the primary gaps the Review warns about, teaching the school's own Year 7 topics alongside the class, and building algebra on number sense. The steps are ours and change after the first class.

1. **A free first class.** The tutor checks fractions, negative numbers and one short algebra task, and asks your child what the class is studying this half term.
2. **Use the school's order.** We ask for the topic list or scheme of work and teach a week or two ahead, so lessons at school feel familiar.
3. **Repair primary number quietly.** Times tables, fraction arithmetic and long division are revisited inside Year 7 topics, never as a step backwards.
4. **Algebra as arithmetic with letters.** Every new rule is checked with numbers first, as in example 3 above.
5. **Talk through misconceptions.** The programme asks teachers to use "discussion to probe and remedy their misconceptions"; a live tutor can do this for one student at a time.
6. **Homework support, not homework done.** We explain the method on a similar question and let your child do their own.

## What parents ask about Year 7 maths

### What maths is taught in Year 7?

There is no official Year 7 list, because the Key Stage 3 programme covers Years 7 to 9 together and each school sets the order. Most Year 7 courses consolidate primary number and add negative numbers, prime factorisation, fraction arithmetic, percentages and first algebra.

### Is Year 7 maths harder than Year 6?

It is more abstract rather than simply harder. Letters, negative numbers and ratio notation are new, and the Curriculum and Assessment Review recognised that moving from primary to secondary maths is often difficult.

### Is there a test in Year 7 maths?

There is no national test in Key Stage 3. Schools set their own assessments, which the Review notes they have done since Key Stage 3 national tests were cancelled in 2008.

### Does my child's secondary school get their SATs results?

Yes. Secondary schools with a Year 7 intake can access results from early July, and the Review notes they can see detailed results, including responses to each question.

### How are Year 7 students put into maths sets?

That is each school's decision, and practice varies. Ask your child's school how and when it groups students for maths; we make no claim about any particular school.

### My child struggled with Year 6 maths. Will Year 7 be a problem?

Not if the gaps are found early. The KS3 programme expects schools to build on Key Stage 2, and a tutor can close fraction, decimal and times tables gaps inside the new Year 7 topics.

### Should my child use a calculator in Year 7?

Only when the teacher asks. Key Stage 3 develops written and mental methods alongside calculators, and secure arithmetic makes algebra far easier.

### How can I help with Year 7 maths homework?

Ask your child to explain the method aloud before writing, and check one answer by putting the numbers back in. You do not need to know the method yourself to ask good questions.

### When can Year 7 lessons be scheduled?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. All times are in UK time.

### Are you connected with my child's secondary school?

No. We run separately from every school. Our tutors teach to the published Key Stage 3 programme and your child's own scheme, but have no tie to any school or to the DfE.
