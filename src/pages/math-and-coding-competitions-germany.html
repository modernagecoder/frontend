Maths and coding competitions in Germany

A pupil in Germany who likes puzzles, numbers or code can enter something in almost every month of the school year. The difficulty is finding them: the competitions belong to different organisers, run on different calendars and are entered in different ways. This page puts all of them on one timeline for 2026/27, then sorts them by Klasse and by the skill they reward, and says who has to sign your child up.

Two crowded months

November 2026 holds five rows of the table, from the first Biber day to the Jugend forscht deadline. A pupil in Klasse 8 who enters the Biber, the Olympiade and the Bundeswettbewerb Informatik meets all three between Monday 9 and Monday 23 November. That is manageable only if the long Bundeswettbewerb tasks were started in September, when round 1 opened.

February 2027 is the second peak: the Olympiade Landesrunde, the Pangea preliminary round and the regional Jugend forscht contests all fall within a few weeks of each other.

One quiet stretch

Between the Pangea final on 12 June 2027 and the IOI opening on 12 September 2027 the national calendar is almost empty. Our own count in the project below makes it 92 days. For a pupil who wants to compete again in the autumn, the summer is the time to learn new material, not to sit contests.

The Känguru moves in 2027. It is normally written on the third Thursday in March, but its organiser has put it on 8 April 2027: in 2027 that March Thursday would sit right on the eve of the Osterferien in nearly all Länder.

Puzzlers and builders

A child who loves riddles but has never coded belongs in the first row. BWINF states that the Biber needs no prior knowledge. In our experience its tasks are about order, rules, patterns and information, the ideas underneath programming, told as small stories.

A child who would rather make something than answer questions belongs in the last row. The BWKI asks for a working project that uses machine-learning methods, with written documentation, runnable code and a two to four minute video, and it accepts projects in German or in English.

Coders and provers

The second and third rows are where weekly practice shows. Bundeswettbewerb Informatik round 1 can be solved without deep informatics knowledge, according to BWINF, but round 2 is clearly harder and must be done alone. The national selection for the IOI then trains in C++.

The maths rows reward a different habit: writing down why. In each home round the Bundeswettbewerb Mathematik sets four problems, from geometry, combinatorics, number theory and algebra, and a pupil needs at least three correct solutions in round 1 for a prize and a place in round 2.

Where it leads

How do pupils in Germany reach the IOI or the IMO team?

Two national ladders start inside these school competitions. Both take more than a year and both end in a team of only a handful of pupils, so for most families they matter as a direction of travel, not a target.

Informatics: to the IOI

BWINF has run the German selection for the International Olympiad in Informatics since 1989. The route goes through the Bundeswettbewerb Informatik: from its top 20 at most, three training camps pick the team that represents Germany at the IOI the following year.

The 39th IOI takes place from 12 to 19 September 2027 at the Hasso-Plattner-Institut in Potsdam, Germany's first time as host since 1992. Its pre-selection began on 5 September 2026 with 72 pupils, trains online on Saturdays in C++, and two qualification contests in November decide who is invited to the camps. The 45th Bundeswettbewerb, which opened on 1 September 2026, is already the entry into the selection for the IOI 2028 in Japan.

Maths: to the IMO

The six-person German team for the International Mathematical Olympiad is chosen in a selection competition called the AIMO. It invites the strongest pupils from the Olympiade Bundesrunde, from round 2 of the Bundeswettbewerb Mathematik and from the maths winners of Jugend forscht at Land level.

The AIMO begins each year at the end of November or the start of December with two papers. The top 16 then attend five preparation seminars from February to May, and the final seminar is held at the Mathematisches Forschungsinstitut Oberwolfach in the Black Forest.

A calendar project

Why does a computer sort German dates in the wrong order?

Our students build this calendar themselves, in Python, from the dates in the first table. The first attempt looks finished and is wrong in a way that every programmer in Germany meets sooner or later.

The plan

German organisers write dates as day, month, year with dots: 11.11.2026, 08.04.2027. Students type twelve of them into a list of pairs, a name and a date, and ask Python to sort the list so the earliest event comes first.

Sorting is one line in Python, which is exactly why it is tempting not to think about what is being sorted.

The trap

A date written as text is just a string of characters. Python compares two strings one character at a time from the left, so for German dates it compares the day first, then the month, and the year last of all.

Any date on the first of a month therefore sorts before any date on the eighth, whatever the month or the year.

An April 2027 contest has jumped ahead of the November 2026 Biber. Nothing crashed and no error appeared, which makes this kind of bug dangerous: the output has the right shape and the wrong order. The fix is to stop sorting text and sort dates. Python's strptime reads the German pattern, day, dot, month, dot, four-digit year, and turns each string into a real date that knows which year it belongs to.

  1. Ages 8 to 10, on paper. Write the twelve dates on cards, shuffle them, and sort them by hand. Then sort them again reading only the first two digits. Which cards move?
  2. Ages 10 to 13, Python. Run Version 1 and explain, in one sentence, why 01.12.2026 comes first. Then change the dates to the year-month-day form 2026-12-01 and sort them as text. Why does that order come out right?
  3. Ages 13 and up. Add the closing date of every registration to the list and print, for a date you type in, the next three deadlines. Test it with 1 December 2026, a day on which one window opens.

The program, its output and the gap of 92 days are our own work, run with Python 3 on 3 November 2026. The dates come from the organisers named in the first table.

Practice

What do competition problems feel like? Three of ours, with answers

We never reproduce an organiser's tasks. BWINF, the Olympiade and the Känguru publish their own past problems for free on their websites, and pupils should use them. These three are written by our mentors in the same spirit.

1. Logic without code (Klasse 3 to 6)

Problem. Five logs lie in a row with the numbers 3, 1, 2, 5, 4. A beaver may only swap two logs that lie next to each other. What is the smallest number of swaps that puts the logs in the order 1, 2, 3, 4, 5?

Answer. 3. Count the pairs that are the wrong way round: 3 before 1, 3 before 2, 5 before 4. Each swap of neighbours fixes exactly one such pair, so three swaps are needed, and three are enough.

The idea underneath. Counting what is out of order tells you how much work a sort must do. That is the beginning of algorithm analysis, at eight years old.

2. Quick maths (Klasse 5 and 6)

Problem. Lena writes every whole number from 1 to 30. How many times does she write the digit 2? (A) 3 (B) 11 (C) 12 (D) 13 (E) 14

Answer. (D) 13. In the ones place: 2, 12 and 22, three times. In the tens place: 20 to 29, ten times. The 22 has been counted once in each place, which is right, because it contains two digits 2.

The trap. Pupils who count numbers that contain a 2 get 12. The question asks for digits.

3. A first proof (Klasse 7 and 8). Problem: show that the sum of any five whole numbers in a row is divisible by 5, and decide whether 2026 and 2030 can be written as such a sum. Solution: call the middle number n. The five numbers are n − 2, n − 1, n, n + 1 and n + 2, and the twos and ones cancel, so the sum is 5n, always a multiple of 5. Since 2030 = 5 · 406, it is 404 + 405 + 406 + 407 + 408. And 2026 leaves remainder 1 when divided by 5, so it can never be written this way. What earns the marks: an Olympiade answer of "2030 yes, 2026 no" with no reason scores little. The sentence about 5n is the solution.

Maths and coding competitions in Germany: common questions

Which coding competitions can pupils in Germany enter?

The Informatik-Biber for Klassen 3 to 13 (9 to 20 November 2026), the Jugendwettbewerb Informatik with its Bronze, Silber and Gold levels, and the Bundeswettbewerb Informatik for young people up to 21, whose round 1 runs from 1 September to 23 November 2026. Its top entrants go on to the selection for the IOI.

Which maths competitions are there in Germany?

The Mathematik-Olympiade (Regionalrunde 11 November 2026, Bundesrunde 30 May to 2 June 2027), the Känguru der Mathematik on 8 April 2027, the Pangea competition with its final on 12 June 2027, and the Bundeswettbewerb Mathematik for pupils on the way to the Hochschulreife.

Do these competitions cost money?

The Informatik-Biber is free, according to its organiser BWINF, whose competitions are supported by the Kultusministerkonferenz. Other organisers set their own terms, so check each organiser's page for its own rules. Our preparation classes are a separate, paid service, with a free first class.

Can my child enter without the school?

For some, yes. The Jugendwettbewerb Informatik, the Bundeswettbewerb Informatik and the Bundeswettbewerb Mathematik accept pupils who register themselves, and Jugend forscht and the BWKI take individual or team entries. The Biber, the Olympiade, the Känguru and Pangea are organised through schools.

Are the classes in German?

No. Every class is live and taught in English only, by a mentor who matches your child's pace and accent. We teach the German words that appear in the tasks, so a pupil knows what Aufgabe, Lösung or Begründung asks of them.

When is the Känguru der Mathematik in 2027?

On Thursday 8 April 2027. It is normally held on the third Thursday in March, but in 2027 that Thursday comes directly before the Osterferien in nearly all Länder, so the organiser moved it. Schools register online from 1 December 2026 to 31 January 2027.

Where is the IOI 2027?

At the Hasso-Plattner-Institut in Potsdam, from 12 to 19 September 2027. It is the 39th International Olympiad in Informatics, and Germany last hosted the event in 1992. BWINF organises it with the HPI and runs the German team selection.

Is AI allowed in these competitions?

It depends on the competition. The Bundeswettbewerb Mathematik excludes using artificial intelligence to solve the problems or to check a solution, while the BWKI is itself an AI competition that requires machine-learning methods. Read the rules of each round. In our classes, pupils solve problems themselves.

When are classes held in Germany?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Most pupils come in the late afternoon in German time or on a weekend morning.

Do you use the official competition tasks?

No. Our mentors write original problems in the same style. Past tasks are published for free by the organisers themselves, and we point pupils to those archives so they practise on the real thing at home.
