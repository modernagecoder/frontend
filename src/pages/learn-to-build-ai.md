## The distinction this page exists to make

**Using AI** is operating a finished product. You type an instruction, you read what comes back,
and the skill is phrasing plus knowing the current product's habits. The ceiling is whatever the
vendor chose to expose. When the product changes, most of what you learned resets. Real value, but
roughly six weeks of it.

**Building AI** is making the thing that answers. You find data, clean it, and defend every
decision you made about it. You pick a model and understand the assumption it quietly makes on your
behalf. Success is an error you can state out loud in units anyone can check. The ceiling is your
mathematics and your patience, and the foundation survives every product cycle.

We teach both, in that order, and we are explicit about which one keeps its value.

## What building AI actually consists of

1. **Programming you can think in.** Python until the language disappears and only the problem is left.
2. **Data, and the arguments inside it.** Loading, cleaning, joining, plotting. A missing value is a decision, not an inconvenience.
3. **The mathematics, taught in place.** Linear algebra for how data is shaped, calculus for how a model improves itself, statistics for whether an improvement is real.
4. **Models, starting deliberately small.** Regression before neural networks, because someone who can interpret a coefficient can later interpret a network.
5. **Evaluation, which is the actual skill.** Splitting data honestly, reporting error in real units, resisting the number that flatters you.
6. **Shipping, and watching it decay.** Deployment, then deep learning, language models, retrieval and agents on top of foundations that hold them up.

## Direct answers

**Do I need to be good at mathematics?**
Less than people fear, more than the marketing suggests. Linear algebra, calculus and statistics,
all taught inside the machine learning rather than as a prerequisite course.

**Is prompt engineering worth learning?**
Yes, briefly and properly, roughly six weeks rather than a career. It goes wrong when it is sold as
an AI education.

**Live or recorded?**
Live, always, with the same teacher week after week. Nothing here is a recorded course sold as a
class, and nothing is auto-graded.

**Can this replace a computer science degree?**
No, and we will not claim otherwise. Most of our university-age students take it alongside a
degree, not instead of one.
