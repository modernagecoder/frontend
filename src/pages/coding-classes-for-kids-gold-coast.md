## How far apart are the G:link stops, and what does a stem-and-leaf plot show?

Twenty-seven stops from Helensvale to Burleigh Heads in OpenStreetMap, 26 gaps measured between neighbours, sorted and drawn.

| 16 | 1 |
| 21 | 1 |

### Shortest and longest

The shortest gap is 340 metres, between Southport South and Broadwater Parklands. The longest is 3,008 metres, between Helensvale and Parkwood at the northern end.

### The middle value

With 26 gaps sorted, the middle sits between the 13th and 14th, 808 and 838 metres, so the median is 823 metres.

### The average

Add all 26 gaps and divide by 26 and you get 959.9 metres, well above the median, because a few long gaps in the north pull it up.

### Why the plot beats a single number

A stem-and-leaf plot keeps every value while showing the shape. Most leaves bunch between stems 4 and 9, then the stems thin out and jump to 16, 21 and 30. That tail is why the mean and median disagree. A child who sees the plot can explain the difference without a formula: a few big gaps make the average look longer than a typical trip between stops.

All gaps are our calculations from the stop positions in an OpenStreetMap snapshot taken on 7 October 2026. They are straight lines between stop points, not the distance along the track, and OpenStreetMap is mapped by volunteers. We make no claim about travel times or timetables. From Helensvale to Burleigh Heads in a straight line is 21,377 metres, less than the gaps added together, because the line bends.

How children learn it

## Sorting numbers into a picture, at every age

Stem-and-leaf plots grow from sorting and place value, so younger children can start with blocks and older ones finish with Python.

| Year level | Task | Takeaway |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prep to Year 2 | Sorts number cards into rows by their tens digit | Place value and grouping |
| Years 3 to 6 | Builds a Scratch program that sorts a list of distances and prints each stem with its leaves | Sorting, splitting a number into parts and loops inside loops |
| Years 7 and 8 | Measures the G:link gaps in Python, draws the plot and compares mean with median | Distances from coordinates, sorted lists and choosing the right average |

### Splitting a number

Turning 582 into a stem of 5 and a leaf of 8 uses whole-number division and remainders, which children meet in maths and then put to work in code.

### Empty stems matter

There are no gaps in the 1,400s or 1,500s, so those stems are missing. Older children argue about whether to show empty stems, and learn that showing them makes the gap in the data visible.

### Check the count

The plot must contain exactly 26 leaves, one per gap. If a leaf is missing or doubled, the child goes looking for the bug before trusting the picture.

## If an AI tool can find the average, why should a Gold Coast child learn to plot the data?

Because an average on its own can mislead, and a child who has drawn the plot knows when to ask for the median too.

Ask an AI tool for the typical gap between G:link stops and it may simply give the mean, 959.9 metres, and sound certain. The stem-and-leaf plot shows why that number is a little misleading: most gaps are shorter, and a handful of long ones in the north drag it up. A child who has built the plot will ask for the median, 823 metres, and explain the difference.

We want young Gold Coast coders to treat a single number with healthy curiosity. In our lessons, AI tools appear only in age-appropriate ways and with a teacher present, and children practise asking what a summary hides as well as what it says.

That is a strong reason to keep learning to code in 2026: it lets a child look behind a number. A Gold Coast child who has drawn 26 gaps as stems and leaves already does. The longer argument is in [the longer argument for learning to code](/blog/is-coding-worth-learning-2026).

## Questions from Gold Coast parents

What Gold Coast families tend to ask first.

### What age can my Gold Coast child start coding?

From age 6, which on the coast usually means Prep or Year 1. Blocks and pictures come first; the keyboard comes in once reading and confidence allow.

### What is a stem-and-leaf plot?

A stem-and-leaf plot is a way of showing numbers in order where each number is split into a stem, such as its hundreds, and a leaf, such as its tens. It keeps every value while showing the shape of the whole set.

### What is the G:link project?

Children take the 27 G:link stops from Helensvale to Burleigh Heads in OpenStreetMap, measure the 26 straight-line gaps between neighbours, and draw them as a stem-and-leaf plot. The median gap is 823 metres and the mean 959.9 metres.

### Why are the mean and median different?

Because a few gaps are much longer than the rest, up to 3,008 metres between Helensvale and Parkwood. Long values pull the mean up but barely move the median.
