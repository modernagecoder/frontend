The region in figures

How many people live in the Piura region, and in which provinces?

The 2025 census counted 2,115,215 people in the department of Piura. That makes it the largest of INEI's department-level units after Lima Metropolitana. Its people live in eight provinces, and the capital is the city of Piura.

The towns people name

INEI has not yet published 2025 figures for cities, so town sizes still come from the 2017 census. That year INEI counted 473,025 people in the city of Piura, taken as one urban area. Among the larger towns, INEI's 2017 directory of populated centres lists Sullana at 136,446, Talara at 91,444, Paita at 81,163, Chulucanas at 40,867 and Sechura at 33,134.

Those directory figures count one populated centre each, so a town that runs into a neighbouring centre is counted short. We print them as published and never stitch two together.

Districts larger than many cities

Inside the capital's province, the 2025 census gives Veintiséis de Octubre 206,998 people, Castilla 193,614 and the district of Piura itself 155,566. Sullana district has 195,448.

For a live online class none of these lines matter much. A teenager in Castilla, a child in Talara and an adult in the Huancabamba highlands open the same video link at the hour they chose.

What school covers

Do schools in the Piura region teach programming?

Not as a subject. Piura's schools follow Peru's national curriculum, the CNEB, which expects digital skills inside every area but gives programming no place of its own on the timetable.

Where the rows come from: the General Education Law 28044 fixes six years of primaria and five of secundaria; MINEDU's normative-age rule takes the age a child has on 31 March; the CNEB study plan gives the secundaria hours. All three documents were checked in November 2026.

One competency, spread thin

Peru's curriculum has one competency about technology, number 28, on working in virtual environments created by ICT. It is transversal: every area is meant to touch it, and no area owns it. Its four capacities go from setting up a digital space to creating virtual objects, but none of them asks a teacher to explain a loop or a variable.

Look at the regular secundaria timetable and the gap is plain: four weekly hours of Matemática, four of Comunicación, three of Inglés, two of Educación para el Trabajo, and nothing labelled programming or computing anywhere in the 35.

What the DRE Piura did in January 2026

The regional education office, the DRE Piura, opened summer workshops in January 2026 "dirigidos a niñas y niños de 8 a 12 años". One of them was CompuKids, offered in a morning and an afternoon shift on Mondays and Wednesdays. It shows the demand: computing for children in Piura lives in the summer break, not in the school week.

A day later the DRE Piura announced teacher programmes on digital competencies, TIC tools and "la aplicación de la inteligencia artificial en los procesos de enseñanza y aprendizaje". That work trains teachers; it does not add a programming class for students.

The ministry has a Python programme, but it is a small one far from Piura. Fábrica de Programadores, announced by MINEDU in July 2025, trains a first group of 1,000 public secundaria pupils in their last two years, all in Lima Norte, and graduates may take the PCEP certification exam. A teenager in Sullana or Paita who wants that kind of grounding has to look outside colegio, and that is the gap our evening and weekend classes in English are built to fill.

Reading the table honestly

In 2025 about 22 homes in every hundred in the department of Piura had a computer, against about 34 in every hundred nationally. The share of Piura homes with an internet service fell from 45.3 per cent in 2022 to 41.2 per cent in 2025, while the share of people who use the internet somewhere, at home, school, work or on a phone, rose to 77.6 per cent.

So most people in Piura are online, but many through a phone and a mobile plan rather than a home connection and a computer. A coding class can be followed on a phone; it cannot really be done on one, because the learner has to type, run and fix code.

What we suggest, by age

Six to eight year olds can start block coding on a tablet or a shared family laptop, because they drag pieces rather than type. From about nine, a keyboard matters: a basic laptop, a desktop at a relative's house or a computer at a parent's workplace after hours all work.

The free first class doubles as a check. The mentor sees the screen, the sound and the connection, and if a phone is the only device, says so kindly and suggests what to try before paying for anything.

Taught in English

Will my child be taught in Spanish or in English?

In English, always. Our mentors teach from India over live video, adjust to how fast and with what accent each learner speaks, and never switch the class to Spanish. We put this before any booking because in Piura it matters.

Where Piura stands in English

The EF English Proficiency Index 2025 gives Peru 519 points, 52nd of 123 countries, in the moderate band. On the same page the Piura region scores 482 and the city of Piura 484, both in EF's low band, below the national figure.

That tells us many Piura children will meet English in our classes as a language they are still learning. We plan for it rather than pretend otherwise.

How a mentor works with that

Short sentences, the same words for the same ideas every week, and code on the screen that says what the sentence said. A mentor waits for an answer, accepts one that starts in Spanish, and gives back the English word without fuss.

Python helps: print, if, while and return are English words, so a child who learns the code also learns the vocabulary. After a few months, many learners follow the class more easily than they speak about it, and that is fine.

If English is a real barrier for your child today, tell us in the free class. A one-to-one plan lets a mentor go slower and repeat more, and some families start with a parent sitting in for the first few weeks. For learners at a bilingual colegio, the classes simply run in the language they already use at school.

A Piura data project

How much more does the market stall charge than the wholesaler?

The Gobierno Regional de Piura publishes the wholesale and retail prices recorded at the Mercado Modelo de Piura as open data. Our students use it for a lesson every programmer meets sooner or later: before you divide one number by another, make sure they are measured in the same unit.

The dataset

GORE Piura shares "Precios mayorista y minorista del Mercado Modelo de Piura" through datosabiertos.gob.pe with an Open Data Commons Attribution licence (ODC-By), which allows reuse with credit. It first appeared in February 2025 and was last updated on 9 September 2026. Our copy, mimercado_dataset.csv, fetched on 9 November 2026, holds 7,464 rows: 51 products across 151 market days between 8 January 2024 and 13 March 2026.

Each row gives a product, its category, a wholesale price and a retail price, and two unit columns, one for each price. Those unit columns are where the lesson lives.

The question

Children who go shopping with their family know the stall charges more than the wholesaler. So we ask: on the last day in the file, which product has the smallest gap, and which the biggest? We measure the gap as retail price divided by wholesale price, so 1.5 means the stall asks one and a half times as much.

The first program reads the 50 products recorded on 13 March 2026, divides, sorts and prints both ends. We never print the prices themselves on this page; the ratios tell the story.

Pork with the smallest gap sounds plausible until a student opens the row. Its wholesale price is labelled per UNIDAD and its retail price per KILOGRAMO. A unit and a kilogram are different things, so Version 1 divided one kind of quantity by another and called the answer a ratio. It is the same mistake as comparing the price of a sack of rice with the price of a kilo.

Version 2 adds two checks. If the two unit columns disagree, the row is set aside with a note. If they agree but the unit makes no sense for the category, the row is also set aside, because a matching label can still be the wrong label.

Now the smallest gap belongs to papaya, at 1.15, and the green broad bean still has the biggest, at 2.63. The second check caught something the first could not: the bellaco plantain has LITRO in both unit columns. The labels agree with each other, and both are surely wrong, because nobody sells plantains by the litre.

Then comes the twist that makes this a real lesson rather than a trick. The dataset's own description says: "La unidad de medida de los precios es por Kilogramo o por Litro, según la presentación del producto." In other words, the publisher says every price is per kilogram or per litre, and the SACO or MALLA in a unit column names how the product arrives at the market, not what the price is for. If that is true, most of Version 2's 19 set-aside rows were fine all along. A good programmer does not choose between the code and the description; they write down both, and ask the publisher. Version 2 still earned its place, because it is what made us go and read the description.

Across the whole file, 2,714 of the 7,464 rows carry two different unit labels, and 10 rows hold a zero price, so a program that divides on every date must handle that too.

  1. Under 8, at the kitchen table. A bag of rice and a single cup of rice both have a price. Which is bigger? Why can you not say which is cheaper until you know how much rice is in each?
  2. Block coding, around 8 to 10. Make two lists, wholesale and retail, for five fruits from the table, and a loop that shows which fruit has the biggest gap.
  3. First Python, around 10 to 12. Run Version 1 yourself, then find the pork row and explain in two sentences why its answer cannot be trusted.
  4. Ages 13 and over. Run Version 1 on every date instead of one. It stops with a ZeroDivisionError. Find the product and month that cause it, and decide what the program should do with a zero price.
  5. Going further. Write Version 3, which follows the publisher's description: treat every price as per kilogram or litre, keep only the plantain on the check list, and compare its smallest gap with Version 2's.

Contests

Can a secundaria student in Piura take part in maths and computing olympiads?

Yes, and the door is the colegio. The ONEM, Peru's school maths olympiad, begins inside each school, and UTEC runs the OPI computing olympiad at no cost to any secundaria pupil. Modern Age Coders has no link with MINEDU, UTEC or either contest.

The ONEM in 2026

El Peruano reported that the 2026 ONEM brought in more than 1.7 million pupils nationwide. Every colegio sat its first round on the same day, Wednesday 8 July 2026, in three levels set by grade; winners then climbed through the UGEL round and the regional round before a national final.

We do not reproduce the organiser's problems. We teach the habits the ONEM rewards with problems we write: reading slowly, testing small cases, and writing down why an answer must be right.

Why code helps with maths contests

A short Python loop can check a guess on a thousand cases in a second. That never replaces a proof, but it tells a student whether a pattern is worth proving, and it catches a wrong answer before the contest does.

Our practice problem for this page: a stall marks every price up by half, then a regular customer gets one third off. Is the customer paying more or less than the wholesale price? (Ours. Answer: exactly the wholesale price, because 1.5 multiplied by two thirds is 1.)

Clock facts: the tz database zone America/Lima puts Peru five hours behind UTC with no daylight saving since 1994, and INACAL keeps Peru's official hour; India sits five and a half hours ahead of UTC. The gap from Piura to India is therefore ten and a half hours, on every date.

Because no clock changes on either side, a slot fixed in January still falls at the same moment in August. Coding and AI classes in the Piura region: questions and answers

Are the classes in Spanish?

No. English is the only language of our classes, which are always live and online. Mentors talk slowly in short sentences, adapt to how each learner speaks, and when a child answers in Spanish they simply offer the English words back.

Is there a coding class for children in Piura?

Yes, held live over video, so the province does not matter: Piura city, Sullana, Talara, Paita, Chulucanas or a village in Ayabaca all join the same way. The youngest begin with blocks at six, and most move to typed Python around nine.

Can my child take the classes on a phone?

A phone is enough to watch, but not to learn coding well, because the learner has to type and fix code. Young children can begin on a tablet. From about nine we recommend a laptop or desktop, and the free first class checks whatever device you have.

What time are the classes in Piura?

Any day, any time, flexible: choose a batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at the hour that suits your family. With India ten and a half hours ahead of Piura, a class at 08:00 in Piura is at 18:30 for the mentor.

Do schools in Piura teach coding?

No timetabled subject covers it. Digital skills are spread across all areas of the national curriculum, and secundaria's regular week gives programming no hours. The DRE Piura did run a CompuKids workshop for children of 8 to 12 in the January 2026 holidays.

Can adults in Piura join?

Yes, in adult-only groups or privately. Most begin with Python and continue with SQL and data analysis; anyone who already keeps prices, stock or sales in spreadsheets tends to get the most from the data courses.

Do you teach AI to children?

We teach how AI works, with small programs children build themselves, and we follow each tool's age rules. Children under 13 do not use adult chatbot accounts in our classes; teenagers use AI tools only where the provider allows their age.

Can you help with the ONEM?

We train the careful reasoning olympiad problems need, using practice sets we write, never the official papers. Entry is always through the student's colegio; in 2026 the first round took place on 8 July. Modern Age Coders is independent of MINEDU and the ONEM.

How much do classes cost?

Each plan has a monthly fee, displayed here and billed in US dollars; the card issuer turns it into soles. Someone opening the page from India is shown rupees instead. No card is needed for the free first class.

Do students get a certificate?

Where a course has one, the learner receives a completion certificate from Modern Age Coders. It carries no Peruvian school credit, and neither MINEDU, the DRE Piura nor any colegio issues or backs it; it simply records what was built.
