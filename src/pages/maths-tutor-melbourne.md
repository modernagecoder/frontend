Stand on Swanston Street and walk one block west to Elizabeth Street. Most people would guess the distance; a map will tell you it is about 225 metres between the middle of one street and the middle of the next. That small investigation uses maps, averages, unit conversion and judgement about measurement error, which is the kind of maths we teach. ## How many chains apart are Melbourne's central city streets?

A chain is an old surveying length: 66 feet, which is exactly 20.1168 metres. We measured the central city grid from the map and asked whether its spacing looks like a whole number of chains.

From OpenStreetMap we took every mapped piece of the five main streets running across the grid (Flinders, Collins, Bourke, Lonsdale and La Trobe) and the nine running up it (Spencer to Spring). The grid is tilted about 20.08 degrees from east-west, so we rotated our axes to match, then found the middle line of each street. The table shows the gap between each pair of neighbouring streets, centre to centre.

**Gaps between neighbouring street centrelines, central city grid (our calculation from OpenStreetMap)**

| From | To | Metres | Chains |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flinders | Collins | 242.5 | 12.06 |
| Collins | Bourke | 225.4 | 11.20 |
| Bourke | Lonsdale | 227.4 | 11.30 |
| Lonsdale | La Trobe | 241.9 | 12.03 |
| Spencer | King | 221.5 | 11.01 |
| King | William | 245.6 | 12.21 |
| William | Queen | 230.9 | 11.48 |
| Queen | Elizabeth | 231.4 | 11.50 |
| Elizabeth | Swanston | 225.4 | 11.20 |
| Swanston | Russell | 236.0 | 11.73 |
| Russell | Exhibition | 223.8 | 11.13 |
| Exhibition | Spring | 240.5 | 11.95 |

### The average

The twelve gaps average 232.7 metres, or 11.57 chains. Eleven and a half chains is 231.3 metres, only 1.4 metres from our average.

### Why single gaps disagree

Individual gaps run from 221.5 to 245.6 metres. Wide streets, dual carriageways and the way the map draws a street's middle line all move a single reading. Averaging many readings cancels much of that noise.

### The simple conversion wins

Using "a chain is about 20 metres" gives 11.63 chains instead of 11.57. For this question the rough conversion is close enough; the exact one matters only when the answer is tight.

### What students practise

Converting between units, rotating axes, finding a median to resist outliers, averaging repeated measurements and judging whether a pattern is real.

Primary learners can measure one block with an online map; VCE students can model the measurement error properly.

### What we did not claim

We do not claim anything about how or why the grid was surveyed; we only report what the map measures today. Across the whole grid, Flinders to La Trobe is 937.2 metres and Spencer to Spring 1855.0 metres between centrelines.

A good lesson keeps the measurement and the story separate.

**Try it at home: the same investigation in five steps**

| Step | What to do | The maths inside |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Open any online map and find two parallel streets one block apart | Recognising parallel lines on a plan |
| 2 | Use the map's measuring tool from the middle of one street to the middle of the next, at right angles | Perpendicular distance between lines |
| 3 | Repeat for four or five other pairs | Why one reading is not enough |
| 4 | Find the mean and the median of your readings | Two kinds of average, and which resists an odd reading |
| 5 | Divide by 20.1168 to convert to chains, then by 20 for the quick version | Unit conversion and estimating error |

A Year 5 student can do the first three steps with a parent. A Year 9 student can add the averages and explain why the median barely moves when one strange reading is included. A VCE student can go further and estimate how far the true spacing could be from the measured one, given how the map was drawn. The same short task scales from primary school to senior study, which is exactly why we like it.

## Questions Melbourne families ask about a maths tutor

### What does a maths tutor in Melbourne do?

A tutor diagnoses gaps, teaches the missing ideas and builds confidence through practice. Our lessons are live online in Melbourne time, privately or in small groups matched by level.

### Which VCE maths studies do you cover?

All four in the VCAA study design: Foundation Mathematics, General Mathematics, Mathematical Methods and Specialist Mathematics.

### What is a chain?

An old unit of length used in land surveying, equal to 66 feet or exactly 20.1168 metres. Our measurements put neighbouring central Melbourne streets about 11.6 chains apart, centre to centre.

### Do you guarantee a study score?

No. We teach thoroughly and help with exam habits, but we make no score or ATAR promises.

### Can Prep children have lessons?

Yes. Early lessons use objects, pictures and short games around number.

### Are lessons in Melbourne time?

Yes, and we adjust when daylight saving starts and ends.

### Do you teach adults?

Yes, in adult groups or privately, for bridging units, work, or simply to understand the maths that school rushed past.

### Why measure streets in a maths lesson?

Because real measurements are messy. Students learn to repeat readings, choose a sensible average, convert units and decide how accurate an answer needs to be, which textbook exercises with tidy numbers rarely teach.
