How the mark is built

How ICS3U and ICS4U are marked, from the curriculum

Before practising, it helps to know what a teacher is looking for. The 2008 Computer Studies curriculum sets the rules every Ontario school follows, and two parts of it matter most at exam time: how the final grade splits, and the four categories on the achievement chart.

The final grade

Most of the grade, 70 per cent of it, is earned before the final, from work evaluated during the course. The curriculum adds that this part “should reflect the student’s most consistent level of achievement throughout the course, although special consideration should be given to more recent evidence of achievement.”

The remaining 30 per cent comes from a final evaluation near the end of the course, which the document allows to be an examination, a performance, an essay or another method that suits the course. In computer science that is often a written exam, a coding task, a project or a combination; your teacher decides.

The credit line

The achievement chart ends with a plain rule: “A student whose achievement is below 50% at the end of a course will not obtain a credit for the course.” Each level on the chart has a percentage band: 50 to 59 for level 1, 60 to 69 for level 2, 70 to 79 for level 3 and 80 to 100 for level 4.

Level 3 is called the “provincial standard”, and the document is reassuring about it: “Parents of students achieving at level 3 can be confident that their children will be prepared for work in subsequent courses.”

The four achievement chart categories, defined in the 2008 Computer Studies curriculum, and what each looks like on an exam (definitions quoted; the last column is ours)
CategoryThe curriculum’s definitionOn a computer science exam (ours)
Knowledge and Understanding“Subject-specific content acquired in each course (knowledge), and the comprehension of its meaning and significance (understanding)”Vocabulary, error types, what a line of code does, number systems
Thinking“The use of critical and creative thinking skills and/or processes”Tracing, finding and fixing a bug, choosing test cases; the chart names “detecting and correcting flaws”
Communication“The conveying of meaning through various forms.”Explaining a concept in sentences, pseudocode, clear names and comments
Application“The use of knowledge and skills to make connections within and between various contexts.”Writing working code for a task you have not seen in exactly that form

What the levels mean

The chart describes each level with one qualifier, used the same way in every category: “limited for level 1, some for level 2, considerable for level 3, and a high degree or thorough for level 4”. A level 4 trace is not a longer trace; it is one done with a high degree of effectiveness.

The document also corrects a common belief: level 4 “does not mean that the student has achieved expectations beyond those specified for a particular course”. Top marks come from doing the course’s own work very well, not from extra topics.

Why our mocks use all four

The curriculum tells teachers to assess “in a balanced manner with respect to the four categories”. Students tend to revise only Application, which means writing code, and then lose marks on a written explanation or a trace.

So every question in our two mocks is tagged with one category, and the marking guides total the marks by category. A student can see at a glance whether the weak spot is knowing, thinking, explaining or doing.

Totals by category

ICS3U, 50 marks: Knowledge and Understanding 12, Thinking 12, Application 18, Communication 8. ICS4U, 40 marks: Knowledge and Understanding 4, Thinking 14, Application 16, Communication 6.

The ICS4U paper leans on Thinking because Grade 12 adds recursion and algorithm analysis, both of which are reasoning before they are code. Your school’s split may differ; ask the teacher how the final is weighted.

The rules for sitting them

Sit each one in one go, on paper, with no notes and no computer: 75 minutes for ICS3U and 60 for ICS4U. Write code by hand where a question asks for code. Mark it with the guide only after the time is up.

Both papers use Python for most code. Where a question gives Java, read it as Java; where it asks you to write code, use whichever language your class uses.

Reading your score

Total your marks by section as well as overall. A student with 16 of 18 in Application but 4 of 12 in Thinking needs tracing practice, not more coding, even though the total looks fine.

Questions 7 and 8 are the ones most students drop marks on. Both reward slowing down: working out what a line really does, and choosing tests at the edges rather than in the middle.

If you scored low on one section

Knowledge and Understanding: make flash cards for error types, operators and number systems. Thinking: trace three short programs a day on paper. Application: rewrite questions 9 to 11 from a blank file, then change the task slightly and write them again.

Communication: explain one concept a day out loud in four sentences, then write it down. Pseudocode improves fastest when someone else tries to follow yours.

What question 7 is really testing

Two languages, one method, two different failures. Python’s floor division sends -1 to -1 forever, which is the infinite recursion pitfall the ICS4U curriculum names. Java’s division reaches the base case and returns a confident wrong answer, which is worse, because nothing warns you.

The lesson: a recursive method needs a precondition, here that the exponent is not negative, and the method should check it rather than trust the caller.

Where Grade 12 students lose marks

On Question 4, most lost marks are the wrap: forgetting that hours must also wrap at 24, or printing 0:0 instead of 00:00. On Question 3, students often check the wrong middle after moving lo or hi by mid instead of mid plus or minus one.

On Question 2, many write that a sorted list makes selection sort faster. It does not; it still compares every pair. Knowing which algorithms care about their input is a classic Grade 12 analysis point.

For the first test of the year

ICS3U Unit 1 test: a quick check, ours

Schools choose their own unit order, so Unit 1 is not the same everywhere. In the classes our students attend, the first test usually covers variables, operators and simple output, so here is a short check on exactly those, with answers. Predict each printed line before you run anything.

  1. 3 3 3.75. // keeps the whole part, % gives the remainder, and / gives the exact decimal. 15 is 3 fours with 3 left over.
  2. 9 9. After a = b, the 5 is gone. Then b = a copies 9 back into b. Swapping needs a third variable, or Python’s a, b = b, a.
  3. hahaha. Multiplying text by a whole number repeats it.
  4. False True. and needs both sides true; or needs at least one.
  5. 77.0. 25 times 9 is 225, divided by 5 is 45.0, plus 32 is 77.0. The / makes it a decimal even though the answer is whole.

Scored four or five?

You are ready for a first unit test on these basics. Spend your remaining time on the habit that matters for the rest of the course: predicting the output of short programs before running them.

Then try Section 1 of the ICS3U mock above. Its questions start at about this level and climb.

Scored three or fewer?

Go back to each one you missed and change a single value, then predict again. The swap question in particular trips up students at every level, and it reappears later when sorting arrays.

A teacher who watches you trace one program out loud can usually find the misunderstanding in a few minutes. That is often the fastest fix.

Using the mocks well

How to turn a mock exam into marks: a six-step routine

A mock is only useful if it changes what you do next. This routine is ours, and it works for these two papers and for your own old tests.

  1. Sit it cold, timed and on paper. No notes, no computer, the full time. The point is to find out what you can do under exam conditions, not what you can look up.
  2. Mark it the same day with the guide. Give yourself only the part marks the guide allows. Be strict; a teacher will be.
  3. Total by category. Knowledge and Understanding, Thinking, Communication, Application. Your lowest category is your revision plan for the week.
  4. Write an error log. For every lost mark, write the question, what you wrote, what was right, and why you missed it in one line. Patterns appear by the third entry.
  5. Redo the missed questions from scratch two days later. Not from memory of the answer: from a blank page. If you get it right, it counts as learned.
  6. Sit the other mock, or one of your own old tests, a week later. Compare category totals. The weak category should have moved.
What to do with a mock score (ours; a mock score is a practice signal, not an achievement chart level)

Exam help, not the credit

What our exam preparation is, and what it is not

We prepare students for their school’s exam. We do not set it, see it in advance or mark it, and the credit is the school’s alone.

What a session looks like

A student brings their course outline, their old tests and the date of the final. We sit a mock together or set one as homework, mark it with them, and spend the session on the lowest category: often tracing for Grade 11 and recursion for Grade 12.

In the last week we switch to short timed sets and explanation practice, because the night before is not the time for new topics.

Where the credit comes from

Only the school, night school, summer school or credit provider that teaches and evaluates ICS3U or ICS4U can grant its credit. Modern Age Coders runs classes beside that course; we do not grant Ontario credits and we never contact a school about a student’s exam.

We will not write any part of a student’s graded work, and we will not help anyone cheat on an exam. Practice papers like the two above exist so that a student can earn the mark themselves.

ICS3U and ICS4U exams: common questions

How much is the ICS3U or ICS4U final exam worth?

Under the 2008 Ontario curriculum, 30 per cent of the final grade comes from a final evaluation near the end of the course, such as an examination, a performance or another suitable method, and 70 per cent from evaluations during the course. Each school decides what its final evaluation contains.

What is on the ICS3U final exam?

It depends on the school and the teacher. ICS3U covers data types, one-dimensional arrays, control structures, subprograms, debugging and testing, software development, computer systems and topics in computer science, so finals usually draw on those. Ask the teacher what the final evaluation includes.

What is on the ICS4U final exam?

Again it depends on the school. ICS4U adds modular and object-oriented design, searching and sorting, recursion, algorithm analysis, formal testing and a team project, so Grade 12 finals usually include tracing recursion, comparing algorithms and writing classes.

Are these real exam questions?

No. Both mock exams were written by our teachers for this page. They are not taken from any school, board or past exam, and they do not predict what any particular exam will contain.

How are ICS3U and ICS4U exams marked?

Teachers use the achievement chart in the 2008 curriculum, which has four categories: Knowledge and Understanding, Thinking, Communication and Application. Our marking guides tag every question with one category so a student can see which one needs work.

What mark do I need to pass ICS3U or ICS4U?

The 2008 curriculum says a student whose achievement is below 50 per cent at the end of a course will not obtain a credit for it. Level 3, 70 to 79 per cent, is called the provincial standard.

How should I study for the ICS3U final?

Sit a timed mock on paper, mark it strictly, total the marks by category, keep an error log, redo missed questions from a blank page two days later, and sit a second paper a week after. Trace short programs by hand every day.

What is usually on an ICS3U Unit 1 test?

Schools set their own unit order. In our experience a first unit often covers variables, data types, operators and simple input and output. The Unit 1 check on this page tests exactly those, with answers.

Do you give credits or official exam results?

No. We prepare students beside the school course and do not grant Ontario credits. Exams, marks and credits come only from the school or credit provider that teaches the course.

When can we book exam practice sessions?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Times are set in your own Ontario time zone.

What do exam practice classes cost?

The first class is free. After that families pay a monthly fee and can stop at the end of any month, including straight after the exam. The fees section shows the current figure.
