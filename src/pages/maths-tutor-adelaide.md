## When Heron's formula says a triangle has no area

Heron's formula finds the area of a triangle from its three sides alone. It is exact in theory. In practice, with rounded measurements, it can be disastrously wrong for thin triangles. Adelaide's squares show why.

We took the position of each city square as the average of every mapped point carrying its name on OpenStreetMap, then joined squares into triangles. For each triangle we found the area two ways: from the coordinates (which is half base times height, done carefully) and from Heron's formula using the side lengths rounded to the nearest metre and to the nearest ten metres, as someone reading a map might.

**Triangles between Adelaide's squares (our calculation from OpenStreetMap)**

| Triangle | Sides in metres | Area from coordinates (m&sup2;) | Heron, sides to 1 m | Heron, sides to 10 m |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Light, Hindmarsh, Victoria | 692.1 / 682.5 / 1107.0 | 225,557 | 225,705 | 222,822 |
| Light, Victoria, Hurtle | 733.2 / 1412.8 / 682.5 | 31,923 | 32,523 | 0 |
| Hindmarsh, Victoria, Whitmore | 725.1 / 1416.9 / 692.1 | 10,309 | 0 | 0 |

### Fat triangles are safe

For Light, Hindmarsh and Victoria, Heron with sides to the metre gives 225,705 square metres against 225,557: close enough for any purpose.

### Thin triangles collapse

Light, Victoria and Hurtle almost lie on a line, so the longest side nearly equals the other two added together. Heron multiplies by "half the perimeter minus the longest side", a tiny number, and rounding wipes it out: zero when sides are rounded to ten metres.

### The simple method wins

Half base times height, using the coordinates directly, gives 31,923 square metres with no drama. The height above the longest side is only 45.2 metres, which is exactly why the triangle is so fragile.

### What students practise

Distances from coordinates with Pythagoras, Heron's formula, half base times height, rounding and why subtracting nearly equal numbers loses accuracy.

Middle years students do the distances; senior students explain the instability algebraically.

### Honest limits

Our square positions are averages of mapped points, not surveyed centres, so the exact areas depend on that choice. The lesson about thin triangles does not.

Map data: OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL.

**Two ways to the same area, step by step for the fat triangle**

| Step | Heron's formula | Coordinates |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Measure the three sides | Write down the three corner positions |
| 2 | Find s, half the perimeter | Take two edges as arrows from one corner |
| 3 | Multiply s, s minus each side | Cross-multiply the arrows' components |
| 4 | Take the square root | Halve the result and drop any minus sign |
| Weak point | Subtracting nearly equal numbers when the triangle is thin | Needs coordinates, not just lengths |

Neither method is wrong. Heron is perfect when only lengths are known and the triangle is reasonably fat, which is why it appears in senior courses. Coordinates are better whenever positions are known, which, in the age of maps, is most of the time. Knowing which tool suits which job is a large part of being good at mathematics, and it is a habit we teach explicitly from the middle years onwards.

Map positions come from [OpenStreetMap](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright) (ODbL), downloaded for this page on 13 October 2026; every centre, length and area above was worked out by us.

## Adelaide maths tutoring: common questions

### How does an Adelaide maths tutor help?

Regular live lessons that find and fill gaps, extend strong learners and build exam habits, privately or in small groups, booked in Adelaide time.

### Which SACE maths subjects do you teach?

Essential Mathematics, General Mathematics, Mathematical Methods and Specialist Mathematics.

### What is Heron's formula?

A rule giving a triangle's area from its three side lengths: the square root of s(s - a)(s - b)(s - c), where s is half the perimeter. It is exact, but very sensitive to rounding for thin triangles.

### Why did Heron's formula give zero for an Adelaide triangle?

Light, Victoria and Hurtle squares almost line up. With sides rounded to ten metres, the longest side equals the other two added together, so the formula multiplies by zero.
