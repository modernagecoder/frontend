## Class 10 AI paper analysis 2026: was the AI paper easy?

It was moderate: easy to start, with marks lost in a handful of precise items rather than in hard topics. The paper kept the familiar frame, 21 questions with 15 to answer in two hours for 50 marks, and covered every theory unit. Our section-by-section reading of set 4, from CBSE's own marking scheme, is below.

- Section | Marks counted (printed) | Our difficulty rating | Where the marks were won or lost

- A, Q1: employability skills objective | 4 (6) | Easy | Two items needed a written line, a definition and a fill-in, not just a choice

- A, Q2 to Q5: AI objective | 20 (24) | Easy to moderate | One two-statement item, two assertion-reason items and a domain question with an unexpected answer

- B, Q6 to Q10: employability two-markers | 6 (10) | Easy | Lists with partial marks, so a full list mattered more than neat prose

- B, Q11 to Q16: AI two-markers | 8 (12) | Moderate | Each wanted a difference or a definition plus an example; half a mark rode on each example

- B, Q17 to Q21: AI four-markers | 12 (20) | Moderate | Two calculation-style questions were the safest picks; the theory four-markers needed structure

### What teachers said on the day

Teachers quoted by India TV and Careers360 on 27 February 2026 described the paper as balanced and within the syllabus, with an overall moderate level. They called the four-mark Bag of Words and confusion matrix questions scoring, said most objective items were application-based, and noted that a couple of objective items had closely related options. One school also reported that two written questions needed careful interpretation.

### How we read it

- Choice was generous. 72 marks were printed for a 50-mark paper, so a student could skip the item they disliked in every block.

- Precision decided the top band. Close options and exact terms separated a high score from a full one, not knowledge of extra topics.

- No unit was skipped. Every theory unit appeared in both sections, so dropping one unit was a gamble.

- Only set 4 was read. Other sets carried different items of the same type; our ratings describe set 4.

## AI question paper solution: what CBSE rewarded in each written answer

The marking scheme gives examiners value points, not model essays, and it splits the marks inside each answer. Knowing that split is the real solution to Section B, because it tells you what to write first. Here is set 4, described in our words.

- Question | What it asked, in our words | How the marks were split | Our note

- 6 | The seven Cs of communication | 2 for all seven, 1.5 for five or six, 1 for three or four, 0.5 for one or two | A pure list; every C named was worth something

- 7 | Emotional intelligence: meaning, and two of its skills | 1 for the meaning, 0.5 per skill | Easy if the skill names were learnt

- 8 to 10 | Uses of ICT skills; qualities of an entrepreneur; sustainable development defined | 1 per relevant point, or 2 for a sound definition | Generous; most students lose nothing here

- 11 | Sector-based against value-based frameworks, an example of each | 1 for a difference, 0.5 per example | Bioethics as the sector example; rights, virtue or utility as the value one

- 12 | Two features of a classification model | 1 per feature | Supervised, labelled, discrete classes

- 13 | How a computer stores a colour image | 2 for a correct account | Three channels, each pixel holding three values from 0 to 255

- 14 | Two differences between supervised and unsupervised learning | 1 per difference; 0.5 per example if only examples given | Labels, and what the model is trying to find

- 15 | The train-test split | 2 for a correct account | Mention the unseen data and a ratio such as 80 to 20

- 16 | Stemming against lemmatisation, shown on an irregular plural | 1 for the difference, 0.5 for the stem, 0.5 for the lemma | The stem may not be a real word; the lemma always is

- 17 | AI, ML and DL told apart, with a labelled Venn diagram | 1 per term, 1 for the diagram | Nested circles, DL inside ML inside AI

- 18 | Two scenarios, each matched to an AI domain with a reason | 1 for the domain, 1 for the reason, in each | Essay feedback was NLP; a vehicle-counting camera was computer vision

- 19 | A cyber-attack detector: matrix, true negatives, precision | 2 for the matrix, 1 for TN, 1 for precision | Either matrix orientation accepted if the labels were right

- 20 | CNN and ANN expanded and defined; two boxes in a network diagram | 0.5 per full form, 1 per definition, 0.5 per box | The boxes were the input and hidden layers

- 21 | Bag of Words on two short documents, all four steps | 1 per step | Steps three and four could be combined without loss

## AI exam review: six lessons from the 2026 paper for the 2027 candidate

A paper analysis is only useful if it changes how the next student prepares. These are the habits the 2026 paper rewarded, each one now built into our mock reviews.

### Learn the frameworks by type
Two items turned on whether a framework is sector-based or value-based. Students who knew bioethics only as "an ethics example" fell into the assertion trap.

### Expect the unusual domain
A price comparison website belongs to Statistical Data, not NLP. Domain questions reward reasoning from the data the system uses, not from what it looks like.

### Pick the calculation four-markers
The matrix and Bag of Words questions gave a mark per step, so a methodical student was close to certain of eight marks from two choices.

### Draw what can be drawn
A labelled Venn diagram carried a full mark, and two network boxes another. A clean sketch is the quickest mark in Section B.

### Give the example every time
Several two-markers split their marks between a point and an example. An answer with the right idea and no example stopped at half.

### Read both statements on their own
Two-statement items ask about each statement separately. Judge statement one, then statement two, then choose; do not let one colour the other.
