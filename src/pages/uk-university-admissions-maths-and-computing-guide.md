# Which universities need the TMUA, STEP or ESAT? UK maths admissions tests explained

A UK sixth former applying for mathematics, computer science, economics or engineering can face an on-screen test in October or January, a written STEP exam the next June and a December interview, on top of A Level grades. Which apply depends on the exact UCAS course code. This guide puts every test in one table, lists the courses that use each, gives the 2026 to 2027 dates and covers the computing qualifications behind a computer science application.

## What are the UK maths and computing admissions tests, and who sets each one?

Four live tests and one retired one. UAT-UK delivers the TMUA, ESAT and TARA on screen at Pearson test centres; OCR sets STEP on paper in June; the Oxford MAT ran from 2007 to 2025.

**UK admissions tests and computing qualifications for 2027 entry, as each owner states them (read 1 November 2026)**

|---|---|---|---|---|
| TMUA (Test of Mathematics for University Admission) | UAT-UK, on screen at Pearson test centres | Seven universities, for some mathematics, economics and computer science courses | Two papers of 20 multiple-choice questions, 75 minutes each; no calculator; one overall score from 1 to 9 | 12 to 16 October 2026 or 4 to 8 January 2027 |
| ESAT (Engineering and Science Admissions Test) | UAT-UK, on screen | Imperial, Cambridge, Oxford and UCL, for engineering and science courses | Five modules of 27 questions and 40 minutes; Mathematics 1 for all, usually plus two more; a score per module | Same two windows as the TMUA |

### Two surprises in the list

UCL Computer Science uses the TARA, a reasoning and writing test, not the TMUA. And Imperial lists eight Computing degrees separately, G400 to GG47, while Warwick leaves Computer Systems Engineering outside its TMUA rule: the code decides, not the department.

### When two sources disagree

For Warwick Mathematics, UAT-UK's list says the TMUA is compulsory, yet Warwick's own page says applicants typically take TMUA or STEP, with offers to those without a TMUA score conditional on grade 2 in any STEP paper. Act on the university's page; it sets the offer.

## Which admissions tests and interviews will a maths applicant face?

A Cambridge mathematics applicant can face all three stages: the TMUA in October, an interview in December and STEP in June. Oxford has the TMUA and interviews; engineers sit the ESAT. These five pages take each stage in depth.

**The TMUA.** Our [TMUA preparation page](/tmua-preparation) is for any Year 13 student whose course list triggers the test, whatever the subject; most of it is about the logic paper, where strong A Level students often lose time.

**STEP.** The [STEP 2 and STEP 3 guide](/step-maths-preparation) suits a student holding, or expecting, a Cambridge or Warwick offer that names a STEP grade. It is unlike the on-screen tests: long written answers, a June date and a grade from S to U.

**The ESAT.** Engineers, physicists and natural scientists need the [ESAT maths page](/esat-maths-preparation), which covers the two mathematics modules and how to book the right modules.

**The retired MAT.** Anyone searching for the Oxford test will find on the [Oxford MAT page](/oxford-mat-preparation) what replaced it and whether the old papers still repay the effort.

**Interviews.** The [Oxbridge maths interview page](/oxbridge-maths-interview-problem-solving) is about speaking mathematics aloud: it is for shortlisted applicants in November and December, and it practises taking a hint rather than reciting technique.

**How the tests compare in time pressure, our arithmetic from the published formats**

| Test | Questions you answer | Time | Time per question (ours) | What that pace rewards |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TMUA, each paper | 20 | 75 minutes | 3 minutes 45 seconds | Precise reading and quick structure-spotting |
| ESAT, each module | 27 | 40 minutes | About 1 minute 29 seconds | Fluent arithmetic without a calculator |
| TARA, each multiple-choice module | 22 | 40 minutes | About 1 minute 49 seconds | Reasoning in words rather than symbols |
| STEP, each paper | 6 count, out of 12 set | 180 minutes | 30 minutes | Stamina and complete, correct arguments |

**Same mathematics, rising depth.** UAT-UK says the TMUA draws on Higher GCSE and AS level mathematics; Cambridge says STEP 2 is based on A Level Mathematics and AS Further Mathematics, and STEP 3 on a typical Further Mathematics A Level syllabus. As the content climbs, the pace falls, so STEP is never just a slower TMUA. Interviews test something else again: Cambridge's mathematics faculty says there are no trick questions, though some problems are meant to leave you stuck for a while.

Formats from UAT-UK's test pages and OCR's STEP scoring page; Cambridge's STEP content summary from the Faculty of Mathematics STEP page; interview statements from the Oxford Mathematical Institute TMUA page and the Cambridge Faculty of Mathematics. Time per question is our division, not a published figure.

## What does a computer science application need besides an admissions test?

Mathematics first, then evidence of computing. Cambridge's Computer Science department puts its main weight on Mathematics and Further Mathematics and calls A Level Computer Science valuable where offered. These seven pages cover the rest.

**Something to write about.** The [personal statement projects page](/computer-science-personal-statement-projects-uk) helps a Year 12 student who has nothing to put in UCAS question 1 yet; it proposes small, measurable builds and a log, and it never touches the statement itself.

**Choosing the A Level.** Start with the [A level Computer Science tutor page](/a-level-computer-science-tutor-online-uk) if you do not yet know your board, or you study at home, since whether you can sit as a private candidate decides between the two main specifications.

**AQA 7517.** Students at an AQA school should read the [AQA 7517 page](/aqa-a-level-computer-science-7517-help), which is built around the on-screen Paper 1 and the program AQA releases before it.

**OCR H446.** The [OCR H446 page](/ocr-a-level-computer-science-h446-help) is its opposite number: two handwritten papers with no calculator, and the only coding done in a project of the student's choosing.

**The project.** For the coursework on either board, the [A-level Computer Science NEA page](/a-level-computer-science-nea-help) sets out how the marks divide and what each board allows on AI help.

**An EPQ artefact.** A student who wants a computing project outside the A Level, such as an app or a model, should open the [EPQ computer science page](/epq-computer-science-artefact-mentoring), which is clear about where a mentor must stop.

**The vocational route.** The [BTEC IT programming page](/btec-it-programming-unit-help) is for college students on Unit 4, whose assignments are assessed internally under strict authenticity rules.

### The personal statement in 2026

UCAS now asks three questions instead of one essay: at least 350 characters each, 4,000 characters in all, spaces included. Applicants must also declare the statement was not copied or provided from another source, artificial intelligence software included.

## How do you work out which admissions tests you need?

Write out your five UCAS choices with their course codes and look each one up. Five steps, then two worked plans of our own.

1. **List the five codes.** Take each course's UCAS code from the university's course page, for example G400 or GG14, not just the subject name.
2. **Look each code up in UAT-UK's course list.** Note TMUA, ESAT or TARA, whether it is compulsory or recommended, and for the ESAT the exact modules.
3. **Read each course page.** UAT-UK says this is your responsibility; STEP requirements, college tests such as the CSAT and later changes appear there.
4. **Choose the window.** An Oxford or Cambridge choice fixes October; otherwise pick either, once per cycle.
5. **Book by the deadline, after any bursary or access request.** January booking closes on 21 December 2026 at 6pm UK time.

Not necessarily, and the test owners say so. UAT-UK writes that "the best way to prepare is to familiarise yourself with the test format and style of questions", and publishes free materials for every test.

## What applicants and parents ask about UK admissions tests

### Which universities use the TMUA?

UAT-UK's course list for 2027 entry names seven: Cambridge, Durham, Imperial, LSE, Oxford, UCL and Warwick. It is compulsory for some courses, such as LSE Economics, and only recommended for others, such as Durham Mathematics.

### Which universities require STEP?

OCR says STEP is used by the University of Cambridge, the University of Warwick and Imperial College London. At Cambridge it normally forms part of the conditional offer for Mathematics, and Warwick accepts it as an alternative to the TMUA for Mathematics.

### What is the difference between the TMUA and STEP?

The TMUA is an on-screen multiple-choice test in October or January, two 75-minute papers with one overall score. STEP is a written OCR exam in June, three hours a paper, where you choose six of twelve questions and receive a grade from S to U.

### Does Oxford still use the MAT?

No. Oxford's Mathematical Institute says the MAT was used from 2007 to 2025 and will not take place again. Applicants for Mathematics, Computer Science and the joint honours courses now take the TMUA in the October sitting.

### Do all computer science courses need the TMUA?

No. Cambridge, Oxford, Imperial and Warwick use it for computer science, but UAT-UK lists the TARA, not the TMUA, for UCL Computer Science. Universities outside UAT-UK's list of seven do not appear in it at all, so each course page is the authority.

### When are the admissions tests for 2027 entry?

UAT-UK runs the TMUA, ESAT and TARA from 12 to 16 October 2026 and from 4 to 8 January 2027. OCR sets STEP 2 on 14 June 2027 and STEP 3 on 21 June 2027.

### Do I need a tutor for university admissions tests?

UAT-UK says familiarity with the format and question style is the main preparation, and its materials are free. A class helps when logic, proof or Further Maths is new; it guarantees no result.

### Will you write or check my personal statement or NEA?

No. We teach computing and project method on our own practice material, but never write, edit or rewrite a statement, NEA, EPQ or BTEC assignment: UCAS and the boards require your own work.
