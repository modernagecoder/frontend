# OCR GCSE Maths J560 help

OCR's GCSE Maths looks like the other English boards from a distance: two tiers, three papers each, 90 minutes a paper. Up close it has its own habits. Its papers are marked out of 100, not 80. It numbers the Foundation papers 1 to 3 and the Higher papers 4 to 6. And it starts each tier with a calculator paper, putting the non-calculator paper in the middle. That last choice shapes how a student should practise, and OCR's own examiners wrote about it in 2025 from both directions: Foundation students who would not use a calculator when they should, and Higher students who used one where it made the question harder. This page sets out the papers with the dates from OCR's own November 2026 and June 2027 timetables, explains the three-column specification, and works three original questions around the calculator decision.

At a glance: Qualification: OCR GCSE (9-1) Mathematics J560; Tiers: Foundation 5 to 1, Higher 9 to 4; Papers: Three per tier, 100 marks, 1 hour 30 minutes; No calculator: Paper 2 (Foundation), Paper 5 (Higher); June 2027: 14 May, 27 May, 14 June; Format: Group of 15 to 20, or 1-on-1; Timing: Weekdays or weekends, flexible; Start: First class free. ## How is OCR GCSE Maths J560 structured?

Two tiers, three papers in each, every paper worth 100 marks over 1 hour 30 minutes. The table follows the specification and OCR's final timetables.

**OCR GCSE Mathematics J560: the papers, calculator rule and 2026 to 2027 dates (specification version 2.1 and OCR's final timetables for November 2026 and June 2027)**

| Paper | Tier | Calculator | Marks | November 2026 | June 2027 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| J560/01 | Foundation | Permitted | 100 | Wed 4 Nov, morning | Fri 14 May, morning |
| J560/02 | Foundation | Not permitted | 100 | Fri 6 Nov, morning | Thu 27 May, morning |
| J560/03 | Foundation | Permitted | 100 | Mon 9 Nov, morning | Mon 14 June, morning |
| J560/04 | Higher | Permitted | 100 | Wed 4 Nov, morning | Fri 14 May, morning |
| J560/05 | Higher | Not permitted | 100 | Fri 6 Nov, morning | Thu 27 May, morning |
| J560/06 | Higher | Permitted | 100 | Mon 9 Nov, morning | Mon 14 June, morning |

A school enters each student for one of two entry options, J560F or J560H, and the student sits all three papers of that tier in the same series. OCR adds the three paper marks together and compares the total with the grade boundaries for that tier and series; no single paper carries a grade. The specification lists Foundation as "grades 5 to 1" and Higher as "grades 9 to 4", with a safety net on Higher: "An allowed grade 3 may be awarded on the Higher tier option for learners who are a small number of marks below the grade 3/4 boundary."

On 14 May 2027, students at AQA and Pearson Edexcel schools face a paper without a calculator while OCR students have one on the desk, so a student who borrows a revision timetable from a friend at another school can easily prepare for the wrong kind of paper. The safe habit is to write the OCR order on the wall: calculator, then non-calculator, then calculator.

### November and resits

OCR runs a November series, but only for "learners who have reached at least the age of 16 on or before 31st August of that calendar year". In practice it is a resit route for students past Year 11.

For November 2026, OCR's final timetable puts J560 on the 4th, 6th and 9th of the month, keeping the June calculator order.

### Codes worth knowing

The Ofqual qualification number is QN 601/4606/0, which is the code to give a college that asks exactly which GCSE was sat.

Private candidates may enter, but the specification says they "must be based in the UK" and need an OCR-approved centre willing to host them.

## Why is OCR J560 Paper 1 a calculator paper, and how should students use it?

OCR simply orders its papers that way; the specification says all content can appear on either kind. What matters is the judgement OCR's 2025 examiners kept returning to: when the calculator helps, and when it gets in the way.

The J560 specification is direct about calculators: "All content may be assessed on either the calculator or non-calculator papers." There is no list of calculator topics and non-calculator topics. Instead, each paper tests the same content with a different tool on the desk, and a student has to decide, question by question, whether to use it.

**What OCR's Summer 2025 examiners' reports said about calculators (quoted and summarised from the J560/01 and J560/04 reports)**

| Paper | The calculator problem | Other points the examiners made |
|---|---|---|
| J560/01, Foundation, calculator | "Candidates are expected to use a calculator on this paper." Yet "many used non-calculator methods that often lead to arithmetical errors." | Challenging topics included straight-line equations, trigonometry, population density, inverse proportion and bearings; no evidence that time ran short |
| J560/04, Higher, calculator | "even on a calculator paper, not every single question will require them to use their calculator." Using one on an indices question produced unmanageable numbers. | Truncated figures in working; missing brackets in algebra; "Show that" questions need working; proportionality equations not formed |

Read together, the two reports describe one skill seen from opposite ends. On Foundation Paper 1, students who did long multiplication by hand when the calculator was allowed made avoidable slips. On Higher Paper 4, students who pressed buttons on an index-laws question found themselves with enormous numbers and no way to show the method being tested. The Paper 4 report also lists "Many candidates truncated figures in their working, which often led to an inaccurate answer", a calculator habit of a different kind: copying four digits off the screen and carrying them forward.

### Use it: arithmetic

On a calculator paper, any multi-digit arithmetic, any trigonometry and any compound calculation goes on the calculator. Hand methods there are a risk with no reward.

### Pause: structure

If a question is about index laws, surds, algebraic proof or exact values, the method is the point. Work it by hand, then use the calculator only to check.

### Keep it: accuracy

Store intermediate values in the calculator memory, or write them to several more figures than the answer needs. Round once, at the end, as asked.

That three-part rule is ours, built from the two reports. We teach it explicitly because it is not something students pick up from practice alone: a student who has only ever sat AQA or Edexcel mocks will usually have practised the non-calculator paper first and the calculator papers later, and comes to OCR's order with the wrong instincts.

Source: OCR, J560/01 and J560/04 Examiners' reports, Summer 2025 series, linked from the [J560 assessment page](https://www.ocr.org.uk/qualifications/gcse/mathematics-j560-from-2015/assessment/). We summarise and quote the reports and do not reproduce any question.

## What is in the OCR J560 specification, and how do the columns work?

Twelve numbered content areas, each written in three columns that build on each other. Foundation is the first two columns; Higher is all three.

OCR lays out the content in a way that differs from AQA's numbered references and from Pearson's typefaces. "The content of this specification is presented in three columns, representing a progression within the content strands." The columns are cumulative: "the expectation of a Foundation tier learner is exemplified by the first two columns and that of a Higher tier learner is the sum of the statements in all three columns." Each statement also carries an OCR reference such as 1.01a and the matching DfE reference, so a teacher can trace any line back to the national subject content.

**The twelve content areas of OCR J560, as numbered in the specification**

| Area | Title in the specification | Area | Title in the specification |
|---|---|---|---|
| OCR 1 | Number Operations and Integers | OCR 7 | Graphs of Equations and Functions |
| OCR 2 | Fractions, Decimals and Percentages | OCR 8 | Basic Geometry |
| OCR 3 | Indices and Surds | OCR 9 | Congruence and Similarity |
| OCR 4 | Approximation and Estimation | OCR 10 | Mensuration |
| OCR 5 | Ratio, Proportion and Rates Of Change | OCR 11 | Probability |
| OCR 6 | Algebra | OCR 12 | Statistics |

Two notes in the content section are worth knowing. First, "Knowledge of the content of Key Stages 1 and 2 is therefore assumed, but will not be assessed directly", so primary arithmetic is never the topic of a question but is needed in almost every one. Second, OCR separates sketching from plotting: a sketch "does not have to be to scale" and is "drawn freehand", while a plot is drawn on graph paper from calculated points. Students who plot when asked to sketch waste minutes; students who sketch when asked to plot lose accuracy marks.

**Marks per paper by assessment objective, from the J560 specification**

| Tier | AO1 standard techniques | AO2 reasoning and communication | AO3 problem solving | Paper total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Foundation (Papers 1 to 3) | 50 | 25 | 25 | 100 |
| Higher (Papers 4 to 6) | 40 | 30 | 30 | 100 |

### The formulae sheet

The specification still says "The assessment for this specification will not include a formula sheet", listing the formulae to recall under reference 6.02d.

That line has been overtaken. Ofqual requires formulae sheets in GCSE Maths for 2025 to 2027 and again for every series from 2028 while this specification lasts, and OCR's J560 assessment page lists Foundation and Higher formulae sheets for the June 2027 and November 2027 series.

### Use the sheet, know the sheet

OCR's Paper 1 report lists among weaker candidates those who "did not know the correct formulae or use the formulae sheet".

Ofqual has also told the boards not to set a question a student could finish by copying from the sheet, so recall of 6.02d still pays: the sheet is a backstop, not a method.

Sources: OCR J560 specification version 2.1 (content sections 2b and 3g); OCR J560 assessment page, formulae sheets for 2027; [Ofqual decisions on formulae sheets](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/proposed-changes-to-the-assessment-of-mathematics-physics-and-combined-science-gcses). Read on 31 October 2026.

## Three J560-style questions we wrote, built around the calculator

Our own questions, not OCR's, on three habits the 2025 reports named: indices by hand, proportionality as an equation, and keeping accuracy through a calculation.

### Example 1, indices: put the calculator down (Higher)

**Question (ours).** Find x if 2x + 1 = 83 ÷ 4.

**Think first.** Every number here is a power of 2. Writing them that way is the whole method; a calculator gives 128 and hides the structure.

**Working.** 8 = 23, so 83 = 29. 4 = 22. So 83 ÷ 4 = 29 &minus; 2 = 27.

**Answer.** x + 1 = 7, so x = 6. Check: 27 = 128, and 512 ÷ 4 = 128. The calculator is used only for that check.

### Example 2, proportionality as an equation (Higher)

**Question (ours).** y is inversely proportional to the square of x. When x = 2, y = 15. Find y when x = 5.

**Set up.** Turn the words into an equation with a constant: y = k ÷ x². The report's weaker candidates left the proportion symbol in place; it has to go.

**Working.** 15 = k ÷ 4, so k = 60 and y = 60 ÷ x². When x = 5, y = 60 ÷ 25 = 2.4.

**Sense check.** x has grown, so y should shrink, and faster than x grows. From 15 to 2.4 fits.

### Example 3, keep the accuracy (Foundation and Higher)

**Question (ours).** A cylinder has radius 3.7 cm and height 12.5 cm. A cone with the same radius has the same volume. Find the cone's height, to 3 significant figures.

**Working.** Cylinder volume = π × 3.7² × 12.5 = 537.6... cm³. Keep this in the calculator memory rather than writing 537.

**Cone.** Volume = ⅓ × π × 3.7² × h. Setting equal: ⅓ × π × 3.7² × h = π × 3.7² × 12.5, so h = 3 × 12.5 = 37.5 cm.

**The lesson.** The algebra cancels π × 3.7² entirely. A student who rounds the first volume to 538 and divides gets 37.53..., and has to hope it rounds the right way. Spotting the cancellation needs no calculator at all.

### The rule behind all three

Example 1 rewards putting the calculator down. Example 2 rewards writing the equation before calculating. Example 3 rewards algebra before arithmetic, then full accuracy if arithmetic is needed.

That is the judgement OCR's examiners describe, and it is a better use of revision time than another unmarked past paper.

**Practice set (ours, answers at the end).** (1) Without a calculator, write 94 × 3&minus;2 as a power of 3. (2) p is directly proportional to the cube of q; p = 54 when q = 3; find p when q = 5. (3) With a calculator, work out (4.6² + 2.9) ÷ (1.85 &minus; 0.37) to 3 significant figures. (4) Simplify √12 + √27 without a calculator. (5) Sketch y = x² &minus; 4 and label where it crosses the axes. **Answers:** (1) 36; (2) 250; (3) 16.3; (4) 5√3; (5) a parabola with its minimum at (0, &minus;4), cutting the x-axis at &minus;2 and 2.

## How should a student revise for OCR J560 before May 2027?

Our seven-month outline from November 2026 to Paper 3 or 6 on 14 June 2027, built around OCR's calculator order.

1. **November 2026: confirm the entry.** J560F or J560H, and write the order on the wall: calculator on 14 May, non-calculator on 27 May, calculator on 14 June 2027.
2. **December 2026: one calculator paper first.** Sit an OCR Paper 1 or 4 timed. Mark every question as "should have used the calculator", "should not have", or "fine".
3. **January 2027: mocks and the twelve areas.** Log lost marks against OCR 1 to OCR 12 and pick the three areas that cost most.
4. **February 2027: non-calculator fluency.** Daily short sets of fractions, indices and surds by hand, ready for Paper 2 or 5.
5. **March 2027: the 2027 formulae sheet.** Practise with OCR's sheet for the June 2027 series beside every paper, and recall the 6.02d list anyway.
6. **April 2027: papers in OCR order.** Calculator, non-calculator, calculator, each marked with OCR's scheme. ExamBuilder, OCR's free past-question service for centres, is worth asking the school about.
7. **May and June 2027: review between mornings.** Use the 13 days after 14 May to fix what Paper 1 or 4 exposed, then the 18 days before 14 June for the last calculator paper.

The plan is ours and is a template, not a promise of any grade. A Foundation student near grade 4 spends longer on OCR 2 and OCR 5, fractions and ratio; a Higher student aiming at 8 or 9 spends the spring on OCR 3, OCR 6 and OCR 7, where the indices, algebra and graphs sit. ## What families ask about OCR GCSE Maths

### How many papers are in OCR GCSE Maths J560?

Three at each tier: Papers 1, 2 and 3 for Foundation and Papers 4, 5 and 6 for Higher. Every paper runs for 90 minutes and carries 100 marks, and a student takes the three together in one series.

### Is OCR GCSE Maths Paper 1 calculator or non-calculator?

Calculator. OCR allows a calculator on Papers 1 and 3 (Foundation) and Papers 4 and 6 (Higher); Papers 2 and 5 are the non-calculator papers. This is the opposite of AQA and Edexcel, whose Paper 1 is non-calculator.

### When are the OCR GCSE Maths exams in 2027?

OCR's final June 2027 timetable places J560 on Friday 14 May 2027, then Thursday 27 May, then Monday 14 June, each in the morning session. The November 2026 papers fall on 4 November, 6 November and 9 November 2026.

### Is OCR GCSE Maths harder than AQA or Edexcel?

All three boards teach the same DfE content and award the same grades, so none is officially harder. OCR differs in layout: 100-mark papers, a three-column specification and a calculator paper first, which some students find suits them better and others less.

### Does OCR GCSE Maths have a formula sheet?

For the 2027 exams, yes. The specification still says there is no formula sheet, but Ofqual now requires formulae sheets in GCSE Maths, and OCR's J560 assessment page lists Foundation and Higher sheets for the June 2027 series.

### What grades can you get on OCR Foundation and Higher?

Foundation awards grades 5 to 1 and Higher awards 9 to 4. On Higher, an allowed grade 3 may be given to a student a small number of marks below the grade 3/4 boundary.

### Where can I find OCR GCSE Maths past papers?

OCR publishes some recent papers, mark schemes and examiners' reports on its J560 assessment page, and more through Teach Cambridge for schools. OCR also runs ExamBuilder, a free past-question service for centres.

### Can my child sit OCR J560 in November?

Only students aged 16 or over by 31 August of that calendar year can enter, according to the specification. That makes November a route for students who have already left Year 11.

### When are classes held?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Times are set in UK time.

### Are you connected with OCR?

No. Modern Age Coders is independent of Cambridge OCR. We teach to the published J560 specification, use OCR's freely available papers and reports, and never promise a grade.
