Coding classes for kids in Düsseldorf

A Düsseldorf child will have Informatik on the timetable at about ten: Nordrhein-Westfalen teaches it as a compulsory subject in Klasse 5 and 6 at every school type, which most Länder do not. That makes the years before Klasse 5 a real chance to arrive ready, and the years after it a question of what comes next. This page covers both, describes what our live English lessons are like for children of 6 to 12, gives the dates of a free puzzle contest, and walks through a project on the city's own list of new street trees, where the obvious answer turns out to be about time rather than trees.

Schools in Nordrhein-Westfalen

When does a Düsseldorf child start Informatik at school?

In Klasse 5, at every school type. Nordrhein-Westfalen is one of the ten Länder with compulsory Informatik, and it starts earlier than most.

Compulsory in Klasse 5 and 6

The Informatik-Monitor 2026/27 states for NRW: "An allen allgemeinbildenden Schularten wird das Pflichtfach Informatik in den Jahrgangsstufen 5 und 6 unterrichtet." Every Hauptschule, Realschule, Sekundarschule, Gesamtschule and Gymnasium in Düsseldorf teaches it in those two years.

Grundschule in NRW runs from Klasse 1 to 4, so a Düsseldorf child usually meets the compulsory subject at ten. Children in the six Länder without compulsory Informatik, from Hessen to Berlin, may never meet it at all.

After Klasse 6, it depends

From Klasse 7 Informatik becomes a choice. A Realschule may offer it as a Wahlpflicht subject within 14 weekly hours across Klassen 7 to 10, a Gesamtschule or Sekundarschule within 12, a Hauptschule within up to 8.

A nine-year Gymnasium must offer Informatik in the Wahlpflicht area in Klassen 9 and 10, six weekly hours in total, either on its own or combined with another subject. Between Klasse 6 and Klasse 9, then, a Gymnasium pupil may have no Informatik at all.

For parents this gives two clear windows. Before Klasse 5, a child who has built games in Scratch and typed a few lines of Python walks into the compulsory course already confident. After Klasse 6, a child who enjoyed it can keep going while the school timetable pauses, so that the Wahlpflicht choice in Klasse 7 or 9 is made from experience rather than guesswork.

Taught in English

What does learning to code in English look like for a Düsseldorf child?

All lessons are live and in English, and the pace is set around the child.

English, plainly

Lessons are in English. There is no German option, and we would rather you knew that now. For children at the international and bilingual schools in and around Düsseldorf, English is already a school language; for children at a German school, coding in English adds a second skill at no extra cost.

Pace and accent

The mentor speaks slowly, checks often, and repeats in shorter sentences. Within a few weeks they are used to how your child speaks, and a child who answers half in German is helped to the English words without any fuss.

The same words as school

NRW Informatik uses the same ideas a coding class does: sequence, loop, condition, variable. When a mentor knows your child has the compulsory course, they point out the German school term next to the English one, so the two support each other.

Our mentors teach from India over live video, which is why Düsseldorf afternoons work well: it is evening for the mentor. Your child keeps the same mentor every week, someone who soon knows whether they like a hard puzzle, need a minute of quiet, or learn best by changing a game that already works.

A real Düsseldorf project

Düsseldorf's new street trees: which season planted the fewest?

The city publishes every new tree planted under its Stadtbaumkonzept, the street tree plan, as open data that anyone may use. Our older children use it to learn a check that is easy to forget: is the last part of the data finished?

The data

The Landeshauptstadt Düsseldorf publishes Stadtbaumkonzept Düsseldorf through Open.NRW under the Datenlizenz Deutschland Zero 2.0. The city describes it as the new tree plantings made under the plan. On the day we downloaded it, 2 November 2026, the file Stadtbaumkonzept.csv had 805 rows, one per tree.

Each row gives a planting season such as 2021/2022, the species, whether a parking space was given up for the tree, and a last-update date. That date is the same on every row: 29 January 2026.

The question

In which planting season did Düsseldorf plant the fewest new trees? The first program counts the rows for each season, prints them in order and picks the smallest.

Before running it, a child should guess. Was there a season when the plan slowed down?

A season with only 37 trees, after six seasons of 99 to 182, looks like a big drop. But look at the last-update date on every row: 29 January 2026. The 2025/2026 season carries 2026 in its own name, and the file was saved in January of that year. Whatever the city planted after 29 January 2026 is not in this file yet. The program did not compare seasons; it compared six whole seasons with part of a seventh.

Our rule is simple and cautious: a season counts as finished only if its second year ended before the file was saved. By that rule 2025/2026 is left out, and the fewest trees among finished seasons were planted in 2024/2025, with 99. The busiest was 2021/2022, with 182. Whether the 2025/2026 season will end higher or lower than 99, nobody can tell from this file; a later download will say.

  1. Ages 6 to 8, no screen. Count the books on a shelf every evening for a week, but stop on Wednesday. Is Wednesday the "smallest week"?
  2. Ages 8 to 10, Scratch. Store the seven totals in a Scratch list, let a sprite name the smallest, then teach it to ignore the final item.
  3. 10 to 12, typed Python. Try Version 1 first, explain why 37 misleads, and only then attempt Version 2 on your own.
  4. Going further. Count the species and find the top three. Then look closely at the names: is "Spitzahorn" really in the file only once?

A free contest

Is there a free computing contest for Düsseldorf children?

There is: the Informatik-Biber, a quiz of logic puzzles that schools run in class. No coding is needed, and the 2026 round is happening now.

The contest

The organiser, Bundesweite Informatikwettbewerbe, calls the Informatik-Biber the largest school informatics competition in Germany. It is free and online, open to Klassen 3 to 13 without any previous experience; more than 580,000 pupils entered in 2025.

For a primary child it means nine puzzles in 30 minutes. In Klassen 5 and 6, the years of compulsory Informatik in NRW, it is twelve puzzles in 35 minutes.

Dates for 2026

This year's contest weeks fall between 9 and 20 November 2026; registration is by schools only. A Düsseldorf parent should ask the class teacher or the Informatik teacher whether the school takes part.

Results appear in December 2026 and certificates follow in January 2027. Anyone can try the practice puzzles of the Schnupper-Biber.

The puzzles are about rules, order, routes and codes, the thinking behind Informatik in Klasse 5 and 6. We do not reuse the real tasks; mentors write their own puzzles in the same style, and children who like ours tend to enjoy the Biber too. We have no connection with the organiser.

Düsseldorf parents ask

Coding classes for kids in Düsseldorf: common questions

Are the classes in German?

No. Lessons are given only in English, always live. The mentor talks calmly, adjusts to the way your child speaks, and repeats things more simply when needed, which lets a beginner in English keep up. Blocks and code read the same in a German or an English home.

Is Informatik compulsory in NRW schools?

Yes, in Klasse 5 and 6. The Informatik-Monitor 2026/27 says the compulsory subject is taught in those two years at every general school type in Nordrhein-Westfalen. After Klasse 6 it becomes an elective, with different rules for each school type.

Is it worth starting before Klasse 5?

For many children, yes. A child who has made Scratch games and typed a little Python starts the compulsory course relaxed and curious rather than catching up. Children can start with us from 6.

Can you help with Informatik homework in Klasse 5 and 6?

Yes. Our Informatik Companion course, for ages 10 to 16, runs alongside school Informatik in English, and a one-to-one mentor can work through your child's own worksheets. We teach alongside school; the school course and its marks are what count.

When are the classes in Düsseldorf time?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Popular choices in Düsseldorf are the hours after school until about six in the evening, and weekend mornings.

How does a Düsseldorf child join the Informatik-Biber?

Through the school, which registers its pupils. In 2026 the contest weeks are 9 to 20 November; it is free and needs no coding, and Klasse 5 and 6 pupils get twelve puzzles in 35 minutes. The class teacher will know if the school is signed up.

Do classes continue in the NRW holidays?

Yes. Mentors do not follow the NRW calendar, so classes run through every break. Some families take a break during the long summer holidays (19 July to 31 August 2027) and pick up again in September.

Where are the mentors?

In India, teaching live over video. 16:55 in Düsseldorf is 21:25 for the mentor in winter. Your child has the same mentor every week.

How are fees charged?

Monthly. You see the fee here in euro, worked out with the European Central Bank reference rate; the actual payment is in US dollars and is converted by your bank, so a small difference on the statement is normal. The trial class costs nothing and needs no card.

What will my child make?

Younger children make Scratch games and animated stories. Nine and ten year olds move to Python: drawings, quizzes and small games. Around ten to twelve they start real data work, like the Düsseldorf tree register above.
