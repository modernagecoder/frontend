## Chatbot vs agent, made concrete

Ask a chatbot to plan your revision week and you get a plan as text, dead the moment training moves to Wednesday. Run your agent on the same goal and it reads the exam dates and the family calendar, builds the week avoiding clashes, shows the six events it would create in dry-run mode, waits for your approval, writes them, logs what it did, and re-plans when training moves, asking again. The dry-run line and the approval line are the difference between an assistant and a hazard, and they are the first thing the course teaches. The component-by-component mechanism lives at /ai-agents-explained; the coding-agent world lives at /agentic-coding-classes.

## The honest teen ladder

Early teens, 13 to 15: rule-based automations that watch, filter and notify, with teacher-arranged access, a parent in the loop, and dry-run always. Older teens, 16 to 17: real assistants with tools that read sources, draft actions and seek approval, one to one only, every new permission discussed before granted. Reserved for 18: the full professional agent platforms and unsupervised deploys, named openly as adult tools. The middle rung is where the education happens: an assistant that drafts but does not send teaches goal decomposition, permission design and approval flows, which the industry currently hires adults to learn on the job.

## The four rails

Dry-run first: an agent shows what it would do before it may do it. Least access: the narrowest key that works, never a whole account's powers. Human approval: anything irreversible waits for a person. Full logs: everything the agent did, written where student, teacher and parent can read it.

## Why one to one only

An agent acts, the actions touch real accounts, and eight students' agents cannot all be watched at the moment each one matters. One to one, the teacher sees the permission about to be granted and the dry-run about to be skipped while it is still a lesson rather than an incident. It also changes what gets built: private tracks grow agents out of the student's actual life, where approval-flow design gets real.
