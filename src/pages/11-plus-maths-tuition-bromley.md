# 11 plus maths tuition in Bromley

Bromley has two grammar schools, and on maths they could hardly differ more. St Olave's Grammar School tests boys in two stages, and maths is in both: a one-hour multiple-choice paper in September, then, for the 450 boys invited back, a written Mathematics Paper in November, sat with pencils, an eraser and a ruler. Newstead Wood School tests girls in verbal and non-verbal reasoning and says: "We do not test on any other subjects." Modern Age Coders teaches upper primary maths live online, to a class or to one child. This page sets out both schools' tests from their own pages and the council's booklet, and teaches the diagram that a ruler in a maths exam is for: the bar model.

## Which grammar schools are there in Bromley, and do they test maths?

Two. The council's booklet names them and says both "admit children based on the outcome of their selection test", with applications "prioritised in score order from highest to lowest." Only one of the two tests maths.

### What the council does

The booklet is clear: "The London Borough of Bromley does not administer the selective/grammar testing for either of the two grammar schools in Bromley." Families register with each school, and the council handles only the common application, open from 1 September and closing on 31 October 2026.

It also warns that "being successful in a test does not guarantee admission". The grammar school still has to be named on the council form, and the council offers the highest named preference that can be met.

### Why the difference matters to a family

A family with a son and a daughter in Year 5 faces two quite different years. For St Olave's, maths is central, and written maths matters by November. For Newstead Wood, our maths lessons would be for school and Year 7, not for the test.

We say that plainly because it would be easy to sell maths tuition for a test that sets none. If your daughter is applying only to Newstead Wood, the school's own advice and its familiarisation links are the right starting point.

## What is in the St Olave's Grammar School entrance test?

Two stages. Stage 1, the Selective Entrance Test, is one hour of multiple choice in English, logic and maths. The 450 highest scorers come back for Stage 2: an English Paper and a Mathematics Paper.

### The awkward timing, explained by the school

Stage 2 falls after the 31 October council deadline. The school's answer is that every boy invited to Stage 2 has already met its minimum standard, so families know before the deadline whether St Olave's is worth a preference.

The reverse is just as firm: "Any boy who does not proceed to the Stage 2 test will not be eligible for an offer." The school advises such families not to name it, since that "would be a waste of one of your school preferences."

### Two different kinds of maths

Stage 1 asks maths inside a single hour shared with English and logic, all multiple choice. Speed and accuracy count, and an answer can be checked against the options.

Stage 2 is a Mathematics Paper of its own, and the school's kit list tells you something about it: a ruler, an eraser and pencils. A boy should be at ease drawing a neat diagram and writing his working, not only choosing a letter.

### No catchment, no siblings

The school says it has no catchment area, no sibling policy and no feeder schools. Entry rests "solely on performance in our entrance tests".

### No past papers

The school publishes sample questions for both stages on its site but says it does not provide past papers. We do not reproduce its samples here.

### Your place on the list

The final rank order goes only to the council. After offer day the school can indicate how many boys remain above a child, but it does not hold the waiting list.

## What is a bar model, and why bring a ruler to a maths paper?

A bar model is a drawn rectangle split into equal parts that stands for the quantities in a word problem. With a ruler it is quick and neat, and once the picture is right the arithmetic is usually one step. These examples are ours, written for this page; they are not St Olave's questions.

### Draw what you know

Read the problem once and draw one bar for each person or quantity. Make bars that are meant to be equal the same length, using the ruler.

### Mark the parts

Split the bars into equal units wherever the problem gives a fraction or a ratio. Label the total or the difference you were told.

### Find one unit

Most problems collapse to "so many units make this number". Divide to find one unit, then build the answer the question asks for.

**Worked examples (ours), at upper primary level**

| Problem (ours) | The bar picture | Working |
|---|---|---|
| Sam has three times as many cards as Ben. Together they have 84. How many has Sam? | Ben: 1 unit. Sam: 3 units. Total: 4 units = 84 | 1 unit = 21, so Sam has 3 x 21 = 63 |
| Ella has 18 more stamps than Ruby. They have 100 altogether. How many has Ruby? | Two equal bars, plus an extra 18 on Ella's | 100 minus 18 = 82; 82 divided by 2 = 41, so Ruby has 41 |
| A ribbon is cut so that 2/5 is used and 45 cm is left. How long was it? | One bar in 5 parts; 3 parts left = 45 cm | 1 part = 15 cm, so the ribbon was 5 x 15 = 75 cm |
| The ratio of red to blue beads is 3 : 5, and there are 24 more blue than red. How many beads in all? | Red: 3 units. Blue: 5 units. Difference: 2 units = 24 | 1 unit = 12; total 8 units = 96 beads |
| After spending a quarter of his money and then 6 more, Leo has half left. How much did he start with? | One bar in 4 quarters; 1 quarter spent, half left, so the 6 fills the last quarter | 1 quarter = 6, so he started with 4 x 6 = 24 |

Our practice set. Draw the bars first, every time. (1) Two numbers add to 70 and one is four times the other. Find both. (2) A jug and a cup hold 1,200 ml together; the jug holds 600 ml more than the cup. How much does the cup hold? (3) Three fifths of a class are girls and there are 8 more girls than boys. How many children? (4) Ali and Zoe share 45 sweets in the ratio 4 : 5. How many does Zoe get? (5) A book costs twice as much as a pen, and both cost 27 points in a class reward shop. What does the book cost? Answers, ours: (1) 14 and 56; (2) 300 ml; (3) 40, since 1 unit is 8 and there are 5 units; (4) 25; (5) 18 points.

### Why this lesson family

Multi-step problems with fractions, comparison and ratio are where a written paper separates careful thinkers from quick guessers. A bar model shows the structure before any number is calculated, which makes the right operation obvious.

It also produces exactly the kind of working a marker can follow, and the ruler on the St Olave's kit list makes the diagram quick to draw cleanly.

### Common slips we correct

Bars of unequal length for equal quantities: the picture then lies. We insist on ruled, matching units.

Answering the wrong question: finding one unit and stopping when the question asked for the total. Our children underline the final question before they write the answer.
