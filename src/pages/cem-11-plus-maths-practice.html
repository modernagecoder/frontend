What is the CEM 11 plus now?

CEM was a university assessment centre founded in 1983 that joined Cambridge in 2019 and was renamed Cambridge Insight in 2025, according to its own history page. Its entrance test today is Cambridge Select Insight, a computer assessment of about 60 minutes with numerical, verbal and non-verbal sections, sold to selective independent schools, grammar schools, partially selective schools and schools that use fair banding, for entry into Years 7 to 10. Cambridge sells no practice papers for it and says it endorses no tuition. Several grammar areas that once used a CEM paper have changed provider; Warwickshire moved to GL Assessment for 2023 entry. Modern Age Coders teaches the number skills a numerical section draws on, live online, with practice questions we write ourselves.

CEM today

Is CEM still an 11 plus exam board?

Not in the way most practice books suggest. CEM is now a brand of Cambridge University Press & Assessment called Cambridge Insight, and it sells mainly classroom assessments, with one entrance product for selective schools.

Parents still type "CEM 11 plus past papers" because, for a decade, CEM papers were the grammar school test in several English counties. The organisation behind them has a long history. Its own timeline records the "First monitoring system established in Newcastle" in 1983, a move to Durham in 1996, then "CEM joins Cambridge" in 2019 and, in 2025, "Cambridge CEM becomes Cambridge Insight". The website at cem.org now describes itself as "Cambridge Insight (formerly CEM)" and lists baseline tests for ages 3 to 19, a wellbeing check and a single entrance product.

What Cambridge Insight mostly does

Baseline assessments such as MidYIS, Yellis and Alis, which schools use to understand pupils after they arrive, not to choose them. Cambridge says it assesses 480,000 students each year in 127 countries across its products.

Its parents page is blunt about these tests: they "are designed to be taken without any need for preparation or study in advance", and there are no practice versions for sale.

What it sells for admissions

Cambridge Select Insight, the product that used to be called CEM Select. It is a computer-based entrance assessment that a school buys, downloads to its own network and runs in its own rooms.

This is the only Cambridge Insight product built to decide who gets a place, and it is the one a family is most likely to meet if a school mentions "the CEM test" today.

Two versions for independent schools

Cambridge Select Insight: Evaluate gives age-standardised results and "year-on-year comparability". Cambridge Select Insight: Challenge offers several versions and reports raw scores, including section totals, so a school can rank its own candidates.

A version for late transfer

Cambridge Select Insight: Mid-Year Transfer is described as "designed explicitly as a late-transfer test for Grammar schools" for entry into Years 8, 9 and 10, benchmarked against the school's existing pupils.

Built against coaching

Cambridge says it introduces "new assessment paradigms to reduce the predictability of assessment content" and writes the content separately from its other tests, so familiarity with one Cambridge product gives no head start on another.

One more detail matters for reading results. Cambridge's FAQ explains that standardised scores "are based on a mean score of 100 and a standard deviation of 15". By our own arithmetic, that puts a score of 115 one standard deviation above the average for the age group, and a score of 130 two above. Schools that use the Evaluate version see age-standardised scores of this kind; schools that use Challenge see raw marks and rank them. Either way, the school sets its own threshold and Cambridge publishes none.

Grammar areas

Which 11 plus areas still use a CEM test?

None of the grammar areas we have read for this site names CEM or Cambridge as its current 11 plus provider. The clearest public record of the change is Warwickshire's.

On 11 March 2022 Warwickshire County Council announced that children then in Year 5 "will now take an 11+ exam from GL Assessment", and explained why: "the previous contract with CEM came to an end". The new provider was chosen in a joint procurement with the King Edward VI Foundation in Birmingham, because Warwickshire and the Birmingham grammar schools share one test. The council added that the GL papers "will test the same four competencies of Maths, English, Verbal and Non-Verbal Reasoning as the CEM papers have in previous years", and that "intensive coaching or tuition is not needed or encouraged."

Why the CEM label survives

Bookshops still sell "CEM style" practice, tutors still advertise it, and some independent schools still buy Cambridge Select Insight for 11+ and 13+ entry. So the word lingers even where the test has gone.

If a school or council page names Cambridge Select Insight, this page applies. If it names GL, Quest or FSCE, the provider page or area page for that test is the better guide.

How to check your own case

Read the admissions page of the school or council, not a tutor's summary, and look for the provider's name and the year of entry it applies to. Providers change at contract ends, so a page about 2027 entry can differ from one about 2028.

Gloucestershire's council page, for example, gives the 2027 intake test date as Saturday 12 September 2026 and then names a different provider for the 2028 intake.

Preparing honestly

Can you prepare for Cambridge Select Insight?

Cambridge says a child should not practise for it, and sells nothing to practise with. What a family can do is make sure the maths underneath is secure and the screen format is not a shock.

Cambridge's parents page answers the question directly: "We strongly advise against practising before an Insight assessment, and we do not provide nor recommend any practice materials." It also explains what happens instead. "Each section of the assessment will include introductory information with examples that allow students to familiarise with the style and format of questions. The introductory examples are not timed, and repeats are permitted." The entrance page adds that "All candidates receive standardised familiarisation materials designed to prevent undue anxiety".

  1. Confirm the test. Ask the school which provider and which version it uses, and whether the numerical section is age-standardised or ranked by raw score.
  2. Use only the familiarisation material the school sends. Cambridge gives every candidate the same material, so nobody gains by buying more.
  3. Secure Year 5 and Year 6 number work at school level: place value, the four operations with whole numbers and decimals, fractions of amounts, simple ratio and reading tables and charts.
  4. Practise reading a maths question on a screen. Many children are fluent on paper and slower when the question and the options sit side by side on a monitor.
  5. Practise reasoning, not recall. The entrance page says the test supports abilities that "rely on genuine understanding rather than learning through repetition", so unfamiliar problems teach more than repeated drills.
  6. Ask about access arrangements early if your child has extra time at school, since a 25 per cent extra-time version exists.

Where we can help

Arithmetic and reasoning are ordinary school maths, and a child who is shaky on fractions or multi-step word problems in Year 5 is short of things the national curriculum expects anyway. That is worth teaching whatever test lies ahead.

We also teach the habits a short numerical section rewards: estimating before calculating, checking an answer against the question, and moving on from a question that is taking too long.

Worked examples, written by us

How should a child think through a CEM-style numerical question?

Three original problems at the top of the primary curriculum, of the kind a numerical reasoning section draws on. None is from a Cambridge test, and none imitates one. The point is the route.

1. Two ages, one bar

Mia is three times as old as her brother. In four years she will be twice as old as him. How old is her brother now? Draw his age as one bar and hers as three. Four years later each bar gains the same 4. For hers to be exactly two of his, three bars plus 4 must equal two bars plus 8, so one bar is 4. He is 4 and she is 12. Check: in four years they are 8 and 16, and 16 is twice 8.

2. Run the machine backwards

A number is multiplied by 4, then 7 is taken away, and the result is 45. What was the number? Undo the last step first: add 7 to get 52. Then undo the first step: divide by 4 to get 13. Check forwards: 13 times 4 is 52, minus 7 is 45. Children who try numbers at random waste a minute here; children who reverse the steps take fifteen seconds.

3. The missing score

Five test scores have a mean of 14. Four of them are 12, 15, 9 and 18. What is the fifth? The mean tells you the total: five scores averaging 14 add up to 70. The four known scores make 54, so the fifth is 16. The habit to build is turning every mean into a total straight away, because totals can be added and subtracted and means cannot.

Every item above can be done without a calculator in under a minute once the method is settled, and each has one place where children usually slip: the order of operations, a common multiple tested on only two of three numbers, area confused with perimeter, the fraction left rather than the fraction done, a pattern read from terms instead of gaps, and a coordinate pair written the wrong way round. In lessons we spend the time on the slip, because that is what transfers to a question nobody has seen before.

Beyond 11 plus

Does the CEM test appear at 13 plus or for late entry?

Yes. Cambridge sells the same entrance family for 13+ entry to independent schools and for transfer into Years 8, 9 and 10 at grammar schools, so the maths moves up with the child.

13 plus entry

Cambridge lists 13+ alongside 11+ for both independent-school versions, Evaluate and Challenge. A candidate at 13 has met Key Stage 3 number and algebra, so a numerical section can reasonably expect negative numbers, simple equations, percentages of amounts and ratio problems with three parts.

Our lower secondary maths course covers that ground. For 13+ entry that uses Common Entrance or scholarship papers rather than a Cambridge test, see our Common Entrance maths page.

Late transfer into a grammar school

The Mid-Year Transfer version is for grammar schools admitting into Years 8, 9 and 10, and Cambridge says the results are "age-standardised and benchmarked against your existing pupils". A child joining in Year 9 is therefore compared with children already in that school's Year 9.

For families moving into a grammar area mid-way through secondary school, the useful preparation is the school's own maths scheme for the years already taught, which the school can usually tell you.

CEM questions

What parents ask about the CEM 11 plus

Is CEM still used for the 11 plus?

CEM is now called Cambridge Insight, and its entrance test is Cambridge Select Insight, which some selective independent and state schools buy. In the grammar areas we have read, the county-wide 11 plus now comes from GL Assessment, Quest Assessments or FSCE instead.

What is Cambridge Select Insight?

It is a computer-based entrance test from Cambridge University Press and Assessment, previously sold as CEM Select. Cambridge describes it as about 60 minutes long with numerical, verbal and non-verbal sections, for entry into Years 7 to 10.

Can I buy CEM or Cambridge Select Insight past papers?

Not from Cambridge. Its entrance page says it does not provide any commercially available practice materials, including practice or past papers, and its parents page says the tests can only be bought by schools and local authorities.

Is the Cambridge Select Insight test adaptive?

No. Cambridge's parents page says Insight entrance assessments are not adaptive, unlike its classroom baseline tests, which do adapt to each answer.

What maths is in a CEM-style numerical section?

Cambridge does not publish a syllabus. It says the test measures mathematical skills that rely on genuine understanding rather than repetition, so we teach upper primary number, fractions, ratio and data, and how to reason through unfamiliar problems.

How do I help my child prepare?

Cambridge advises against practising and gives every candidate familiarisation material. The honest help is secure school maths, reading questions on a screen, and calm handling of questions that look new.

When are results available?

Cambridge says feedback is available to the school within 72 hours. The school decides what to share with families and sets its own threshold.

Is there extra time for children who need it?

Cambridge offers 25 per cent extra-time versions for candidates with special educational needs. Ask the school early, since it runs the sitting and needs evidence.

When are your lessons?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Times are confirmed in UK time.

Are you connected with Cambridge or CEM?

No. Modern Age Coders is an independent online school. We have never seen a Cambridge Select Insight question and we write all of our own practice material.
