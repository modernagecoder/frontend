# 11 plus maths tuition in Lincolnshire

Lincolnshire's 11 plus is set by the Lincolnshire Consortium of Grammar Schools, with papers from GL Assessment, for the county's 15 grammar schools. It is two tests: verbal reasoning, and non-verbal reasoning with spatial awareness. There is no maths paper. Modern Age Coders teaches maths live online, in a group or one to one, and we would rather say that plainly than sell 11 plus maths tuition for a test that does not set any. This page explains what the consortium tests, how the qualifying total of 220 works, what the council and consortium say about preparation, and where good maths teaching still earns its place for a Lincolnshire child.

## What is in the Lincolnshire 11 plus?

Two reasoning tests on two days, a week apart, marked by machine and standardised for age. The dates below are the consortium's own for the September 2026 sitting.

**Lincolnshire Consortium of Grammar Schools, 11+ key dates 2026**

| Date | What happens |
|---|---|
| Wednesday 7 January 2026 | Registration for the September tests opens on the consortium and grammar school websites |
| Tuesday 31 March 2026 | Deadline for registration forms to reach the individual grammar schools |
| Friday 26 June 2026 | Familiarisation materials due at primary schools |
| Friday 11 or Saturday 12 September 2026 | First test: verbal reasoning |
| Friday 18 or Saturday 19 September 2026 | Second test, which the consortium calls Non-Verbal Reasoning and Spatial Awareness |
| Friday 9 October 2026 | Results emailed to parents at 12:00 midday, and to primary schools at 16:00 |
| Saturday 31 October 2026 | Closing date for Lincolnshire local authority secondary applications |
| Monday 1 March 2027 | National Offer Day |

### How the papers are sat

Children answer by shading choices on an optical mark reading sheet. The verbal reasoning test opens with a ten-minute Preliminary Practice Test, and the non-verbal test is split into sections, each with a few practice questions that are not marked.

Depending on the area of the county, children sit in their own primary school or in the grammar school where they registered. The consortium explains the split as a local decision made when the County Council delegated selection to the schools.

### Who runs what

The consortium runs the test. Lincolnshire County Council runs admissions. The consortium says "The Local Authority is not responsible for selection arrangements and testing", and the council's own guide sends parents to the grammar schools for testing questions.

The consortium says it "currently uses GL Assessment to provide 11+ Testing, this arrangement may change over time." Check the consortium's site each year rather than assuming last year's format.

Sources: [LCGS 11+ Key Dates 2026](https://grammarschools.lincs.sch.uk/the-11-plus/11-key-dates-2026/) and [Testing Arrangements](https://grammarschools.lincs.sch.uk/the-11-plus/testing-arrangements/), read 31 October 2026. ## Does my child need 11 plus maths tuition in Lincolnshire?

For the test, no. The papers are verbal and non-verbal reasoning. Maths teaching helps a Lincolnshire child in other, real ways, and we would rather sell those honestly.

Families who know the 11 plus from Kent, Buckinghamshire or Birmingham may expect a maths section, because GL Assessment writes those tests too and they include one. Lincolnshire's consortium orders a different shape. Its list of recommended practice packs, published on its own site, names verbal reasoning and non-verbal reasoning packs only. A family who buys maths practice papers for the Lincolnshire test is preparing for something the child will not sit.

### What the test measures

The consortium says the tests "are designed to identify potential. They are not tests of attainment and do not reflect the amount of work done or the teaching styles of different primary schools."

### What it advises

"No special preparation is needed." The consortium adds that "Too much preparation puts children under pressure and may be counter-productive" and that it "does not endorse tutoring".

Not test coaching. Maths for school, for children who also sit a test elsewhere that includes maths, and for the step into a grammar or any other secondary school in Year 7.

The consortium notes that passing an 11 plus elsewhere does not usually exempt a child: "In most circumstances, it will still be necessary to take the Lincolnshire tests." Some families therefore sit two different tests.

A child who has qualified and wants to start Year 7 secure. The consortium describes its 15 schools as offering "a distinctive academic curriculum that stretches able children", and secure arithmetic is part of being ready for that.

As a substitute for the consortium's free familiarisation materials, which come through Lincolnshire feeder primary schools. Those are the right way for a child to see the format.

## What is the Lincolnshire 11 plus pass mark?

The consortium calls it a qualifying level, not a pass mark: a total standardised score of at least 220 across the two papers.

**How the consortium explains its scores**

| Question | The consortium's answer |
|---|---|
| What is the qualifying level? | "a total standardised score of at least 220 on both papers" |
| Why 220? | It is "set to identify the most able 25% of pupils in the age group in primary schools in the selective areas of Lincolnshire" |
| Is there an age allowance? | Yes, comparing a pupil only with pupils of the same age; it "is not fixed in advance" |
| Are boys and girls treated differently? | "There is no adjustment for the sex of the pupil taking the test." |
| Must every question be answered? | "It is possible to return a high standardised score without answering every question" |
| Can parents see raw scores? | No: "The Consortium has therefore resolved not to give parents the raw scores." |
| Can a child sit twice? | "The selection tests may only be taken once in the academic year." |

### Standardisation in plain terms

A raw score is the number of correct answers. The consortium converts it to a scale that compares a child with children of the same age, so that a child born in July or August is not at a disadvantage against one born the previous September. Because the allowance is based on how older pupils actually scored that year, it cannot be known before the test.

The two standardised scores are then added, and a total of 220 or more qualifies.

### What the consortium publishes

After each sitting the consortium publishes the spread of total scores and pupil ages; its 2025 table was published on 9 November 2025, with counts of five or fewer pupils suppressed so no child can be identified. It also lists how many pupils completed both papers at each test venue.

It does not publish an individual breakdown, and says giving separate paper scores "did not help parents understand or come to terms with their child's overall score".

Source: [LCGS Selection Process FAQ](https://grammarschools.lincs.sch.uk/the-11-plus/selection-process-faq/) and the consortium's 11+ data pages, read 31 October 2026. We print no prediction of any child's score.

## Does qualifying guarantee a Lincolnshire grammar school place?

No. Qualifying makes a child eligible; the place comes through the council's normal application, judged against each school's oversubscription criteria.

1. Register for the test with a grammar school in Year 5, by the consortium's deadline (31 March in 2026).
2. Sit both tests in September of Year 6.
3. Receive the total score by email in early October (9 October in 2026), before applications close.
4. Apply through Lincolnshire County Council between early September and 31 October of Year 6, naming any grammar school you want; the council says "you can select up to four schools".
5. Receive the offer on National Offer Day (1 March 2027 for this cohort).

### From the council's guide

"If your child reaches the required standard in the 11+, they will not automatically be offered a place. You must still name the grammar school on your application."

And the other direction: "If your child does not meet the required standard at a grammar school, a place will not be offered." Children who did not qualify cannot be added to a grammar school's reserve list unless found qualified at appeal.

### Two small things families miss

The council's application page allows up to four preferences, while the consortium's admissions page still says "up to three". The council runs admissions, so its number is the one to plan around; ask the council if in doubt.

Selective and non-selective areas differ. The consortium notes that families outside Lincolnshire, or in non-selective parts of the county, make their own transport arrangements to grammar schools.

Sources: [Lincolnshire County Council, Going to school in Lincolnshire](https://www.lincolnshire.gov.uk/school-admissions/going-school-lincolnshire-guide); [Apply for a school](https://www.lincolnshire.gov.uk/school-admissions/apply-school); [LCGS Admissions](https://grammarschools.lincs.sch.uk/admissions/). Admissions questions belong with the council and the schools; we do not advise on them.

## What can families do at home instead of test coaching?

Broad thinking and spatial play, the kind of everyday habit that helps any child and imitates no test. The warm-ups below are ours; they are not verbal or non-verbal reasoning items.

### Fold and predict

Our warm-up: draw a cross of six squares on paper, mark one square with a star, and ask which square will be opposite the star when it folds into a cube. Then fold it and check. Being wrong and seeing why is the point.

### Turn it in your head

Our warm-up: write a capital F on card, then ask what it looks like after a quarter turn clockwise, then in a mirror. Draw the prediction before turning the card. Two minutes, at the kitchen table.

### Say the rule

Our warm-up: one person makes a number sequence with a secret rule, the other finds the rule and must say it in a full sentence. "Add three" is fine; "it goes up" is not. Precise words are a reasoning habit.

**Maths that pays off in Year 7, our readiness list for Lincolnshire families**

| Skill (ours) | Why it matters in Year 7 |
|---|---|
| Times tables to 12, recalled instantly | Every fraction, ratio and algebra lesson leans on them |
| Fractions, decimals and percentages as one idea | Secondary maths moves between the three constantly |
| Written long multiplication and division | Needed before calculators arrive in secondary lessons |
| Reading a word problem for the operation | The habit behind every multi-step question that follows |
| Explaining a method in words | Grammar and comprehensive schools alike ask pupils to justify answers |

## What Lincolnshire parents ask

### Is there a maths paper in the Lincolnshire 11 plus?

No. The consortium sets a verbal reasoning test and a non-verbal reasoning and spatial awareness test. Its recommended practice packs are verbal and non-verbal reasoning only.

### What is the Lincolnshire 11 plus pass mark?

The consortium calls it a qualifying level: a total standardised score of at least 220 across the two papers. It says the line is set to identify the most able 25 per cent of pupils in the selective areas of Lincolnshire.

### When are the Lincolnshire 11 plus dates for 2026?

Registration opened on 7 January 2026 and forms were due by 31 March 2026. The verbal reasoning test was on 11 or 12 September 2026, the non-verbal test on 18 or 19 September 2026, and results were emailed at midday on 9 October 2026.

### Who provides the Lincolnshire 11 plus test?

The consortium says it currently uses GL Assessment and that this arrangement may change over time. Check the consortium site each year.

### Should I hire an 11 plus tutor in Lincolnshire?

The consortium says no special preparation is needed and that it does not endorse tutoring. We do not offer Lincolnshire test coaching; we teach maths for school and for Year 7.

### Can I see my child's raw scores?

No. The consortium says it has resolved not to give parents raw scores, and issues the overall standardised score only.

### My child passed an 11 plus in another county. Is that enough?

Usually not. The consortium says that in most circumstances it will still be necessary to take the Lincolnshire tests.

### Does qualifying guarantee a place?

No. Lincolnshire County Council says a child who reaches the standard is not automatically offered a place; you must still name the grammar school on your application, and oversubscription criteria apply.

### How many schools can I list on the application?

Lincolnshire County Council's application page says up to four, while the consortium's admissions page says up to three. The council runs admissions, so check with the council.
