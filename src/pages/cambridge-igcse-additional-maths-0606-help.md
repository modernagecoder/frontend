# IGCSE Additional Maths 0606 help

Cambridge IGCSE Additional Mathematics, code 0606, is the second maths qualification that strong pupils take alongside 0580 or 0980. Cambridge describes it as designed "to stretch the more able candidates" and as a smooth transition to AS and A Level. It also has a feature families rarely hear about until results day: the grade scale stops at E. This page covers the two papers, the fourteen topics, what changed for 2025 and why the calculus has to be done by hand.

## How is IGCSE Additional Maths 0606 assessed?

Two written papers of equal length and weight. The only difference between them is the calculator.

Unlike 0580, Additional Mathematics has no tiers. Every candidate sits the same two papers, and the syllabus says each "consists of questions based on any part of the content." There is no split where one paper takes algebra and the other takes calculus, so a pupil cannot hope to bank marks on a favourite topic in one sitting and avoid a weak one in the other.

**Cambridge IGCSE Additional Mathematics 0606: the two components**

| Paper | Calculator | Time | Marks | Weight | Content |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | Not allowed | 2 hours | 80 | 50 per cent | Any part of the syllabus |
| Paper 2 | Scientific calculator required | 2 hours | 80 | 50 per cent | Any part of the syllabus |

Eighty marks in two hours is roughly one and a half minutes per mark, which is more generous per mark than 0580's Extended papers. The extra time is there because the marks are earned through longer chains of algebra. The assessment objectives are weighted evenly: knowledge and understanding of techniques (AO1) and analysing, interpreting and communicating (AO2) each carry 45 to 55 per cent of the qualification.

Source: Cambridge IGCSE Additional Mathematics 0606 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027, "Assessment overview" and "Details of the assessment", from the [Cambridge International 0606 page](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/programmes-and-qualifications/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-additional-0606/). The minutes-per-mark figure is our arithmetic.

## What happens if you fail IGCSE Additional Maths?

The result is unclassified. 0606 has no grades F or G to fall back on.

The syllabus is unusually direct about this: "Grades A* to E will be available for candidates who achieve the required standards. Grades F and G will not be available. Therefore, candidates who do not achieve the minimum mark for grade E will be unclassified." In 0580 a weak Core paper still earns a G. In 0606 there is no equivalent; a pupil either reaches E or leaves with no grade for the subject.

**How 0606 grading compares with the main IGCSE, from the two Cambridge syllabuses**

|  | 0580 Core | 0580 Extended | 0606 Additional |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grades available | C to G | A* to E | A* to E |
| Tiers | Two | Two | None |
| If below the lowest grade | Ungraded | Ungraded | Unclassified |
| Calculator ban | Paper 1 | Paper 2 | Paper 1 |

### What this means for entry

A school enters 0606 candidates because it believes they will reach at least E. That judgement rests on algebra: the syllabus assumes the whole of 0580 or 0980 content, "This includes the use of indices and surds."

A pupil who is still shaky on Extended 0580 algebra is the pupil most at risk of an unclassified 0606 result. It is better to secure that algebra first than to take both qualifications under strain.

### What it means for the upside

For a pupil who is secure, 0606 is a strong signal. Cambridge says candidates achieving A* to C "are well prepared to follow a wide range of courses including Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics."

It also gives a running start on A level: logarithms, radians, the binomial expansion and calculus all appear again in the first year of sixth form.

Our first class for a 0606 candidate includes a short algebra check we wrote ourselves: rearranging, factorising cubics, index laws and surds. If it shows gaps, we say so before any Additional Maths topic is taught, because the risk of an unclassified result is too high to discover in a mock.

### Calculus without a formula list

The syllabus states: "No formulas will be given in the List of formulas for the Calculus section." Pupils must know the derivatives of xn, sin x, cos x, tan x, ex and ln x, the chain, product and quotient rules, and the matching integrals.

Only "an informal understanding of the idea of a limit is expected", and differentiation from first principles is not required. The weight falls on using the rules accurately, not on proving them.

### Answers that lose marks on format

Cambridge lists conventions that cost marks if ignored. "A numerical answer should not be given as a combination of fractions and decimals". "When asked to 'simplify', the candidate must simplify fully", and the same applies to factorise.

On graphs, the modulus of a non-linear function "should have cusps", and values should be read to within half of the smallest square on the grid.

## What changed in 0606 for 2025, and is 2028 different?

One topic in, one topic out, and a calculator ban on Paper 1. The syllabus for 2028 to 2030 makes no significant teaching change.

**The 2025 revision of 0606, from Cambridge's change list**

| Change | Cambridge's wording |
|---|---|
| Topic removed | "The Indices and surds topic has been removed from the subject content (this is now assumed knowledge)." |
| Topic added | "A new topic on Coordinate geometry of the circle has been added to the subject content." |
| Calculator | "Paper 1 is now a non-calculator paper, calculators are not allowed in the exam." |
| Standard | "The questions in the examinations are the same standard as in the existing assessment." |
| Marking | "Mark schemes have been updated to award more marks for working where appropriate" |
| Teaching time | "The teaching time has not changed." |

Two practical consequences follow. First, any past paper from before 2025 has a calculator on both papers and no circle questions, so it gives a misleading picture of Paper 1. Second, surds and indices did not disappear: they moved from a taught topic to an assumed skill, which means they now turn up inside other questions without warning. Cambridge's own teacher factsheet puts it simply: "Learners will need to practise solving problems and showing their working without a calculator."

Looking further ahead, the syllabus for examination in 2028, 2029 and 2030 says: "There are no significant changes which affect teaching." A pupil starting 0606 in Year 10 in September 2026 for exams in 2028 can use the same preparation.

Sources: "Changes to this syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027" in the current syllabus, the Cambridge "Getting ready to teach" factsheet for 0606, and the 0606 syllabus for 2028 to 2030.

## Two Paper 1 style problems, worked by hand

Both written by us at 0606 level, and both solvable without a calculator: one from the new circle topic, one from calculus.

**Our first problem.** A circle has equation x2 + y2 &minus; 6x + 4y &minus; 12 = 0. (a) Find its centre and radius. (b) Find the coordinates of the points where the line y = x &minus; 10 meets the circle.

1. Complete the square in x and in y: (x &minus; 3)2 &minus; 9 + (y + 2)2 &minus; 4 &minus; 12 = 0, so (x &minus; 3)2 + (y + 2)2 = 25.
2. Read off the centre (3, &minus;2) and the radius 5. The sign flip inside each bracket is the step most often lost.
3. Substitute y = x &minus; 10 into the original equation: x2 + (x &minus; 10)2 &minus; 6x + 4(x &minus; 10) &minus; 12 = 0, which simplifies to 2x2 &minus; 22x + 48 = 0.
4. Divide by 2 and factorise: x2 &minus; 11x + 24 = (x &minus; 3)(x &minus; 8) = 0, so x = 3 or x = 8.
5. Find y from the line: the points are (3, &minus;7) and (8, &minus;2). Check: each is 5 units from (3, &minus;2), which confirms both lie on the circle.

**Our second problem.** The curve y = 2x3 &minus; 9x2 + 12x + 1 has two stationary points. Find them and determine their nature.

### Finding the points

Differentiate: dy/dx = 6x2 &minus; 18x + 12 = 6(x2 &minus; 3x + 2) = 6(x &minus; 1)(x &minus; 2). Setting this to zero gives x = 1 and x = 2.

Substitute back into the curve: when x = 1, y = 2 &minus; 9 + 12 + 1 = 6; when x = 2, y = 16 &minus; 36 + 24 + 1 = 5. The points are (1, 6) and (2, 5).

### Deciding their nature

The second derivative is d2y/dx2 = 12x &minus; 18. At x = 1 it is &minus;6, which is negative, so (1, 6) is a maximum. At x = 2 it is 6, positive, so (2, 5) is a minimum.

Every number here is small on purpose. On Paper 1 the factorisation of the derivative is the whole question; a pupil who cannot factorise by inspection cannot finish.

## What families ask about IGCSE Additional Maths

### How many papers are in IGCSE Additional Maths 0606?

Two. Paper 1 and Paper 2 are each 2 hours and 80 marks and each is worth half of the qualification. Paper 1 is sat without a calculator and Paper 2 requires a scientific calculator.

### Is a calculator allowed in 0606 Paper 1?

No. Since the revision for first examination in 2025, Paper 1 is a non-calculator paper. Calculators are still allowed and required in Paper 2.

### What grades can you get in IGCSE Additional Maths?

Only A* to E. The syllabus says grades F and G will not be available, so a candidate who does not reach the minimum mark for E is unclassified.

### Is IGCSE Additional Maths hard?

It is harder than 0580 Extended, because it adds calculus, logarithms, radians and series, and both papers can test any topic. Cambridge says it is designed to stretch the more able candidates.

### Do you need 0580 to take 0606?

The syllabus assumes knowledge of 0580 or 0980 content, including indices and surds. Most schools teach them together in Years 10 and 11, and 0606 can be sat in the same series as either.

### What topics are in 0606?

Fourteen: functions, quadratic functions, factors of polynomials, equations and graphs, simultaneous equations, logarithms and exponentials, straight-line graphs, the circle, circular measure, trigonometry, permutations and combinations, series, vectors and calculus.

### Are formulas given in the 0606 exam?

A list of formulas is printed in the paper, but the syllabus states that no formulas will be given for the calculus section. Derivatives and integrals of the standard functions must be learned.

### Can a home-educated pupil sit 0606?

Yes. Private candidates can enter for this syllabus through an exam centre that accepts them. The centre makes the entry and sets its own arrangements.
