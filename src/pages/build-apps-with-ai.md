## The layer map

The model's half, ask and receive: screens, layouts and dark mode; forms with validation; standard save-and-fetch plumbing; polish like empty states and spinners. This half used to cost months and now costs sentences.

Your half, decide or inherit guesses: what the data means (is a booking owned by a student, a parent, a family), permissions (the model will guess "everyone", silently), collisions (two users, last slot, same second), and trust (what a hostile visitor tries first). Undecided is not empty; undecided means the model decided for you. Nothing in this column appears in a screenshot, which is why one-prompt apps demo perfectly and collapse at the second user.

## App types ranked by real buildability

A single-purpose tool: a beginner, weeks one to three, no catch, start here. A dashboard over live data: a beginner with one term done; the data source failing is the lesson. A booking or signup app: months two to three; collisions and permissions are your-half problems. A phone app in the stores: store developer accounts require an adult, so teens ship through a parent's account or go web-first. A game beyond a toy: models write game code well and design fun badly. A marketplace or social app: a team project, not a first one; it is a thousand permission and trust decisions wearing a trench coat.

Difficulty in the AI era tracks the number of your-half decisions, not the amount of code. A web app that installs from the browser reaches every phone, updates instantly, needs no store approval, and covers the overwhelming majority of student and first-product needs.

## The five-stage pipeline

1. Decide the v1 slice in writing: one user type, one core action; everything else is v2 by definition.
2. Answer the your-half questions before generating: data, ownership, permissions, collisions, as plain sentences pasted into every prompt.
3. Build the walking skeleton with the model, reading as it arrives, committing at every working step.
4. Harden it like a stranger is coming: empty input, double click, back button, edited URLs, no keys in the page source.
5. Launch small, watch one real user silently for fifteen minutes; their hesitations become v1.1.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can AI build a whole app from one prompt?** It builds something that looks like one. The invisible half arrives only when someone who knows to ask for it asks. One-prompt apps die on contact with a second user.

**Cost to build?** Near zero for a first product on free tiers; real costs start with real usage and with store accounts, which require an adult.

**Idea to launch, how long?** A single-purpose web tool: a weekend when experienced, two to three weeks as a taught beginner. Products with accounts and payments: months at any age. Distrust promises measured in hours.

**Backend and database?** AI writes the code fluently and cannot know your data. Deciding what the data means is the least glamorous, most valuable work.

**Safe to share my idea with a model?** For learner projects yes; the real rules are no personal data of real people, no credentials in chats, obviously fake test data.

**Can kids build apps?** From about 6, in block-based environments, with AI demonstrated by the teacher because of account age floors. The apps are real.
