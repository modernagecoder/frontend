## What is changing, and what has not

On 4 November 2024 the State Education Department presented **NY Inspires**, carrying forward the recommendations of the **Blue Ribbon Commission** on Graduation Measures. Four transformations sit at its centre:

| Transformation | What it means for a student |
|---|---|
| Adopt the Portrait of a Graduate | A menu of ways to demonstrate skill across areas including critical thinking, effective communication and innovative problem solving |
| Redefine credits | Credit tied to demonstrated learning rather than only to seat time |
| Sunset diploma assessment requirements | Passing Regents exams would stop being the gate to a diploma |
| Move to one diploma | Local, Regents and Advanced Regents collapse into a single credential |

**Nothing has changed yet.** Until the Board of Regents approves specific changes, current graduation requirements remain in effect for every public school student in New York State. Reporting has pointed at the 2027 to 2028 school year for the exam requirement to end. Treat that as direction, not as a date to plan around, and confirm your child's cohort with their school.

The strategic point: a child in Grade 6 today cannot know which regime they will graduate under. Coaching for a specific paper is fragile. Genuine capability is robust, because it helps under the current Regents system, it is exactly what a Portrait of a Graduate model asks for, and a university or employer wanted it either way.

## The other New York exam

While the state moves to retire the Regents as a diploma gate, New York City still admits to its most sought-after high schools on a single test.

The **Hecht-Calandra Act** of 1971 requires admission to the specialized high schools to be determined solely by performance on the Specialized High Schools Admissions Test. It originally covered **Stuyvesant** High School, the Bronx High School of Science and Brooklyn Technical High School. The SHSAT is now the sole criterion for eight of the nine specialized high schools; the ninth, LaGuardia, admits by audition or portfolio.

So a New York City family may be preparing simultaneously for a diploma that rewards demonstrated capability and an entrance exam where one morning decides everything. Both are real.

Mathematics carries both, which is why it is not an upsell here: [middle school mathematics](https://learn.modernagecoders.com/courses/comprehensive-middle-school-mathematics-mastery), [high school mathematics](https://learn.modernagecoders.com/courses/complete-high-school-mathematics-mastery), and for the competitive edge, [olympiad and competition mathematics](https://learn.modernagecoders.com/courses/olympiad-competition-mathematics-mastery).

## The New York student data project

Each market gets a project on local data containing one particular way of being wrong. New York's is the denominator problem.

**NYC Open Data** publishes every **311 Service Requests** record. The data runs from 2010 to the present, updates daily, and had passed forty million rows by the end of 2025. Each row carries a complaint type, responding agency and location.

The question: which part of the city has the worst noise problem? A student groups noise complaints by borough and ranks them.

**The trap.** Boroughs are not the same size. Ranking by raw count largely ranks boroughs by population, so the student has produced an expensive way of discovering where the most people live. Divide by residents and the order can change completely. The count was correct; the comparison had no denominator in it.

It deepens: per resident is one choice, per household another, per licensed premises another again for bar noise, per mile of road for street noise. Each answers a different question, and the student must say which. Stating the denominator before computing the ratio removes a large fraction of the bad statistics an adult will ever meet.

**A second layer.** A 311 record is not a noise event, it is somebody choosing to report one. Neighbourhoods differ in willingness to call the city, for reasons including trust, language and whether anybody expects it to help. Even a perfectly normalised rate measures reported noise, not noise. Saying so is the difference between an analysis and a claim.

## The arrangement

A class is one hour, live, with a teacher present throughout. Groups hold five to eight learners and keep the same teacher; private tuition is one learner and one teacher. Two classes a week, eight a month. Every course finishes in something built rather than something revised, which is the artefact that carries weight in a Portrait of a Graduate world.

Equipment: a laptop or desktop, a microphone, a connection that holds a video call. Everything runs in the browser, so nothing needs installing on a school-issued device.
