# Coding Classes in Bahrain

> Bahrain has an independent authority, BQA, that examines students at Grade 9 and Grade 12 against the national curriculum separately from their own school, and separately reviews the schools themselves. Modern Age Coders teaches live online coding and mathematics to ages 6 to 67 in Bahrain, in groups of five to eight or one to one, at USD 100 and USD 150 a month. The first class is free.

## What does BQA actually do?

Bahrain's Education and Training Quality Authority does two things that matter to a family choosing anything extra for a child.

**It examines.** BQA runs national examinations at **Grade 9** and at **Grade 12**, testing skills and knowledge against Bahrain's national curriculum, and it assesses student performance independently of the school the student attends.

**It reviews.** BQA inspects institutions and publishes what it finds, so a school's performance is a public record rather than a claim in a prospectus.

Its own sector performance report for the 2025 to 2026 academic year gives the scale: **172 of the 547 institutions in scope were reviewed, stated as 31 per cent.**

|---|---:|
| Government schools | 50 |
| Private schools | 22 |
| Kindergartens | 51 |

## Why does an independent examiner change what a parent should look at?

When the same body teaches and marks, a good report tells you the school is pleased. When a different body examines, a good result and a good report are two separate pieces of information.

If a learner is strong in class and weaker on an external paper, **that gap is the most useful signal available**, and it usually points at reasoning under unfamiliar conditions rather than at effort.

That is the specific thing a coding class addresses. Reasoning under unfamiliar conditions is what programming is: a learner is handed a problem they have not seen, breaks it into parts, is wrong in a way they can detect, and fixes it. Nobody marks their own work in that process either. The program either runs or it does not.

## Which curricula does this suit in Bahrain?

Bahrain runs the national curriculum alongside Indian curriculum schools on CBSE, and British, American and International Baccalaureate schools.

For a CBSE family the consequence is concrete: the Class 9 to 12 computer science sequence is the one Modern Age Coders teaches in India daily, so a learner is not translating between two systems and joins an existing group rather than needing one built around them.

For everyone else the useful point is the one BQA's existence makes. What matters is not which system a learner is in but what they can do when handed an unfamiliar problem, which is what the free first class measures.

## The Bahrain student data project

Every market page here works through one analytical trap. Bahrain's comes straight out of BQA's own figures: **counting unlike things as one.**

The learner is given the published breakdown and asked what share of Bahrain's education institutions were reviewed. The answer is in the report: 172 of 547, which is 31 per cent. The arithmetic is correct and the source is primary. A defensible headline in about a minute.

Then they are asked to write the sentence underneath, and it falls apart.

The 172 is not 172 of one thing. It is 50 government schools, 22 private schools, 51 kindergartens, 24 special education institutions, 22 vocational providers and 3 higher education institutions. **Six populations**, each with a different size, a different total in scope, and a different meaning attached to being reviewed.

So the 31 per cent answers a question nobody asked. A parent wants the share of *schools*. A vocational trainer wants vocational providers. Neither can get their answer from the single figure, and both would be misled: the kindergartens alone are 51 of the 172, so nearly a third of the headline describes institutions most readers of the phrase "education institutions" were not picturing.

The fix is not more arithmetic. It is refusing to divide until you can say what the numerator and denominator are made of. The learner ends up writing six rates instead of one, finds they cannot complete four because the report gives reviewed counts but not in-scope totals per type, and writes that limitation down instead of hiding it. That last step is the lesson.

This is deliberately not a units problem. Adding tonnes to cubic metres is one quantity measured two ways. This is six different entities counted as one, which is harder to spot because every number involved is an integer and looks clean.

## Will AI replace programmers before my child finishes school?

Bahrain already answered a version of this at national scale, and the answer is why BQA exists.

The country could have let every school report its own results. It built an independent examiner instead, because a claim needs checking by somebody other than the party making it. An AI is a party making claims. Hand it BQA's table, ask for the share of institutions reviewed, and it returns 31 per cent, well formatted, with no mention that the figure mixes kindergartens with universities. It is not lying. It answered the question asked, and the question was wrong.

What remains is deciding what to ask, noticing the denominator is six things, and being unwilling to publish a rate you cannot describe. That is judgement, it is learnable, and the syntax is the part the machine took.
