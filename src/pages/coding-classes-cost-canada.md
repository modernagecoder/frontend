### How to read the table

The published fee column is not a ranking. A four-week club and a monthly membership are different products, and the cheapest line on the page is free. What the table shows is the shape of the Canadian market: free self-paced and volunteer options at one end, short university terms in the middle, and monthly memberships at private centres, with franchises that quote by phone.

The University of Ottawa kids' clubs describe hands-on activities in "engineering, science and computer programming", but the themes listed for 2026 to 2027 are space, building, environment and chemistry, so we list them as general STEM clubs. The teen bootcamp and the game studio are the coding rows from that university.

### What no table can show

Quality is missing from every column. Two classes at the same fee can differ completely in who teaches, how many children share that teacher, and whether a child leaves able to write a program alone. The steps further down this page turn those questions into things you can ask.

Most fees here also come with conditions: a registration window that opens on a fixed morning, a term that started weeks ago, a campus a long drive away. A cheap class that runs on the wrong side of the city on the wrong day costs more than its fee.

### Why university clubs are cheaper per hour

The university outreach programs in Waterloo and Ottawa are run by engineering faculties, and Waterloo's says its bursaries come "With generous support from our supporters". Per hour they are the least expensive paid option we found. If you live near one and the dates suit, they are a good first taste of building and coding.

They are also short. A four-week or five-week block ends, the next one is on a different theme, and registration opens on a set morning and can fill: two of the Ottawa teen bootcamps were marked full when we read the page. They are a series of sessions rather than a curriculum that builds month after month.

### Why a weekly class costs more per hour

A private centre or an online school is paying for a continuing programme: a teacher who knows your child, a sequence of lessons, and timetables that run all year. MakerKids states a ratio of five children to one staff member for its in-person weekly classes, and a small room is part of what that fee buys.

Our group fee sits between the university clubs and the private centre per live hour. The difference you are paying for is the steady path: eight classes every month with the same teacher, from first Scratch game to Python projects, which is the part of learning to code that a short club cannot give.

## Our worked example: a cost calculator a student can write

This is the arithmetic from the table above as a short Python program. We wrote it for this page and ran it on 31 October 2026. It doubles as a first lesson in functions and loops, which is how we would use it in class with a twelve-year-old.

**Our worked example: price per live hour** (Python)
```python
# Our worked example: cost per live hour, from fees as each provider publishes them
offers = [
    # (name, fee, sessions, hours per session)
    ("University after-school club", 130, 4, 2.5),
    ("University Saturday club, kids", 145, 5, 2.5),
    ("University teen bootcamp", 160, 5, 2.5),
    ("University game studio, teens", 340, 12, 3),
    ("Centre, virtual, 1-hour class", 159, 4, 1),
    ("Centre, virtual, 2-hour class", 249, 4, 2),
]

def per_hour(fee, sessions, hours):
    live_hours = sessions * hours
    return fee / live_hours, live_hours

for name, fee, sessions, hours in offers:
    rate, live = per_hour(fee, sessions, hours)
    print(f"{name:32} {live:5.1f} h  {rate:6.2f} per live hour")
```
Add the quote you were given as one more line in the list, in the same form: a name, the fee, the number of sessions and the hours in each.

**What it prints** (Output)
```output
University after-school club      10.0 h   13.00 per live hour
University Saturday club, kids    12.5 h   11.60 per live hour
University teen bootcamp          12.5 h   12.80 per live hour
University game studio, teens     36.0 h    9.44 per live hour
Centre, virtual, 1-hour class      4.0 h   39.75 per live hour
Centre, virtual, 2-hour class      8.0 h   31.12 per live hour
```

### What the program teaches

A list of tuples holds the data, a function does one job and returns two values, and a loop prints a line per offer. A student who can explain why live_hours is calculated inside the function, and not typed in, has understood the idea of a unit rate in both math and code.

Changing one input and predicting the output before running it is the habit we build in every class. Try it: if the Waterloo club ran three hours a session, what would the last column say?

### Where the program stops being enough

A price per hour treats every hour as equal, and they are not. An hour with a teacher reading your child's own code is worth more than an hour of a child working alone in a room with a supervisor nearby. The steps below add the questions that a single number leaves out.

The program also ignores anything paid on top: registration fees, kits, a missed session that cannot be made up. Add those to the fee before you divide, and the comparison stays honest.

## Seven steps to work out what a coding class really costs

Our method for any quote, from a library club to a private tutor. It takes about ten minutes with the provider's page open, and it works for in-person and online classes alike.

1. Write the fee in its own unit. Per participant, per month, per course or per session. Never convert one into another in your head; write the unit next to the number.
2. Count the live sessions the fee buys. Four Thursdays, five Saturdays, twelve weeks, or eight classes a month. If the page does not say, ask, and do not guess.
3. Multiply by the length of each session. Use the times the provider prints. That gives the hours of live teaching, the number the whole comparison rests on.
4. Divide the fee by those hours. That is your price per live hour. Label it as your own calculation when you share it with a partner or another parent.
5. Add whatever sits outside the fee. Registration, kits, software, materials and travel. A class across town on a weekday evening has a cost that never shows on the receipt.
6. Ask who teaches and how many share them. A ratio, a batch size, or a one-to-one booking. Then ask whether the same teacher stays for the next term.
7. Ask what happens after this block ends. Is there a next level, a sequence, a plan for the year? A cheap term with nothing after it is a sampler, which is fine if a sampler is what you wanted.

### Questions to ask a centre that does not publish fees

Code Ninjas' own FAQ answers its cost question with "Please contact a nearby center for pricing information." That is normal for franchises, where each centre is a local business. When you call, ask for the fee in writing with its unit, the number of visits it covers, and how long each visit lasts.

Also ask whether there is a registration or enrolment fee, whether a missed visit can be made up, and how much notice is needed to stop. Then run the seven steps above on the answer.

### Questions to ask any online school

Is the class live, with a teacher talking to your child, or recorded? How many students are in a live batch? Is the fee billed monthly, or sold as a block of credits that expire? Will the timetable work in your province's time zone after the clocks change?

Some Canadian online schools sell high school credit courses, which are a different product with their own fees and their own rules from the province. A coding class that teaches alongside school is not a credit course, and we do not grant Ontario credits or any provincial credit.

## Coding class costs in Canada: common questions

### How much do coding classes for kids cost in Canada?

It depends on the format. Free options include CEMC's Computer Science Circles and library clubs. University outreach clubs in Waterloo and Ottawa charge a fee per term block of four or five sessions, private centres such as MakerKids publish monthly memberships, and franchise centres quote locally. The fee table on this page prints each one as published.

### How much does Code Ninjas cost in Canada?

Code Ninjas does not publish a national price. Its FAQ says rates are set by its locally owned and operated centres and asks families to contact a nearby centre for pricing. Ask the centre for the fee in writing, the number of visits it covers and how long each visit lasts, then compare it per hour.

### Are there free coding classes for kids in Canada?

Yes. CEMC at the University of Waterloo runs Computer Science Circles as a free service, and its other resources need no registration or fee. Some public libraries run coding clubs, such as Coding Buddies at Calgary Public Library, and university outreach programs run free library sessions and clubs such as Technovation Girls Ottawa.

### What is the fairest way to compare two coding classes?

Work out the price per live hour: count the sessions the fee buys, multiply by the length of each, and divide the fee by the result. Then add anything outside the fee, and ask who teaches and how many students share that teacher. The seven steps on this page set it out.

### Are online coding classes cheaper than in-person ones in Canada?

Not always. Short university clubs we found cost less per live hour than any weekly class, online or in person. What online classes save is travel time and the choice of a teacher and timetable that a local centre may not have. Compare per hour, not by format.

### What does the Modern Age Coders fee include?

Eight one-hour live classes a month with the same teacher, following a full course from start to finish. There is no registration fee and no kit. The fee table on this page shows the monthly fee for a group batch and for one to one.

### Is there a discount for paying in advance?

Yes. Paying for six months at once takes a quarter off the monthly fee. Paying monthly lets you stop at the end of any month.

### How big are your group classes?

A group batch has fifteen to twenty students with one teacher. If you would like a teacher to yourself, one to one is the other option; we do not sell a smaller batch to Canadian families.

### When do classes run for Canadian families?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Classes are timed for Canadian time zones, from British Columbia to Newfoundland.

### Do your coding classes give Ontario high school credits?

No. We teach coding alongside a student's school course and we do not grant Ontario credits or credits in any province. Credit courses are a different product, sold by schools that issue credits.
