# Online vs in-person coding classes

Some families picture coding classes as a room of children at laptops after school; others picture a teacher on a screen at the kitchen table. Both can teach well, and both can go badly. This page sets out where the formats genuinely differ, puts numbers on two things parents ask about most, the internet data an online lesson uses and the hours a weekly drive adds up to, and ends with a short checklist for a home setup that makes online lessons work.

## What actually changes between online and in-person classes

Most comparisons stop at "convenient" versus "social". For coding in particular, the differences are more specific than that.

**Online and in-person coding classes, factor by factor**

| Factor | Online, live | In person |
|---|---|---|
| Seeing the learner's code | Teacher sees the exact screen, line by line, through screen sharing | Teacher walks between desks and sees one screen at a time |
| Travel | None | A drive or a trip on public transport, twice for each lesson |
| Choice of teacher | Any teacher, anywhere, at a time that suits your state | Whoever teaches at the nearest venue on that afternoon |
| Social contact | Classmates on video, talking and sharing screens | Classmates in the same room |
| Distractions | Home: siblings, pets, other tabs | Venue: noise, waiting for help, other children's screens |
| Equipment | The family's own computer and internet | Often the venue's computers |
| Supervision | A parent nearby at home | Staff at the venue |
| What happens when sick | Can often still join from home if well enough | Usually a missed session |

The single biggest difference for coding is visibility. In a room, a teacher sees a child's program for a few seconds while walking past. Online, with the learner sharing their screen, the teacher reads the whole program and can ask the learner to scroll, run it, or change one line while everyone watches. That is why we teach live online rather than in a hall, and why we think the format suits coding better than, say, robotics with physical kits.

### Where in person wins

Hands-on hardware, such as robotics kits, soldering or drones, is easier with a teacher in the room who can hold the part and show where it goes.

Some children concentrate better away from home, especially if the family computer sits in a busy living area with a television nearby.

### Where online wins

There is no drive across town on a weeknight, which matters a great deal in outer suburbs and regional areas with no local provider at all.

The learner works on the same computer they will code on at home, so projects carry straight on between lessons without copying files across.

## How much internet data does an online lesson use?

Families on limited plans, or sharing a connection with a busy household, sometimes ask whether video lessons will eat their allowance. Microsoft publishes the bandwidth its Teams video calls use, and turning that into data per hour is a neat lesson in bits and bytes.

**Microsoft Teams one-to-one video, bandwidth by quality level (Microsoft Learn), and our data-per-hour arithmetic**

| Quality level | Bitrate, kilobit/s up/down | Data per hour, one way | Data per hour, both ways |
|---|---|---|---|
| Minimum | 150/150 | 67.5 megabytes | 135 megabytes |
| Recommended | 1,500/1,500 | 675 megabytes | 1.35 gigabytes |
| Highest listed level | 4,000/4,000 | 1,800 megabytes | 3.6 gigabytes |

### The arithmetic, step by step

A bitrate is measured in bits per second, but data allowances are counted in bytes, and one byte is eight bits. So 1,500 kilobits per second is 1,500 &divide; 8 = 187.5 kilobytes every second.

An hour has 3,600 seconds: 187.5 &times; 3,600 = 675,000 kilobytes, or 675 megabytes, in each direction. Sending and receiving together, that is 1.35 gigabytes for an hour at the recommended level.

### Over a whole year

At two one-hour lessons a week for about 40 school weeks, a learner has roughly 80 lessons a year. At the recommended level, 80 &times; 1.35 gigabytes comes to 108 gigabytes for the year.

Microsoft notes that Teams "can deliver HD video quality in under 1.5 Mbps", and calls adapt downwards when bandwidth is short, so a real lesson usually uses less than the recommended figure.

This is a calculation children can do themselves in Python: kbps * 3600 / 8 / 1000 gives megabytes per hour. The classic mistake, forgetting to divide by eight, makes every answer eight times too large, which is exactly why internet plans advertised in megabits per second seem to download files more slowly than the number suggests. On the speed side, nbn co now advertises "new 2000Mbps wholesale download speed options, available from participating providers", far above what a lesson needs; for a video lesson, a steady upload matters more than a fast download.

Sources, read 7 October 2026: [Microsoft Learn, Prepare your network for Teams](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoftteams/prepare-network) (bandwidth table, bitrate kilobit/s up/down) and [nbn co, speed](https://www.nbnco.com.au/residential/learn/speed). Data-per-hour and yearly figures are our own arithmetic. We teach on our own video platform; Microsoft's published figures are used here as a well-documented reference point for video calls in general.

## The hidden cost of in-person classes: time in the car

A weekly class twenty minutes away sounds small. Added up over a school year, it is not.

**Victorian school term dates, 2027 (Victorian Government)**

| Term | Start | Finish | Mondays inside the term |
|---|---|---|---|
| Term 1 | Wednesday 27 January | Thursday 25 March | 8 |
| Term 2 | Monday 12 April | Friday 25 June | 11 |
| Term 3 | Monday 12 July | Friday 17 September | 10 |
| Term 4 | Monday 4 October | Friday 17 December | 11 |

### A weekly class, worked through

Counting the Mondays between each term's start and finish gives 40 for the 2027 school year (public holidays are not removed, so a few of these will be days off).

A class 20 minutes away means 40 minutes of driving per lesson, since you go there and back. Over 40 Mondays that is 1,600 minutes, or about 26.7 hours, before counting the wait while the lesson runs.

### What that time is worth

For a parent, 26.7 hours is more than three full working days a year spent driving to one activity. For a second sibling's activity on another night, double it.

Online, the same lesson starts when the learner opens the laptop. The time saved is not a reason to choose online on its own, but it belongs in any honest comparison.

Other states' terms differ by a few days, and travel times range from a five-minute walk to an hour in regional areas, so plug in your own numbers: lessons per year &times; minutes each way &times; 2 &divide; 60 gives hours a year. A family 45 minutes from the nearest provider, which is common outside the capitals, would spend 60 hours a year on the same weekly class.

Source: [Victorian Government, school term dates and holidays](https://www.vic.gov.au/school-term-dates-and-holidays-victoria), read 7 October 2026. Monday counts and driving totals are our own arithmetic.

## Making online lessons work at home

Most problems with online classes are not about the teaching. They come from where and how the learner sits.

**A home setup checklist for online coding lessons**

| Item | What works | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Device | A laptop or desktop with a keyboard | Typing code on a tablet or phone is slow and hides the screen |
| Place | A shared family space, not a bedroom | Parents can see and hear the lesson |
| Connection | Wired or close to the router if possible | A steady upload keeps screen sharing smooth |
| Headset | Wired earphones with a microphone | Clearer audio and no echo for younger children |
| Tabs | Only the lesson and the code editor open | Fewer distractions, and easier screen sharing |
| Start | Join two minutes early | Sound and screen checks do not eat the lesson |

### Supervision, in a sensible place

The Australian Tutoring Association's guidance on in-home tutoring says tutoring "should take place in an open area where the parent/guardian can view and hear the process", and that it "should NOT take place in the student's bedroom".

That advice was written for a tutor visiting the house, and it makes just as much sense for a video lesson: the kitchen table or the family study is the right spot.

### For younger children

Children of six to eight usually need a parent nearby for the first few lessons to help with logging in and the microphone.

Once the routine is set, most children join on their own, with an adult within earshot rather than beside them.

Source: [Australian Tutoring Association, in-home tutoring](https://ata.edu.au/parent-info/in-home-tutoring/), read 7 October 2026.

## Which format suits your child?

A quick way to decide, based on the questions we ask families in the free first lesson.

**Online or in person: a short decision guide**

| If your child | Lean towards | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Wants to code games, apps or AI on a computer | Online | The work lives on screen, where the teacher sees it most clearly |
| Wants to build with robots or electronics kits | In person, or online with a kit at home | Hardware is easier with hands nearby |
| Gets distracted easily at home | In person, or online in a quieter room | A change of place can help focus |
| Lives far from any provider | Online | No travel, and a wider choice of teachers |
| Is shy in a room of strangers | Online, possibly one to one | Less pressure, and the teacher still sees everything |
| Has a parent who works late | Online | No pick-up needed |

You do not have to choose forever. Some of our learners take a holiday camp in person and keep their weekly lessons online; others start online, then add a school code club. ## How online learners progress

What online coding lessons look like as a learner grows.

**Four stages of live online coding lessons**

| Stage | What happens on screen |
|---|---|
| 1. Settling in | Logging in, sharing a screen, finding the code editor without help |
| 2. Guided building | The teacher watches the learner's screen and prompts each step |
| 3. Independent building | The learner builds alone, shares when stuck, and explains their code |
| 4. Presenting | The learner runs their project for the group and answers questions |

### For younger children

Screen sharing becomes second nature within a few lessons, and children enjoy showing their work to classmates on video.
A parent nearby helps with the first logins, then steps back.

### For teens and adults

Online lessons fit around school, sport and work, with no travel at the end of a long day.
Projects stay on the learner's own computer, ready to continue between lessons.

## Online or in-person coding classes: common questions

### What is the difference between online and in-person coding classes?

Online classes are taught live over video, with the teacher seeing the learner's screen through screen sharing; in-person classes happen in a room, with a teacher moving between desks. The teaching can be equally good; travel, visibility of the code and the home setup differ.

### What is the difference between a bit and a byte?

A byte is eight bits. Internet speeds are usually given in bits per second, while data allowances and file sizes are counted in bytes, so speeds must be divided by eight to compare them with data.

### How much data does an online coding lesson use?

At Microsoft's recommended 1,500 kilobits per second for one-to-one video, about 675 megabytes each way per hour, or 1.35 gigabytes in total. Real lessons often use less, because calls adapt to the connection.

### Are online coding classes as good as in-person ones?

For coding on a computer, often better, because the teacher can read the learner's whole program through screen sharing. For robotics and other hardware, in-person teaching has a real advantage.

### What internet speed do we need?

A steady connection matters more than a fast one. Microsoft lists 1,500 kilobits per second up and down as recommended for one-to-one video, which most Australian home connections exceed.

### Where should my child sit for online lessons?

In a shared family space rather than a bedroom, so a parent can see and hear the lesson, in line with the Australian Tutoring Association's guidance on home tutoring.

### Can my child use a tablet?

A laptop or desktop with a keyboard works much better for coding. Tablets make typing slow and screen sharing awkward.

### How much time does online save?

For a weekly class 20 minutes away, about 26.7 hours a year of driving, based on the 40 Mondays inside Victoria's 2027 term dates.

### Do online learners still make friends?

Yes. Groups of five to ten talk, share screens and present projects to each other, and many keep the same classmates for months.
