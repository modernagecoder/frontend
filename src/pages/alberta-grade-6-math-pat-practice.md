### Tools that are allowed

Manipulatives are welcome in both parts. The bulletin&rsquo;s list includes &ldquo;a protractor, a ruler, tracing paper, pattern blocks, tiles and cubes, geoboards, tangrams, counters, spinners, unlabeled number lines, graph paper&rdquo;, with one condition: the tool &ldquo;cannot perform the mental conversion or provide the solution to a problem.&rdquo;

A 12 by 12 multiplication grid may be used in both parts as a regular accommodation for children who use one in class, recorded on the platform. And the bulletin is firm on calculators: &ldquo;If a student requires the use of a calculator to complete Part A and Part B, the student should be excused from the provincial achievement test.&rdquo;

### What Alberta expects at each standard

At the acceptable standard, the bulletin says students can typically &ldquo;apply appropriate strategies and standard algorithms to solve routine one-step problems&rdquo;. At the standard of excellence they can &ldquo;apply appropriate and efficient strategies and standard algorithms fluently and accurately to solve multi-step novel problems&rdquo;.

The difference between those two sentences is a good map for practice: first one-step accuracy with the standard methods, then several steps chained together, then problems the child has not seen in that form before.

### Who writes it, and how

Alberta&rsquo;s Grade 6 Parent Guide says &ldquo;All students registered in grades 6 and 9 or those in their sixth or ninth year of schooling are expected to write the tests for their grades.&rdquo; Under each schedule Alberta adds that every student may have up to double the official time.

Grade 6 children now sit the PATs on a screen through Alberta&rsquo;s digital assessment platform, the same place Alberta keeps its practice tests, which offer read-aloud, coloured backgrounds and enlarged text.

### Results

Parents hear two raw scores, one per part, never added together: Alberta&rsquo;s bulletin forbids combining them into one total. A fuller report, the Individual Student Profile, is due in late September 2027 under the bulletin&rsquo;s timetable of significant dates.

The same parent guide asks parents to keep the test in perspective: children&rsquo;s feelings about a test &ldquo;are very much influenced by what adults close to them say about those tests.&rdquo; A calm week before the test is worth more than an anxious one.

A note on the new curriculum. Alberta&rsquo;s renewal timeline records that &ldquo;New grades 4 to 6 mathematics&rdquo; was implemented across the province in the 2023-24 school year. A child in Grade 6 in 2026 to 2027 has therefore been taught from the new curriculum since Grade 3 or Grade 4, and the PAT now uses its organizing ideas. ## How we help with the Grade 6 PAT without coaching to it

One line in Alberta&rsquo;s Grade 6 Parent Guide shapes how we work: the province &ldquo;discourages test rehearsal and coaching.&rdquo; We take that at face value. A Grade 6 child who understands the math does not need rehearsal; one who does not understand it is not helped by it.

### Standard methods, understood

Column addition, long multiplication and division, done by hand and explained aloud. The new curriculum names these &ldquo;standard procedures (algorithms)&rdquo;, and the PAT allows no calculator.

### Fractions as numbers

Placing fractions on a number line, comparing them and multiplying them by whole numbers, so that 3 &times; 2/7 is a picture before it is a rule.

### Reading the question

Alberta notes that many questions are &ldquo;placed in real-life contexts&rdquo;. We practise turning a short paragraph into a calculation, and writing the answer back in words.

### What we leave out

No released test items, no question-spotting and no multiple-choice tricks. Every question on this page was written by us for the Grade 6 content, and none is copied or adapted from an Alberta test.

And no promises about results. The PAT is one picture of one week; Alberta itself says it is part of a larger picture that teachers build all year.

## Two Grade 6 problems, solved without a calculator

Our own problems, not Alberta Education questions. The first is a one-step fraction problem like Part A; the second is a multi-step measurement problem like Part B. Both are done by hand, as the test requires.

Problem 1. A recipe uses 3/4 of a cup of oats for one tray of granola. How many cups are needed for 6 trays?

1. Choose the operation. Six trays of 3/4 each means 6 &times; 3/4, a fraction multiplied by a natural number, which is on Alberta&rsquo;s Grade 6 list.
2. Multiply the numerator only. 6 &times; 3 = 18, so the product is 18/4. The denominator stays 4 because the pieces are still quarters.
3. Simplify. 18/4 = 9/2 = 4 1/2.
4. Check with a picture. Two trays use 3/4 + 3/4 = 1 1/2 cups, so six trays use three times that: 4 1/2 cups.
5. Answer in words. Six trays need 4 1/2 cups of oats.

Problem 2. A garden bed is 3 m long and 2 m wide, and it will be filled with soil 0.5 m deep. Soil comes in bags of 0.25 cubic metres. How many bags are needed?

1. Find the volume. Volume = length &times; width &times; depth = 3 &times; 2 &times; 0.5. Work it as 6 &times; 0.5 = 3, so the bed holds 3 cubic metres of soil.
2. Decide the next operation. The question asks how many bags of 0.25 fit into 3, which is a division: 3 &divide; 0.25.
3. Divide without a calculator. 0.25 is one quarter, and there are 4 quarters in 1, so there are 4 &times; 3 = 12 quarters in 3.
4. Check. 12 bags &times; 0.25 = 3 cubic metres, exactly the volume of the bed.
5. Answer in words. 12 bags of soil are needed. A real gardener might buy one more for settling; on the test, the answer is 12.

**Python · a growing pattern, checked against a rule (ours)** (python)

```python
# Growing pattern: start at 3, add 4 each step (ours)

term = 3
for step in range(1, 11):
    print("Term", step, "=", term)
    term = term + 4

# Check the shortcut rule: term n = 4n - 1
n = 10
print("Shortcut for term 10:", 4 * n - 1)
```
Output: Term 1 = 3 through Term 10 = 39, then &ldquo;Shortcut for term 10: 39&rdquo;. The loop builds the pattern by adding 4; the rule 4n &minus; 1 jumps straight to any term. Question 3 in the Part B practice asks the same thing on paper.

## Ten Part A style questions: number, no calculator

Our own questions on the two Part A categories: integers, decimals and powers; fractions, rates and ratios. They are not Alberta Education questions. Give it a quarter of an hour, pencil only.

1. Evaluate &minus;7 + 12.
2. Evaluate 3 &minus; 9.
3. Add 4.6 + 2.75.
4. Multiply 5.2 &times; 3.
5. Evaluate 24.
6. Write 103 as a whole number.
7. Add 2/5 + 1/5.
8. Multiply 3 &times; 2/7.
9. A bag holds 12 red counters and 18 blue counters. Write the ratio of red to blue in lowest terms.
10. A train travels 150 km in 3 hours at a steady speed. What is its rate in kilometres per hour?

1. 5. On a number line, start at &minus;7 and move 12 to the right.
2. &minus;6. Start at 3 and move 9 to the left, passing zero.
3. 7.35. Line up the decimal points: 4.60 + 2.75.
4. 15.6. 5 &times; 3 = 15 and 0.2 &times; 3 = 0.6.
5. 16. 2 &times; 2 &times; 2 &times; 2. A power means repeated multiplication, not 2 &times; 4.
6. 1000. 10 &times; 10 &times; 10.
7. 3/5. Same denominator, so add the numerators.
8. 6/7. 3 &times; 2 = 6 sevenths.
9. 2:3. 12:18, then divide both by 6.
10. 50 km per hour. 150 &divide; 3 = 50.

## Eight questions in the spirit of Part B

Also ours and not from Alberta: algebra and patterns, geometry and coordinates, measurement and data, still with no calculator. Allow about 25 minutes.

1. Solve n + 17 = 41.
2. Solve 4m = 36.
3. A pattern starts 3, 7, 11, 15, ... Describe the rule and find the 10th term.
4. Points A(2, 3), B(6, 3) and C(6, 7) are three corners of a square. What are the coordinates of the fourth corner?
5. A triangle has a base of 10 cm and a height of 6 cm. What is its area?
6. A box measures 5 cm by 4 cm by 3 cm. What is its volume?
7. Find the mean of 4, 7, 9, 10 and 10.
8. A rectangle has a perimeter of 30 cm and a length of 9 cm. Find its width and its area.

1. n = 24. Subtract 17 from both sides: 41 &minus; 17 = 24. Check: 24 + 17 = 41.
2. m = 9. Divide both sides by 4.
3. Start at 3 and add 4 each time; the 10th term is 39. From the 1st term to the 10th there are 9 steps of 4: 3 + 36 = 39. The program above prints the same value.
4. (2, 7). AB and BC are each 4 units long, so the fourth corner is 4 units above A.
5. 30 cm2. Half of base times height: (10 &times; 6) &divide; 2.
6. 60 cm3. 5 &times; 4 &times; 3.
7. 8. The total is 40, and 40 &divide; 5 = 8.
8. Width 6 cm, area 54 cm2. Two lengths make 18 cm, leaving 12 cm for two widths, so each width is 6 cm. Area = 9 &times; 6 = 54.

If the fraction or ratio questions caused trouble, that is the place to spend the next few weeks, because the two Number categories together carry a large part of both parts. If the word problems were the trouble, read each one aloud and draw a quick sketch before calculating.

## Grade 6 math PAT: common questions

### What is the Grade 6 math PAT in Alberta?

Every June, Alberta's Grade 6 classes sit a provincial math test called a Provincial Achievement Test, or PAT. It has two parts: Part A, 15 questions on number, and Part B, 40 questions on the whole Grade 6 math curriculum.

### When is the Grade 6 math PAT in 2027?

Part A is on Monday 14 June 2027 from 9 AM to 9:30 AM, and Part B on Wednesday 16 June 2027 from 9 AM to 10:15 AM. Every student may take up to double the official time.

### Are calculators allowed on the Grade 6 math test?

No. Alberta's bulletin says a calculator is not permitted in Part A or Part B. Manipulatives such as a ruler, a protractor or graph paper are allowed.

### What is on Part A?

Part A has 15 questions from the organizing idea Number, in two reporting categories: integers, decimals and powers, and fractions, rates and ratios. It is designed for 30 minutes, with up to 60 allowed.

### What is on Part B?

Part B has 40 questions from all organizing ideas in Grade 6 math: number, algebra and patterns, geometry and coordinate geometry, and measurement and statistics. It is designed for 75 minutes, with up to 150 allowed.

### Is the Grade 6 PAT based on the new curriculum?

Yes. Alberta implemented new Grades 4 to 6 math in the 2023-24 school year, and the 2026 to 2027 Grade 6 math bulletin describes the test in the new curriculum's terms of organizing ideas and reporting categories.

### What is a sensible way to get ready for the Grade 6 PAT?

Know the Grade 6 math, done by hand. The province does not want PATs rehearsed or coached, so two short sessions a week on fractions, decimals, ratios and word problems beat any last-minute cramming.

### Is there coding on the Grade 6 PATs?

Not on the math test. The Grade 6 science PAT reports on six categories, one of which is Computational Thinking.
