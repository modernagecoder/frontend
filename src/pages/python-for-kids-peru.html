The right age

What age can a child start learning Python?

Usually around nine, which in Peru is 4th grade of primaria. The deciding factor is not age on its own but three skills: reading a short instruction, typing without hunting for every key, and staying with a puzzle for ten minutes.

Ages 6 to 8

Blocks first. Scratch lets a child build stories and games by dragging pieces, with almost no typing. The ideas are the same ones Python uses (order, repetition, choices), so nothing learnt is wasted.

Ages 9 to 10

Most children are ready for typed Python here, especially with drawings, where every line of code makes something visible on screen. Short programs, a lot of trying, and quick wins.

Ages 11 to 12

Children of this age can handle longer programs, lists of data and their first small AI projects. Many go on to the teenage Python course at 13 with a year of real code behind them.

Peruvian school uses the same ages as a guide. MINEDU sets a "normative age" for each grade, measured on 31 March of the school year: a child of 6 on that date belongs in 1st of primaria, a child of 9 in 4th and a child of 11 in 6th. Primaria itself lasts six years under Ley 28044, the General Education Law, and its regulation says it serves children from the age of six.

If you are not sure, the free first class settles it. The mentor gives your child a small puzzle, watches how they read it, type and think, and then tells you honestly whether Python or blocks is the better place to begin. Families who would rather read in Spanish can see our curso de Python para niños page, and parents of younger children our guide to clases de programación para niños en inglés.

English inside Python

Is Python in English? Which English words will my child learn?

Yes. Python has 35 reserved words, the commands a program is built from, and every one is an English word or a short form of one. A child who learns Python in English meets each word once, with its everyday meaning and its programming meaning together.

Why this matters for a child in Peru

A child taught Python in Spanish still types while, if and return, because the language only understands English. The teacher says "mientras" and the screen says while, so the child learns two words for one idea and links neither to real English.

In our classes, the mentor says "while the score is less than ten" and the child types exactly that idea. The spoken word and the typed word are the same, and a few hundred repetitions later they are simply part of the child's English.

What a parent will notice

Children start reading error messages aloud, in English, and guessing what they mean. "Name is not defined" becomes a clue rather than a wall.

They also learn that spelling and capitals matter. True works and true does not, which the Language Reference makes plain: the words "must be spelled exactly". That kind of precision helps with English spelling too.

What primaria gives

Does primaria in Peru teach coding or enough English for this?

Primaria in Peru teaches no coding as a subject and, on paper, two or three English periods a week. Maths gets more time. Here is what the national plan of studies sets out, and what that means for a Python class.

Maths and English on the timetable

The CNEB, Peru's national curriculum, gives primaria 30 teaching periods of 45 minutes a week. Maths has 5 periods a week in grades 1 and 2 and 4 in grades 3 to 6, which works out by our arithmetic at 225 and 180 minutes. English has 2 periods in the first two grades and 3 after that, about 135 minutes a week from 3rd grade.

Even those English hours depend on the school. The CNEB says English in primaria is phased in depending on whether the school has hired English teachers, so some children meet much less English than the table promises, and children at bilingual schools meet far more.

And no coding subject

Nowhere in the primaria plan is there an area called computing or programming. Digital skills sit in a competency that every subject is supposed to share, about working in virtual environments, and it names no programming language.

That leaves a gap for children who are curious about how games and apps are made. A Python class fills it, and because the class is in English, it also adds an hour or two of English with a clear purpose: the program only runs if the instruction was understood.

Python classes are extra, never a replacement for school. When the 2026 school year opened on 16 March 2026, MINEDU counted 6,225,000 pupils in 53,631 schools of regular basic education; the national curriculum applies to all of them, and our classes sit alongside it in the hours the family chooses. A first lesson

What does a first Python lesson look like? A star pyramid

This is a lesson we teach to children of about 10 in their first weeks of Python. It uses a loop, which repeats a line of code, and asks the child to count carefully, which turns out to be the hard part.

The challenge

Draw a pyramid of stars four rows tall, with one star at the top and the rows getting wider. Most children start with Version 1: repeat four times, and each time print a row of stars as long as the row number.

They run it and see two surprises. The first line is empty, and the "pyramid" leans to one side like a staircase.

What went wrong

The empty line comes from range(height), which counts 0, 1, 2, 3. Python's documentation says that if you leave out where to start, it starts at 0, so the first row has zero stars. Counting from zero is one of the first things every programmer has to learn.

The leaning comes from forgetting the spaces. A pyramid needs empty space on the left of each row, and that space has to be counted too.

The last two lines are where the maths appears. One plus three plus five plus seven is sixteen, and so is four times four. We ask the child to change height to 5, then 6, and predict the total before running it each time. Most notice by the third try that the odd numbers always add up to a square, and some can explain why by drawing the pyramid as rings of stars. That is the moment a child stops copying code and starts thinking with it.

  1. Without a computer. Draw the pyramid on squared paper and count the empty squares on the left of each row. Write the numbers down.
  2. Predict. Before running Version 1, say aloud, in English, how many lines it will print and what the first one will look like.
  3. Fix one thing at a time. First make the empty line disappear, then add the spaces. Run the program after each change.
  4. Make it yours. Ask the user for the height with input(), or draw the pyramid upside down. Then explain the change to a parent in two sentences.

Thinking, not copying

Will my child just copy answers from AI?

Not in our classes. Children solve each puzzle themselves, explain it to the mentor, and only then see other ways to do it. They do not use adult chatbot accounts.

Think, then type

Every new program begins with a prediction: what will happen when it runs? A wrong prediction is useful, because finding out why is exactly the skill we are building.

Age rules respected

ChatGPT's own terms set a minimum age of 13 and require a parent's permission under 18, and Claude is for adults of 18 and over. Children of 9 to 12 learn without these tools in class.

Same standard everywhere

A child in Piura, Lima or Puno gets the same lesson plan, the same patient mentor and the same insistence on understanding. Where you live changes the hour on the clock, not the depth of the class.

When children are older and ready, we teach how AI works from the inside, with small models they train themselves in Python. The habit we protect from the first week is the one shown in the pyramid lesson: count, predict, check, and never accept a result you cannot explain.

Class times

What time would classes be in Peru?

At an hour you choose. Peru keeps UTC minus 5 all year and India keeps UTC plus 5:30, so India is always 10 hours 30 minutes ahead, in the school term and in the summer holidays alike. These rows only convert clocks; they are not fixed class times.

Python for kids in Peru: questions from parents

Are the classes in Spanish?

No. All our classes are live online in English, and only in English. Mentors speak slowly and clearly, adjust to your child's pace and accent, and show each step on screen. Python's commands are English words anyway, so a child learning it in English hears and types the same words.

What age is right for Python?

Around 9, which is usually 4th grade of primaria in Peru. Children need to read short instructions and type without too much trouble. Younger children, or those not ready to type, start with Scratch blocks and move to Python later.

My child's English is basic. Will they understand?

Usually, yes, if they can follow simple spoken instructions. Each step is shown on the shared screen, and the mentor repeats in simpler words. For a child with very little English we suggest one-to-one classes at first. The free class will show you.

Is Python too hard for a nine year old?

Not when the lessons are built for children. We start with drawings and short programs that show a result straight away, add one idea at a time, and only move on when the child can explain what they did.

When are the classes in Peru time?

Any day, any time, flexible: choose a batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at the hour that suits your family. India is 10 hours 30 minutes ahead of Peru all year, so 09:30 in Peru is 20:00 in India.

Does primaria in Peru teach programming?

Not as a subject. The national curriculum has no computing area in primaria; digital skills are shared across subjects. Our classes add coding alongside school, never instead of it.

Will my child use ChatGPT or other AI tools?

Not adult accounts. ChatGPT's terms set a minimum age of 13 with a parent's permission under 18, and Claude is for users of 18 and over. Children learn to solve problems themselves first, and later learn how AI works by building small models in Python.

What does my child need at home?

A laptop or desktop computer with a keyboard, a steady internet connection and ideally a headset with a microphone. Python is free, and the mentor helps set it up in the first class. A phone or a tablet without a keyboard is not enough for typing code.

Do children get a certificate?

Yes, our own certificate of completion when they pass the course's final assessment. It is from Modern Age Coders, not a school or ministry qualification, and we are not connected with MINEDU or any school in Peru.

How do we pay?

By card, in US dollars, monthly. Your bank converts the payment into soles, so the amount on your statement can differ slightly between months. The first class is free and needs no card.
