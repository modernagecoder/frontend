Informatik in Mecklenburg-Vorpommern

Is Informatik compulsory in Mecklenburg-Vorpommern?

Yes, in every year from grade 5 to grade 10 and at every school type, as the GI's Informatik-Monitor 2026/27 records.

Curricula exist for Informatik und Medienbildung in grades 5 to 10 at the Regionale Schule, the Gesamtschule and the Gymnasium, and for Informatik in the Qualifikationsphase. The Oberstufe at the Gesamtschule and the Gymnasium runs as G8, with grade 10 as the Einführungsphase.

Near the front nationally

The GI's national summary names Mecklenburg-Vorpommern, Saarland and Thuringia as the three Länder meeting the medium-term recommendation of six compulsory Informatik hours in lower secondary, and pairs Mecklenburg-Vorpommern with Saxony as the leaders in the Oberstufe.

Its Mecklenburg-Vorpommern page puts Informatik in the timetable of 40 % of the Land's Oberstufe pupils in 2024/25 (KMK data), well over twice the roughly 15 % across Germany; 6 % of the year's Abitur candidates chose it as an exam subject.

Teachers for the subject

The monitor reports 419 Informatik teachers in the Land for 2024/25, 6 % of all teachers, with 7 newly hired in 2025/26. Informatik can replace a pupil's second science in the Oberstufe, though not the first.

With Informatik every week from grade 5, a pupil arrives at the Oberstufe with a base many pupils elsewhere lack. Our lessons build on that base rather than repeat it.

Mecklenburg-Vorpommern has 1,573,597 residents, 98,308 of them in Schwerin, the Land capital (Destatis, 31 December 2024); Rostock, on the coast, is its largest city. After four years of Grundschule, pupils pass through a two-year Orientierungsstufe in grades 5 and 6, usually at a Regionale Schule or Gesamtschule, before continuing at a Regionale Schule, a Gesamtschule or a Gymnasium. The KMK lists the Regionale Schule as a school that combines two tracks.

The Informatik Abitur

How is the Informatik Abitur set in Mecklenburg-Vorpommern?

Centrally, by a commission of teachers and the subject adviser, with compulsory and optional tasks. The details come from the Informatik-Monitor.

Source: Gesellschaft für Informatik, Informatik-Monitor 2026/27, Mecklenburg-Vorpommern page, read 2 November 2026. All tasks may include practical parts. Permitted aids are the Tafelwerk (formula book) used at the school and supplements supplied by the exam commission (Tafelwerksergänzungen). The 2027 exam dates could not be fetched from the education ministry's server, so this page prints none; your school has them.

A choice inside the exam

Because a candidate chooses one of two optional tasks, part of the skill is deciding quickly which one suits them. That decision is easier for a pupil who has seen many kinds of problem: data structures, automata, databases, algorithms.

Practical parts are allowed in every task, the monitor notes, so working fluently at a computer matters as much as writing clearly.

How we help

Oberstufe pupils practise with original tasks in the same compulsory-plus-choice pattern, timed, then talk through why one optional task was the better pick. They write and test code, and they write short explanations in German exam style.

Abitur results rest on the school course alone. Our role is extra practice; school work is never written or marked by us.

A Baltic island project

How big is Rügen? Measuring islands from their corners

The Land's surveying office publishes its topographic base data as open data, including every island as a polygon of coordinates. Our pupils use it to learn a piece of maths that feels like a magic trick, the shoelace formula, and a habit that keeps it honest: test a formula on shapes you can check by hand before you trust it on real data.

The data

We requested the island layer (AX_Insel) from the web feature service of the Landesamt für innere Verwaltung M-V on 3 November 2026. It is published under Creative Commons BY 4.0 with the credit © GeoBasis-DE/M-V, which allows reuse with that credit. Our script saved its 608 records as inseln_mv.json: a name and a list of corner points in metres (the ETRS89 UTM zone 33 grid used in the Land).

A polygon's area is not stored in the file. We have to compute it.

The formula

Walk round the corners in order. For each pair of neighbouring corners, multiply each x by the next y and subtract the next x times this y; add all of these up and halve the result. Because the formula multiplies coordinates crosswise, like the laces of a shoe, it is called the shoelace formula.

It is easy to get a sign or an index wrong, so Version 1 tests it on two shapes whose areas any pupil can work out by hand.

A 1 km by 1 km square should be 1 km² and a right triangle with legs of 4 km and 3 km should be half of 12, so 6 km². Both match, and the conversion from square metres to square kilometres (divide by 1,000,000, not 1,000) is checked along the way. Now the formula has earned some trust.

The largest single record is a piece of Usedom of 32.9 km², which cannot be the whole island. A look at the names shows why: Rügen and Usedom are each stored as many separate pieces, and 311 small records have no name at all. Version 3 adds the pieces up by name.

Areas are our own shoelace calculations from the published corner coordinates and our own sums of the pieces; they are not official area statistics. In this layer every outline runs anticlockwise and none has a hole, which we checked before using the absolute value.

  1. Ages 10 to 12, squared paper. Draw an L-shaped "island" on a grid, count the squares, then work out its area with the shoelace rule from its six corners. Do the two answers agree?
  2. Python, ages 12 and up. Run Version 1, then add a rectangle of your own and a shape with a dent in it. Does the formula still work?
  3. Ages 14 to 16, Python. Remove abs() from the function and walk the square clockwise. What happens to the sign, and why does it matter when you add pieces together?
  4. Going further. Compute each island's perimeter from the same corners and compare perimeter and area. Which island has the most coastline for its size?

Contests

Which informatics contests can pupils in Mecklenburg-Vorpommern enter?

The national ones, through school.

A weekly Informatik lesson from grade 5 is good preparation for the Informatik-Biber, the free puzzle contest that Bundesweite Informatikwettbewerbe runs for grades 3 to 13, this year from 9 to 20 November 2026 with schools handling entry. Older pupils can enter the first round of the 45th Bundeswettbewerb Informatik until 23 November 2026, the route into the national olympiad team. Our contest lessons use problems we write ourselves, never the organisers' tasks, and we have no tie to BWINF.

Coding classes in Mecklenburg-Vorpommern: questions

Are the classes in German?

No. Teaching is always in English, live, from mentors working in India; they match the speed and accent your child needs and mention German school words where useful.

Is Informatik compulsory in Mecklenburg-Vorpommern?

Yes. The Informatik-Monitor 2026/27 says Informatik und Medienbildung is taught at all school types in grades 5 to 10, at least one lesson a week each year.

If school already teaches it, why add lessons?

One weekly lesson, shared with media education, gives a good base. Pupils who want the Leistungskurs, a contest or their own projects need more practice than the timetable gives.

How is the Informatik Abitur set?

Centrally, by a commission of teachers and the subject adviser. The Grundkurs paper has one compulsory and one of two optional tasks; the Leistungskurs has two compulsory and one of two optional tasks, and practical parts are allowed.

Can my child take a written Informatik exam in the Grundkurs?

Yes, as the third exam subject, according to the Informatik-Monitor. It can also be an oral fourth or fifth subject, or a special project instead.

Do your lessons count towards the Abitur?

No. Abitur marks come from school only; what we offer is practice, with no grade or certificate attached.

When are classes in German time?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Slots after about 14:15 suit most school days.

How many learners are in a group?

Our batches hold fifteen to twenty, sorted by level; private lessons pair one learner with one mentor.

How is the fee paid?

We show the monthly fee in euro at the European Central Bank reference rate and charge it in US dollars; your bank converts it, so small differences are normal. The first lesson is free.

Are you connected to the ministry, the LAiV or a school?

No. We run independently; the education ministry, the Landesamt für innere Verwaltung, BWINF and the Land's schools have nothing to do with our classes.
