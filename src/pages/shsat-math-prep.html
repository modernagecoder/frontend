The test

What is on the SHSAT math section in 2026?

The math section has 50 items drawn from the New York State standards, with no calculator and no formula sheet. Students choose whether to start with math or English Language Arts, and the whole test runs 180 minutes.

The grade 7 point matters most

An eighth grader sitting the SHSAT in November is tested on New York State math standards through grade 7, not on the algebra they are learning that fall. In the guide’s words, the items "will require you to apply familiar Math skills to complex, multi-step problems."

So the preparation is less about new topics and more about using familiar ones in longer chains: a ratio feeding a percent, an equation built from a sentence, a shape whose area needs two formulas.

Formulas from memory

The 2027 Admissions guide is blunt that definitions and formulas "will NOT be given in the test booklet". It names perimeter, area, volume and surface area of 2D and 3D figures as formulas to know.

We keep a one-page formula list for each student and quiz it in the first five minutes of every class until recall is instant. Writing them out from memory once a week works better than rereading them.

New for fall 2026

How does the computer-adaptive SHSAT work?

From fall 2026 the test picks each next item from how the student has answered so far. A correct answer tends to bring a harder item, a wrong one an easier item, and every student still answers the same number of questions.

NYC Public Schools states it plainly: "Beginning in fall 2026, the SHSAT will move to a computer-adaptive test." Each section opens with an item or passage set of average difficulty. After every response the system re-estimates the student’s score and chooses what comes next. Because items carry an established difficulty on one shared scale, the final score depends on the pattern of right and wrong answers against item difficulty, not just a count of correct answers.

What the change means at the desk

The old habit of skipping a hard question and coming back to it no longer works in math. Each item must be answered, and once answered it is gone, so a student needs a decision rule for the item that is not yielding: how long to try, how to eliminate, and when to commit.

NYC Public Schools also warns that students "should not assume that rapidly guessing on remaining questions in order to finish is preferable to making the best attempt on each item." On an adaptive test, a run of rushed guesses at the end can pull the score estimate down.

Scores and field-test items

Each subject includes 10 unscored field-test items, which the city says have no impact on the adaptive nature of the exam. By our arithmetic that leaves 40 scored items per subject. The guide does not say which items are field tests, so we teach students to treat every item as one that counts.

Scaled scores for ELA and math are added to make a composite score, which NYC Public Schools uses to rank students for offers. That process, the city says, is unchanged from earlier years.

Practice on the real platform. The NYC SHSAT Portal hosts the Student Readiness Tool and four full-length practice tests for students testing in grade 8 in fall 2026, two with new content this year, plus mini practice tests for ninth graders. They are free, run in a browser and include explanations. We send every student to them and never copy their items into our lessons.

Dates and eligibility

When is the SHSAT in 2026, and who can take it?

The fall 2026 SHSAT runs on four days in November 2026, for students seeking a place in September 2027. Registration through MySchools closed on October 30.

Who is eligible

To register, a student must be a New York City resident and a current eighth grader or a first-time ninth grader who is not repeating the grade. Public, private and parochial school students all take the same test.

The guide sets two limits that shape planning: an eighth grader cannot test again until the following school year, and a ninth grader has no further chance after grade 9. So grade 8 and grade 9 are two attempts at most.

If your child is in grade 7 now

NYC Public Schools has not yet published dates for the fall 2027 test on the pages we read. Going by the 2026 pattern, registration opened in early October and testing ran in November, but treat that as a pattern, not a promise, and check the city’s page next autumn.

A grade 7 student has the most room: the grade 7 standards the test samples are the ones they are studying this year, so school math and SHSAT practice pull in the same direction.

How offers work

When registering, the student ranks the testing schools in order of preference. The city then works down the list of scores from the top: each student is offered their highest-ranked school that still has seats. In its own words, "The SHSAT is the only criteria used to admit students to the eight testing Specialized High Schools; no other criteria are used."

Two consequences follow. A student is never considered for a school left off their list, and the Specialized High Schools do not have waitlists.

What we will and will not say

No tutor can promise a place, because offers depend on every other student’s score and on seats. We teach the math and the test habits and report honestly on progress; we make no prediction of offers.

NYC Public Schools also runs the DREAM program, which prepares eligible public school students for the SHSAT, and Discovery, a summer program for some students who score below the cutoff. Ask your school counselor about both.

Pacing

How much time should each SHSAT math item get?

With 180 minutes for 100 items, an even split gives 90 minutes for the 50 math items, about 108 seconds each. The arithmetic is ours; the split is the student’s choice on the day.

Time per math item under three splits of the 180 minutes (our calculation from the published format)
Split, ELA then mathMath minutesAverage per math item
100 and 808096 seconds (4,800 / 50)
90 and 9090108 seconds (5,400 / 50)
80 and 100100120 seconds (6,000 / 50)
  1. Pick the split in practice, not on the day. Take one full timed practice test on the NYC portal with each order, math first and ELA first, and keep the order that leaves more time in the section that is weaker.
  2. Set a checkpoint. At the 90 second mark on any math item, the student either has a plan or switches to elimination. This replaces the old skip-and-return habit, which the adaptive format removes.
  3. Eliminate with the tool. The Answer Eliminator crosses out options. Removing two impossible choices often makes a hard item a fair guess.
  4. Commit and move. Because the item cannot be revisited, the student writes nothing they plan to check later; they check before pressing next.
  5. Watch the half-way mark. At item 25 the clock should show roughly half the math time used. More than that means speeding up on the easy steps, not rushing the hard ones.

Why rushing the end is a poor bet

On a fixed paper test, a quick guess on the last five questions cost nothing. On an adaptive test the city advises against it, because every response feeds the score estimate. We teach students to finish with steady, real attempts, even if one or two are educated guesses.

The practical fix is earlier: spending forty seconds less on each of the first ten items creates more than six minutes for the end.

Notepad or scrap paper

The digital notepad refreshes for each item, so working left on it disappears when the student advances. Scrap paper is provided and collected afterwards.

Many students find scrap paper quicker for math, because handwriting fractions and diagrams beats typing them. We practice both in class and let each student choose.

Worked examples

Three worked SHSAT-style math examples

These are our own problems, written for this page at the grade 7 level the test draws on. They are not SHSAT items and are not taken from any NYC guide or practice test.

Example 1: a ratio feeding a percent. In September a school store sold 120 notebooks, spiral and bound in the ratio 7 to 5. In October total sales rose by 15 percent while bound sales stayed the same. How many spiral notebooks were sold in October? Reasoning: 7 + 5 = 12 parts, so one part is 120 / 12 = 10, giving 70 spiral and 50 bound in September. October’s total is 120 x 1.15 = 138. Bound stays at 50, so spiral is 138 minus 50 = 88. The trap is applying 15 percent to the spiral count alone, which gives 80.5 and is not even a whole number.

Example 2: an equation from a sentence (fill in the blank). Maya is 4 times as old as her brother. In 6 years she will be twice as old as he is then. How old is Maya now? Let b be the brother’s age, so Maya is 4b. In 6 years: 4b + 6 = 2(b + 6), so 4b + 6 = 2b + 12, 2b = 6 and b = 3. Maya is 12. Check: in 6 years they are 18 and 9, and 18 is twice 9. A fill-in-the-blank item gives no choices to test, so the check is the student’s only safety net.

Example 3: area with no formula sheet. A garden is a 10 m by 6 m rectangle with a semicircle on one 6 m side. What is its total area, in terms of pi? The rectangle is 10 x 6 = 60 square meters. The semicircle has diameter 6, so radius 3, and area half of pi x 3 x 3 = 4.5 pi. The total is 60 + 4.5 pi square meters, about 74.1. The two formulas, rectangle and circle, must come from memory, which is why we drill them.

What the three examples share

Each one is two or three short steps chained together, which is what the NYC guide means by "complex, multi-step problems". None needs content beyond grade 7.

In every case the mistake that loses the item happens at the join between steps: the wrong base for a percent, a forgotten 6 on both sides, a diameter used as a radius.

How we turn them into lessons

The student first says the plan out loud before writing anything: "parts, then the new total, then subtract." Saying the plan is what makes the chain visible.

Then we change one number and solve again. If the method survives a new ratio or a new length, the student owns the idea rather than the answer.

Practice set

Ten original SHSAT math practice problems

Written by our teachers across the six content strands the city lists. No calculator. Aim for about 108 seconds each, the even-split pace from the table above.

  1. Paint is mixed blue to white in the ratio 5 to 3. How many liters of blue are needed to use 24 liters of white?
  2. A price rises by 20 percent and then falls by 25 percent. What is the overall percent change?
  3. Solve 3(2x minus 5) = 4x + 9.
  4. What is the greatest integer n for which negative 4n + 7 is greater than negative 13?
  5. Two fair number cubes are rolled. What is the probability that the sum is 8?
  6. A circle has circumference 10 pi centimeters. What is its area?
  7. The mean of six numbers is 14. One number is removed and the mean of the rest is 13. Which number was removed?
  8. Evaluate (negative 3/4) divided by (3/8), then subtract negative 2.
  9. Two angles are supplementary. The larger is 20 less than 4 times the smaller. What is the larger angle?
  10. On a map, 1 inch stands for 15 miles. How many miles do 3.5 inches represent?

Where students slip

Problem 2 catches many students, who answer "5 percent down" by subtracting 20 from 25. Problem 4 catches anyone who forgets that dividing an inequality by a negative number reverses it.

Problem 9 asks for the larger angle. Solving correctly and then reporting 40 is the most common way to lose an item that was really solved.

Then use the official tests

Once these feel comfortable, the next step is a full timed practice test on the NYC SHSAT Portal, in the real platform with the real tools. Afterwards, the summary report and the Explanations Appendix show which items were missed.

We go through those missed items in class by topic, not one by one, and set our own fresh problems on each weak topic.

Four signs a student is ready for SHSAT math

For parents

Steps 3 and 4 are rarely part of a school math class. They are what SHSAT practice adds, and they show up most clearly on a timed practice test.

Ask to see the summary report after each practice test. A list of missed strands is far more useful than a single raw number.

For students

Before pressing next, ask one question: did I answer what was asked? It saves more items than any trick.

Keep a notebook of every item you missed and the idea behind it. Reread it the week before the test.

Questions from NYC parents

SHSAT math: what families ask

How many math questions are on the SHSAT in 2026?

From fall 2026 the math section has 50 items, the same as ELA, within 180 minutes for the whole test. Ten items in each subject are unscored field-test items, according to NYC Public Schools.

Is the SHSAT computer-adaptive now?

Yes. NYC Public Schools says that beginning in fall 2026 the SHSAT is a computer-adaptive test, which chooses each item based on earlier answers. Every student still answers the same number of questions.

Can you go back to a question on the SHSAT math section?

No. In the math section a student must answer each item before moving on and cannot return to it after advancing. That is a change from the fall 2025 test, which had a bookmark tool.

Can you use a calculator on the SHSAT?

No. Calculators are not permitted, and a watch is allowed only if it is silent and is not a calculator. Scrap paper is provided for math working and collected at the end.

When is the SHSAT in 2026?

NYC Public Schools lists Wednesday, November 18, 2026 for eighth graders at public middle schools ending in grade 8, Saturday, November 14 or Sunday, November 15 for other eighth graders, and Saturday, November 21 for ninth graders. The test ticket confirms each student's date.

What math is on the SHSAT for eighth graders?

Grade 8 forms are based on New York State math standards through grade 7: ratios and proportional relationships, the number system and expressions, equations, inequalities, statistics and probability, and geometry. Grade 9 forms go through grade 8.

Is the SHSAT the only thing that counts for the specialized high schools?

For the eight testing schools, yes. NYC Public Schools says the SHSAT is the only criteria used and that no other criteria are used; LaGuardia High School admits by audition instead.

Do you use real SHSAT questions in class?

No. We write our own problems at the right level and send students to the free official practice tests on the NYC SHSAT Portal. We never copy items from the NYC guides or practice tests.

Can you guarantee an offer to Stuyvesant or Brooklyn Tech?

No one honestly can. Offers depend on every student's score and the seats at each school. We teach the math and the habits the adaptive test rewards, and we report progress plainly.

What days and times are SHSAT classes?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays.

Are you connected with NYC Public Schools?

No. Modern Age Coders is an independent online school with no connection to NYC Public Schools, the SHSAT or any specialized high school. Families register for the test through MySchools themselves.
