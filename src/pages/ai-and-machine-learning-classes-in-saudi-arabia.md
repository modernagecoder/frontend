## Project Mawsim: a capacity planning model for the Hajj

Every market we teach in gets a first project built on that country's own data, because a model your family can argue with is a model you remember. In Saudi Arabia that is the Hajj register. GASTAT publishes the pilgrim count each year from Ministry of Interior administrative records, so it is a register rather than a survey estimate. The published timeline runs from 1970 to 2025 and the 1447 AH bulletin adds 2026, giving 58 real annual observations.

The framing matters as much as the data. This is not a demand forecast. It is capacity and logistics planning, which is the question the Kingdom actually asks of these figures through the Pilgrim Experience Program, the Makkah Route Initiative and Nusuk: how many people are we building for. The framing is what decides which errors are expensive.

The features: year, Hijri year, the previous total and the total three years back, a quota_cut flag for 1434 to 1437 AH, a disruption flag for 2020 to 2022, the Gregorian month of Arafat derived by converting Dhul Hijjah 9, the days since the previous Hajj, and Saudi population that year from GASTAT, 35.3 million at the end of 2024.

Two columns are deliberately excluded. GASTAT publishes the external versus domestic split and the air, road and sea split only for recent years, so adding them would buy two years of detail at the cost of fifty five missing values. Knowing when not to add a feature is a lesson most courses never reach. Later projects come from the Saudi Open Data Portal, which held over 11,439 datasets from 289 organisations as of June 2025.

1. #### Type the series in by hand and plot it before anything else

  Fifty eight rows, entered from the GASTAT bulletins rather than downloaded. Three rows are keyed to straddling Gregorian years, because a Hijri year crosses two Gregorian ones, so the student chooses a key and writes the decision down. The 2020 row is marked disputed in the file itself. Then plot it: three regime breaks are visible to the naked eye before any model exists.
2. #### Install a Hijri converter and build the calendar features

  Using hijridate or hijri-converter, compute the Gregorian date of Dhul Hijjah 9 for every year, then derive the month of Arafat and the days since the previous Hajj, 354 or 355. The student produces two facts rather than being told them: Hajj moves about eleven days earlier each Gregorian year, and laps the seasons roughly every thirty three.
3. #### Set a baseline and write it on the board

  Predict that this year equals last year, then compute the mean absolute error of that rule across the series. That single number is now the bar. Every model built afterwards has to beat it or be thrown away, and plenty of models students are proud of do not.
4. #### Fit a line on year alone, watch it fail, then tell the model about the decisions

  A linear regression on year is wrong, and the residual plot shows exactly where: 2013 to 2016 and 2020 to 2022. Add the quota_cut and disruption dummy variables, refit, and watch the error collapse. A dummy variable is how you inform a model about a decision a government made. No amount of curve fitting will infer a policy from a curve.
5. #### Split by time, never at random

  Train on 1970 to 2022 and test on 2023, 2024, 2025 and 2026. Training has to include 2020 to 2022, otherwise the disruption dummy is always zero and the model never learns a coefficient for it. Then explain out loud why shuffling before splitting leaks the future into the past on a time series.
6. #### Run the counterfactual, and label it as an estimate

  Re-score 2013 to 2016 with quota_cut set to zero. The gap between those predictions and the recorded totals estimates how many pilgrim places were traded away to rebuild the Mataf, a project that took its capacity from about 50,000 to about 107,000 people per hour. The student then has to say plainly that this is a number produced by a model, not a measurement.
7. #### Swap in a stronger model, reject a hypothesis, and commit a forecast you cannot edit

  Replace the regression with HistGradientBoostingRegressor using the lag features, then run permutation importance. The month of Arafat should score near zero, because Hajj volume is set by per country quotas rather than by season. A hypothesis correctly rejected is a result, not a failure. Finish by forecasting 1449 AH, writing the number to a file and leaving it untouched until GASTAT publishes. A prediction you cannot edit afterwards is the only honest kind.

The distinction

## Using a model and building one are separate educations

We teach both, in that order, and we tell parents plainly which one holds its value. Prompting tracks a product, and products get rewritten. Modelling rests on mathematics and mathematics does not.

#### Using AI tools

- Write a prompt, read the answer, adjust the wording, repeat
- Learn this year's popular interface
- Success means an answer that looks plausible
- Transfers poorly when the tool changes, or the pricing does
- Worth roughly six weeks of anyone's time, and no more
- Covered here quickly and properly, including how to catch a confident mistake

#### Building AI

- Assemble and clean data, then account for every choice in it
- Choose a model and understand the assumption it is making on your behalf
- Train it, then report the error in units a non specialist can check
- Trace a failure back to the data, the features or the model
- Deploy it, then watch it degrade against inputs nobody planned for
- Founded on statistics, linear algebra and code, which do not date

This distinction lands harder in Saudi Arabia than almost anywhere, precisely because the state has already taught everyone to use AI. More than 1.1 million citizens have been certified through SAMAI, and six million school students meet AI in the ordinary timetable. That is a floor, and a floor is not a differentiator. The national strategy below rests on a sovereign Arabic language model, a national research centre and a regulator issuing binding guidance, and a generation trained only to prompt cannot staff any of it.

Why now, in Saudi Arabia

## The National Strategy for Data and AI, and what it means for a student

The National Strategy for Data and AI was published in October 2020 and is owned by SDAIA, the Saudi Data and Artificial Intelligence Authority, which was established by royal order in August 2019. Its headline targets for 2030 are specific enough to be checked: rank among the world's top 15 countries in AI, and train or host more than 20,000 data and AI specialists. The priority sectors named in the strategy are healthcare, energy, smart cities, logistics and financial services. Implementation was split into a capability building phase from 2020 to 2025 and a specialisation and commercialisation phase from 2025 to 2030, which means the Kingdom is now inside the second half.

On 10 March 2026 the Saudi Cabinet, chaired by Crown Prince and Prime Minister Mohammed bin Salman, approved the designation of 2026 as the Year of Artificial Intelligence, and SDAIA, under its president Dr Abdullah Alghamdi, issued the official guidelines and logo. SAMAI, the One Million Saudis in AI programme, launched at the Global AI Summit in Riyadh in September 2024, passed 424,000 enrolments in its first year and reached its full target in November 2025, having trained and certified more than 1.1 million citizens, 52 per cent of them female. SAMAI 2, aimed at the government workforce, followed with eleven partner ministries.

Read that as a parent and the shape of the opportunity changes. In most countries the bottleneck is awareness. Here awareness has been solved at national scale and the remaining bottleneck is depth. A million certificates is a million people who can hold a conversation about AI. It is not a million people who can open a dataset, notice that the split was done wrong, and fix it.

The building work points the same way. SDAIA's National Center for Artificial Intelligence produced ALLaM, the sovereign Arabic large language model, using more than 400 specialists and over 500 billion Arabic text units; it took the top spot on the Arabic MMLU benchmark and is served on IBM watsonx and the Azure AI model catalog. Arabic natural language processing is a scarce and genuinely local skill. Meanwhile AWS launched its Riyadh region in January 2026 with three availability zones, and Google Cloud runs a Dammam region with Aramco, so cloud machine learning work now runs inside the country.

The regulation points there too. The Personal Data Protection Law has been in force since 2023 with SDAIA as regulator, and SDAIA has issued ten regulatory documents on data and AI, including AI Ethics Principles and Generative AI guidelines built around transparency, accountability, privacy, human oversight and risk management. Human oversight of a system you cannot inspect is a signature, not oversight.

A child who is twelve today turns twenty two in 2036 and will be applying into exactly the institutions this strategy is now building, against a cohort that all sat the same school AI curriculum. That is the whole reason this page exists, and the reason we will not sell a prompt engineering course as an AI education.

School and university

## How this fits the board your child already sits

### The national AI curriculum

This is the single biggest fact about school in Saudi Arabia right now. From the 2025-26 academic year, AI is taught inside the ordinary public school day, developed jointly by the National Curriculum Center, the Ministry of Education, the Ministry of Communications and Information Technology and SDAIA. It reaches more than six million general education students, begins in elementary school from Grade 4 and continues through intermediate and secondary. It sits under the Vision 2030 Human Capability Development Program and is matched at university level by the Saudi Academic Framework for AI Qualifications. Teachers across a system of roughly 32,000 schools are being trained to use AI tools in lesson design.

We take that as the starting line rather than the finish. A school course introduces concepts to a full class on a fixed timetable. What it cannot do at that scale is sit with one child while they debug a data loading error at nine in the evening, or push them to state their model's error in units a parent could check.

### The exam boards expatriate families actually sit

CBSE is the largest expatriate school system in the Kingdom by student numbers, followed by Cambridge IGCSE and A Level, the IB Diploma and the American high school diploma. The established CBSE names include International Indian School Jeddah, founded in 1969 as the Embassy of India School, International Indian School Riyadh, founded on 9 October 1982 with twenty students and running separate buildings for boys and girls since 1995, and International Indian School Dammam, among others. Beyond the Indian system, American International School Riyadh and Reigate Grammar School Riyadh serve the American and British curricula. Saudi nationals sit the General Secondary Certificate, the Shahadat Al-Thanawiyya Al-Amma, and the Ministry of Education returned to a two-semester year from 1447 AH after several years of a three-term calendar.

### KFUPM and the AI+X mandate

King Fahd University of Petroleum and Minerals in Dhahran runs the most aggressive undergraduate AI mandate in the Gulf. Under AI+X, every undergraduate takes AI coursework regardless of major, built on four core courses: Introduction to Programming in Python and C, Introduction to Data Science, Introduction to Artificial Intelligence, and Business and Entrepreneurship. The stated target is to graduate 10,000 AI-skilled professionals by 2030. KFUPM was the first Saudi university to launch ChatGPT Edu with OpenAI, and at graduate level it runs a Master of Artificial Intelligence alongside a joint SDAIA and KFUPM MSc AI scholarship initiative.

The implication for a fifteen year old is obvious. A teenager who wants KFUPM should arrive already fluent in Python, because that first core course assumes nothing but rewards everything. Filling that gap is exactly why our Python for Teens track exists as a separate ladder rather than a footnote to the AI course.

### King Saud University, KAUST and the rest

King Saud University in Riyadh runs an MSc in Artificial Intelligence, asking for IELTS 4.5 or equivalent and a GAT quantitative section of at least 70, or a GRE quantitative score of at least 140. KAUST in Thuwal is graduate only: TOEFL iBT 81 or IELTS 6.5, the GRE encouraged but not required, and three letters of recommendation from professors or research advisers. Entry for 2026 is closed and applications for 2027 open in mid-August. KAUST Academy separately runs an AI Specialization programme for university students. Alfaisal University, Prince Sultan University, Imam Abdulrahman Bin Faisal University and Effat University all offer named AI or data science degrees.

### The two tests that decide places

Both are run by ETEC through the National Center for Assessment. Qudurat, the General Aptitude Test, pairs a verbal section with a quantitative one covering arithmetic, algebra, geometry and statistics; it is available in Arabic and English and can be retaken across grades 11 and 12. Tahsili is a curriculum achievement test sat at the end of Grade 12. Together with high school GPA those two typically carry 30 to 40 per cent of the admission decision. The quantitative half of Qudurat is the same mathematics we teach inside the machine learning, which is why our teens track never separates the two.

How the classes run

## Sunday to Thursday, built around a school day that starts before sunrise

Saudi Arabia observes Arabia Standard Time, UTC+3, with no daylight saving and a single time zone across the whole Kingdom. India is UTC+5:30, so Riyadh, Jeddah, Dammam and Madinah run 2 hours and 30 minutes behind our teaching base. Two local facts shape the rest. The weekend is Friday and Saturday, not Saturday and Sunday, so a batch pitched at the weekend without checking is half wrong here. And the school day starts brutally early: around 06:45 in winter, and in summer assembly at 06:15 with the first period at 06:30. Children are home in the early afternoon, but they woke around 05:15.

16:30 AST

Equivalent to 19:00 in India, Sunday to Thursday. Ages 6 to 12. After school, after a rest, and finished before Maghrib and the family meal. We do not move this age group later, because they are up again before 05:30.

19:30 AST

Equivalent to 22:00 in India, Sunday to Thursday. Teenagers, university students and working adults. The standard post-Maghrib study slot in the Gulf, and the busiest block we run for the Kingdom.

Saturday 10:00 AST

Equivalent to 12:30 in India. The weekend intensive, deliberately on Saturday and not Sunday, because Sunday is a school day here. Friday mornings and early afternoons stay clear for Jumu'ah.

### What a session is

Ninety minutes of live teaching from someone who picks up exactly where your child stopped. Screens are shared in both directions, so the teacher watches the student type and interrupts at the moment the mistake happens rather than marking it three days later. Batches are five to eight students, or one to one.

Between sessions a student gets a task, not a worksheet: something to build, break and bring back. Questions during the week go to the same teacher, not into a support queue. Nothing here is pre-recorded and nothing is auto-graded.

### The Saudi school year

The 1448 AH year runs on two semesters. Semester 1 begins on Sunday 23 August 2026, the mid-year break runs from 8 to 16 January 2027, and the Ramadan and Eid al-Fitr break falls from about 26 February to 13 March 2027, the longest interruption in the calendar. There is a further break in May 2027 for Eid al-Adha, and the academic year ends on 17 June 2027.

Admission is continuous, so there is no closed intake to wait for. During Ramadan we move the children's slot to about 21:30 after iftar and pause the Saturday intensive. Because Islamic dates shift roughly eleven days earlier each Gregorian year, we rebuild the timetable every year rather than reusing last year's.

Minimum ages on commercial AI products are set by the products themselves, several are 18 and up, and we publish what each one requires in our AI tools age guide and teach around it. Younger children work almost entirely in Python with pandas, matplotlib and scikit-learn, none of which carry an age gate, because at that stage the point is to build rather than to prompt. Arabic capability is a real local question: SDAIA's National Center for AI built ALLaM on more than 500 billion Arabic text units, it took the top place on the Arabic MMLU benchmark, and HUMAIN ships HUMAIN Chat on ALLaM 34B. Teaching is in English, and Arabic natural language processing is one of the scarcest skills in the Kingdom.
