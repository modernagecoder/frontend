What is the ISEB Common Pre-Test?

The ISEB Common Pre-Tests are a set of four online tests in English, mathematics, verbal reasoning and non-verbal reasoning, used by many independent senior schools as one part of admissions. ISEB says they are multiple choice and adaptive, take 2 hours 15 minutes in total, and can be sat in one day or across several. A child sits them once in an academic year, usually at their own school, and the results are shared with every senior school the family gives the Applicant ID to. The maths test covers the national curriculum up to the end of Year 5: number, calculation, fractions, decimals and percentages, measurement, geometry and statistics. No calculator is allowed; pen and paper are. For the 2026-27 session ISEB opened registration on 9 June 2026 and live testing from 1 September 2026. Modern Age Coders teaches the maths and the one-way technique live online, with practice we write ourselves.

The test

Four short online tests, sat once, shared with many schools. ISEB describes them in its 2026-27 pages and its test framework.

Shared, so sat once

The "common" in the name means one sitting serves many schools. Parents create an Applicant Profile and an Applicant ID, then give that ID to each senior school applied to. ISEB says this means pupils do not miss lessons for several separate tests.

Fees, ISEB says, are paid by the senior school, per result accessed. Individual schools set their own deadlines inside the session, so the date that matters is the earliest one among the schools on your list.

Two windows in print

ISEB's web pages give the 2026-27 testing window as 1 September 2026 to late May 2027. Its 2026-27 framework PDF says tests "can be taken between the beginning of October and the end of June". We print both because both are ISEB's; ask your child's school and the senior schools which applies.

If a child were somehow tested twice in a year, the framework says "the first set of results will stand." There is no practice run that counts.

Sources: ISEB Common Pre-Tests, information for families, information for schools, and the ISEB Common Pre-Tests Framework 2026-27, read 31 October 2026. Modern Age Coders is not connected with ISEB or with any senior school.

Adaptive and one-way

What does "adaptive" mean for a child sitting the maths test?

Each answer helps choose the next question, the number of questions varies, and there is no going back. Those three facts change preparation more than any topic list.

ISEB explains it directly: "The test system will assess the pupil's ability after each response and select the next question to have the most appropriate level of difficulty." It adds that "pupils may be presented with differing numbers of questions, and the system does not allow them to go back and change previous answers". It also says "it is highly unlikely that two pupils will see the same series of questions." So two children leaving the same room may have answered different questions, a different number of them, at different levels, and both had a fair test.

Skipping is not a tactic

On a paper 11 plus, leaving a hard question and returning is good technique. Here there is nothing to return to. A child needs to decide, with a sensible estimate behind the choice, and move on.

Harder can be good news

In an adaptive test, a run of harder questions usually follows correct answers. Children who expect every question to feel easy can panic at exactly the moment they are doing well. We tell them so in advance.

Checking comes first

Because a click is final, the check must happen before it: does the answer fit the estimate, the units, the question actually asked? Practising that two-second habit is the core of our Pre-Test lessons.

A simplified illustration of our own, not ISEB's algorithm: picture a ladder of question difficulty. A correct answer usually moves a child up a rung, a wrong one down, and the test stops when it is confident where the child stands. That is why ISEB can say the test "can be shorter while still giving an accurate score", and why a careless slip on an easy rung costs more time than it would on paper: the test has to climb back.

The maths

What maths is on the ISEB Pre-Test?

The national curriculum for England up to the end of Year 5, in six strands. ISEB describes some questions as plain arithmetic and others as problems that join more than one topic.

What ISEB tells families

ISEB's preparation leaflet says "The ISEB Maths test is based on the National Curriculum Programme of Study, up to the end of Year 5", and that children will be "allowed a pen and paper during the test to jot down any calculations or notes that they want to make. This working paper is not marked."

It names two common errors: pupils who "haven't read the question properly or have used the wrong operation". It recommends times tables as a route to confidence.

Attainment, not potential

ISEB separates the four tests into two kinds. English and mathematics measure attainment, "in line with topics and knowledge that pupils will be taught in class". Verbal and non-verbal reasoning are meant to show potential through unfamiliar question types.

That distinction is useful. Maths is the test where ordinary, steady teaching shows up most directly, because it measures what has been learned.

Worked examples, written by us

How should a child work a Pre-Test style maths question?

Three original questions in a one-at-a-time, choose-one style, at Year 5 level. None is an ISEB question; ISEB's own Test Walkthrough shows the real look of the platform.

1. Two prices, one total

An adult ticket costs three times a child ticket. Two adults and three children cost 27 tokens. What does an adult ticket cost? Draw a bar for one child ticket. An adult is three bars, so two adults are six bars, plus three child bars: nine bars make 27, so one bar is 3 and an adult ticket is 9. Check before choosing: 2 times 9 plus 3 times 3 is 27. No algebra, one picture.

2. Fractions of one amount

What is five sixths of 48 minus three eighths of 48? One sixth of 48 is 8, so five sixths is 40. One eighth is 6, so three eighths is 18. The answer is 22. The trap is subtracting the fractions first without a common denominator; working each part on paper takes twenty seconds and cannot go wrong that way.

3. Angles on a line

Three angles sit on a straight line. One is 47 degrees and the other two are equal. What is each of the equal angles? Angles on a line make 180 degrees, so the two equal angles make 133, which is odd, so each is 66.5 degrees. An estimate catches errors: each should be a little under 70. If the options include 66.5, choose it; if a child got 133, they forgot the halving.

Preparing well

How much preparation does the ISEB Pre-Test need?

Less than many families fear, by ISEB's own account. A sensible plan secures Year 5 maths, practises the one-way format briefly, and protects the child's calm.

  1. Read ISEB's families page and framework, and note each senior school's own deadline within the session.
  2. Check the Year 5 strands one at a time: place value, the four operations, fractions, decimals and percentages, measures, geometry and data.
  3. Fix gaps with short daily practice, times tables first, as ISEB's own leaflet suggests.
  4. Practise one-way answering: one question on screen, an estimate on paper, a check, then a choice that cannot be undone.
  5. Use ISEB's free Test Walkthrough to try the platform, the coloured overlays and the font size controls, so nothing on the day is new.
  6. Stop timed practice a week or so before the sitting, and keep the last days light.

What ISEB says about practice

From ISEB's preparation leaflet: "Additional test practice is not a requirement of the ISEB Pre-Tests; however, some parents, guardians and families may benefit from preparation work to reduce test anxiety."

ISEB endorses one commercial practice platform and warns that it "cannot guarantee the accuracy of third-party Common Pre-Tests practice resources that are not endorsed by ISEB". Our questions are third-party too, which is why we label them as ours and teach maths rather than imitate the test.

Access arrangements

Where a senior school agrees, ISEB says applicants with special educational needs can have extra time of 25 per cent on each test, requested by the parent at registration with evidence such as an educational psychologist's report.

The visible timer can be hidden if a countdown makes a child anxious. There is no extra time for English as an additional language, though a bilingual paper dictionary may be allowed.

Families aiming at 13 plus entry may meet Common Entrance after the Pre-Test; our Common Entrance maths page covers that stage. For grammar school tests, which work quite differently, see GL Assessment 11 plus maths and the national 11 plus maths page.

ISEB Pre-Test questions

What parents ask about the ISEB Pre-Tests

What is on the ISEB Pre-Test maths test?

Mathematics from the national curriculum for England up to the end of Year 5. ISEB's framework lists number and place value, calculations, fractions, decimals and percentages, measurement, geometry and statistics, with some arithmetic questions and some problems that mix topics.

How long is the ISEB Pre-Test?

ISEB says the four tests together take 2 hours 15 minutes. They can be sat on one day or split across several days, as the invigilation centre arranges.

Can my child go back and change an answer?

No. ISEB says the adaptive system does not allow pupils to go back and change previous answers. That is why we teach children to estimate and check before they choose.

Can a calculator be used?

No. ISEB says calculators must not be accessible. Pupils do have pen and paper for rough work, and ISEB says that working paper is not marked.

Why did my child get different questions from a friend?

Because the test is adaptive. ISEB says each answer helps choose the next question and that it is highly unlikely two pupils will see the same series of questions, or even the same number.

How many times can a child sit the Pre-Tests?

Once in an academic year. ISEB's framework adds that if a child were tested more than once in the same year, the first set of results would stand.

When can the 2026-27 Pre-Tests be taken?

ISEB's web pages give live testing from 1 September 2026 to late May 2027, after registration opened on 9 June 2026. Its framework PDF gives a window from the beginning of October to the end of June, so confirm the date with your child's school and each senior school.

How are ISEB Pre-Test results reported?

As a Standardised Age Score, adjusted for age and compared with a sample of pupils applying to independent schools, where the average is 100. Schools also see stanine and percentile rank.

Does my child need tutoring for the Pre-Tests?

ISEB says additional practice is not a requirement, though some families find preparation reduces anxiety. We teach Year 5 maths and the one-way habit; we do not claim to know the live questions.

When are your lessons?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Every lesson is set in UK time.
