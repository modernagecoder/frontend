## Twenty years of traffic counts on Mississauga roads, as an exponential function

The Region of Peel publishes counts from the traffic stations on its regional roads. We kept the stations inside Peel&rsquo;s own Mississauga boundary and asked a Grade 11 question: what constant yearly rate turns the 2004 count into the 2024 count? Every rate below is our calculation.

Peel&rsquo;s layer, Traffic Count Stations, holds Average Annual Daily Traffic for each station and direction. In Peel&rsquo;s words, AADT is the total volume of vehicles on a roadway for a 24-hour period, measured with road tubes that are installed on the road for at least one day but can be down for up to seven consecutive days. Peel also warns that years with a 0 or no value as their count reflect that no count was taken for that year, which matters the moment a student starts dividing.

**Our results from the Region of Peel Traffic Count Stations layer and Peel Municipal Boundary file, both downloaded 22 October 2026**

| What we measured | Our result |
|---|---|
| Stations in the Peel layer | 529 |
| Stations whose position falls inside the Mississauga polygon | 124 |
| Year columns in the layer | 1996 to 2019, then 2022 to 2024 (no 2020 or 2021 column) |
| Mississauga stations with a 2024 count in either direction | 59 |
| Direction series with counts in both 2004 and 2024 | 36 |
| Median constant yearly change across those 36 series | &minus;0.98 percent a year |
| Series with a positive yearly rate | 5 of 36 |
| Highest 2024 direction value inside Mississauga | 33,469, Airport Road 0.4 km north of Thamesgate, northbound |

The model behind the table is the one MCR3U teaches: a quantity that changes by the same factor each year follows A = A0 &times; bt. If a road carried A0 vehicles a day in 2004 and A in 2024, the yearly factor is b = (A &divide; A0)1/20, and the yearly rate is b &minus; 1. The program below applies that to every Mississauga series, after testing each station&rsquo;s position against the city boundary with a ray-casting check:

### Read the median, not the extremes

Our 36 rates ran from a fall of about 17 percent a year to a rise of about 3 percent a year. The median, close to a one percent fall, is a fairer single summary than either end.

### Respect the zeros

Because Peel records a missing count as 0 or blank, the program only keeps a series when both years hold a real number. Dividing by a zero that means &ldquo;not counted&rdquo; would invent an answer.

### Two directions, two stories

Each station reports NE and SW separately; on a north-south road NE means northbound. One direction can rise while the other falls, so we never average them into a single road figure.

## Erin Mills Parkway: one station, one rate, and a logarithm

Station 00100100 sits on Erin Mills Parkway, 0.1 km north of the QEW. Its northbound counts make a clean MHF4U problem with a twist that MCR3U students should learn to spot. The question and working are ours.

**Northbound AADT at station 00100100 in selected years, from the Region of Peel layer (downloaded 22 October 2026)**

| Year | 2004 | 2009 | 2011 | 2012 | 2016 | 2024 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Northbound count | 28,568 | 28,071 | 27,408 | 18,481 | 20,113 | 17,758 |

**Part (a).** Find the constant yearly factor that takes 28,568 in 2004 to 17,758 in 2024. Solve 28,568 &times; b20 = 17,758, so b20 = 17,758 &divide; 28,568 &asymp; 0.6216 and b &asymp; 0.62161/20 &asymp; 0.9765. The count fell by about 2.35 percent a year on average.

**Part (b).** At that rate, how many years does it take for the count to halve? Solve bt = 0.5 with logarithms: t = log 0.5 &divide; log 0.9765 &asymp; 29.2 years. This is the MHF4U move, using a logarithm to bring the unknown down from the exponent.

**Part (c), the twist.** Look at 2011 and 2012: 27,408 then 18,481, a drop of about 32.6 percent in a single year. From 2004 to 2011 the yearly rate was only about &minus;0.59 percent, and from 2012 to 2024 about &minus;0.33 percent. Almost all of the twenty-year decline happened in one step, so the answer to part (a) describes no actual year on this road. The Peel file does not say why the step happened, and a good answer says so rather than guessing.

**Try it yourself.** The southbound count at the same station was 8,452 in 2023 and 30,137 in 2024. Compute the one-year growth factor, then write two sentences on whether you would trust it in a model, and what you would check first.

Four questions in seventy-five minutes sounds gentle until a student meets the last part of each one, where a full written argument is expected. The habit that wins marks here is the same one in part (c) above: state what you assumed, show why the step follows, and stop when the evidence stops. ## Can a Mississauga student let AI do the algebra?

It can do the arithmetic. It cannot decide whether the model was the right one to use.

An AI tool will compute 0.6216 to the power 1/20 instantly and report a tidy yearly rate. It will not notice, unless asked, that the decline on Erin Mills Parkway happened in one year, or that a zero in Peel&rsquo;s file means &ldquo;not counted&rdquo;. Spotting those things is the mathematics.

So our teachers keep the reasoning with the student: predict the shape before graphing, write the equation by hand, then use technology to check. Marked school work stays the student&rsquo;s own work.

The Ontario courses ask students to model, reason and communicate. A tool can help with the first draft of a calculation; the judgement remains a human job. The longer argument is in [why learning to think still matters when software can calculate](/blog/is-coding-worth-learning-2026).
