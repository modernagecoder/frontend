The German side of the diploma

Does IB Computer Science count as a science for German universities?

No. In the KMK agreement on recognising the IB Diploma, the natural science has to be Biology, Chemistry or Physics. Computer Science appears in a different list: the subjects allowed for the sixth slot.

What the agreement asks for

The Kultusministerkonferenz first agreed how Germany recognises the IB Diploma on 10 March 1986; the version in force is dated 15 June 2023. It recognises the diploma as a university entrance qualification after at least twelve ascending school years of full-time schooling, if the six examined subjects follow a set pattern.

Among the six there must be two languages at level A or B, one natural science from Biology, Chemistry and Physics, a maths course (Analysis and Approaches or Applications and Interpretation) and one social science such as History, Geography, Economics, Psychology, Philosophy, Business and Management or Global Politics.

Where Computer Science fits

The sixth subject may be another of those, or one from a further list that names Computer Science, Design Technology, Digital Society, Sports Exercise and Health Science and others. So a pupil can take Computer Science, even at Higher Level, as long as a Biology, Chemistry or Physics course is also in the plan.

From the 2025 exams, the agreement adds that among the three Higher Level subjects at least one must be a language, maths or one of the three natural sciences. Every subject must be taken continuously over the two years of the Diploma Programme.

Our worked example

Can a short Python program check an IB subject plan against the KMK pattern?

Yes, and writing it is good Computer Science practice. A rulebook is a specification; a program that applies it is only trustworthy once it has been tested with plans that ought to fail.

From sentences to sets

Each list in the agreement becomes a Python set. A subject plan is a list of pairs: the subject and its level, SL or HL. The function check walks through the rules one by one and collects every problem it finds, instead of stopping at the first.

Collecting all the problems is a design choice. A pupil fixing a plan wants the full list in one go, not one complaint at a time.

Three test pupils

Pupil A has English, German, Maths AA, Physics, Economics and Computer Science. Pupil B has dropped Physics for Computer Science HL and put Higher Level on Economics and Visual Arts. Pupil C has typed a subject the lists do not contain.

All three pupils are invented by us. Our copy of the lists is shortened for the example and is not the full KMK text.

Pupil B is the case this page exists for. Three Higher Level subjects, one of them Computer Science, and still two problems: no listed natural science, and none of the HL subjects is a language, maths or a science. Pupil C shows why the third rule matters. Without it, a misspelt or unlisted subject would pass in silence, and a checker that never says no is not a checker at all.

  1. Run it. Copy the program, run it and confirm the three lines above before changing anything.
  2. Break it on purpose. Give Pupil A a third language instead of Economics. Predict the output first, then run it.
  3. Add a rule. The agreement wants every subject graded at least 4, with one 3 allowed if another subject at the same level or higher has at least a 5 and the total is at least 24. Add grades to each pair and write that rule.
  4. Test the edges. Write one plan that passes by exactly one point and one that fails by exactly one point. Edge cases are where real rule engines go wrong.

Points and grades

How do IB points turn into a German average grade?

The agreement gives a formula. It takes 42 points as the maximum and 24 as the minimum, and every total from 42 to 45 counts as the best German average, 1.0.

IB total points and the German average the formula gives (our calculation, two decimals)
IB pointsGerman average

381.67
332.5
293.17
244.0

Formula: KMK IB recognition agreement, section on the Durchschnittsnote: N = 1 + 3 (Pmax minus P) divided by (Pmax minus Pmin), with Pmax 42 and Pmin 24, bonus points included. The agreement says the average is worked out in the Land that assesses the certificate, under a separate 1991 KMK agreement on overall grades, which also governs how decimals are handled. We print two decimals; the official figure may be stated differently.

Two things are worth noticing as a programmer. The formula is a straight line between two fixed points, so every IB point is worth exactly one sixth of a German grade step: three grade steps spread over eighteen points. And the special case at the top, where 42 to 45 all become 1.0, has to be written as its own branch before the formula, otherwise 45 points would produce an average better than 1.0, which does not exist. Spotting that kind of boundary is exactly the habit the IB Computer Science exams reward.

IB schools in Germany

Where do pupils in Germany take the IB Diploma?

At international schools, and at a number of German state and private schools that run an IB stream alongside the Abitur.

What the association says

AGIS, the association of international schools in Germany, says all its member schools are authorised IB schools offering at least one of the four IB programmes. It describes them as bringing together children from up to 65 nations, with English as the common language of teaching.

AGIS also says the IB Diploma offered at its schools is, with the prescribed subject combinations, equivalent to the German Abitur. Those prescribed combinations are the pattern in the table above.

A list worth knowing about

The KMK agreement has an annex of schools whose graduates can gain general university access even with maths at Standard Level, because of a separate arrangement with the IB. In the annex dated 26 March 2026, the section for Germany numbers 59 schools.

For a pupil elsewhere, the agreement says maths at Standard Level, from the 2021 exams on, opens only subject-restricted access to degrees outside mathematics, the sciences and engineering. That matters for anyone planning to study computing.

Sources: AGIS, agis-schools.org, homepage and Wer wir sind, read 2 November 2026; KMK IB recognition agreement, footnote 3 and Anlage 1 (Stand 26.03.2026). We name no school as a source of our students and we are not connected with AGIS or any school.

Computer Science is taught at many but not all of these schools, and some offer it only at one level. Pupils sometimes come to us because their school cannot fit the subject into their timetable, sometimes because it is taught but the class moves faster than they can follow in their second or third language, and sometimes because they want the internal assessment project to be genuinely their own. Our classes are an addition to school, never a replacement: the school enters the pupil, marks the internal assessment and sends it for moderation.

What tutoring looks like

What does an IB Computer Science tutor actually do each week?

Mostly three things: code written live by the pupil, exam-style explanation in full sentences, and steady progress on the internal assessment.

Write, run, explain

Every class has the pupil at the keyboard. The mentor asks why a loop stops where it does before moving on.

Exam language

IB answers are marked on precise words. We practise turning a working idea into a short, exact written answer.

The project

For the internal assessment we coach the plan, the testing and the write-up. The code and the choices stay the pupil's own.

We do not write any part of an internal assessment, and we tell pupils plainly why: the IB requires the work to be their own, and a project a pupil cannot explain is worth less to them than a modest one they can defend line by line. The same goes for AI tools. Our under-18 pupils never use tools whose providers set 18 as the minimum age; the AI tools age guide lists each provider's own rule.

IB Computer Science tutoring in Germany: common questions

Are the classes in German?

No. Every class is live and in English only. AGIS says English is the common teaching language at its member schools, so the classes match the language of school. The mentor adjusts to how fast the pupil works and to their accent, and explains again in other words whenever needed.

Does Computer Science count as the science subject for German recognition?

No. The KMK agreement on the IB Diploma asks for Biology, Chemistry or Physics as the natural science. Computer Science is allowed as the sixth subject. Ask your IB coordinator how your own plan reads.

Can I take Computer Science at Higher Level?

The agreement does not forbid it. From the 2025 exams, at least one of the three Higher Level subjects has to be a language, maths or Biology, Chemistry or Physics, so Computer Science HL works next to such a subject.

How are IB points converted into a German grade?

The KMK agreement uses a formula with 42 points as the maximum and 24 as the minimum, and treats 42 to 45 points as 1.0. With it, 33 points give 2.5. The Land that assesses the certificate makes the official calculation.

Do you help with the internal assessment?

We coach it: choosing a problem, planning, testing and writing up. We never write code or text for it. The work has to be the pupil's own, and the school marks it.

Are you connected with the IB or my school?

No. We are an independent online school. We do not enter pupils for exams or award grades; the school and the IB do that.

Which programming language do you teach?

The language your school uses for the course. Many IB schools teach Python or Java, and we teach both. Ask the school which one its Computer Science teachers use.

When are classes in German time?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Most IB pupils come after school or on Saturday mornings.

How do I pay?

You see euro on this page, worked out from the ECB reference rate; the card payment itself is made in US dollars and converted by your bank, so a small difference on the statement is normal. No card is needed for the free first class.

Can my child start before the Diploma begins?

Yes, and it helps. A pupil who arrives in the Diploma already able to write and test small programs has more time for the theory and the project.
