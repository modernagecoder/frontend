> The passing marks for CBSE Class 10 Artificial Intelligence (417) are 33 out of 100: 33 percent of a subject split into a 50-mark theory paper and 50 marks of school practical. CBSE says its old rule of passing each component separately was relaxed in 2018, but its 2026-27 wording mentions both "separately" and "combined total" and has not been clarified for AI, so aim for at least 17 in each half. AI is the optional sixth subject: passed, it can replace one failed subject out of Science, Mathematics and Social Science. ## What are the passing marks for class 10 AI?

A student needs 33 percent of the subject total to pass CBSE Class 10 Artificial Intelligence, which is 33 marks out of 100. That figure is not special to AI: it is the same 33 percent line CBSE applies to every Class 10 subject.

- Part of the 417 subject | Maximum | 33 percent of it | Who marks it

- Theory paper, two hours (Part A employability skills 10, AI units 40) | 50 | 16.5 | CBSE examiners, from the board answer book

- Practical: file 15, practical exam 15, viva 5, project 10, project viva 5 | 50 | 16.5 | Your school, with an examiner, inside the CBSE window

- Whole subject | 100 | 33 | Added together on the marksheet

Read on 6 November 2026: CBSE's Secondary Curriculum for 2025-26 and for 2026-27 (cbseacademic.nic.in), the two-examination notification of 14 February 2026, the circular on the second examination of 17 March 2026, the post-result notice of 5 August 2026 and the 2027 date sheet issued on 6 November 2026 (cbse.gov.in). What follows reports those documents in plain words.

### What the 2025-26 curriculum says

It sets the general rule as 33 percent in each component of a subject, then adds that this condition was relaxed by a CBSE notification of 11 October 2018. News coverage of that 2018 decision reported the relaxation as theory and internal marks being added together for the pass. Since then, families and schools have read the Class 10 pass line as 33 percent of the combined subject total.

### What the 2026-27 curriculum says

The newer document, written for the revised scheme that begins with Class IX in 2026-27, describes success in a subject as a minimum of 33 percent "separately in external and internal assessments, calculated on the combined total". That one sentence uses both ideas. CBSE has not published a note explaining how it applies to a skill subject like AI, where the split is 50 and 50 rather than 80 and 20.

The honest summary: 33 of 100 is certain; whether each half must also reach 16.5 on its own is not settled in writing for 417. The practical answer is to be nowhere near either line. How the 100 marks are built, unit by unit, is on [the exam date and paper pattern page](/cbse-class-10-ai-exam-date-and-paper-pattern).

## AI theory passing marks out of 50, and the 417 practical passing marks

If the pass is read on the combined total, no single half has a minimum. If it is read separately, a student needs 16.5 of 50 in the theory paper and 16.5 of 50 in the practical. The table shows where the two readings agree and where they part.

- Theory (of 50) | Practical (of 50) | Total (of 100) | Combined reading | Separate reading

- 30 | 45 | 75 | Pass | Pass

- 17 | 17 | 34 | Pass | Pass, by half a mark each side

- 12 | 40 | 52 | Pass | Fail: theory below 16.5

- 20 | 13 | 33 | Pass, exactly on the line | Fail: practical below 16.5

- 16 | 16 | 32 | Fail | Fail

- 10 | 22 | 32 | Fail | Fail

### The row that worries parents

The third row is the real case: a strong practical carrying a weak theory paper. Under the combined reading that student passes comfortably. Under the separate reading the same marks are a fail. Because CBSE has not settled the point for 417 in writing, we plan every student as if the separate reading applied, which means a theory score well above 16.5, not a practical score large enough to cover a thin paper.

### Why the theory half is where the risk sits

- The practical is spread over months. A file of at least 15 programs, a project and two vivas reward steady work the student controls.

- The theory is one morning. Two hours, 15 of 21 questions answered, marked by an examiner who has never met the student.

- Section A is the safety net. 24 objective marks, with no negative marking, are enough on their own to clear 16.5 in theory.

- The vocabulary matters. Value points reward the official terms, so a right idea in loose words can still lose marks.

What the school checks in the practical half is set out on [CBSE Class 10 AI practical exam questions](/cbse-class-10-ai-practical-exam-questions), and how examiners award each theory mark is on [the CBSE Class 10 AI marking scheme page](/cbse-class-10-ai-marking-scheme).

## Minimum marks in a skill subject: how AI sits in the Class 10 result

In the CBSE Class 10 scheme, the five compulsory subjects are two languages, Mathematics, Science and Social Science. Artificial Intelligence is an optional skill subject taken as the sixth, and that position gives it a job beyond its own marks: it can stand in for a failed main subject.

### The replacement rule
If a student fails one of Science, Mathematics or Social Science and passes the skill subject offered as the sixth, CBSE replaces the failed subject with the skill subject and works out the Class 10 result on that basis.

### Only one subject
The rule speaks of failing "any one" of the three. A passed AI can cover one failed main subject, not two. With more than one fail, the result category comes from the board, and your school will explain it.

### Languages work differently
A failed language is replaced by a language taken as an extra subject, and either Hindi or English must remain a passed language. A skill subject is not the route for a failed language.

- Situation in the result | What the CBSE scheme does | What it means for AI

- Passes all five main subjects and AI | A normal pass | AI shows on the marksheet with its grade

- Fails Mathematics, passes AI with 33 or more | AI replaces Mathematics and the result is computed as a pass | Those AI marks just decided the result

- Fails Mathematics and fails AI | No passed skill subject to replace it | The student is placed in the category the board sets, usually with a chance in the May sitting

- Passed by replacement, wants the main subject back | The second examination lists improvement for students passed by replacement | The failed main subject can be sat again in May

One more fact from the same scheme: 417 cannot be taken together with Computer Applications (165) or Information Technology (402). If a school register shows the wrong code, no pass mark will help, so check it early. The choice between AI and its neighbours is compared on [AI 417 vs IT 402](/ai-417-vs-it-402-class-10).

## What happens if a student fails AI in Class 10?

A fail in AI on its own does not undo the five main subjects, because AI is the optional sixth. It does remove the safety net for a weak main subject, it stays on the marksheet, and the May sitting is not a guaranteed second try for AI. Here is what each route looks like, from CBSE's 2026 notices.

-
**Check the marks before anything else** After the 2026 results, CBSE offered three post-result steps for Class X: a scanned copy of the evaluated answer book, verification of marks and re-evaluation of whole questions, each online within fixed dates. A student a few marks short of the line should look at the theory answer book first: an untotalled part or a wrongly carried mark is exactly what verification exists for.

-
**Know that the practical cannot be redone** CBSE's circular on the second examination says there is no improvement of internal assessment, because it is a yearlong exercise. The 50 school marks in AI are fixed once uploaded. Only the theory paper can ever be sat again.

-
**Read the May rules carefully** Improvement in the second examination is allowed in up to three of Science, Mathematics, Social Science and the languages. AI is not on that list. Separately, students placed in compartment may sit under the compartment category. The May 2026 date sheet did carry a 417 paper, on 20 May, and CBSE published a marking scheme for it, but no public notice spells out which AI candidates sat it.

-
**Ask the school, in writing, before the LOC window** For the second examination, schools submit the list of candidates in phases, the later ones within days of the main result. That leaves little time to find out. If a fail in AI is a real risk, ask the school in January what category and route would apply, so nobody is guessing in April.

Everything about the May sitting, including who should and should not use it, is on [the CBSE second board exam and AI page](/cbse-class-10-ai-second-board-exam). CBSE has not said whether a 417 paper will appear in the May 2027 sitting, so plan as if February is the only attempt.

## Frequently asked questions

**What are the passing marks for class 10 AI?** The pass mark for CBSE Class 10 Artificial Intelligence (417) is 33 percent of the subject total, which is 33 marks out of 100. The subject is made of a 50-mark theory paper written in the board exam and 50 marks of practical assessed in school. The same 33 percent line applies to every Class 10 subject, so AI is not easier or harder to pass than any other on paper.

**What are the AI theory passing marks out of 50?** If the theory paper had to be passed on its own, the line would be 33 percent of 50, which is 16.5. CBSE notes that its separate-component condition was relaxed in 2018, but its 2026-27 curriculum wording mentions both separate and combined, and it has not clarified this for AI. The safe plan is to treat 17 in theory as a floor and aim far above it.

**What are the 417 practical passing marks?** The practical is worth 50 marks: 15 for the practical file, 15 for the practical exam, 5 for its viva, 10 for the project and 5 for the project viva. A third of 50 is 16.5. A student who finishes the file and project on time and rehearses both vivas is working for marks far above that, and our Wednesday classes keep both on schedule.

**Do you need to pass theory and practical separately in class 10 AI?** CBSE has not given a written answer specific to AI. Its 2025-26 curriculum says the rule of 33 percent in each component was relaxed by a 2018 notification, which is why the pass is commonly read on the combined total. Its 2026-27 document uses both separately and combined in one sentence. Until CBSE clarifies, plan to clear 16.5 in each half.

**Can AI replace a failed subject in CBSE Class 10?** Yes, within limits. CBSE's scheme says that if a student fails one of Science, Mathematics or Social Science and passes the skill subject taken as the sixth, the skill subject replaces the failed one and the result is computed accordingly. It covers one failed subject only, and it does not apply to a failed language.

**What happens if I fail AI in class 10 but pass all other subjects?** AI is the optional sixth subject, so the five compulsory subjects carry the pass. The AI result still appears on the marksheet, and a fail removes the safety net that a passed AI gives a weak main subject. AI is not on the list of subjects that can be improved in the May sitting, so check the exact route with your school.

**Can I improve my AI marks in the second board exam?** Not under the improvement category. CBSE's 2026 notices allow improvement in up to three of Science, Mathematics, Social Science and the languages. A 417 paper was held on 20 May 2026 for other categories, such as compartment, but CBSE has not said whether AI will be in the May 2027 sitting. Internal assessment can never be improved.

**Is there grace or re-evaluation if I miss the pass mark by a little?** After the 2026 results CBSE offered a scanned copy of the answer book, verification of marks and re-evaluation of full questions, all online within fixed dates. A student just below the line should start with the scanned copy of the theory answer book. We do not quote grace marks, because CBSE does not publish a fixed grace rule in its curriculum.

**When is the CBSE Class 10 AI exam in 2027?** CBSE released the 2027 date sheet on 6 November 2026. The Artificial Intelligence (417) paper is on Wednesday 24 February 2027, from 10:30 AM to 12:30 PM IST, on the same morning as Computer Applications and Information Technology. Class 10 main examinations run from 16 February to 9 March 2027.
