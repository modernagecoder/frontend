Informatik in Hamburg

Is Informatik compulsory at Hamburg schools?

Yes, since the 2025/26 school year, at both school types of the Sekundarstufe I. The detail is drawn from the 2026/27 edition of the Informatik-Monitor, a survey of Land ordinances by the Gesellschaft für Informatik; Modern Age Coders is independent of it and of the school authority.

Four hours since 2025/26

At Stadtteilschulen and Gymnasien, four weekly hours of Informatik have been compulsory since 2025/26. The schools decide how to place those four hours across grades 7 to 10, and they may add Informatik as a Wahlpflicht elective on top.

The monitor is careful about what it does not count: the subject Naturwissenschaften/Technik in grades 5 and 6 contains an Informatik strand, but too little of it to be treated as Informatik teaching.

Two school types, two routes

After four years of Grundschule, Hamburg pupils go to a Stadtteilschule or a Gymnasium; the KMK names the Stadtteilschule as Hamburg's school offering all three tracks. At the Gymnasium the Abitur comes after grade 12, with grade 10 as the Einführungsphase. At a Stadtteilschule with an Oberstufe it comes after grade 13.

At the Stadtteilschule, Informatik can be an elective in grade 11 for two to four hours in total.

The written Informatik Abitur at both levels is developed centrally and is the same across Hamburg; at present no practical computer work is allowed in it, and the permitted aids are a non-programmable, non-graphing calculator and a spelling dictionary.

That last rule matters for how a pupil prepares. A Hamburg Abitur candidate writes and reads programs on paper, without a compiler to point out the mistakes. We train exactly that: predicting what code does before running it, tracing variables by hand, and writing a class or an algorithm cleanly in one go.

The monitor's figures for Hamburg are higher than in most Länder: in 2024/25 about 22 percent of Sekundarstufe II pupils took Informatik and about 2 percent of Abitur candidates had it as an exam subject. In 2025/26 Hamburg had 610 qualified Informatik teachers, about 5 percent of all teachers.

Grade 9 and 10: ESA and MSA

Hamburg's school authority states that the ESA and the MSA include a final exam in German and maths with centrally set tasks. For a grade 10 pupil in spring 2027, that means one maths paper on Tuesday 13 April, a month after Hamburg's spring break ends.

Central tasks reward steady skills: fractions and percentages, linear functions, equations, geometry with Pythagoras, and reading data from charts. Gaps from grade 7 show up here, which is why we start by testing the basics.

Abitur maths: the two-part format

Across the Länder, the IQB's common pool describes a maths exam in two parts: Part A without any aids, Part B with them. At the raised level the paper lasts 300 minutes including choice time, at the basic level 255 minutes. Each Land's commission decides which pool tasks it uses and how many of its own it adds.

In our maths classes, Part A is the hard part to practise, because it needs fluent algebra and calculus with nothing but a pen.

Our lessons sit beside school, never in its place. Only Hamburg's exams and the school's marks count towards a certificate, which we neither give nor imitate, and our practice problems are written by us, never copied from published exam papers.

For pupils at German schools

A maths or coding lesson in English is a second subject practised for free. Mentors speak clearly, adjust speed and vocabulary, and know German school words such as Stadtteilschule, Profilfach or Ableitung when a pupil uses them.

Maths and code travel

A derivative, a for loop and a SQL query are written the same way in Hamburg, Chennai or Chicago. The notation on the screen does most of the work; the English around it is the easy part.

Our mentors are in India and teach the same way for every learner, wherever they are: a mentor who gets to know each pupil, the pupil doing the work on their own keyboard or paper, and every problem argued out before it is solved. We teach pupils to think, not to copy a worked answer or an AI tool's output.

A Hamburg project

How many bridges are in Hamburg's bridge register? A counting project

Hamburg is proud of its bridges, and the city's Landesbetrieb Straßen, Brücken und Gewässer publishes its register of them, free to reuse. Counting them looks like a one-line program. It is a lesson in deciding what "one bridge" means.

The data

The dataset is Brücken und sonstige Ingenieurbauwerke Hamburg, from the Transparenzportal under the Datenlizenz Deutschland Namensnennung 2.0. Its description says it lists the existing structures in the LSBG's area of responsibility: road bridges, foot bridges, tunnels, noise barriers, retaining walls and sign gantries. We downloaded the road and foot bridge files on 3 November 2026.

Each record has a name, an internal structure number, a sub-structure number, a construction type, a status and, for most, a year of construction.

The question

How many road and foot bridges are in the register? A pupil counts the records and has an answer in a minute. Then a second pupil notices that the Traute-Lafrenz-Brücke appears three times: east footway, middle section, west footway.

Is that three bridges or one? The register answers it for us: all three records share one internal structure number and differ only in the sub-structure number.

Version 1 counts parts. A bridge with separate decks, ramps or footways has one record per part. Version 2 collects the internal structure numbers in a set, so each bridge is counted once however many parts it has.

So the two files hold 1,497 records but 1,344 bridges: 128 road-bridge records and 25 foot-bridge records are extra parts of a bridge already counted. Neither number is "wrong". They answer different questions, and a report that does not say which one it gives has not finished its job.

Hamburg's LSBG bridge register, 3 November 2026 (our count)
FileRecordsDistinct bridges
Road bridges (strassenbruecken)881753
Foot bridges (fussgaengerbruecken)616591

Data: Landesbetrieb Straßen, Brücken und Gewässer, via Transparenzportal Hamburg, Brücken und sonstige Ingenieurbauwerke Hamburg, Datenlizenz Deutschland Namensnennung 2.0; we fetched both files on 3 November 2026 and every count in the table is ours. The register covers the LSBG's area of responsibility, so it is not a count of every bridge in the city. The 1802 record is named Franzosenbrücke.

Version 3 is where the maths joins in. For each bridge it keeps the earliest year among its parts, then takes the median. The median, 1976, is the year that splits the dated road bridges into two halves; the mean, 1969.5 in our own check, sits earlier because 29 of the dated road bridges were built before 1900 and drag it back. Ask a pupil to compute both and explain the gap, and you have a statistics lesson that no textbook had to invent.

  1. Without a computer, grades 6 and 7. A club list has a row per pupil per club. Ten pupils, fourteen rows. How do you find how many pupils there are? What would you need on every row?
  2. Grade 8 or 9, Python. Run Version 1. Find one bridge that appears more than once and write down which column tells its parts apart. Then write Version 2.
  3. Grade 10, maths and Python. Compute the mean year as well as the median in Version 3. Which is earlier, and why? Draw a rough sketch of the distribution.
  4. Oberstufe. Thirty-one road-bridge records have no year. Could leaving them out move the median? Argue for the largest and the smallest median they could produce.

One city, one Land

Destatis counts 1,862,565 people in Hamburg on 31 December 2024, the same figure for the city and the Land because they are one. Germany's second-largest city therefore has a single school authority and a single set of exam dates.

Online classes make the district irrelevant: a pupil in Harburg and a pupil in Wandsbek have the same mentors and the same lessons.

A spring to plan

Hamburg's spring break, 1 to 12 March 2027, comes earlier than in most Länder, and the central exams start a month later.

For grade 10 and Oberstufe pupils, we use the spring break for a full practice paper under time, then spend the four weeks to mid April on whatever that paper exposed.

Coding and maths classes in Hamburg: common questions

Are the classes in German?

No. All classes are live and in English only. Mentors adjust their pace and get used to each pupil's accent, and they understand German school terms such as Stadtteilschule or Profilfach when a pupil uses them.

Is Informatik compulsory in Hamburg?

Yes. The Informatik-Monitor 2026/27 of the Gesellschaft für Informatik states that four weekly hours of Informatik have been compulsory at Stadtteilschulen and Gymnasien since 2025/26. Each school decides how to place them in grades 7 to 10.

Is the Hamburg Informatik Abitur done on a computer?

No. According to the Informatik-Monitor, the written Informatik Abitur in Hamburg is set centrally at both levels and currently allows no practical computer work. The aids are a non-programmable, non-graphing calculator and a spelling dictionary.

When is the MSA maths exam in 2027?

Hamburg's school authority lists the central MSA maths exam on 13 April 2027 and the ESA maths exam on 12 April 2027, as of 9 September 2026. The German papers are on 9 and 8 April 2027.

When is the Abitur in Hamburg in 2027?

The school authority gives the regular written Abitur dates as 13 April to 5 May 2027, with resits from 25 to 31 May 2027. It does not list dates by subject in that document, so the school's own plan gives the exact day.

Can one mentor teach both coding and maths?

Often, yes, and some families prefer it. In a one-to-one plan we can split the two weekly classes between maths and coding. In group classes each course has its own mentor.

What time are the classes?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. India is 4 hours 30 minutes ahead of Hamburg in winter and 3 hours 30 minutes in summer.

Do you award grades or certificates for school?

No. We teach alongside school and do not award German grades or qualifications. Hamburg's central exams and the school's marks are the ones that count.

Where are the mentors?

In India, teaching live over video. That is why Hamburg afternoons and Sunday mornings suit the timetable. Your child keeps the same mentor from week to week.

How do payments work?

Monthly. Fees are shown in euro at the European Central Bank reference rate and charged in US dollars, so your bank converts the amount. The first class is free and needs no card.
