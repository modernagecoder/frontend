Why C++

Why learn C++ in Peru rather than another language?

Because it is fast, because it teaches you how a computer really handles memory, and because in Peru it is the language of the olympiad final. Few languages give a teenager that combination.

The olympiad route

The OPI final is C++ only. A secundaria pupil who wants to compete seriously needs C++ sooner or later, and the earlier the language feels natural, the more energy is left for the actual problems.

Speed and control

C++ compiles to fast machine code and lets the programmer decide how memory is used. That is why it runs game engines and other software where every millisecond counts.

Understanding, not magic

Learning C++ means learning what a vector, a reference or a copy really costs. Those ideas make every other language easier to understand, including Python and Java.

C++ is harder than Python at the start, and we are honest about that. For a ten year old or a teenager who has never coded, Python or Scratch can be a gentler first step; our clases de programación para adolescentes page explains the options for secundaria. For a determined teenager, a university student or an adult, starting directly with C++ works well with a patient mentor.

All the teaching is in English, live. That matters more for C++ than for most languages: compiler messages are long and dense, and the standard library is documented in English. Learners practise reading those messages aloud with their mentor until they stop being frightening.

The OPI

How does the Peruvian Informatics Olympiad work, and why is it C++?

The OPI is organised by UTEC and is free for every secundaria pupil in Peru. Its 2025 rules set two phases: an online round in C++ or Python, then an in-person final in C++ alone, from which Peru's teams for the Ibero-American and International Olympiads are chosen.

The same rules say both phases run on the omegaUp judge, problems come from topics in the official IOI syllabus, contestants must be at most 20 years old on 1 July 2025 and enrolled in a Peruvian school in 2025 or have finished in 2024, and generative AI is forbidden. The OII 2025 was set for 14 and 15 June 2025 and the IOI 2025 for 27 July to 3 August 2025 in Sucre, Bolivia.

Why the final is C++

The rules do not give a reason. Our reading is practical: C++ is fast and predictable, its standard library has the data structures contest problems need, and a final in a single language keeps the judging simple and fair.

The 2026 edition followed a similar calendar: UTEC's OPI page gave 18 March 2026 as the last day to register and 11 April 2026 for the Phase 2 final. The page did not yet list 2027 dates when we checked.

Peru at the IOI

The OPI rules note that Peru has taken part in the IOI since 2022. The IOI's own statistics for Peru record 5 years of participation and 13 contestants, with one bronze medal and one honourable mention so far.

That is a young record with room to grow, and every place on the IOI team runs through the C++ final. We are not connected with UTEC, the OPI or the IOI, and we never promise a qualification or a medal; our job is the language and the reasoning, practised on problems of our own.

What you learn

What does a C++ course cover, and which C++ should you learn?

Modern C++, in a fixed order. The current ISO standard is C++23, published as ISO/IEC 14882:2024, and a good course teaches the modern style from the start instead of the C-flavoured C++ of old textbooks.

  1. Compiling and running. What a compiler does, how to read its errors, and the edit, compile, run, test loop that every class repeats.
  2. Types and control flow. int, long long, double, bool, conditions and loops, with a first warning about values that grow too large for their type.
  3. Functions and references. Passing values, passing references, and why copying a large object by accident makes a program slow.
  4. The standard library. vector, string, map, set, sort and friends, which do most of the work in real programs and in contests.
  5. Memory and objects. Classes, constructors and destructors, ownership and smart pointers, and the bugs that come from getting them wrong.
  6. Algorithms. Searching, sorting, prefix sums, recursion, graphs and dynamic programming, with the cost of each worked out before it is coded.
  7. Contest practice or a project. Teenagers aiming at the OPI move to timed problem sets; others build a game, a simulation or a tool and explain it to their mentor in English.

A real lesson

What is a prefix sum, and why do olympiad problems need it?

A prefix sum is a running total kept in an array, so the sum of any stretch of the original array takes one subtraction instead of a loop. The IOI 2025 syllabus lists prefix sums among the array techniques it treats as fully included. Here is the lesson we use to introduce it, for learners of about 13 and over.

The problem

You have eight numbers, perhaps daily scores, and you will be asked many questions of the form "what is the total from day l to day r?". Version 1 answers each question by adding the numbers in the range again.

With eight numbers and three questions, that is fine: 13 additions. Contest problems use hundreds of thousands of numbers and questions, and then the same idea takes far too long.

The idea

Build an array p where p[i] is the total of the first i numbers, so p[0] is 0. Then the sum from l to r is p[r + 1] - p[l]: one subtraction, whatever the length of the range.

The array p has one more entry than the data, and getting that wrong is the classic bug. Write p[r] - p[l] by mistake and the one-element range from 3 to 3 comes out as 0.

Forty billion operations against four hundred thousand: that is the difference between a program that runs out of time and one that finishes almost instantly. The sizes of 200,000 numbers and 200,000 questions are our own example, not limits from any OPI problem. Notice too that we store the totals in long long; with large values, a running total can outgrow an int, and C++ will not warn you.

The habit this lesson builds is the one that matters in every contest: work out the cost before you write the loop, and test the smallest case, a range of one element, where off-by-one bugs live. The numbers and exercises are ours, written for our classes; no problem from the OPI or any other olympiad is reproduced here.

  1. By hand. Write out p for the eight numbers on paper. Check that p[8] equals the sum of the whole array.
  2. Break it on purpose. Change the formula to p[r] - p[l] and find every query in the program that now gives a wrong answer. Explain why in English.
  3. Two dimensions. Extend the idea to a grid, so the sum of any rectangle takes a few additions and subtractions. Draw the picture first.
  4. Count, then code. For a problem with a million numbers and a million questions, estimate both versions' operation counts before writing anything.

Thinking, not copying

Can AI tools help me learn C++ for the olympiad?

Not in the way you might hope. UTEC's 2025 rules for the OPI ban generative AI outright when solving problems and remove anyone caught using it, so the skill that counts is reasoning on your own, in C++, against the clock.

Cost first

Before coding, estimate how many operations a solution needs, as in the prefix-sum lesson. That single habit separates contest solutions that pass from those that time out.

Read the error

C++ compiler messages are long. We teach learners to find the first real error and fix it, instead of pasting the whole message into a chatbot.

Age rules apply

Anthropic restricts Claude to adults of 18 and over, and OpenAI asks for a parent's consent before anyone under 18 uses ChatGPT. Teenagers in our classes work without adult AI accounts; adults may use AI to question their code, not to produce it.

The standard is the same for a teenager in Juliaca, Iquitos or Lima: understand every line, test the edge cases yourself, and be able to explain your solution in plain English. That is what an olympiad final rewards, and it is just as valuable at university and at work.

Questions from Peru

C++ course for Peru: your questions answered

Are the classes in Spanish?

No. Our C++ classes are live online and taught only in English. Mentors speak clearly, match the learner's pace and accent, and show every step on screen. C++ error messages and documentation are in English, so learning in English helps from the very first compile.

Is C++ too hard as a first language?

It is harder than Python at first, but very learnable with a patient mentor. Teenagers and adults can start directly with C++. Children of 9 to 12 have a gentler C++ for Kids course, and some learners start with Python and switch later.

Why is the OPI final only in C++?

The OPI 2025 rules allow C++ or Python in the online first phase but only C++ in the in-person final. The rules do not explain why; in our view, C++ is fast and predictable, which suits timed problems with strict limits.

Can you prepare my child for the OPI?

We teach C++ and olympiad-style reasoning with original problems, and our competitive programming course practises under time limits. We are not connected with UTEC or the OPI, never reproduce their problems, and never promise a place or a medal.

Who can take part in the OPI?

Under the 2025 rules, pupils enrolled in a Peruvian school in 2025 or who finished school in 2024, aged at most 20 on 1 July 2025. UTEC says the competition is free for all secundaria pupils. Check UTEC's current rules before each edition.

When are classes in Peru time?

Any day, any time, flexible: choose a batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at the hour that suits your family. India is ten and a half hours ahead of Peru all year, so 15:00 in Peru is 01:30 the next day in India.

Which C++ standard do you teach?

Modern C++. The current ISO standard is C++23 (ISO/IEC 14882:2024). Beginners learn the modern style, with vectors, strings and the standard library from the start, and our examples compile under C++17 and later.

Do learners get a certificate?

Learners who pass a course's final assessment receive our own Modern Age Coders certificate of completion. It is not a Peruvian qualification and is not issued by any school, university or olympiad.

Can I use AI tools while learning?

Adult learners may ask an AI to review code they wrote. Claude has a minimum age of 18 and ChatGPT requires parental consent below 18, so our teen groups use no adult AI accounts. Generative AI is banned during the OPI, which is why solo reasoning is the skill we train.

What equipment is needed?

A laptop or desktop computer, a stable connection and ideally a headset with a microphone. Compilers are free, and the mentor helps with installation in the first class. Phones are not suitable for writing C++.

How are fees paid?

Monthly, by card, in US dollars. Your bank converts each payment into soles, so the amount may vary slightly from month to month. The first class is free and needs no card.
