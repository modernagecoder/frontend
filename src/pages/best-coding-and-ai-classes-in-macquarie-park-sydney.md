## Do as many people tap out as tap in?

Transport for NSW publishes monthly Opal entries and exits for every metro, train and light rail station. We took the 23 months from October 2024 to August 2026 for the two metro stations in Macquarie Park and added each column ourselves.

| Station | Entries | Exits | Entries minus exits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Macquarie Park Station | 2,597,760 | 2,566,270 | +31,490 |
| Macquarie University Station | 8,678,337 | 8,818,944 | -140,607 |
| Both stations together | not added | not added | -109,117 |

| Pattern | Macquarie Park Station | Macquarie University Station |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Months with more entries than exits | 21 of 23 | 0 of 23 |
| Largest monthly gap | +3,176 (August 2026) | -8,177 (March 2025) |
| Smallest monthly gap | +244 (September 2025) | -1,865 (December 2024) |
| Gap as a share of all entries | 1.21% | 1.62% |

### The law

In a closed network every trip that starts must end somewhere in it, so over a long period total entries and total exits across the network should balance.

### One station is not closed

A single station can run a surplus or a deficit for ever, because people can arrive by metro and leave by bus, on foot or by car, which the station counts never see.

### The running total

Adding each month's gap to the last gives a cumulative flow. For Macquarie University Station it falls every single month, a pattern too steady to be noise.

### What the gap does not tell us

The figures show where taps happen, not why. A steady deficit at one station is consistent with people arriving by metro and leaving another way, but the published table cannot say which way, and we do not guess at a cause.

Opal counts cover tapped trips only. Trips on other tickets, or by people who did not tap, are not in the table, so the balance check is a check on the recorded data, not on every person who used the stations.

How learners build it

## From two columns to a conservation check

The project uses only lists, a loop and one running total, which is why we teach it early.

| Step | What the code does | What it teaches |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Read | Keep the rows for one station and one direction | Filtering a published file without changing it |
| Pair | Match each month's entries with the same month's exits | Joining two columns on a shared key |
| Difference | Entries minus exits for every month | Signed numbers and what the sign means |
| Accumulate | Add each difference to a running total | State carried through a loop |
| Check | Compare the final total with the sum of the columns | Testing a result two independent ways |

The last step is the habit we care about. The running total after 23 months must equal total entries minus total exits; if the two disagree, the code has a bug, and learners find it before trusting any chart.

### Primary learners

### Secondary students

## AI can total a column in a second. Why should a Macquarie Park learner check it?

Because a total is only useful if you know what it should equal, and only a person who understands the data can say that.

Ask an AI assistant for the net flow at Macquarie University Station and it may well return the right number, -140,607. It will not, unless asked, point out that the figure cannot be a closed-network balance, that it leaves out untapped trips, or that a steady deficit says nothing about cause. Those judgements come from knowing how the data was made.

A learner who has written the running total and checked it against the column sums reads every AI answer the same way: what should this equal, and does it? That question catches mistakes in spreadsheets, dashboards and AI output alike.

The Macquarie Park learner who can explain a balance check is the one an AI tool cannot mislead with a confident number. The longer argument is in [why teenagers should still learn to code in 2026](/blog/is-coding-worth-learning-2026).

### What is flow conservation in data?

It is the idea that, in a closed system, everything that goes in must come out somewhere. For a transport network it means total entries should match total exits over a long period, so a mismatch points to trips that leave or join the network unseen.
