CEGEP computer science: programs and preparation

In Quebec, the road to a computer science degree or a programming job passes through CEGEP, the college level between secondary school and university. There are two main doors: a two-year pre-university program, Computer Science and Mathematics (200.C0), and a three-year technical program, Computer Science Technology (420.B0). This guide explains what CEGEP is from Québec.ca, then reads both programs from the ministry's own program documents: what they teach, how many hours they take and what they ask for at admission. The last part is ours: how a student in Secondary III, IV or V can prepare in programming, with a practice program written in the style the documents describe.

What CEGEP is

CEGEP in Québec.ca's own words

Families arriving from other provinces often have no equivalent in mind. Québec.ca puts it plainly: "College is the first level of education at which schooling is not compulsory in Québec. College studies follow secondary school and precede university education."

Why the R score matters to a computer science student

Québec.ca says universities use the R score to analyze applications, "particularly when a program has a quota, or as a minimum threshold for admission to certain programs". It is calculated from college results, so what counts for university entry is how the student does at CEGEP, not in Secondary V.

For a student aiming at a university computer science program after 200.C0, the first term of programming matters as much as any later one. Arriving already fluent in a language makes that first term calmer.

If the student is unsure

Québec.ca describes a "Springboard to a DCS" pathway for students who want to start college before choosing a program. It offers common courses and orientation activities, and a person may stay on it "for no more than three consecutive terms".

Some students try a programming course there and decide; others come to CEGEP already sure. Either way, programming practice in secondary school costs nothing but time.

The two programs

Computer Science and Mathematics (200.C0) and Computer Science Technology (420.B0)

Both programs lead to the same diploma, the Diploma of College Studies (DCS). They differ in length, in purpose and in what comes after. The figures below are from the ministry's program documents.

Choosing between them

200.C0 is a science program with computer science inside it: calculus, linear algebra, physics and chemistry share the timetable with four programming and discrete-math objectives. It suits a student who wants a university degree in computer science, mathematics or engineering.

420.B0 is a programming program from the first week, built to lead to work, with a placement in industry. It suits a student who wants to build software sooner. Québec.ca's overview adds, in its description of technical programs, that this training "may also provide access to university, provided that certain requirements related to the chosen university program are met".

What neither document names

Neither document names a programming language. They describe competencies, such as "Use programming languages" and "Develop programs to solve simple problems", and leave the language to each college. The 200.C0 document says it outright: "Each college determines the ways in which the educational aims, common competencies, goals, objectives and standards are implemented."

So no one can promise which language a given CEGEP will teach in first term. What a student can do is arrive fluent in one language and comfortable with the ideas, which carry over to any.

Sources: ministry program documents Computer Science and Mathematics (200.C0) and Computer Science Technology (420.B0), linked from Québec.ca's pre-university and technical program lists, read 22 October 2026.

What they teach

The programming competencies, objective by objective

The ministry documents set out each competency with its elements and performance criteria. These are the ones about programming and the mathematics behind it, shortened. They are the clearest description anywhere of what a CEGEP computer science student is expected to do.

The pattern across both programs

Read the criteria together and one habit keeps returning: test what you build. 020W asks for "a complete set of tests"; 00Q2 ends with implementing "the functional test plan". Neither treats a program that merely runs as finished.

The other recurring idea is analysis before code: identifying the data, the results wanted and the processing required, then designing the algorithm, then translating it. That is the order we teach in, at every age.

The technical program's options

Beyond its 13 compulsory competencies, 420.B0 lists 15 from which each college picks 6 or 7. They range from networks and servers to software: "Develop transactional Web applications", "Develop gaming or simulation applications" and "Develop applications for connected objects", among others.

Two CEGEPs offering 420.B0 can therefore have quite different second and third years. Comparing those choices is the strongest reason to read each college's own program page before applying.

A practice program

Practice in the style of 020W and 00Q3: number bases, Euclid and a test plan

A program we wrote for this page. It touches three of the objectives above: converting numbers between bases (00Q3), Euclid's algorithm (020V), and validating code with a complete set of tests (020W). It is our practice material, not taken from any college course.

  1. Analyze the problem. Inputs: a whole number and a base from 2 to 16. Output: a string of digits. The awkward case is zero, which the repeated-division loop would turn into an empty string, so it gets its own line.
  2. Design the algorithm. Divide by the base, keep the remainder as the next digit from the right, repeat with the quotient until it reaches 0. For Euclid: replace (a, b) with (b, a mod b) until b is 0; a is the answer.
  3. Write the test plan first. One ordinary case per feature, plus the edge cases: zero, a one-digit result, and two numbers with no common factor.
  4. Translate and debug. Write the code, run the plan, and fix the first failing case before the next. A test that has never failed has not yet proved anything.
  5. Extend it yourself. Add a test for base 16 with 4096 (expected 1000), then write from_base, the reverse conversion, and test that from_base(to_base(n, b), b) gives n back for many values of n.

Admission

Applying to CEGEP: eligibility, the regional services and the dates

Québec.ca's admission page sets out who is eligible for a DCS program, how to apply and when. Program conditions such as the math above sit on top of these general rules.

Who is eligible

For a program leading to a DCS, a student needs a Secondary School Diploma (SSD), or a Diploma of Vocational Studies with certain secondary subjects passed, among them the Secondary IV mathematics program, or instruction or experience the college accepts.

Then the program's own conditions apply. Québec.ca: "Some college programs also have specific requirements for the program you choose." For 200.C0 those include Secondary V math in TS or Science; for 420.B0, Secondary IV TS or Science, or Secondary V CST.

How and when to apply

Applications to most CEGEPs go through a regional admission service; in the Montreal area that is the "Service régional d'admission du Montréal métropolitain (SRAM)". Québec.ca lists a few CEGEPs that take applications directly instead, so check each college's instructions.

Québec.ca gives the deadlines as "Fall term: March 1" and "Winter term: November 1". In our reading, a student entering in the fall of 2027 applies by 1 March 2027; the page itself gives no year.

  1. Secondary IV: choose a math option that keeps the program open. For 200.C0, that means TS or Science, continued into Secondary V.
  2. Autumn of Secondary V: read each college's program page for 200.C0 or 420.B0, including its optional competencies, and visit if you can.
  3. By 1 March of Secondary V: apply through the regional admission service, or directly to a college that asks for that.
  4. Spring of Secondary V: finish the required courses. The math, chemistry and physics conditions are checked against final results.
  5. Summer before CEGEP: keep programming. The first term starts in August, and its first programming course rewards a student who arrives fluent.

Source: Québec.ca, Admission to CEGEP, read 22 October 2026. Deadlines and procedures can change; the regional service and the college have the last word.

What we do not do

We do not teach students to paste AI-generated code. The ministry's criteria ask for analysis, design, debugging and validation, and a student who skips them with an AI tool arrives at CEGEP unable to do any of the four. ChatGPT needs a parent's permission for anyone under 18.

We use AI the other way round: a student may ask an AI tool to explain an error message, then must fix the bug and explain the fix themselves.

Alongside the secondary school courses

Programming does not replace the Secondary V math, chemistry and physics that 200.C0 asks for. A student short on Secondary V math is better served by math help first; our online math tutoring for Canada covers it.

Questions from Quebec families

CEGEP computer science: common questions

What is CEGEP?

It is the college level in Quebec. Québec.ca describes college as the first level of education at which schooling is not compulsory, after secondary school and before university. There are 48 CEGEPs, which are public colleges, five of them English-language, plus about 70 private colleges.

Which CEGEP programs teach computer science?

The two main ones are the pre-university program Computer Science and Mathematics (200.C0), two years, and the technical program Computer Science Technology (420.B0), three years with at least one workplace practicum. Both lead to a Diploma of College Studies.

What math do I need for CEGEP computer science?

For 200.C0, the ministry document lists Secondary V math in the Technical and Scientific option or the Science option, with Secondary V Chemistry and Physics. For 420.B0, it accepts Secondary IV TS or Science, or Secondary V CST.

What programming language do CEGEPs teach?

The ministry program documents do not name one. They describe competencies such as using programming languages and developing programs, and each college decides how to implement them. A student who is fluent in one language, such as Python, adapts quickly to another.

When is the CEGEP application deadline?

Québec.ca gives March 1 for the fall term and November 1 for the winter term. A student starting in the fall of 2027 would apply by 1 March 2027. Most applications go through a regional admission service, such as SRAM in the Montreal area.

What is the R score?

It is the cote de rendement au collégial, a measure of college performance. Québec.ca says universities use it to analyze applications, particularly for programs with a quota or a minimum threshold. It is based on CEGEP results.

How should a secondary student prepare for CEGEP computer science?

Learn one language well, practise designing algorithms before coding, write test plans, then move on to data structures and objects. Keep the math option that the program asks for. Our plan on this page maps each step to the ministry's objectives.

Is 200.C0 or 420.B0 better for me?

Neither is better in general. 200.C0 prepares for university programs in computer science, mathematics and engineering; 420.B0 prepares for work, with a practicum, and Québec.ca notes that technical training may also open the way to university when a university program's requirements are met.

Are your classes a CEGEP or connected with one?

No. We are an online school that teaches programming and math alongside secondary school. We are not a college, we grant no credits and we have no connection with any CEGEP or with the Ministère de l'Éducation.

When are your classes for Quebec students?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Classes are in English, in Eastern time.
