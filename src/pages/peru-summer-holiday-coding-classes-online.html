The school calendar

When is the summer school holiday in Peru?

Roughly from the last week of December to early March. For 2026, MINEDU's technical norm RM 501-2025 put the last teaching day on 18 December 2026, and the 2026 year had started with teaching on 16 March 2026, so the long break sits between those two points of the calendar.

The norm sets 36 teaching weeks and 8 management weeks for 2026, and lets regional bodies (DRE and UGEL) set a different distribution for climate or social reasons, so a school outside Lima may differ.

What this means for January to March

Pupils stop on 18 December 2026; the last two weeks of December are for school staff. The 2026 year opened with two staff weeks from 2 March and pupils back on 16 March 2026, so in that year the summer break for children ran from Christmas to mid-March. Families use that stretch for the vacaciones útiles, the summer programmes run by councils, schools and private academies.

Each school still sets its own calendar inside the norm: the RM lets schools organise the year in bimestres, trimestres or another pattern, and private schools often start or finish a few days apart. Check the dates your own school sends home.

What we do not know yet: 2027

As of 8 November 2026 we found no MINEDU norm for the 2027 school year on gob.pe, so nobody can yet say on what date classes will restart in March 2027. MINEDU has started preparing that year: on 14 October 2026 it began dispatching 13.3 million units of school materials for the 2027 school year.

Because the restart date is still open, we do not print a fixed length for a summer programme. Your child simply begins our normal classes when the break begins and keeps going as long as suits the family.

The scale is large. In the Censos Nacionales 2025, INEI counted 818,096 children of 6 to 11 and 867,253 young people of 12 to 17 living in Lima Metropolitana (población censada, Cuadro N.° 3). Almost all of them are at school from March to December and free from late December, which is why every district council in the capital runs some kind of summer offer, and why searches for vacaciones útiles climb in December.

How our summer works

Is this a summer camp or regular classes?

Regular classes, started in the summer. We do not run a fixed summer camp for Peru with its own dates. Your child joins the same live course any of our students would join, at the moment the school break begins, and that is a strength rather than a compromise.

Why not a short camp

A two-week camp is long enough to make one game and too short to learn how to make the next one alone. Children forget a programming idea quickly if they meet it once; they keep it when they use it again a week later in a new project.

A summer start avoids that problem. Your child has a calm stretch with no homework to build habits, and the course simply continues, at a lighter pace if you wish, when school returns in March.

What stays the same all year

The same mentor teaches your child from the first summer class, knows how they think, and sends you a short note on what was practised and what comes next.

You choose a group of fifteen to twenty learners of a similar age or one-to-one classes, and you choose the hours. You can pause or stop at the end of any month.

Nothing here is connected with MINEDU, a UGEL, a council or any school. Our classes run alongside school and do not give Peruvian grades or credits. When a child finishes a course, we give our own certificate of completion.

A holiday plan of our own

What could a summer of coding look like for my child?

Here is the plan our mentors suggest for a first summer, in four stages. It is our own suggestion, not a fixed programme: a stage usually takes a couple of weeks at two classes a week, and the mentor stretches or shortens it to match your child.

Our suggested first-summer plan by age (ours; projects are examples)
StageAges 6 to 8Ages 9 to 12Ages 13 to 17
1. First stepsMake a cat walk, jump and talk in ScratchScratch game with a score, then a first line of PythonPython basics: variables, input, if and else
2. Repeat and decideAn animated story with loopsA Python quiz that keeps scoreLoops and lists: a playlist or a reading log
3. DataA catch game that gets fasterA drawing program with the turtleDates and files: the calendar lesson below
4. Show itPresent the game to the familyA Roblox obstacle course or small websiteA web page or AI helper, explained line by line

This plan is ours, written by our mentors for this page. Every child is placed after the free first class, so a 10-year-old who already knows Scratch may start at stage 2.

  1. Book the free class before the break. December is a good time: your child meets a mentor, solves a small puzzle and you agree a starting stage and an hour.
  2. Fix two hours a week. Two classes on fixed days give the summer a rhythm, even when the rest of the day is free. Pick hours that do not clash with the beach, council workshops or family trips.
  3. Add short practice. Twenty minutes between classes, on a project the child chose, does more than one long session on Sunday.
  4. Keep a project log. A notebook with the date, what was built and one new English word. By March it becomes a record your child is proud of.
  5. Decide in late February. Continue into the school year at the same pace, slow down to one class a week, or pause. Any of the three is fine.

Taught in English

Are the summer classes taught in Spanish?

No. Every class is live, online and in English only. Our mentors are based in India; they speak slowly and clearly, repeat in other words, show every step on the shared screen, and adapt their pace and accent to your child. A summer is a very good time to start this way.

Why the summer helps

During the school year a child switches between Spanish lessons, homework and family life all day. In January the mind is fresher. The first two or three weeks of getting used to a mentor's accent happen without any school pressure, and by March the English of the lesson feels ordinary.

The words of code are already English: print, if, while, return. Hearing them spoken, in a sentence, is what turns them from symbols into meaning.

What EF's index says about Peru

In the EF English Proficiency Index 2025, Peru scored 519 ("Moderate", 52nd of 123 countries) and Lima 555, the one Peruvian city in EF's "High" band, which starts at 550. Peru's speaking score, 480, is its weakest skill.

EF tests adults, so these figures describe parents more than children. They still show the gap a spoken class works on: Peruvians read English better than they speak it, and a coding lesson asks a child to speak, explain and ask questions every few minutes.

Your child does not need to arrive speaking English. Young children start with blocks, pictures and one new word per class; older children pick up the technical vocabulary as they type. You can always write to us on WhatsApp in Spanish if that is easier for you; the lessons themselves stay in English.

A real lesson

What does a summer coding lesson look like?

This is a lesson our mentors use with teenagers of 13 and over in the first summer stage, and with keen 11 and 12 year olds. The code is ours; the data are the four teaching blocks of the 2026 school calendar from the table above. The question is simple: does the calendar really give nine weeks per block?

  1. Find the fencepost. end - start counts the gaps between days, not the days. From Monday 16 March to Friday 15 May there are 60 gaps but 61 days. It is the old puzzle of fence posts and fence panels.
  2. Find the second mistake. Even 61 days divided by 7 is 8.7, not 9. A school week is Monday to Friday; the last week of the block ends on a Friday, so dividing calendar days by seven will never land on a whole number. The program is measuring the wrong thing.
  3. Count what the table counts. The norm counts school weeks. So the new version walks through every day and counts the Mondays: each Monday inside the block starts one school week.

The lesson is not really about calendars. It is about the habit we teach in every class: predict, run, notice the surprise, and find out whether the data or the program is wrong before changing anything. When MINEDU publishes the 2027 calendar, the student only needs to change four lines of data to check it the same way. An AI tool would have given a number; the student now knows why the first number was wrong.

At home

What does my child need at home for online summer classes?

A computer with a keyboard, a camera and a microphone, a stable connection and a quiet corner. A phone is fine for watching but not for typing code.

What INEI says about homes in 2026

INEI's survey of information technology in households for the second quarter of 2026 found that 84.7% of households in Lima Metropolitana, which in INEI's figures includes Callao, had internet at home. Across the whole country, 36.4% of households had at least one computer.

The same survey found that internet use was lowest among children of 6 to 11 (68.2%) and much higher among 12 to 18 year olds (92.5%). For many younger children, a summer coding class is their first serious use of a computer, which is another reason we start them with blocks.

A simple checklist

A laptop or desktop, even a shared family one. A headset with a microphone helps a child hear the mentor's English clearly.

The same table and chair for every class, away from the television, and the class link opened five minutes early. Scratch runs in the browser; for Python the mentor helps install free software in the first lesson.

If your home has only one computer, choose class hours when nobody else needs it; that is one of the reasons we let families choose the hour instead of fixing a timetable.

Peru time

What time are the classes in Peru?

Whatever hour you choose. Our mentors are in India, and because neither country changes its clocks, the gap between Peru time and India time is the same in January as in July.

Peru keeps UTC minus 5 all year and has not used summer time since 1994 (tz database, zone America/Lima; INACAL's official clock read as UTC minus 5 on 8 November 2026). India keeps UTC plus 5:30 all year, so India is 10 hours 30 minutes ahead of Peru every day. INEI's Compendio Estadístico 2026 gives the same gap: 12:00 noon in Lima is 10:30 p.m. in Delhi.

In the summer many families pick a morning hour, while the day is still cool and before the beach or a council workshop, or a late afternoon slot. When you book the free class you pick the hour on your own clock, in Peru time, and the mentor sees it in India time.

After the break

What happens when school starts again in March?

You decide. Many families keep going at the same two classes a week; some slow to one; a few pause and return in the July break. The course and the mentor stay the same.

Continuing alongside school

Peruvian secondary school has no computing subject: the national curriculum, the CNEB, treats digital skills as a transversal competency shared between other areas. A child who keeps coding through the school year therefore builds something school does not teach directly.

If hours change when school starts, or when a school moves between morning and afternoon shifts, you simply move the class hour.

Pausing without losing the summer

If the school year is too full, pause at the end of a month. Before you stop, the mentor gives your child one small project to keep alive, so the summer's work is not forgotten.

Children who started in a region outside Lima follow exactly the same plan; our page on coding classes in Arequipa shows how families there fit classes around school.

Progress

How will I know the summer worked?

We use five levels of our own. By March, children in their first summer have usually moved up one or two.

Five coding levels we use with families in Peru (ours)

1. Sequence6 to 8put steps in order so a character reaches its goal
2. Repeat and choose7 to 10use loops and if blocks, and say in English what each one does
3. Type it9 to 12write a short Python program and predict its output before running it
4. Data11 to 15store data in lists and dates, and check a result against a source
5. Own project13 to 17plan, build and present a game, web page or small AI tool

For parents

Ask your child to show you one thing a week and explain it. If they can explain it, even mixing Spanish and English, they understood it.

The jump from level 2 to level 3, from blocks to typing, is the hardest. It is normal for it to take a few weeks of the summer.

For learners

Say what you expect before you press run. Then check whether you were right.

When the answer looks wrong, ask first whether the data or the program is wrong.

Summer holiday coding classes for Peru: questions and answers

Are the classes in Spanish?

No. Every class is live, online and taught in English only. Our mentors in India speak clearly, repeat in simpler words and adapt their pace and accent to each child. Many families choose us for exactly that reason: a summer of coding is also a summer of spoken English practice.

When is the summer school holiday in Peru?

For 2026, RM 501-2025-MINEDU set the last teaching day as 18 December 2026, and the 2026 year began with pupils on 16 March 2026. The 2027 calendar had not been published when we checked on 8 November 2026, so the exact March 2027 restart is not known yet.

Is this a summer camp with fixed dates?

No. We do not run a separate summer camp for Peru. Your child starts our normal live course when the break begins, in a group or one to one, and can continue into the school year, slow down or stop at the end of any month.

What age can my child start?

From 6. Children of 6 to 8 start with Scratch blocks and stories; from about 9 they can begin typed Python; teenagers of 13 to 17 can start with Python, web or AI projects straight away. The free first class places each child.

What time are the classes?

Any day, any time, flexible: choose a batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at the hour that suits your family. India is 10 hours 30 minutes ahead of Peru all year, so 11:00 a.m. in Peru is 9:30 p.m. in India.

Does my child need to speak English already?

No. Understanding simple instructions is enough to begin. The first two or three weeks are for getting used to the mentor's accent, and a summer start gives that time without school pressure.

Can my child do a council summer workshop as well?

Yes. A live class takes about an hour twice a week at the hour you choose, so it fits easily beside a morning sport or arts workshop. We are not connected with any district council.

Do you teach robotics?

We teach coding, not hardware, and we never send a robot kit. Robots in school workshops are programmed with the same loops and conditions children learn with us, so the two fit together well.

Will the classes count for school?

No. We are not connected with MINEDU, any UGEL or any school, and we do not give Peruvian grades. When a child finishes a course we give our own certificate of completion.

How do we pay?

By card, in US dollars, monthly. Your bank converts the payment into soles at its rate. The first class is free and needs no card.
