## How far are Apurímac's secondary schools from Abancay or Andahuaylas?

Measured as a straight line on the globe, about a third of active secundaria entries in the regional school list are more than 50 km from both towns. Getting there needs a formula with sines and cosines, and one small unit mistake turns the answer into nonsense.

### The list

The Gobierno Regional de Apurímac publishes "Instituciones Educativas de Apurímac" on datosabiertos.gob.pe and describes it as the list of the region's educational institutions "elaborado a partir del padrón del Ministerio de Educación". It was released on 30 October 2026 under the Open Data Commons Open Database License (ODbL), which allows reuse, commercial included, with credit, and asks that adapted databases are shared on the same terms.

Our copy, ie-apurimac.csv, has 4,113 rows with a cut-off of 26 October 2026: 2,846 active and 1,267 inactive, 3,258 rural and 855 urban. Every row has a latitude and longitude. School names are in the file, and this lesson does not print them.

### The method

Latitude and longitude are angles. The usual way to turn two of them into a distance over the Earth's surface is the haversine formula, which uses sines, cosines and the Earth's radius of about 6,371 km.

We need a point for each town. As a simple, stated choice of ours, we take the average position of the list's rows in Abancay district and in Andahuaylas district. It is not the exact town centre, and for straight-line distances of tens of kilometres it is close enough.

**Version 1: the formula, typed straight from a textbook** (Python)
```python
import csv
import math

with open("ie-apurimac.csv", encoding="utf-8-sig", newline="") as f:
    rows = list(csv.DictReader(f, delimiter=";"))

def centre(district):
    pts = [(float(r["latitud"]), float(r["longitud"])) for r in rows if r["nom_dist"] == district]
    return sum(p[0] for p in pts) / len(pts), sum(p[1] for p in pts) / len(pts)

def km(a, b):
    dlat, dlon = b[0] - a[0], b[1] - a[1]
    h = math.sin(dlat / 2) ** 2 + math.cos(a[0]) * math.cos(b[0]) * math.sin(dlon / 2) ** 2
    return 2 * 6371 * math.asin(math.sqrt(h))

abancay = centre("ABANCAY")
far = max(km(abancay, (float(r["latitud"]), float(r["longitud"]))) for r in rows)
print("Farthest row from Abancay:", round(far), "km")
```
It prints: Farthest row from Abancay: 6964 km.

Nothing crashed, and the answer is impossible. The rows in the file span only about one and a half degrees of latitude, under 200 km from north to south, so no school can be almost 7,000 km from Abancay. The reason is units: math.sin and math.cos in Python expect radians, and Version 1 gives them degrees. Sin of 13.6 degrees is about 0.24, but math.sin(13.6) treats 13.6 as radians and returns about 0.86. The program is not wrong in a way the computer can see; only a person who asks "is this possible?" will notice.

**Version 2: convert to radians first, then use the nearer town** (Python)
```python
import csv
import math

with open("ie-apurimac.csv", encoding="utf-8-sig", newline="") as f:
    rows = list(csv.DictReader(f, delimiter=";"))

def centre(district):
    pts = [(float(r["latitud"]), float(r["longitud"])) for r in rows if r["nom_dist"] == district]
    return sum(p[0] for p in pts) / len(pts), sum(p[1] for p in pts) / len(pts)

def km(a, b):
    lat1, lon1, lat2, lon2 = map(math.radians, (*a, *b))
    h = (math.sin((lat2 - lat1) / 2) ** 2
         + math.cos(lat1) * math.cos(lat2) * math.sin((lon2 - lon1) / 2) ** 2)
    return 2 * 6371 * math.asin(math.sqrt(h))

towns = [centre("ABANCAY"), centre("ANDAHUAYLAS")]
far = max(km(towns[0], (float(r["latitud"]), float(r["longitud"]))) for r in rows)
print("Farthest row from Abancay:", round(far), "km")

near = []
for r in rows:
    if r["nivel"] == "Secundaria" and r["estado"] == "Activa":
        here = (float(r["latitud"]), float(r["longitud"]))
        near.append(min(km(t, here) for t in towns))
print("Active secundaria rows:", len(near))
print("Over 50 km from both towns:", sum(d > 50 for d in near))
print("Median distance:", round(sorted(near)[len(near) // 2], 1), "km")
```
It prints: Farthest row from Abancay: 145 km, Active secundaria rows: 355, Over 50 km from both towns: 122 and Median distance: 34.5 km.

Version 2 converts all four angles with map(math.radians, ...) before any trigonometry. The farthest row from Abancay is now 145 km away, which fits the size of the region. It then looks only at the 355 active secundaria rows, measures each to both towns and keeps the smaller distance with min(). Half of them are within 34.5 km of the nearer town in a straight line, and 122 are more than 50 km from both. Roads in the Andes wind, so the real journeys are longer than these lines; the numbers are a floor, not a travel time.

1. **For ages 6 to 8.** On a map of your town, stretch a string from home to school, then lay it along the streets you really walk. Which is longer, and why?
2. **Ages 8 to 11, with blocks.** Make a sprite move from one point to another and count its steps along x and y. Is the straight path shorter than going across then up?
3. **Ages 11 to 13.** Print math.sin(30) and math.sin(math.radians(30)). Which one is 0.5, and what does the other one mean?
4. **Teenagers.** Add a third reference town, such as Chalhuanca in Aymaraes, using the same averaging method. How many of the 122 rows come within 50 km?
5. **University and adults.** Compare the haversine result with a flat-map approximation that multiplies degrees by 111 km. How large is the difference at Apurímac's latitude?

Asking "could this possibly be right?" before trusting a number is one of the most useful habits in programming, and in using AI tools. It is the habit our page on [building with AI without copying](/build-with-ai-without-copying-peru) keeps returning to.

## Is programming part of school in Apurímac?

No, not as a subject. Apurímac follows the national curriculum, which spreads technology across every area. The regional education office's recent news is about equipment for institutes and free enrolment.

### National framework

The General Education Law sets six years of primaria and five of secundaria. A child's grade depends on their age on 31 March, with six as the normative age for first grade and sixteen for fifth of secundaria.

The CNEB names ICT as competency 28 and makes it transversal, so it belongs to all areas at once. The regular secundaria week totals 35 hours: Matemática four, Inglés three, and programming none.

### From the DRE Apurímac

In a press note of 15 December 2025, the Dirección Regional de Educación Apurímac reported handing computer equipment and furniture to 12 public technological institutes, and also to the Escuela Superior de Formación Artística Chabuca Granda in Abancay.

On 15 January 2026 it reminded families that school enrolment for 2026 is free and done in person, following the published schedule.

MINEDU's Python initiative for secundaria, Fábrica de Programadores, started in July 2025 with 1,000 pupils in Lima Norte; we found nothing showing it in Apurímac. Every pupil in Peru can take part in contests: the ONEM maths olympiad's school round was on 8 July 2026 and drew more than 1.7 million secundaria pupils, and the OPI computing olympiads from UTEC are free. We have no link with the DRE, MINEDU, UTEC or any contest, and we teach with our own problems.
