## How many students in La Libertad take part in the ONEM?

A great many. The Gerencia Regional de Educación de La Libertad reported 177,797 secundaria pupils from 936 public and private schools in the first round of the 2026 ONEM, the national school maths olympiad.

### What the GRE reported in July 2026

In its note of 8 July 2026, the GRE wrote that "En esta primera fase participan 177 797 estudiantes de Educación Secundaria", from 936 schools across the region. The contest runs in three levels: first and second of secundaria, third and fourth, and fifth on its own, each split into Alfa and Beta categories.

Pupils who did best in their school went on to the UGEL round, with registration open from 15 July to 12 August 2026, and from there towards the national phase.

### The national picture

Nationally, El Peruano reported that the 2026 ONEM drew more than 1.7 million secundaria pupils, and that the national stage on Sunday 11 October 2026 at Huampaní brought together 237 students.

Students take part through their colegio; nobody enters as an individual. Modern Age Coders has no tie to MINEDU, the GRE or the ONEM, and we never reproduce or sell official problems.

Why does a coding school care about a maths contest? Because the habits overlap almost completely. Olympiad problems reward reading slowly, trying small cases, spotting a pattern and then proving it. Programming rewards reading slowly, testing small inputs, spotting a bug and then explaining the fix. A secundaria student who does one well is usually ready for the other.

**A problem of ours in the olympiad spirit.** A weaver in a highland town finishes one shawl in her first week, two in the second, three in the third, and so on, one more each week. After how many weeks has she finished more than 100 shawls in total? Solve it with a short sum first, then check it with a three-line loop. (Written by us for this page. Answer: 14 weeks, because 1 + 2 + ... + 13 = 91 and adding 14 gives 105.)

Teenagers who enjoy this kind of puzzle often want the computing olympiad too. UTEC's OPI is open free of charge to all secundaria students in Peru; its 2025 rules allowed only C++ in the final phase and banned generative AI for solving the problems, which suits students who learn to write their own code.

## Is programming taught in La Libertad's schools?

Not as its own subject. La Libertad follows the national curriculum, which wants every subject to build digital skills but gives programming no lesson of its own. The newest regional effort is in technical institutes, after secundaria.

**The school ladder in Peru and what each step offers in computing**

| Stage | Years | Expected age (at 31 March) | Programming in the plan? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primaria | Six grades | From 6 in first grade | No; digital skills cross all areas |
| Secundaria | Five grades | From 12 in first year, 16 in fifth | No computing area in the regular 35-hour week |
| Public technical institute (IEST) | After secundaria | Usually 17 and over | Depends on the career chosen |

Built from Ley 28044 (six years of primaria, five of secundaria), MINEDU's normative-age table, the CNEB secundaria study plan, and the GRE La Libertad note of 13 July 2026 on technical institutes. The age in the last row is our general description, not a rule.

### Inside the colegio

The national curriculum lists a transversal competency for working in virtual environments built with ICT. Every area is supposed to develop it, so in practice it depends on each teacher. The regular secundaria week gives Matemática and Comunicación four hours each, Inglés three, Educación para el Trabajo two, and no hours to programming.

MINEDU's Python pilot, Fábrica de Programadores, started in July 2025 with 1,000 pupils in Lima Norte, not in La Libertad.

### After the colegio

In July 2026 the GRE announced that La Libertad had become "la primera región en implementar el modelo de Formación Dual y en Alternancia" in public technical education, in six institutes, among them Chocope, Otuzco, Virú, Paiján and Nueva Esperanza, for more than 2,000 students. The aim is for all 27 public technical institutes in the region to follow.

The first specialities are agricultural production, industrial electricity, mechanics, industrial electronics and automotive mechatronics. Programming is useful in nearly all of them, and is a natural thing to learn alongside.

## Which province of La Libertad has the most registered artisans?

It depends on what "most" means. Our older students answer the question twice with the regional artisan register, once by counting and once by comparing with population, and learn why the two answers disagree.

### Where the data comes from

The Gobierno Regional de La Libertad publishes "Artesanos registrados en el RNA De La región La Libertad 2025", its list of artisans in Peru's Registro Nacional del Artesano, on datosabiertos.gob.pe under the Open Data Commons Attribution Licence (ODC-By). The publisher describes it as updated to 7 October 2025; the dataset page was last modified on 12 October 2026.

We saved the CSV as artesanos.csv on 9 November 2026. It has 4,104 rows, one per registration, with the province and the craft line. It also has e-mail addresses, which we never print, share or use; the lesson needs only the province column.

### The two questions

First question: in which province are the most artisans registered? A count answers it.

Second question: in which province is craft work most common? A count cannot answer that, because provinces differ hugely in size. Trujillo has more than a million people, Bolívar about sixteen thousand. We need artisans per 10,000 people, which means bringing in a second table, the 2025 census, and matching the two by province name.

**Version 1: count registrations per province** (Python)
```python
import csv

count = {}
with open("artesanos.csv", newline="", encoding="latin-1") as rna:
    for row in csv.DictReader(rna, delimiter=";"):
        province = row["PROVINCIA"]
        count[province] = count.get(province, 0) + 1

ranking = sorted(count.items(), key=lambda pair: pair[1], reverse=True)
print("Provinces:", len(ranking), "Artisans:", sum(count.values()))
for province, n in ranking[:3]:
    print(province, n)
```
It prints: Provinces: 12 Artisans: 4104, then PATAZ 1074, SÁNCHEZ CARRIÓN 973 and TRUJILLO 753.

Students usually expect Trujillo to win, and are surprised to see Pataz first. Then someone points out that Trujillo comes third by count while having about thirteen times as many people as Pataz. The count is correct; it just answers the first question, not the second.

**Version 2: join the census and divide by population** (Python)
```python
import csv

# INEI, Censos Nacionales 2025, Cuadro 1: total population by province
people = {"TRUJILLO": 1090001, "ASCOPE": 127622, "BOLÍVAR": 16127,
          "CHEPÉN": 96338, "JULCÁN": 29980, "OTUZCO": 92560,
          "PACASMAYO": 123508, "PATAZ": 84293, "SÁNCHEZ CARRIÓN": 173502,
          "SANTIAGO DE CHUCO": 58138, "GRAN CHIMÚ": 30356, "VIRÚ": 134020}

count = {}
with open("artesanos.csv", newline="", encoding="latin-1") as rna:
    for row in csv.DictReader(rna, delimiter=";"):
        province = row["PROVINCIA"]
        count[province] = count.get(province, 0) + 1

rates = []
for province, n in count.items():
    per_10k = n / people[province] * 10000
    rates.append((round(per_10k, 1), province, n))

rates.sort(reverse=True)
for rate, province, n in rates[:3]:
    print(province, n, "artisans,", rate, "per 10,000 people")
print("Lowest:", rates[-1][1], rates[-1][0])
place = [r[1] for r in rates].index("TRUJILLO") + 1
print("Trujillo comes", place, "of", len(rates), "with", rates[place - 1][0])
```
It prints: PATAZ 1074 artisans, 127.4 per 10,000 people, SÁNCHEZ CARRIÓN 973 artisans, 56.1 per 10,000 people, SANTIAGO DE CHUCO 320 artisans, 55.0 per 10,000 people, Lowest: VIRÚ 2.1 and Trujillo comes 10 of 12 with 6.9.

The dictionary called people is the join: for each province in the register, the program looks up its 2025 population by name. That only works because both tables spell the names identically, accents included. Type "SANCHEZ CARRION" without accents and the program stops with a KeyError, which is a good thing; a silent wrong match would be worse. Once joined, the rate shows the region's shape: craft registration is most common in the highland provinces, and Trujillo, third by count, drops to tenth of twelve.

1. **Under 8, with counters.** One basket holds 3 red beads out of 4. Another holds 10 red beads out of 100. Which basket has more red beads? Which is redder?
2. **Block coding, around 8 to 10.** Make a list of the twelve counts and find the largest. Then make a second list of populations and ask what you would need to do to compare fairly.

## When are classes held for families in La Libertad?

At the hour you choose. Trujillo, Huamachuco and the whole region run on Peru time, and the mentor fits around it.

**How a few Peru times read on the mentor's clock in India (examples, not a schedule)**

| In La Libertad | In India | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 07:30 | 18:00 | Indian evening |
| 09:30 | 20:00 | Indian evening |
| 11:30 | 22:00 | Late evening in India |
| 14:00 | 00:30 | Just after midnight in India |
| 17:30 | 04:00 | Early morning in India |
| 20:30 | 07:00 | Morning in India |

Peru keeps one time all year, five hours behind UTC, with no summer change since 1994 (tz database, zone America/Lima). India keeps UTC plus five and a half hours. The difference is a steady 10 hours 30 minutes.

Since the difference never moves, a slot you agree in the summer break stays put through the school year. You pick a time on your own clock when booking; the system shows the mentor the India time. The 2026 school year in Peru began on 16 March 2026, so many La Libertad families start in January or February and keep the same hour when classes resume.

## Online coding and AI classes in La Libertad: questions

### Are the classes in Spanish?

No, never. Every lesson is live, online and in English, given by mentors in India who slow down, rephrase and adapt to each learner's pace and accent. If a child answers in Spanish, the mentor gives the English back and carries on.

### Can a child in Huamachuco or Otuzco join?

Yes. The class happens over video, so any province of La Libertad works, Trujillo or the highlands alike. All that is needed is a steady connection and, from about nine, a computer with a keyboard.

### Will this help with the ONEM?

It helps with the thinking the ONEM rewards: careful reading, small cases and clear explanations. We use problems we write ourselves and never the official ones. Pupils enter through their colegio, and we have no link with MINEDU, the GRE La Libertad or the ONEM.

### Do you prepare students for the OPI?

Our C++ and algorithms courses build the skills the OPI tests. The OPI is run by UTEC and is free for secundaria students; its 2025 rules required C++ in the final and banned generative AI. We are not connected with UTEC.

### What time are classes in La Libertad?

Any day, any time, flexible: choose a batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at the hour that suits your family. India is ten and a half hours ahead of Trujillo every day, so a 09:30 class there is 20:00 for the mentor.

### Is programming part of school in La Libertad?

Not as a subject. The national curriculum asks all subjects to build digital skills, and the regular secundaria week has no programming hours. The region's new dual-training model applies to public technical institutes after secundaria.

### From what age can my child start?

From six, with coding blocks that need no typing. Around nine or ten most children are ready for Python. Teenagers and adults can start from zero at any age.

### Are there classes for university students and adults?

Yes, in adult groups or one to one. Java, Python, data structures and algorithms, and data analysis are the usual choices, scheduled around lectures, shifts or family time.

### How is the fee paid?

Monthly, in US dollars, by card; your bank converts the charge to soles. The page shows the fee in dollars, or in rupees for visitors from India. The trial class is free.

### Do you award a certificate?

Courses that include one end with our own certificate of completion. It is not a Peruvian qualification, and it is not issued or recognised by MINEDU, the GRE or any school; it describes the work the learner completed.
