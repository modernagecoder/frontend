Coding classes for kids in Oakville

Should an Oakville child aged 6 to 12 start with robotics or with coding?

If your child loves building with their hands, a school or club robotics team is a fine first step; if they want to make games, apps or solve puzzles, start with coding, which every robot needs anyway. Modern Age Coders teaches coding to Oakville children aged 6 to 12 live online, in groups of 15 to 20 or one to one, from Coding for Kids to Python and AI for Kids. We do not sell robot kits. The first class is free.

Oakville parents often search for robotics and coding in the same breath, and providers blur the two. A robotics class spends much of its time on parts: motors, sensors, wheels and the kit that holds them. A coding class spends its time on the instructions. Both are worthwhile, and they ask different things of a child. This page helps you decide which your child needs first, using what Ontario already asks of an Oakville student in Grade 6 math and science, where code must be efficient and must read input in different ways. It then shows a project our older students run on a Town of Oakville open data file, where the real work is cleaning messy text before anything can be counted. We teach the coding side only, and we rank no other provider.

Grade 6 in Ontario

Efficient code and input: what Grade 6 asks of an Oakville student

Oakville schools teach the Ontario curriculum, in which Grade 6 asks for code in two subjects. We pulled both from the Ontario curriculum’s own data service on 22 October 2026. Quotes are the ministry’s; the reading of them is ours.

Math C3.1Students solve problems by writing and executing “efficient code”, with conditional statements and other control structures.
Working code is no longer the finish line. A Grade 6 student is asked whether the code does its job without wasted steps.
Math C3.2Students describe how changes affect “the outcomes and the efficiency of the code”.
Two programs can print the same answer; one may take far longer. Children learn to compare, not just to finish.
Science A2Students “use coding in investigations and to model concepts” and assess the impact of coding on everyday life.
Code turns up in science class too, as a tool for experiments and models.
Science A2.1Code in investigations, “with a focus on obtaining input in different ways”.
This is the expectation closest to robotics: reading a sensor, a keyboard or a file is all input.

A small example of efficiency, written by us for Grade 6 students. Both lines of output are 5050, the sum of the whole numbers from 1 to 100, but the first takes a hundred additions and the second takes one multiplication and one division:total = 0 for n in range(1, 101): total = total + n print(total) print(100 * 101 // 2)Asking why the shortcut works, and when a loop is still the better choice, is exactly the conversation C3.2 wants.

EQAO lists the Grade 6 provincial assessment window for 2026 to 2027 as 4 May to 8 June 2027. We do not run test preparation for it; careful reasoning about patterns and code is useful there anyway.

Oakville data project

How many Oakville drop-in programs suit a 9-year-old? First, clean the labels

The Town of Oakville publishes its recreation and culture drop-in programs as open data, with an AGES column. Before our students aged about 10 to 12 can filter by age, they have to turn messy words into numbers. All counts here are ours, not the Town’s.

What the AGES column looks like: our count of the Town of Oakville Recreation and Culture Drop in Programs file, downloaded 22 October 2026 (200 rows)
What we foundRowsWhat the cleaning step does
“50+ ” with a trailing space52strip() removes the space, so it matches “50+”
“All Ages” and “All ages”35 and 1lower() makes them one label
“13-17” and “13 to 17”1 and 1Replacing the hyphen with “ to ” makes them one shape
“8 years and under” and similar9Read as 0 up to the first number
“Grades 6 to 12”11Skipped: grades are not ages, and guessing would be wrong
A row whose age label is simply “Test”1Skipped: a leftover test entry, not a program

Our script, standard library only. The function age_range returns a (lowest, highest) pair or None, and the main loop counts programs a 9-year-old fits:import csv def age_range(label): text = label.strip().lower().replace("-", " to ") if text == "all ages": return (0, 120) if text.endswith("+"): return (int(text[:-1]), 120) if text.endswith("years and under"): return (0, int(text.split()[0])) if " to " in text and not text.startswith("grades"): low, high = text.split(" to ") return (int(low), int(high)) return None fits = {} skipped = [] with open("drop_in_programs.csv", encoding="utf-8-sig") as f: for row in csv.DictReader(f): span = age_range(row["AGES"]) if span is None: skipped.append(row["AGES"].strip()) continue if span[0] <= 9 <= span[1]: section = row["SECTION_HEADER"] fits[section] = fits.get(section, 0) + 1 print(fits) print(sorted(set(skipped)), len(skipped))

What it found

Of the 200 rows, 188 turn into an age range and 12 are skipped. Of the 188, 44 fit a 9-year-old: 15 recreational swimming, 13 recreational skating, 8 drop-in sports, 4 museum programs, 2 fitness classes and 2 art studios.

Messy categories too

Those two art rows sit under two different headings, Drop-in Art Studios and Drop-in Arts Studio. Likewise Senior Services has 57 rows and Seniors Services has 1. Cleaning is never only about one column.

Change the age

Swap the 9 for 6 and the count is 46; for 12 it is 51. A child who changes one number and predicts the result before running it is doing the Grade 6 expectation in miniature.

What the file cannot tell you

The catalogue record says 2024 Town of Oakville and was last modified in May 2024, so this is a snapshot, not today’s schedule. A row is a program listing, not a time slot or a place available. Before a family plans a week around it, they should check the Town’s current listings. Writing that sentence is part of the assignment.

Coding and AI

Why should an Oakville child learn to clean data when AI can do it?

Because the cleaning rules are decisions, and somebody has to make them.

An AI tool asked to count programs for a 9-year-old might quietly turn “Grades 6 to 12” into ages 6 to 12, or keep the row called Test. Either choice changes the answer, and neither is announced. A child who has written age_range by hand knows those rows exist and decides, on purpose, what to do with them.

Our classes for children put that judgment first: look at the data, decide the rule, write it, check it. Children learn how AI tools work and how they fail, and they use what they learn to question answers rather than accept them. At home, remember that OpenAI’s rules require a parent’s permission for anyone under 18 using ChatGPT.

Ontario asks Grade 6 students to judge whether code is efficient; judging whether an answer is right comes before that. The longer argument is in is coding still worth learning in 2026.

Questions Oakville families send us about coding and robotics.

Do you have robotics classes in Oakville?

No. We teach coding, not robot hardware. For building robots, look at school or community teams such as FIRST LEGO League, which FIRST Robotics Canada describes for ages 4 to 14. Our coding classes pair well with a team.

Do you have coding classes for kids in Oakville?

Yes, live online. Children join from home with a real teacher, in a group of 15 to 20 or one to one. We have no Oakville building.

Should my child do robotics or coding first?

Builders who love working with their hands often enjoy robotics first; children who love games, stories and puzzles usually prefer coding. Every robot runs on code, so coding is never wasted.

What age is right to start?

Six, with blocks. Ontario math asks for simple code from Grade 1, and these courses run up to age 12.

What does Grade 6 ask about coding in Ontario?

Grade 6 math asks for efficient code and for explaining how changes affect efficiency. Grade 6 science asks students to use code in investigations, with a focus on getting input in different ways.

Does my child need a robot kit or special software?

No kit. A laptop or desktop with a browser is enough; the teacher sets up free tools in the first class.

What time are classes for Oakville families?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Times are confirmed in Eastern time.

Can my child enter a competition?

Robotics competitions run through FIRST teams. For logic and math, CEMC contests such as the Beaver Computing Challenge are written through your child's school.

How do I compare classes in Oakville?
