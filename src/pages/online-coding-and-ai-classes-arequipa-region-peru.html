Online coding and AI classes across the Arequipa region

Most writing about Arequipa stops at the white city. This page is for the rest of the department as well: the irrigated plain of Majes, the coast at Camaná and Mollendo, the Colca valley, Caravelí, Castilla, Condesuyos and La Unión. It gives each province's 2025 population, explains the region's new ten-year education plan, checks how many homes can support an online class, and asks a sharp question of the region's own innovation data: how many of its funded projects are really about artificial intelligence?

Straight answer

Modern Age Coders runs live online coding and AI classes in English for learners anywhere in the Arequipa region of Peru, from the city itself to Majes, Camaná, Mollendo, Chivay and the highland provinces. Children start at six with coding blocks and move to Python at around nine; teenagers study Python, AI and machine learning; adults learn Python, data and AI agents. A mentor teaches each class live on video, to fifteen to twenty learners or to one, on days and at hours the family picks, and the first class is free. Students learn to understand what AI does, not to paste what it writes.

Eight provinces

How many people live in each province of the Arequipa region?

Majes, a city in the desert

The largest place outside the capital's province is the irrigated district of Majes, in Caylloma, with 119,744 people in 2025. Its main town, El Pedregal, appears with 44,264 people in INEI's 2017 list of populated centres, a figure for that single centre only.

Families there are a long drive from the city, which makes a live online class a practical option rather than a second choice.

Inside the capital's province

The province of Arequipa contains many large districts. In 2025 Cerro Colorado counted 273,354 people, Paucarpata 153,044 and Cayma 111,606, each bigger than several whole provinces of the department.

Families in the city itself will find a page written for them: coding classes in Arequipa city, with its own data project on the regional tourist inventory.

The regional plan

What is Arequipa planning for its schools between 2026 and 2036?

In October 2026 the regional government presented its Proyecto Educativo Regional for 2026 to 2036, the strategic plan that is meant to steer education in the department for the next decade.

A ten-year plan, built with ten UGELs

According to the Gobierno Regional de Arequipa's note of 21 October 2026, its Gerencia Regional de Educación "presentó el Proyecto Educativo Regional (PER) Arequipa", covering 2026 to 2036. The plan came out of "un proceso participativo iniciado en febrero de 2025", with workshops held with the region's ten local education offices, its UGELs.

A regional plan sets priorities; it does not change the national curriculum, which still decides what is taught in each grade.

Tablets for rural schools

Two weeks later, on 4 November 2026, the regional government reported the delivery of 16 technology kits to rural schools under the UGEL La Joya, in a pilot with two schools. Each kit includes a tablet with a keyboard and headphones, plus routers and USB memory, and the PerúEduca content on them is preloaded for use without an internet connection, in Spanish and Quechua Collao.

It is a careful, small start, and it shows the real constraint in rural Arequipa: devices and connection come before any coding lesson.

We are not part of these programmes and make no claim about them beyond what the regional government published. What they tell a family is simple: the region is investing in digital access, but the timetable still has no programming lesson, so a child who wants to learn to code needs a class outside school.

In the classroom

What do Arequipa's schools teach about computers and code?

Arequipa's colegios follow the same national curriculum as the rest of Peru. Computers appear as a skill to be practised in every subject, but nowhere as a subject with its own hours.

Sources: the General Education Law, Ley 28044, for the length of each stage; MINEDU's table of normative ages, measured on 31 March; the CNEB plan of studies for secundaria hours. Checked in November 2026.

Digital skills everywhere, programming nowhere

The CNEB treats its technology competency, number 28, as transversal: it belongs to all the areas at once. A teacher of science or communication may use it well, but none is required to teach a programming language.

In the secundaria week there are four hours of maths, four of communication and three of English. Not one hour is labelled computing.

The national Python pilot

MINEDU's Fábrica de Programadores began in July 2025 with Python for 1,000 secundaria pupils in their final two years, in Lima Norte only, with the option of sitting the PCEP certification exam at the end.

For a student in Mollendo or Chivay, the closest equivalent is a class taken from home. Our PCEP and PCAP course follows a similar path, in English and at the student's own hours.

At home

Can families in the Arequipa region follow classes from home?

More of them than in most of Peru. In INEI's 2025 household survey, Arequipa is well above the national figures for home computers, home internet and internet use.

What the numbers allow

In 2025 just over half of Arequipa homes had a computer, against about a third nationally, and seven in ten had internet. That makes the department one of the easier places in Peru to start online coding without buying anything new.

Averages blur differences, though. The La Joya tablet pilot exists precisely because some rural schools are still offline.

What we check

In the free first class the mentor looks at sound, screen sharing and whether the learner can type comfortably. A tablet is fine for a six year old using blocks.

From about nine, when children start typing Python, a laptop or desktop makes the difference between following along and actually programming.

English only

Is any part of the class in Spanish?

No. Classes are live, online and entirely in English, taught by mentors in India who adjust to the learner's pace and accent. Here is why that works for most Arequipa learners, and what we do when it does not.

The 2025 index

EF's English Proficiency Index 2025 puts Peru at 519 points, 52nd of 123 countries, in its moderate band. The Arequipa region scores 509, also moderate.

Moderate means a learner usually understands more than they can say. A class built on showing, doing and short spoken steps suits that well.

When English is the hard part

Some children need more time. In a one-to-one plan the mentor can slow right down, repeat key phrases each week and build a small word list with the learner.

Over a few months, the vocabulary of code, words like loop, list, error and return, becomes familiar, and many families find the English improves as a side effect.

An Arequipa data project

How many of Arequipa's innovation projects are really about AI?

The regional government publishes the list of projects in the region co-financed by ProInnovate, Peru's innovation fund. A first program says 158 of them mention AI. The real number is three, and finding out why is a lesson in how computers search text.

The dataset

GORE Arequipa shares "Proyectos Cofinanciados por ProInnovate en Arequipa" through Peru's open data portal with an ODC-By (Open Data Commons Attribution) licence, so it may be reused with credit. It was released in May 2025 and last updated on 22 August 2025. The spreadsheet lists 368 projects from 2019 to 2024, with the call, the organisation, the title, the amount and the status: 199 still running and 169 finished.

Because our students' computers may not have a spreadsheet library, we exported the sheet to a plain file, proinnovate-arequipa.csv, on 9 November 2026. We never print the amounts; the titles are all this project needs.

The question

In Spanish, artificial intelligence is often shortened to IA. So the obvious first attempt is to look for "ia" in every title, ignoring capitals, and count the matches.

Teenagers who have used AI tools expect a big number, maybe a quarter of all projects. The program agrees with them, and that agreement is exactly what should make us suspicious.

Read the first match. It is about virtual tools and marketing strategies, and it was caught because "estrategias" contains the letters i and a side by side. The test "ia" in t.lower() asks whether those two letters appear anywhere, not whether the word IA appears. In Spanish that is very common: comercial, eficiencia, industria and experiencia all contain it.

Version 2 splits each title into words first, using a regular expression that keeps letters and digits together and drops spaces and punctuation. Then it checks for the whole word "ia", or for the phrase "inteligencia artificial" written out. It also counts which words fooled the first version.

Three projects out of 368 name AI in their titles. That does not mean only three use it; a title is a short summary, and some projects may use machine learning without saying so. What it does mean is that the honest answer to the question we asked is three, not 158, and that a program which agrees with what you expected deserves the hardest check.

Data: Gobierno Regional de Arequipa, Proyectos Cofinanciados por ProInnovate en Arequipa (2019 to 2024), ODC-By. The counts are ours. "digital" shrinks because titles also say digitales and digitalización; "software" does not change because no other Spanish word in these titles contains it. The three AI titles are the 1 with the word IA plus 2 that write out inteligencia artificial.

  1. Under 8, a word hunt. Write the word MARIA on paper. Can you find the word IA inside it? Is MARIA a word about computers?
  2. Blocks, ages 8 to 10. Build a list of six short words and a block that checks whether each one contains the letters "ia". Which ones say yes?
  3. Early Python, ages 10 to 12. Start from Version 1, print the first ten matches, and for each one underline the word that caused it.
  4. Ages 13 and over. Version 2 finds "IA" but would miss "I.A." with dots. Add a test case with that spelling, watch it fail, and fix the program.
  5. Going further. Count the titles that mention datos, plataforma or aplicación as whole words, then write a short paragraph on what kind of innovation the region funds most.

Hours

At what times can a learner in the Arequipa region have classes?

At any hour the family chooses. The whole department keeps Peru time, and the mentor in India plans around that.

Peru keeps UTC minus 5 hours all year with no daylight saving (tz database, America/Lima). India keeps UTC plus 5 hours 30 minutes. India is therefore 10 hours 30 minutes ahead of Arequipa, and the gap never changes.

With no clock changes in either country, a slot agreed now stays the same all year. You choose your time in your own clock when booking. Peru's school year began on 16 March 2026, and the summer before it is a popular moment to start, keeping the same hour once colegio returns.

Arequipa region families ask

Online coding and AI classes in the Arequipa region: answers

Are the classes in Spanish?

No. Classes are only in English, always live and online. Mentors in India keep a steady, clear pace, adapt to each learner's accent, and if a learner replies in Spanish they reply with the English words and continue.

We live in Majes, Camaná or Chivay. Can we join?

Yes. Classes are online, so any province of the department works exactly like the city. You need a stable connection and, for typed code from about age nine, a computer with a keyboard.

Is this the same as the Arequipa city page?

It covers the same courses, but this page is about the whole department and its eight provinces. Families in the city can also read our page on coding classes in Arequipa city, which has a different data project.

Do schools in the Arequipa region teach programming?

No subject is dedicated to it. Digital skills are spread through every area of the national curriculum, and secundaria timetables leave no slot for programming. Arequipa's new education plan for 2026 to 2036 sets priorities but does not change the curriculum.

Do you teach AI to children?

Yes, in a way suited to their age. Children build small AI features and learn why they work or fail. Under-13s never use adult chatbot accounts with us, and teenagers only use AI tools that allow their age.

What time are classes?

Any day, any time, flexible: choose a batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at the hour that suits your family. Arequipa runs 10 hours 30 minutes behind India on every date, so 08:30 for you is 19:00 for the mentor.

Can adults take the classes?

Yes. Adults study in adult-only groups or one to one, most often Python, data analysis or AI agents, at hours that fit their work.

What device does my child need?

A tablet is enough for block coding at six or seven. From about nine, a laptop or desktop is best, because the learner types and runs code. The free class includes a short check of sound, screen and connection.

How do we pay?

By card, once a month, in US dollars; the soles amount is set by your bank. Visitors from India see the fee in rupees, everyone else in dollars, and the first class costs nothing.

Is there a certificate at the end?

Some courses end with a Modern Age Coders certificate of completion. That document is ours alone: no Peruvian qualification comes with it, and MINEDU, the regional government and schools play no part in issuing or backing it.
