# IGCSE maths for home-educated students in the UK

A home-educated teenager can sit GCSE or IGCSE maths, but not at home and not through the family. The entry is made by an exam centre that agrees to take a private candidate, and that centre sets its own fees, deadlines and rules. That single fact shapes everything else: the series you can sit, the board you can use and, for GCSE, the tier. This page sets out what the Department for Education, Ofqual and the exam boards say, the steps to register, how the main maths options compare for a private candidate, and how we teach the course itself live online.

## Can a home-educated child sit IGCSE or GCSE maths?

Yes. Nothing requires it, nothing prevents it, and the family carries the cost and the organising.

In England, the Department for Education's guidance for parents lists among the things parents are not legally required to do: "aim for the child to acquire any specific qualifications". Its guidance for local authorities adds that "there is no requirement to enter children for public examinations." So sitting maths is a choice. The same guidance for parents asks families to think early about "whether you expect your child to sit public examinations such as GCSEs or not", because the decision affects what is taught from Year 9 onwards.

**What the Department for Education says about exams for home-educated children (England)**

| Point | The guidance, quoted or closely restated |
|---|---|
| Who pays | Parents must be prepared to bear "the cost of any public examinations" |
| How the entry is made | Through an outside examinations centre, which the guidance notes could be a long way from home |
| Local authority help with costs | Some councils may offer money or other help towards exams, at their own discretion, so ask yours directly |
| Local authority help finding a centre | Support can include "providing assistance with identifying exam centres willing to accept external candidates" |
| Part-time college | Children aged 14 to 16 educated mainly at home may attend state-funded FE or sixth form colleges part-time for specific subjects, if a college agrees |
| Tutors and online tuition | Optional; the guidance reminds parents these settings are often unregulated |

## How do you register as a private candidate for maths?

Through a centre, as early as you can. Ofqual's 2026 guide says to register "as soon as possible".

Ofqual's student guide for 2026 explains the route: "If you are a private candidate (so you do not go to school or college), you will need to register, as soon as possible, at a school or college or other exam centre to sit your exams." It adds a warning every home-educating family meets: "Not all schools and colleges accept entries for private candidates." For GCSE, AS and A level, Ofqual points to a list of centres that accept private candidates published by the Joint Council for Qualifications, which "is updated at the end of each calendar year."

1. Decide the target year and the qualification you would like, but hold the choice loosely until a centre confirms what it can enter.
2. Find centres near you that accept private candidates: the [JCQ list](https://www.jcq.org.uk/private-candidates/) for GCSE, Cambridge's private candidate pages for Cambridge IGCSE, and your local authority, which may help identify centres.
3. Contact several centres early, well before the entry deadline for your series. Ask what they enter, for which series, and what they charge. Every centre sets its own fees and rules, and they vary.
4. If your child needs access arrangements, such as extra time, raise it at this first contact. Ofqual advises discussing them with the centre "as early as possible", for example at the point of asking for the entry.
5. Agree the exact entry: board, syllabus code and, for GCSE, the tier. Ofqual says private candidates should discuss the tier "with the school or college that you have registered with."
6. Pay the centre, then check the statement of entry when it arrives. Cambridge lists "Check your statement of entry is correct" among a private candidate's responsibilities.
7. On each exam day bring photo identification. Cambridge's list includes "Take ID to confirm your identity to each exam." Results and certificates are collected from the centre.

Sources: Ofqual, [What you need to know before your exams](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ofqual-student-guide-to-exams-and-assessments-in-2026/what-you-need-to-know-before-your-exams) (2026); Cambridge International, [Private candidates](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/why-choose-us/find-a-cambridge-school/private-candidates/). The numbered steps are our summary of that guidance. We could not read the JCQ site directly when writing this page, so the list is described from Ofqual's page.

**Who does what, from Cambridge's private candidate responsibilities**

| Checks the statement of entry | Issues the statement of entry and the timetable |

## Is IGCSE or GCSE maths better for a private candidate?

Neither is better in general. The deciding questions are which one a nearby centre will enter, and in which series.

Families often hear that IGCSE suits home education because it has no coursework. For maths that is true of Cambridge IGCSE 0580, whose four papers are written exams set and marked by Cambridge, and of Edexcel International GCSE 4MA1, whose two papers are "set and marked by Pearson". The bigger practical differences are the series on offer and the calculator.

**Three maths options compared for a private candidate, from each board's documents and Ofqual**

|  | Cambridge IGCSE 0580 | Edexcel International GCSE 4MA1 | GCSE maths (England) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exam series | June and November | January and June | Summer; 2026 exams ran from 7 May to 23 June |
| Papers | Two per candidate, all written | Two per candidate, both written, same series | Set by the board the centre uses |
| Tiers and grades | Core C to G, Extended A* to E | Foundation 5 to 1, Higher 9 to 4 with 3 allowed | Foundation 5 to 1, Higher 9 to 4 with 3 possible |
| Calculator | Banned on one of the two papers | Allowed on both papers | Depends on the paper |
| Private candidates | "Private candidates can enter for this syllabus." | Entered through a Pearson centre | Entered through a centre on the JCQ list or similar |
| UK regulation | Compared with GCSE by UK ENIC, per Cambridge | Not regulated by any UK regulatory body, per Pearson | Regulated by Ofqual |

Sources: Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for 2025 to 2027; Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Mathematics A specification, Issue 2; Ofqual student guide for 2026. Pearson's own private-candidate page could not be found when we checked, so ask the centre about Pearson entries directly.

### When IGCSE tends to fit

A November or January series gives a second chance in the same school year, or an earlier sitting for a pupil who is ready. That flexibility matters when the family, not a school, sets the pace.

Which boards a given centre enters is for that centre to say, so confirm the code before any teaching starts.

### When GCSE tends to fit

If a sixth form or college the student hopes to join asks for a regulated GCSE, or if the nearest centre only enters GCSE, the decision is made for you.

Ofqual confirms that GCSE maths students sitting in 2026 and 2027 receive formulae and equation sheets, which helps a student working without a teacher every day.

One rule to avoid wasting an entry: Pearson warns that colleges, universities and employers are very likely to count two qualifications in the same subject as one. Sitting both GCSE and IGCSE maths rarely adds anything. If a strong student wants a second maths qualification, a different subject such as Cambridge Additional Mathematics 0606 or Pearson's Further Pure Mathematics is the usual choice.

| Ask | Why it matters |
|---|---|

| For GCSE, how is the tier agreed? | The tier changes which grades are possible |
| Where and when are the exams sat, and what ID is needed? | The nearest willing centre may be a long journey away |
| If exams were disrupted, would you assess private candidates? | Ofqual says a centre may agree to assess them during the year |

The last question comes from Ofqual's 2026 guide, which says a private candidate who has registered "might want that school or college to assess you during the academic year, alongside their students, in line with the published resilience guidance." The centre may agree, and would need to make sure any assessment only covers content the candidate has studied. Ofqual also says that if national exams could not take place, the Department for Education would explore ways to encourage schools and colleges to work with private candidates.

Families looking at A level maths later should know that board rules differ. AQA's A level Mathematics 7357 specification, for example, accepts private candidates who are self-taught, home schooled or privately tutored, but only if they live in the UK. Check the rule for the exact specification before relying on it.

## How long does IGCSE maths take to study at home?

Cambridge designs IGCSE syllabuses around 130 guided learning hours. Here is how we spread that over a year for a home-educated student.

The 0580 syllabus says Cambridge designs IGCSE syllabuses "to require about 130 guided learning hours for each subject", and adds that this "is for guidance only". A home-educated student starting from secure Key Stage 3 maths can cover the course in about a year with steady weekly teaching and independent practice. One starting with gaps needs longer, and we say so at the first class.

**A twelve-month plan for a June IGCSE sitting (our plan, adjusted per student)**

| Months | Focus | Evidence before moving on |
|---|---|---|
| 1 to 2 | Number by hand, fractions, percentages, ratio, standard form | Our tier check below answered accurately without a calculator |
| 3 to 5 | Algebra: equations, inequalities, sequences, graphs, rearranging | Unprompted solving of mixed equations |
| 6 to 8 | Geometry, mensuration, trigonometry, vectors and transformations | Topic papers at the likely tier |
| 9 to 10 | Probability and statistics, then the tier decision with the centre | Timed mixed papers at both tiers |
| 11 to 12 | Full papers under exam timing, with and without a calculator | Two full papers a fortnight, reviewed line by line |

**A six-question tier check, written by us (answers checked in Python)**

| # | Our question | Answer | What it tells us |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Work out 3/5 of 45 without a calculator. | 27 | Basic fraction fluency |
| 2 | Solve 4(x &minus; 3) = 2x + 6. | x = 9 | Equation solving with brackets |
| 3 | Expand and simplify (x + 5)(x &minus; 2). | x2 + 3x &minus; 10 | Algebraic manipulation |
| 4 | Factorise x2 &minus; 7x + 12. | (x &minus; 3)(x &minus; 4) | Readiness for the higher tier |
| 5 | Make t the subject of v = u + at. | t = (v &minus; u)/a | Rearranging formulae |
| 6 | Simplify (2x3)2. | 4x6 | Index laws |

A student who gets all six right quickly is usually ready to aim at Extended 0580 or Higher 4MA1. A student who stumbles on questions 4 to 6 can still reach a good result, but may need either more time or a Core or Foundation entry. These are our questions, not board questions; past papers come from the board through the centre or the board's own public pages.

## What families ask about private candidate maths

### Can home-educated children sit GCSE or IGCSE maths?

Yes. They sit as private candidates through a school, college or exam centre that accepts them. There is no legal requirement in England to sit exams, so it is the family's choice.

### How do I find a private candidate GCSE centre near me?

Ofqual points families to a JCQ list of schools and colleges that accept private candidates, updated at the end of each calendar year. For Cambridge IGCSE, use Cambridge's private candidate pages, and ask your local authority, which may help identify centres.

### How much does it cost to sit GCSE maths as a private candidate?

Each exam centre sets its own fee, and fees vary, so ask several centres. The Department for Education says parents bear the cost of exams, though some local authorities may help at their discretion.

### When should I register as a private candidate?

As early as possible. Ofqual says private candidates should register as soon as possible, and centres set entry deadlines well before the exams. In our experience, contacting centres a year ahead leaves the most choice.

### Is IGCSE maths easier for home-educated students?

Not easier, but often more practical. Cambridge 0580 and Edexcel 4MA1 are assessed by written exams only, and they offer November or January series as well as June, which gives more flexibility on timing.

### Who decides the tier for a private candidate?

The family and the centre together. Ofqual advises private candidates to discuss the tier with the school or college they have registered with, and to know the tier before preparing.

### Can a private candidate get extra time?

Access arrangements are possible, but they must be raised with the centre as early as possible, ideally when asking for the entry. The centre requests them from the board.

### Should my child sit both GCSE and IGCSE maths?

Usually not. Pearson warns that colleges, universities and employers are very likely to count two qualifications in the same subject as one. A different second subject, such as Additional or Further Pure maths, adds more.

### When are your classes for home-educated students?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Daytime weekday classes are available, all shown in UK time.

### Are you an exam centre?

No. We teach the course live online and have no connection with any exam board or centre. The family registers with a centre, which makes the entry and runs the exams.
