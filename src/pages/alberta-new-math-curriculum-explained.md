# Alberta&rsquo;s new math curriculum, explained for parents

Alberta has been replacing its math curriculum in stages. Kindergarten to Grade 6 already has the new version; Grades 7 to 9 are on a draft that some schools are piloting this year, with every school moving to the new version in September 2027. For a family that means two practical questions: which curriculum is my child&rsquo;s class using, and what is different about it? This page answers both from Alberta&rsquo;s own curriculum pages, with examples of the new Grade 9 ideas worked by us.

### Field testing, piloting, implementation

Alberta moves each new curriculum through three phases. Field testing &ldquo;is an optional, pre-pilot phase&rdquo; in which schools try selected parts. Piloting is next: &ldquo;During classroom piloting, teachers may pilot some or all of the draft curriculum.&rdquo; Implementation is when the curriculum becomes required everywhere.

For Grades 7 to 9 math, field testing finished in March 2026 and piloting is happening now. Alberta&rsquo;s history page says the draft will keep being refined &ldquo;ahead of mandatory implementation in September 2027.&rdquo;

### Why two different K to 6 dates

The renewal timeline gives K to 3 math in September 2022 and Grades 4 to 6 math in the 2023-24 school year. The history page files the whole K to 6 math change under 2022/2023. Both are Alberta&rsquo;s own pages; we print both rather than choose.

For families the practical point is the same either way: any child now in Kindergarten to Grade 6 in an Alberta school is learning from the new math curriculum, and has been for at least two school years.

### What stands out

Three changes reach furthest. Functions arrive in junior high, where the 2016 curriculum did not introduce them at all. Quadratic equations, until now a high school topic, appear in Grade 9 in simple forms. And notation becomes something students are taught to use deliberately: set-builder notation, function notation, symbols for angles and lines.

Those are exactly the habits Alberta&rsquo;s Grade 12 diploma bulletins say students struggle to show on the written responses. Starting them earlier is, in our reading, the point.

### What teachers told Alberta

Alberta lists what it heard in 2025 engagement. On content load: &ldquo;Content load is heavy, particularly in Grade 7, and too much content results in surface-level learning&rdquo;. Partners also asked for better sequencing between grades, clearer wording, and more real-world examples.

Alberta says it responded by refining content &ldquo;identified as redundant or repetitive&rdquo;, adjusting the order of learning, revising statements for clarity, and adding &ldquo;real-world examples and applications throughout grades 7 to 9&rdquo;. After the 2026 field test it made further changes to sequencing, clarity and real-world content.

Source: [Alberta.ca, Mathematics (K to 12 curriculum renewal)](https://www.alberta.ca/curriculum-mathematics), sections Current and draft curriculum comparison, What we heard during engagement in 2025, and What we updated, read 22 October 2026.

### K to 6: numeracy and standard algorithms

Alberta describes Kindergarten to Grade 6 as having &ldquo;a focus on numeracy skills, which provides students with a solid foundation of mathematical knowledge.&rdquo; The phrase that recurs in the snapshot is &ldquo;standard procedures (algorithms)&rdquo;: column addition and subtraction from Grade 3, and multiplication algorithms in Grades 4 and 5.

The Grade 6 math PAT is now organized the same way. Its 2026 to 2027 bulletin describes Part A as &ldquo;15 questions from Organizing Idea: Number&rdquo;, the vocabulary of the new curriculum.

### Grades 7 to 9: from concrete to abstract

For the junior high grades, Alberta writes that &ldquo;students connect concrete experiences to their comprehension of abstract concepts&rdquo;, and that &ldquo;They communicate by using conventional mathematical symbols, notation and vocabulary.&rdquo;

In practice that is a Grade 9 student writing {x | x &le; 5, x &isin; R} instead of &ldquo;x is 5 or less&rdquo;, and knowing why the two say the same thing. The examples below show what that looks like.

## Two new Grade 9 ideas, worked through

Both examples are ours, written to the draft Grade 9 snapshot; they are not Alberta Education material. The first uses function and set-builder notation, the second a simple quadratic equation. A short Python program at the end checks the first one.

Example 1: a linear function and its solution set. For f(x) = 2x &minus; 3, find f(4), then find every real x with f(x) &le; 7 and write the answer in set-builder notation.

1. Read the notation aloud. &ldquo;f of x equals two x minus three&rdquo; means: take the input, double it, subtract three. f(4) means the input is 4.
2. Evaluate. f(4) = 2(4) &minus; 3 = 5.
3. Turn the condition into an inequality. f(x) &le; 7 becomes 2x &minus; 3 &le; 7.
4. Solve it like an equation, carefully. Add 3: 2x &le; 10. Divide by 2 (a positive number, so the sign stays): x &le; 5.
5. Write the set. {x | x &le; 5, x &isin; R}: &ldquo;the set of all x such that x is at most 5, where x is a real number.&rdquo; Every part of the notation says something the sentence says.
6. Check a value on each side. f(5) = 7, which satisfies &le; 7. f(6) = 9, which does not. The boundary behaves as the set says.

Example 2: a simple quadratic equation. Solve x2 = 49, then solve (x &minus; 3)2 = 16.

1. Ask what number squares to 49. Both 7 and &minus;7 do, because (&minus;7)2 = 49 as well. So x = 7 or x = &minus;7, often written x = &plusmn;7. Forgetting the negative root is the classic first mistake.
2. Treat the bracket as one quantity. In (x &minus; 3)2 = 16, the quantity x &minus; 3 squares to 16, so x &minus; 3 = 4 or x &minus; 3 = &minus;4.
3. Solve each case. x = 7 or x = &minus;1.
4. Check both. (7 &minus; 3)2 = 16 and (&minus;1 &minus; 3)2 = (&minus;4)2 = 16. Two solutions, both valid: exactly the &ldquo;understanding of multiple solutions&rdquo; Alberta&rsquo;s comparison table says the draft is aiming for.

**Python · checking example 1 (ours)** (python)
```python
# A linear function, its table, and a solution set (ours)

def f(x):
    return 2 * x - 3

print("x   f(x)")
for x in range(-1, 6):
    print(f"{x:<3} {f(x)}")

# Which whole numbers from -5 to 10 satisfy f(x) <= 7?
solutions = [x for x in range(-5, 11) if f(x) <= 7]
print("f(x) <= 7 for", solutions)
print("Set-builder form: {x | -5 <= x <= 5, x is an integer}")
```
Output: a table from x = &minus;1 (f = &minus;5) to x = 5 (f = 7), then f(x) <= 7 for &minus;5, &minus;4, ..., 5. The program only tests whole numbers in a window, while the real answer covers every real x up to 5; seeing the difference is part of the lesson.

## Eight practice questions across the new curriculum

Our own questions, one or two per grade band, written to Alberta&rsquo;s snapshot. They are not Alberta Education questions. Answers follow.

1. Grade 4. Add 3482.6 and 1937.5 with the column method.
2. Grade 5. Evaluate 3n + 4 when n = 6.
3. Grade 6. Multiply 4 &times; 3/8 and write the answer as a fraction in lowest terms.
4. Grade 7. Evaluate (&minus;6) &times; (&minus;4) &minus; 10.
5. Grade 8. Find the slope of the line through (1, 3) and (5, 11).
6. Grade 9. Expand (x + 3)(x &minus; 3). Which identity does it show?
7. Grade 9. Solve 2x2 = 50.
8. Grade 9. In a class of 30, 12 students play piano, 10 play guitar and 4 play both. What is the probability that a student chosen at random plays piano or guitar?

1. 5420.1. Line up the decimal points; the tenths give 6 + 5 = 11, so write 1 and carry 1.
2. 22. 3(6) + 4 = 18 + 4.
3. 3/2. 4 &times; 3/8 = 12/8, which simplifies to 3/2 (or 1 1/2).
4. 14. A negative times a negative is positive: 24 &minus; 10 = 14.
5. 2. Rise over run: (11 &minus; 3) / (5 &minus; 1) = 8/4 = 2.
6. x2 &minus; 9. The middle terms cancel. It is the difference of squares, (a + b)(a &minus; b) = a2 &minus; b2, the kind of identity the draft uses to support factoring.
7. x = 5 or x = &minus;5. Divide by 2 to get x2 = 25, then take both square roots.
8. 3/5, or 0.6. The events overlap (not mutually exclusive), so subtract the overlap once: (12 + 10 &minus; 4)/30 = 18/30 = 3/5.

## What the change means for your child this year

The renewal affects families differently depending on the grade. Our advice, in order.

1. Find out which curriculum the class is using. For Kindergarten to Grade 6 it is the new one. For Grades 7 to 9 in 2026 to 2027, ask the school whether the math class is piloting the draft; Alberta set a 27 November 2026 deadline for schools to tell it about optional piloting.
2. If your child is in a Grade 9 pilot class, note the PAT rule. Alberta&rsquo;s Grade 9 math bulletin says pilot classes &ldquo;will not be required to write the Grade 9 Mathematics Provincial Achievement Test&rdquo; in 2026 to 2027.
3. If your child is in Grade 6, expect the PAT in the new curriculum&rsquo;s terms. The Grade 6 math PAT is organized by the new organizing ideas, starting with Number.
4. Watch the bridge years. A student finishing Grade 9 on the 2016 curriculum in June 2027 and a student finishing on the draft will reach Math 10C with different experience of functions and quadratics. Gaps are fixable; they just need noticing.
5. Practise notation, not just answers. Ask your child to read an expression aloud and to write one sentence as symbols. Both directions matter under the new curriculum.
6. Use Alberta&rsquo;s own material. The K to 6 curriculum and the draft 7 to 9 curriculum are published on new LearnAlberta for anyone to read, and alberta.ca summarizes each grade.

### Where coding fits

Alberta&rsquo;s math page closes its overview with careers: mathematics &ldquo;can lead to jobs in computer science, construction, artificial intelligence, teaching, engineering and many other fields.&rdquo; Its K to 6 science curriculum is where computing shows up by name: the Grade 6 science PAT reports on a category called &ldquo;Computational Thinking&rdquo;.

Alberta&rsquo;s history page also says the renewed curriculum will integrate &ldquo;topics like artificial intelligence, computer science and money management&rdquo;. That sentence sits with the draft science 7 to 9 and CALM 10 work, which Alberta plans to require from 2028/29, so it is not a statement about math.

### Why we teach functions through code

A function in the draft Grade 9 sense (an input, a rule, an output) is the same idea as a function in a program. Students who write f(x) = 2x &minus; 3 as three lines of Python, as in the example above, tend to understand domain and output faster than students who only see it on paper.

That is why our Maths through coding course sits beside the algebra courses for this age group. It does not replace learning the notation; it gives the notation something to do.

## Alberta&rsquo;s new math curriculum: common questions

### When did Alberta's new math curriculum start?

Alberta's renewal timeline gives September 2022 for new Kindergarten to Grade 3 math and the 2023-24 school year for Grades 4 to 6. Alberta's history page files the whole K to 6 math change under 2022/2023.

### When does the new Grade 7 to 9 math curriculum start?

Alberta plans mandatory implementation of the new Grades 7 to 9 math curriculum in September 2027. In the 2026 to 2027 school year some schools are optionally piloting the draft.

### What is different in the new Grades 7 to 9 math?

Alberta's comparison lists, among other changes, functions in junior high, irrational numbers and set notation, absolute value and scientific notation, simple quadratic equations, polynomial identities, more theorems, pyramids and composite shapes, and data distributions.

### Is my child's school piloting the new math curriculum?

Piloting is optional in 2026 to 2027, and teachers may pilot some or all of the draft. Ask the school directly; it will know whether your child's math class is using the draft.

### Does the new curriculum affect the Grade 9 math PAT?

Yes, this year. Alberta's 2026 to 2027 Grade 9 math bulletin says classes in schools piloting the new Grade 9 math curriculum will not be required to write the Grade 9 math PAT. Other classes write it in June 2027.

### Does the new curriculum affect the Grade 6 math PAT?

The Grade 6 math PAT now uses the new curriculum's structure: its 2026 to 2027 bulletin describes Part A as questions from the organizing idea Number, and Part B as drawing on all organizing ideas.

### Where can I read the new math curriculum?

Alberta publishes the K to 6 curriculum and the draft Grades 7 to 9 curriculum on new LearnAlberta, and summarizes them on its alberta.ca mathematics page, including a snapshot for each grade.

### Is coding part of Alberta's new math curriculum?

Alberta's math page does not say so. It names computer science and artificial intelligence among careers math can lead to, and the Grade 6 science PAT reports on Computational Thinking. We teach coding alongside math for students who want it.

### What times are classes for families in Alberta?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Classes are set in Alberta time, which is UTC-6 throughout the year.
