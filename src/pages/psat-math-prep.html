PSAT math prep for the PSAT/NMSQT

The PSAT/NMSQT is the October test most US high schools give in the fall, and for juniors it is also the entry test for the National Merit Scholarship Program. Its Math section is 44 questions in two 35-minute adaptive modules, with a built-in Desmos calculator throughout. For most students it is a practice run for the SAT; for a smaller group it is the score that decides National Merit recognition. This page explains, from College Board's own pages and student guide, what the Math section tests, how the Selection Index is built, who is eligible, and how to prepare, with problems we wrote.

The calculator rule changes the strategy

College Board's SAT Suite calculator policy allows the Desmos calculator in the Bluebook app, with graphing and scientific options, or an approved handheld calculator. Calculators with a computer algebra system (CAS) are not allowed.

Graphing both sides of an equation and reading the intersection is legitimate on every PSAT math question. Students who practise this in Bluebook beforehand save real time.

Easiest to hardest, twice

Because each module climbs from easier to harder questions, the last few in a module are usually the most demanding. A student who spends four minutes on question 5 is spending time the hard questions at the end will need.

College Board also says it is better for most students to guess than leave a question blank, especially after ruling out one or two options.

Four domains

What math is on the PSAT/NMSQT?

Four content domains. Algebra and Advanced Math are the two largest in College Board's question distribution; geometry is the smallest.

PSAT/NMSQT Math domains and question distribution (Fall 2026 PSAT/NMSQT Student Guide; counts are of the 40 operational questions)
DomainQuestionsSkills College Board lists
Algebra13 to 15Linear equations in 1 and 2 variables; linear functions; systems of 2 linear equations; linear inequalities
Advanced Math12 to 14Equivalent expressions; nonlinear equations and systems; nonlinear functions
Problem-Solving and Data Analysis7 to 9Ratios, rates and units; percentages; 1-variable data; 2-variable data and scatterplots; probability and conditional probability; inference from sample statistics
Geometry and Trigonometry4 to 6Area and volume; lines, angles and triangles, including right triangle trigonometry

Why grade 10 and grade 11 feel different

Advanced Math, at 12 to 14 questions, covers quadratic, exponential, polynomial, rational and radical equations. A sophomore still in Geometry may not have met some of it in class yet; a junior in Algebra 2 usually has.

So a grade 10 result is best read as a baseline: some of the Advanced Math on it may simply not have been taught yet.

Geometry is small but not optional

Geometry and Trigonometry is 4 to 6 questions. That is too few to build a plan around but too many to ignore, because each one is a full question in a short section.

A list of common formulas is available in the Math section, so the work is in knowing which formula applies, not in memorising it.

National Merit

Does the PSAT count for National Merit, and in which grade?

Only the PSAT/NMSQT counts, and for most students only the one taken in grade 11. The NMSC section of College Board's fall 2026 student guide spells out the rules.

Which test, which year

The guide says "The 2026 PSAT/NMSQT is the qualifying test for entry to the 2028 National Merit Program," and that the PSAT 10 and PSAT 8/9 "will NOT be considered for entry."

For a student spending the usual four years in high school, the guide says "you must take the qualifying test in your third year of high school (grade 11, junior year)." Sophomores who sit the 2026 test and plan four years "must take the PSAT/NMSQT again in 2027" to enter.

Who can enter

Entrants must be enrolled as a high school student, traditional or homeschooled, progressing normally toward graduation, and planning to accept admission to college no later than the fall after they finish. They must attend high school in the United States, DC or a US commonwealth or territory, or meet citizenship rules if studying abroad.

College Board says you cannot opt out of sending PSAT/NMSQT scores to NMSC; a student who does not want to be considered contacts NMSC directly.

The Selection Index

How is the PSAT Selection Index calculated?

College Board gives the formula: double the Reading and Writing score, add the Math score, and divide by 10. The result runs from 48 to 228.

PSAT/NMSQT scores (College Board, What Do My SAT Suite Scores Mean?, read 31 October 2026)
ScoreRange
Total score320 to 1520
Reading and Writing section160 to 760
Math section160 to 760
NMSC Selection Index (PSAT/NMSQT only)48 to 228
A Selection Index worked through (our example scores)
StepCalculation
1. Reading and Writing score690
2. Double it690 x 2 = 1,380
3. Add the Math score1,380 + 710 = 2,090
4. Divide by 102,090 / 10 = 209
5. Check the range209 sits inside 48 to 228

What the formula means for math

Because Reading and Writing is doubled, each Math point moves the index by 0.1 and each Reading and Writing point by 0.2: our arithmetic from College Board's formula. Math still matters, but for National Merit it counts at half weight.

A student whose math is already strong and whose reading is not may help their index more by working on Reading and Writing, even on a math page. We say so because it is true.

What it does not mean

The index is a screening number, not a scholarship. College Board says NMSC uses Selection Index scores "to screen students who enter its scholarship programs", and NMSC sets the qualifying levels, which vary by state for Semifinalists.

For most students, the PSAT's main value is the practice it gives for the SAT and the score report that shows which skills to work on next.

Worked examples

Three PSAT-level math problems, worked by reasoning

Our own problems in three of the four domains. They are not College Board items, and the official practice is in Bluebook.

Advanced Math (ours): where is the minimum of y = x^2 - 6x + 5, and where does it cross the x-axis?
StepReasoning
1. Complete the squarex^2 - 6x + 9 - 9 + 5 = (x - 3)^2 - 4
2. Read the vertexThe minimum is at (3, -4), since (x - 3)^2 is never negative
3. Find the zeros(x - 3)^2 = 4, so x - 3 = 2 or -2, giving x = 5 or x = 1
4. Cross-check by factoringx^2 - 6x + 5 = (x - 1)(x - 5), the same zeros
5. Desmos checkGraph it: the vertex and both intercepts appear when you tap the curve
Problem-Solving and Data Analysis (ours): 120 students were asked whether they take a bus (B) and whether they play a sport (S)
Plays a sportNo sportTotal
Takes a bus183048
No bus423072
Total6060120

Question (ours): a student is chosen at random from those who take a bus. What is the probability they play a sport? Restrict to the bus row only: 18 of 48, so 18/48 = 3/8. The trap is dividing by 120 or by 60. Conditional probability always shrinks the denominator to the group named after "given" or "from those who".

Algebra (ours): an arcade card costs 7 credits to activate and 3 credits per game. With 40 credits, how many games can you play?
StepReasoning
1. Write the inequality7 + 3n <= 40, where n is the number of games
2. Subtract the fixed part3n <= 33
3. Dividen <= 11
4. InterpretAt most 11 games, since games come in whole numbers
5. Check7 + 3 x 11 = 40 exactly; a 12th game would need 43 credits

Questions families ask

PSAT/NMSQT math: common questions

How many math questions are on the PSAT?

The PSAT/NMSQT Math section has 44 questions in two modules of 22. In each module, 20 questions are scored and 2 are unscored pretest questions.

How long is the PSAT math section?

Seventy minutes: two modules of 35 minutes each. College Board says the whole PSAT/NMSQT takes 2 hours and 14 minutes.

Can you use a calculator on the PSAT math?

Yes, on the entire Math section. You can use the Desmos calculator built into Bluebook or an approved handheld calculator; CAS calculators are not allowed.

What math is on the PSAT?

Four domains: Algebra (13 to 15 questions), Advanced Math (12 to 14), Problem-Solving and Data Analysis (7 to 9), and Geometry and Trigonometry (4 to 6), according to College Board's fall 2026 student guide.

How is the PSAT Selection Index calculated?

Double your Reading and Writing score, add your Math score, and divide by 10. The index runs from 48 to 228; for example, 690 and 710 give 209.

Does the sophomore PSAT count for National Merit?

Not for a student spending four years in high school. The NMSC section of College Board's guide says such students must take the PSAT/NMSQT in grade 11 to enter, and the PSAT 10 never counts.

When is the PSAT in 2026?

College Board lists the fall 2026 PSAT/NMSQT window as October 1 to 30, 2026, and schools could also offer it on Saturday, October 17, 2026. Schools register their own students.

Is PSAT math harder than SAT math?

No. College Board gives both Math sections 44 questions in two 35-minute modules across the same four domains, and the PSAT/NMSQT section scale tops out at 760 rather than the SAT's 800. Good PSAT preparation is good SAT preparation.

When are your PSAT math classes?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays.

Are you connected with College Board or National Merit?

No. We are an independent online school, not connected with College Board or National Merit Scholarship Corporation, and we cannot promise any score or recognition.
