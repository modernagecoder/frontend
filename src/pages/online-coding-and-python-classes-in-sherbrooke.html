Online coding and Python classes in Sherbrooke

Can a Sherbrooke learner take a live coding and Python class online?

Yes. Modern Age Coders teaches coding and Python live online, in English, to Sherbrooke learners from age 6 to adult: in a group of fifteen to twenty or one to one with a teacher. Most children begin in Python and AI for Kids, most teenagers in Python for Teens and most adults in the Python Masterclass. The first class is free.

"Online" covers everything from a video you watch alone to a teacher who reads your code while you type it. Ours is the second kind, and this page explains plainly what that looks like from a kitchen table in Fleurimont or Lennoxville. It then sets out a Python path for each age, a Python exercise our teachers wrote around the Ville de Sherbrooke bike path file (which hides a lesson about maps that most adults miss), and how all of it sits beside Quebec school, CEGEP and the CEMC contests.

The difference between this and a recorded course is that somebody notices. That habit, reading the error instead of guessing, is most of what separates people who can program from people who have watched programming.

Quebec schooling

Where coding meets a Sherbrooke student at school and at CEGEP

Sherbrooke follows the Québec Education Program set by the Ministère de l'Éducation. We read the English versions of the ministry documents on 22 October 2026. Words in quotation marks come from the ministry; everything else is our summary.

The 2026 Digital Competency Framework

The ministry's framework is "organized into 12 dimensions" and names, as a guiding principle, the "integration of AI as a component of digital technology". In our reading, one of its elements does ask students to build or adapt a digital tool with programming, and computational thinking appears among its sample themes.

Secondary IV and V math

In Secondary Cycle Two students choose between "Cultural, Social and Technical (CST), Technical and Scientific (TS) and Science (S)". The program says "All of these options allow students to enroll in a preuniversity program." Our guide to the three options goes further.

CEGEP and the R score

Quebec describes a pre-university CEGEP program as one "that requires two years of full-time study", and a technical program as three years. It adds that "Universities use the R score to analyze applications". Our CEGEP computer science page covers preparation.

What the framework does not do is put a weekly programming course on every timetable. Computing in Quebec is spread across subjects and competencies, so how much code a Sherbrooke student actually writes depends a great deal on the school and the teacher. A learner who wants to be fluent by CEGEP, especially one aiming at a science or computer science program, usually needs time outside school to write programs long enough to go wrong in interesting ways.

What we are, and what we are not

Our classes run alongside school and CEGEP; they do not replace either. Diplomas, credits and R scores come from schools and CEGEPs alone, and we are not connected with the Ministère de l'Éducation, any school service centre, any CEGEP or the Ville de Sherbrooke.

Sherbrooke data project

How long are Sherbrooke's bike paths? Python, a city file and a map that lies a little

The Ville de Sherbrooke publishes its bike path layer, "Pistes cyclables", as open data under CC BY 4.0. Our students count it in Python and then discover that the length column is not in ground metres. Every figure below is our own calculation from the file downloaded on 22 October 2026.

Our count of the Ville de Sherbrooke file (1,257 rows), by TYPEVOIE. "Map km" adds up the SHAPE__Length column; "ground km" applies our latitude correction. English glosses are our translations

This is the Python that prints the right-hand columns. It needs nothing beyond the standard library and the CSV in the same folder:import csv import math SCALE = math.cos(math.radians(45.4)) # Sherbrooke sits near latitude 45.4 N counts = {} km = {} with open("pistes-cyclables.csv", encoding="utf-8-sig") as f: for row in csv.DictReader(f): kind = row["TYPEVOIE"] metres = float(row["SHAPE__Length"]) * SCALE counts[kind] = counts.get(kind, 0) + 1 km[kind] = km.get(kind, 0) + metres / 1000 for kind in sorted(km, key=km.get, reverse=True): print(kind, counts[kind], "segments", round(km[kind], 1), "km")

1. The naive sum

Students first add up SHAPE__Length and get 445.0 km. It looks like an answer. Then they read the dataset description, which gives the coordinate system as WGS 1984 Web Mercator, WKID 3857, and ask what a "metre" means on that map.

2. Measure it a second way

From the GeoJSON copy of the same layer, they convert every vertex back to latitude and longitude and add great-circle distances. That gives 312.2 km on the ground, about 0.70 of the map figure, across latitudes 45.274 to 45.518.

3. Explain the gap

Web Mercator stretches distances by roughly one over the cosine of the latitude. At 45.4 degrees north the cosine is 0.7022, so multiplying by it brings the map lengths within a fraction of a kilometre of the careful measurement in every row of the table.

What the file still cannot tell you

A one-way lane painted on both sides of a street appears as two segments, so lane kilometres are not street kilometres. The file says nothing about surface, width, winter clearing or how busy a path is. Three rows have an empty network name, and 818 rows belong to the "Réseau utilitaire". A student who writes those limits down beside the number has done real data work.

Contests and clocks

CEMC, based at the University of Waterloo, sets the contests that most Canadian schools order, and its language rules are worth knowing in a province where many schools teach in French. Dates are as CEMC listed them on 22 October 2026.

CEMC says "all students must participate through their school", so a Sherbrooke student cannot enter alone; a teacher or school has to order the contest. CCC writers may use C, C++, Python or Java, and CEMC warns that "it may not always be possible to achieve a perfect score" in some languages, Python among them. We prepare students with original problems of our own and point them to CEMC's free archive, and we are not connected with CEMC. Our CCC preparation page has the detail.

Python and AI

An AI tool would have given you 445 km

Which is the point of learning to check.

Ask a chatbot to total the Sherbrooke bike path file and it will write a tidy script that sums SHAPE__Length and prints 445.0. The code runs. The answer is wrong by more than 130 km, because the question of what a map metre means never came up. Nothing in the output warns you.

We teach Python so that learners ask that question themselves: what unit is this, where did it come from, does the result make sense against something I know? Older students use AI tools under the age rules we follow, but as a draft to inspect, never as an answer to hand in.

Quebec's own framework talks about integrating AI into digital competency; integration starts with knowing when the tool is confidently wrong. The longer argument is in why coding is still worth learning in 2026.

FAQ

Questions from Sherbrooke

Asked by parents, students and adult learners in Sherbrooke and the Eastern Townships.

Are your Sherbrooke coding classes in person or online?

Online only, and live. There is no Sherbrooke centre. A teacher teaches the class in real time and the learner codes on their own computer at home.

Are the classes taught in French?

No. Every class is taught in English. Many of our Quebec learners study in French at school, and Python code itself is the same in either language.

What happens in the free class?

A real lesson of 30 to 45 minutes with a teacher, matched to the learner's age. It ends with a suggested level, a course and a time. No card is needed.

What does my child need for an online Python class?

A laptop or desktop with sound and a steady internet connection. Python 3 is free, and the teacher helps install it in the first lessons. A tablet is not enough for typed code.

At what age can a child start Python?

Most children are ready for typed Python at about 9. From 6 to 8 we start with blocks and puzzles. Teens and adults can start Python straight away.

Do you have Python classes for adults in Sherbrooke?

Yes. The Python Masterclass takes adults from no experience to writing useful scripts, and the automation course follows for people who want to use Python at work.

Do your classes affect a Quebec diploma or an R score?

Not directly. Those come only from school and CEGEP; we issue none of them. What we offer is practice and understanding that a learner carries into the courses that do count.

Can you help a Sherbrooke student prepare for the CCC?

Yes. The 2027 CCC falls on Wednesday 17 February 2027 according to CEMC, and it can be written in English or French. Students write it through their school; we prepare them with original problems.

When are classes held for Sherbrooke learners?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Times are always confirmed on Eastern time.

Will a Sherbrooke phone number work in your form?

Yes. Numbers on the 819, 873 and 468 area codes work. Enter the ten digits without the leading 1.

How do I see the fees?

The fees section above shows the free first class, the monthly group fee and the monthly one-to-one fee, with your currency shown beside each figure. Nothing is charged before the free class.
