What school covers

Does secundaria in Sullana teach programming?

No, not as a subject. Sullana follows the national CNEB, and the secundaria week has no computing, informatics or programming area.

The week, hour by hour

On the regular school day, secundaria runs a 35-hour week: 4 hours of Matemática and 4 of Comunicación in every grade, 3 of Inglés, and 2 of Educación para el Trabajo. No hour is set aside for writing programs.

Digital skills appear in the CNEB as competency 28, which is transversal: every area is expected to use technology, and no area is asked to teach coding. A good teacher may still do so, but the curriculum does not require it.

How Piura's second-year students did in 2025

MINEDU's 2025 census assessment of 2° de secundaria, reported in INEI's 2026 compendium, found 8.3 per cent of Piura pupils at the satisfactory level in maths and 14.0 per cent in reading, against 12.7 and 18.5 nationally.

Those are region-wide figures and say nothing about any one student. But they explain why we start every teen course with careful reasoning, small steps and checking, rather than assuming the maths is already in place.

AI, safely

How should a teenager use AI tools while learning to code?

Inside the tool's own age rules, and never as a replacement for understanding. We teach both parts on purpose.

Age rules come first

AI providers set their own minimum ages. Some chat assistants accept users from 13 only with a parent's or guardian's permission until 18; others, Claude among them, accept no one under 18 at all. We check each tool's current terms before a teenager uses it in class, and families can see the list in our AI tools age guide.

Where a tool is not allowed for a learner's age, the class uses a different tool or none. Learning how AI works does not require an account: teenagers can write and train small models themselves in Python.

Explain every line

The rule in our teen classes is simple: if an AI tool wrote it, you must be able to explain it. A teenager who submits code they cannot explain rebuilds it with the mentor, one step at a time.

This is not a rule against AI. It is how professionals use it: as a fast first draft that a person checks. The Peruvian computing olympiad agrees in its own way; under the OPI's 2025 rules, using generative AI to solve the problems was not allowed.

  1. Read before you run. Before running any AI-suggested code, say in your own words what you expect it to do.
  2. Test the edges. Try an empty input, a very large one and a strange one. AI code often works for the obvious case only.
  3. Ask why, not just what. If the suggestion uses something new, look it up and write a two-line note on how it works.
  4. Keep your own version. Write the first attempt yourself, then compare it with the AI draft. The differences are where you learn.

For teenagers who want to go further with AI agents, our Codex and Claude Code course for teens follows the same principle, using only tools the learner's age allows. We never recommend an 18+ tool to anyone under 18.

National contests

Can a Sullana teenager enter the ONEM or the OPI?

Yes. Both are open to any secundaria student in Peru. Neither organiser has any tie to us: what follows is taken from their own published material, and our preparation uses problems our mentors invent.

ONEM: the school maths olympiad

MINEDU organises the ONEM, Peru's national school maths olympiad, and pupils sign up through their own colegio. Over 1.7 million took part in 2026. Every school sat its first round on 8 July 2026; the strongest moved up through the UGEL and then the region, and 237 reached the final at Huampaní on 11 October 2026.

Pupils compete at one of three levels by grade (1° and 2°, 3° and 4°, or 5°), and within each level state-school pupils (Alfa) are ranked apart from private-school pupils (Beta). At the first round they get two hours for ten problems, writing their own answers rather than ticking options.

OPI: the computing olympiad

UTEC runs the OPI, Peru's programming olympiad for school students, and charges nothing to enter. For the 2026 edition sign-ups ended on 18 March, and finalists competed on 11 April 2026.

The 2025 rulebook let contestants answer round one in Python or C++, but the final, held on site, was C++ only. So our competitive route starts in Python for the ideas and switches to C++ once those feel natural.

A practice problem of ours. A queue of applicants is sorted by score. The lowest admitted score is 170.65 and the highest score that missed out is 170.50. A new applicant scores 170.60. Without knowing anything else, can you say whether they would have been admitted? (Ours, written for this page. Answer: no, you cannot be sure; it depends on how many places were left and on the rules for ties, which the scores alone do not tell you.)

ONEM details: UGEL 06 circular (Oficio Múltiple 164-2026) and an El Peruano report of 11 October 2026. OPI details: UTEC's site opi.utec.edu.pe and its 2025 rulebook. No contest problem or official solution appears on this page.

For a fuller picture of olympiad preparation, our page on maths olympiad coaching in Peru describes how we train reasoning with original problems.

Taught in English

Will a teenager in Sullana manage classes in English?

Most secundaria students manage better than they expect, but it is not automatic. Every class is in English only, and the free first class is where we check.

An honest fit note

EF's English Proficiency Index 2025 rates the Piura region at 482 and Piura city at 484, both in its low band, and gives no figure for Sullana. Peru overall scores 519, 52nd of 123 countries; Lima city scores 555. Secundaria gives three hours of Inglés a week, which for many teenagers is not yet enough to follow a fast lesson.

So we do not promise every teenager will be comfortable on day one. A teenager who watches series or plays games in English usually follows quickly; one with only school English may do better one to one for the first months.

How mentors help

Mentors speak clearly, slow down when they see hesitation, and write key words in the chat. Code is largely language-neutral: a loop, a list or a function looks the same whatever language the class is spoken in.

Teenagers often say their English improved more in a term of coding than in a year of grammar, because they had to explain their own programs. That is a side benefit, not the purpose, and we never claim English results.

A Piura project

Why did a score of 250 miss a place that a score of 0 won?

The Universidad Nacional de Piura publishes the list of everyone who applied to its undergraduate programmes in 2024 and 2025, with scores and results. Our teenagers use it to learn how to group data correctly before comparing anything.

The data

The file is the Lista de Postulantes Pregrado Universidad Nacional de Piura 2024-2025, published by the university on datosabiertos.gob.pe under the ODC-By licence (Open Data Commons Attribution). We downloaded it on 10 November 2026 and saved its single sheet as postulantes-unp-2024-2025.tsv: 22,759 applications across the 2024-1, 2024-2 and 2025-1 cycles, of which 3,330 were admitted.

Each row is an application, not a person: the file has no applicant number, so someone who applied twice appears twice. One column, oddly, is called ESPECIALIDAD,ANIO_POSTULA and holds the programme and the year together, separated by a comma, so the programs split it on the last comma.

The question

Many Sullana teenagers ask what score gets you into a programme. We pick Ingeniería Informática, the computing degree, and ask the obvious question: what was the lowest score admitted, and the highest score that missed out?

Version 1 collects every Informática application, splits them into admitted and not admitted, and prints the two numbers. If the university simply took the top scores, the lowest admitted should be above the highest rejected.

A score of 0 admitted and a score of 250.65 turned away looks like a scandal. It is not; it is a grouping mistake. Applicants do not all compete in one queue. The MODALIDAD column shows several admission routes, each a separate competition with its own places: general exams held in April 2024, September 2024 and March 2025, and two rounds the file labels ADES and ADES 2025. Comparing scores across routes is comparing different races.

With the key changed from the programme alone to the pair (programme, route), three of the five queues behave exactly as a ranking should: in the September 2024 exam the last admitted score, 170.65, beat the first one left out, 170.50, by fifteen hundredths of a point. The 250.65 that missed out was in the ADES round, where the lowest admitted score was 254.75.

Two queues still break the rule. In the April 2024 exam, five admitted scores, 0.0, 87.0, 99.9, 108.25 and 143.3, sit far below the next one, 174.75; in March 2025 four sit below 162.0. A good analyst does not invent a reason. Some places may be reserved under separate rules, but this file has no column that says which applicants those were, so the honest program prints "check by hand" and stops there. The missing key is not in the data.

  3. Ages 16 to 18. Repeat Version 2 for every programme in the file. How many (programme, route) queues are consistent, and how many need checking by hand?
  4. Going further. Filter applications by the province of the applicant's school. Explain why you must still compute each cut-off over the whole queue, not just over applicants from Sullana.

Coding and AI for teens in Sullana: questions and answers

Are the classes in Spanish?

No. Classes are in English only, live and online, with mentors who speak clearly, slow down when needed and put key words in the chat. A teenager who answers partly in Spanish is understood and helped to say it in English.

Is there coding for teenagers in Sullana?

Yes, live online. Teenagers in Sullana, Bellavista or Marcavelica join from home on a computer with a mentor on video, in groups of teenagers only or one to one.

Does secundaria teach programming?

No. Of the 35 weekly hours in secundaria, none belongs to computing; the CNEB spreads digital skills through every subject instead. The one MINEDU Python scheme, Fábrica de Programadores, is in Lima Norte.

Can my teenager use ChatGPT or Claude in class?

Only where the provider's age rules allow. Claude accepts no one under 18, and some assistants allow 13 to 17 year olds only with a parent's permission. We check current terms, never recommend an 18+ tool to a minor, and teach teenagers to explain every line an AI suggests.

What time are classes?

Any day, any time, flexible: choose a batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at the hour that suits your family. The mentor's clock reads 10 hours 30 minutes later than Sullana's every day of the year: 18:00 for you is 04:30 tomorrow in India.

Will this help with university admission?

It builds reasoning, maths habits and real projects, but we promise no admission. University entrance exams in Peru are in Spanish and set by each university, which alone decides who is admitted.

Can you prepare my teenager for the ONEM or the OPI?

We teach the reasoning both contests reward, using our own problems, never the organisers'. ONEM entry goes through the colegio; OPI entry is free, directly with UTEC. We have no connection with MINEDU or UTEC.

What does my teenager need at home?

A computer with a keyboard, a stable connection for an hour of video and headphones with a microphone. A phone is not enough. INEI found a computer in 22.3 per cent of Piura homes in 2025, so check first.

How much does it cost?

There is one monthly fee, displayed here in US dollars and charged in dollars; the soles amount is your bank's conversion. From India the page shows rupees instead. The first class is free.

Is there a certificate?

Courses with a certificate give our own certificate of completion, which records the work done. No Peruvian school or university recognises it as a qualification, and MINEDU plays no part in it.
