Coding and AI classes in Oregon

Do Oregon students need computer science to graduate?

No separate computer science credit appears in the Oregon Department of Education’s 24-credit Oregon Diploma table, though its three math credits may come from courses aligned to Algebra, Geometry and Data Reasoning standards. In 2023 to 2024, 195 of 347 Oregon high schools offered computer science, according to Code.org (2025). We teach Oregon students live online, from game making to Python, data science and AP Statistics. Modern Age Coders teaches this live online for US students, in a batch or one to one, and the first class is free.

Oregon’s diploma is built around credits, Essential Skills and personalized learning requirements, and computer science is not one of the named credit areas. That does not make coding irrelevant to an Oregon transcript. The math requirement names Data Reasoning alongside Algebra and Geometry, and the department lists several kinds of evidence a school can accept when it awards credit. This page lays out the diploma as ODE publishes it, the access and participation figures for Oregon high schools, the two time zones inside the state, and a project on fifteen years of State Library data that teaches students how dates in a dataset can quietly mislead.

The Oregon Diploma

Twenty-four credits, and none labelled computer science

The credit table as the Oregon Department of Education publishes it on its Oregon Diploma page, read on 18 October 2026. The total of 24 is the department’s own figure.

Three parts to a diploma

ODE lists three things a student must do: demonstrate proficiency in the Essential Skills, complete personalized learning requirements, and earn the credits above. The State Board has suspended the Essential Skills assessment as a diploma requirement through the 2027 to 2028 school year.

Districts can add more

The department notes that districts may set requirements beyond the state minimum, so a family’s own district website is the place to check for anything extra, computer science included.

Where coding can sit

With no computer science line, a coding course usually appears as an elective or within one of the grouped areas, depending on how the district codes it. We do not assume which; the counselor knows.

We looked for an Oregon computer science standards page from the ODE standards index and site map and did not find one on 18 October 2026, so this page makes no claim about Oregon computer science standards. What the diploma page does say plainly is that Data Reasoning is one of the standards families behind the math credits, which is a natural home for programming with data.

How credit is awarded

Four kinds of evidence Oregon schools may use

ODE’s Options for Awarding Credit page says credit may be awarded when a student meets defined levels of proficiency, and lists approved sources of evidence, established in OAR 581-022-2025.

Approved sources of evidence for awarding credit, as ODE lists them
Source of evidenceWhat ODE describes
Evidence generated in coursesStudent work, teacher-student contacts and class sessions, judged by the teacher
Stand-alone assessmentsSummative assessments designed to certify the defined level of proficiency
Collections of evidenceA student-created collection of evidence scored against rubrics
Prior masteryDocumentation such as certification of training, letters, diplomas or awards

This is the department describing what schools may accept, not a promise that any particular outside class earns credit. We make no such claim. A student who builds real projects outside school does, however, end up with exactly the kind of work that can be shown, explained and assessed if a school chooses to look at it, and the decision rests entirely with the school and district.

A portfolio habit worth starting early

Students in our classes keep every finished project, with a short note on what it does and what they learned. Whether or not it is ever used for credit, a folder of working code is the clearest answer to the question of what a student can actually do.

Signature project

Fifteen years of Oregon libraries, and the trap in the Year column

The State Library of Oregon publishes annual statistics for every public library on Oregon’s Open Data Portal, collected under the state’s annual report statute. Students build a time series and meet a classic date problem on the first line.

The label is not the year

The dataset says reporting periods are identified by the year the report was submitted, so FY2009-2010 is stored as 2010. A student who plots Year without reading that note shifts every point by months.

Use the real dates

Each row also carries a start date and an end date, July 1 to June 30. Students parse those into proper dates and plot against the fiscal-year end, which makes the series honest on a calendar axis.

Fields arrive late

The e-book circulation field is filled only in the 2025 rows, for 133 libraries, with a median of 4,722. Fifteen years of zeros would be wrong; the honest chart starts where the data does.

The working code is short: df["fy_end"] = pd.to_datetime(df["enddate"]), then df.groupby(df["fy_end"].dt.year)["programsya12_18"].median(). Students use the median rather than a sum because library sizes vary enormously, and because adding up every library would produce a state total the State Library may report differently. The table has 2,183 rows across sixteen report years, with between 133 and 144 libraries in each. The data records what each library reported, not why a year rose or fell, and we draw no conclusions about the changes.

Every median here is ours

The medians are our calculation from one download of the State Library of Oregon dataset, which sets no licence field and credits the State Library. They are teaching results, not official statistics, and a student rerunning the code after the next annual update will get slightly different numbers.

Class times

Pacific Time for most of Oregon, Mountain for most of Malheur County

In the IANA time zone database, Oregon is on Pacific Time except the northern three quarters of Malheur County, which keeps Mountain Time under the America/Boise zone, described as Mountain for south Idaho and east Oregon.

One example lesson in both Oregon zones and in India (our worked example)

Tell us your zone

Families in the Mountain Time part of the state simply tell us so, and every time we send is written in their own zone rather than Portland’s.

The changeover dates

Both zones follow the US rule: clocks fall back on November 1, 2026 and spring forward on March 14, 2027, the first Sunday of November and the second of March.

Why India moves

India stays on one offset all year, so the teacher’s hour shifts when Oregon’s clocks do, while the Oregon class time stays fixed.

Two zones, one bug to avoid

A scheduler that assumes all of Oregon is Pacific sends Malheur County families the wrong hour. Students who look up a zone by place, America/Los_Angeles or America/Boise, before formatting a time have learned to check an assumption that most code never questions.

Coding and AI

Would an AI notice that 2010 means the 2009 to 2010 fiscal year?

Only if someone points it to the dataset note, which is exactly the habit we teach.

Ask an AI assistant to chart the Oregon library series and it will happily plot the Year column as calendar years. The chart will look fine. It will also be shifted, because the State Library labels each fiscal year by the year the report was submitted. Nothing in the numbers themselves reveals that.

Students who have read the description and parsed the start and end dates know what to tell the assistant, and they check its chart against their own. That is the working pattern we want for every student: use AI to move faster, and keep the responsibility for what the result means.

Oregon’s math credits already name Data Reasoning; reading the small print on a dataset is where that reasoning starts. The longer argument is in why learning to code still pays off in 2026.

FAQ

Oregon coding and AI questions

Short answers from ODE, the published data and how our classes run.

Is computer science required to graduate in Oregon?

Not as a separate credit. The Oregon Diploma table on the ODE website lists 24 credits and includes no computer science line, though districts may add their own requirements.

How many credits does the Oregon Diploma require?

ODE lists a total of 24 credits, including 4 in Language Arts, 3 in Mathematics, 3 in Science and 3 in Social Sciences.

What changes for students graduating in 2027?

Under Senate Bill 3, students graduating on or after January 1, 2027 need 0.5 credit in Personal Financial Education and 0.5 credit in Higher Education and Career Path Skills.

What counts as Oregon math credit?

ODE says math credits must be Algebra I and above, and that courses aligned to the adopted Algebra, Geometry and Data Reasoning standards meet the requirement.

How many Oregon high schools offer computer science?

Code.org (2025) reports 195 of 347 Oregon high schools, or 56.2%, offering computer science in 2023 to 2024.

How many Oregon students take computer science?

The latest Oregon figure in the same data is 12,717 of 183,221 high school students, or 6.94%, in 2022 to 2023.

Can your classes earn Oregon credit?

We make no such claim. ODE lists the evidence schools may accept, but credit decisions belong to each school and district.

What time zone are Oregon classes in?

Pacific Time for most of the state, and Mountain Time for families in the part of Malheur County that uses it. We write every time in your own zone.

When can classes be booked?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays.

Is there an Oregon office?

No. All lessons are live online from India, and no Oregon premises exist or are claimed.
