A Sydney worked example

## Finding a hidden total from rounded percentages

The Census table of highest education for Greater Sydney gives a count and a percentage on every row, but the percentages are rounded to one decimal place. Working backwards from them is a classic reverse-percentage problem, with a twist.

If 679,878 people are 15.9% of a total, the total is 679,878 divided by 0.159, which is about 4.28 million. But "15.9%" really means anything from 15.85% up to 15.95%, so the total could be anywhere from 4,262,558 to 4,289,451. Every row of the table gives its own window like that. Because every row shares the same total, the true value must sit inside all of the windows at once.

| Highest level | Count | Rounded share | Count divided by share | Window allowed by rounding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bachelor Degree level and above | 1,423,358 | 33.3% | 4,274,348 | 4,267,940 to 4,280,776 |
| Year 12 | 679,878 | 15.9% | 4,275,962 | 4,262,558 to 4,289,451 |
| Advanced Diploma and Diploma level | 412,740 | 9.7% | 4,255,052 | 4,233,231 to 4,277,098 |
| Certificate level III | 389,224 | 9.1% | 4,277,187 | 4,253,814 to 4,300,818 |
| Year 10 | 359,130 | 8.4% | 4,275,357 | 4,250,059 to 4,300,958 |
| Year 9 or below | 266,766 | 6.3% | 4,234,381 | 4,201,039 to 4,268,256 |
| Year 11 | 125,161 | 2.9% | 4,315,897 | 4,242,746 to 4,391,614 |

### Where the windows overlap

The overlap of all seven windows runs from 4,267,940 to 4,268,256, a range only 316 people wide. The large rows set the lower edge and the "Year 9 or below" row sets the upper edge.

### The simple method wins, nearly always

One division using the biggest row gives 4,274,348, off the middle of the overlap by 6,250, or 0.15%. For almost any real question, that one line of arithmetic is enough.

### Why small rows mislead

The "Year 11" row alone suggests 4,315,897, nearly 1% too high, because rounding 2.9% hides a lot. A small percentage carries a wide window.

### What students practise

Reverse percentages, the meaning of rounding to one decimal place, writing an interval as an inequality and finding the intersection of several intervals.

Years 7 and 8 students do the single division; Years 9 and 10 add the windows; senior students can prove why larger rows give tighter windows.

### What we did not do

We never add the rows to make a total. Published parts need not add exactly to a published total, because of how the ABS protects small counts and how people answered.

The interval is our calculation from the published figures; the ABS publishes its own counts in its own tables.

## How online maths tutoring works in Sydney time

Sydney keeps Australian Eastern Standard Time in winter and moves to daylight saving time from the first Sunday in October to the first Sunday in April. Our teachers work in India time, which never changes, so the gap shifts by an hour twice a year.

| Period | Sydney clock | Sydney ahead of India by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| April to October | AEST, UTC+10 | 4 hours 30 minutes |
| October to April | AEDT, UTC+11 | 5 hours 30 minutes |

## Questions Sydney families ask about a maths tutor

### What does a maths tutor in Sydney do?

A maths tutor finds what a learner has missed, teaches it clearly and gives practice until it is secure. Our lessons are live online in Sydney time, one to one or in small level-matched groups.

### Which HSC maths courses do you teach?

All four NSW senior courses: Mathematics Standard, Mathematics Advanced, Mathematics Extension 1 and Mathematics Extension 2, in their 11 to 12 (2024) versions.

### What is a reverse percentage?

It is working back from a part and its percentage to the whole. If 679,878 people are 15.9% of a group, the group is about 679,878 divided by 0.159, roughly 4.28 million.

### Can you guarantee an HSC band or ATAR?

No. We teach the content and exam technique carefully, but results depend on the student and the exams, so we promise no band, mark or ATAR.

### Do you teach primary school maths?

Yes, from Kindergarten to Year 6, focusing on number sense, fractions and measurement.

### Are lessons in Sydney time?

Yes. Every lesson is booked in Sydney time, and we adjust for daylight saving in October and April.

### Do you help adults with maths?

Yes, in adult groups or one to one, for bridging courses, work or personal interest.

### How much do lessons cost?

The first lesson is free and needs no card. Monthly fees for group and private lessons are shown in the fees section of this page.
