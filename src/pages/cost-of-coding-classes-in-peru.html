Cost of coding classes in Peru, planned over a year

Most families ask what coding classes cost per month, which is a fair question with a misleading answer. Learning to code takes most of a year, a Peruvian school year has breaks in May, July, October and over the summer, and a computer at home costs more than any month of classes. This page sets out the full picture for children, teenagers and adults, with no competitor prices and our own fee shown in the cards near the end.

The short answer

There is no single price for coding classes in Peru: cost depends on live versus recorded teaching, group versus one to one, how many months you study, and whether you already have a computer. Plan for a year, not a month. Why prices differ

How much do coding classes cost in Peru?

Prices in Peru range widely because providers sell very different things under the same name. Five things move the cost far more than the brand, and any honest comparison has to hold them steady.

  1. Live or recorded. A video library costs little because nobody watches the learner. A live class pays a mentor for every hour. Compare recorded with recorded and live with live.
  2. Group size. One to one costs more than a group because the mentor's whole hour goes to one learner. Our groups are fifteen to twenty learners of similar age; one-to-one classes are the alternative.
  3. Classes per month. Some fees cover four lessons, others eight or more. Our monthly fee covers eight live classes, two a week.
  4. How long the course runs. A short Scratch course may take a school term; a full Python or Java course takes most of a year. The monthly fee matters less than the number of months.
  5. What you must already own. A computer and a steady connection are the largest costs that never appear in a quote. The section on costs at home has INEI's figures for Peru.

We do not print other providers' prices, and we do not print ours in the text either: our fee appears in the fee cards below, in US dollars, and a visitor from India sees it in rupees. Fitting the school year

Which months should a family pay for coding classes?

Not necessarily all twelve. Peru's school year has four teaching blocks separated by short breaks, and families often pause, slow down or speed up around them. Here is the 2026 calendar MINEDU set out for public and private schools.

What the calendar means for a budget

Our count: from 16 March to 18 December 2026 is forty weeks, of which thirty-six are teaching weeks. January and February sit outside the school year entirely, along with the first two weeks of March.

That gives three natural shapes for a year of classes: steady all year; paused in one or two school breaks; or lighter during term and heavier in the summer, when there is no homework competing for time.

What we see families do

Younger children often keep going through the short breaks, because a week off school is a good week for a project. Teenagers in 5th of secundaria often slow down in the months before university admission exams.

Summer is the busiest start time. Families planning January to March can read our summer holiday coding classes page, or the Spanish vacaciones útiles 2027 page.

A real lesson

How do I work out what a year of coding classes will cost?

Write down a plan for each month, add it up as you go, and see where the running total crosses your budget. It is a small program and a useful first lesson in Python for teenagers and adults. The amounts are invented "units", not anyone's price.

The plan

A family plans twelve months starting in March: ten units a month for group classes, nothing in July while the family travels, and twenty a month in January and February for one-to-one summer classes. The budget for the year is 120 units.

Adding the months by hand is easy to get wrong. accumulate from Python's itertools produces the running total after every month in one line.

What the program finds

The year costs 130 units, ten over budget, and the program names February as the month the total crosses 120. The overshoot comes entirely from the summer plan.

Version 2 compares three whole-year plans side by side. Keeping groups all year, or pausing in July and December and still doing one-to-one in the summer, both land exactly on budget.

Two pieces of Python do the work. accumulate(plan) turns a list of monthly amounts into a list of totals so far. next(..., "never") walks those totals and stops at the first one over budget, returning the default if none is. The habit worth keeping is the comparison of whole plans: the month that looks expensive is rarely the one that breaks the budget.

  1. Predict. Before running Version 2, guess which plans stay within 120.
  2. Add a plan. Write a fourth plan that keeps one-to-one classes all year but stops after eight months. Is it under budget?
  3. Show the margin. Change the print line to show how far under or over budget each plan ends.
  4. Use real numbers. Replace the units with the fee in our cards, in your own copy of the program, and run it for your family.

Costs nobody quotes

What else does a family in Peru need to pay for besides the classes?

A computer and an internet connection, if you do not already have them, and a way to pay by card. For many households in Peru these matter more than the monthly fee.

The computer

Coding needs a keyboard and a screen big enough to read code. A modest second-hand laptop is enough for everything up to university projects; a tablet suits only young children using blocks.

The connection

A live class needs a steady connection more than a fast one. If the home connection is shared, choose class hours when fewer people are streaming.

The card

We charge by card. The World Bank's Global Findex found that in 2024 36.7% of adults in Peru owned a debit card and 12.6% a credit card, so check whose card will pay before the first month.

A note on what is free. Peruvian school includes no computing subject, but UTEC's computing olympiads are free for every secundaria pupil, and MINEDU's Fábrica de Programadores pilot has offered Python to 1,000 pupils in Lima Norte. If your teenager qualifies for either, use it; paid classes should add to free routes, not replace them.

The teaching language

Do I pay more because the classes are in English?

No. English is simply the language we teach in, for every learner in every country, and it costs nothing extra.

What English does change is value. The learner practises technical English alongside coding, in the language of the documentation and error messages they will use for the rest of their life. EF's English Proficiency Index 2025 rates Peru 519 (moderate) and Lima 555 (high), so most teenagers and adults in the capital follow a slow, clear class comfortably, and children usually adapt within a few lessons. Our mentors, based in India, keep code on screen and type key words in the chat. More about this for families in the capital is on our page about English-medium coding classes in Lima.

Questions from Peru

The cost of coding classes in Peru: questions and answers

Are the classes in Spanish?

No. We teach in English alone, live and online, in every class and for every age. Teaching in English costs nothing extra; mentors keep their speech simple and slow, and keep the code on screen for learners still building their English.

How much do coding classes cost in Peru?

It depends on whether teaching is live or recorded, group or one to one, how many classes a month and how many months. Our own monthly fee is in the fee cards on this page, in US dollars. We do not quote other providers' prices.

How many months will my child need?

A short Scratch course can fit in a school term. A full course in Python, Java or web development takes most of a year at two classes a week. Plan the year, then review progress every three months.

Can we pause during school breaks or holidays?

Yes. Payment is monthly with no long contract, so you can pause, for example in July or December, and restart later. Tell us in advance so the mentor can plan where the learner picks up.

Is one to one worth the extra cost?

For a learner with a clear goal, a deadline, or who needs a slower or faster pace, often yes. For steady progress and the company of other learners, a group is usually enough. The free class helps decide.

Are there extra costs for materials or kits?

No. We teach coding, not robotics hardware, and every tool we use is free or runs in a browser. The only extras are a computer and an internet connection at home.

Are there free coding classes in Peru?

Some routes are free: UTEC's computing olympiads for secundaria pupils and MINEDU's Fábrica de Programadores pilot for 1,000 pupils in Lima Norte. Coding is not a school subject. Our first class is free.

What time are classes in Peru?

Any day, any time, flexible: choose a batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at the hour that suits your family. Because Lima is ten and a half hours behind India whatever the season, 18:00 in Lima is 04:30 the following morning for the mentor.

How do I pay from Peru?

Monthly, by debit or credit card, in US dollars. Your bank converts the amount into soles. A small payment-gateway charge appears at checkout before you confirm. Booking the free class needs no card.

Do you charge more for adults?

The fee cards on this page show the current monthly fee for learners in Peru for a group and for one to one. The free first class is the place to ask about the course that fits an adult learner.
