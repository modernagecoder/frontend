## Who in Miraflores takes coding classes in English?

Three groups, mostly. They share one need: they would rather learn a technical subject in the language they already use for school or work than translate it twice.

### Pupils at bilingual and international schools

A child who studies science and maths in English is better served by a coding class in English, because the words for a loop, a list or an algorithm are the words their Cambridge or IB teachers will use. Our IGCSE and IB courses follow those syllabuses and leave the marking to the school.

### Families on a posting or newly arrived

Diplomatic, corporate and academic families often reach Lima mid-year from a school year that starts in September. A live online class can begin before the move, keep the same mentor through it, and keep running while the child settles into a Peruvian school calendar that starts in March.

### Adults who work in English

Many people who live in Miraflores work for international firms, embassies or universities, or freelance for clients abroad. Python, data analysis and AI agents, taught live in English, fit around a working day and add skills that travel with the learner.

INEI's census of 2025 puts the total population of Miraflores at 126,049, about 1.2% of the 10,141,775 people in the 43 districts of Lima Metropolitana. It is a district of working adults more than of children.

### What the census shows

Of the 121,203 people the census actually counted in Miraflores (INEI adds 4,846 more it estimates were missed), 6,204 were aged 6 to 11 and 6,575 were aged 12 to 17. The largest group by far is adults of 30 to 44, at 31,906, followed by people aged 60 and over, at 29,900.

### Why the calendar matters to us

The norm sets thirty-six teaching weeks and eight management weeks, with at least 160 teaching days. MINEDU reported that 53,631 schools of regular basic education reopened on 16 March 2026 for 6,225,000 pupils across the country, so the March start is shared by almost every child in Peru.

Regional education offices and UGELs may shift the weeks for local reasons, and private schools, including international ones, often run their own term dates. Your school's calendar is the one to trust; the table is the national pattern.

### Why it matters less than you think

A live online class does not follow any school's bell. It runs on the household's calendar: lessons continue through the management weeks unless you ask us to pause, and they carry on through January and February, when Peruvian schools are on their long summer break.

Many Miraflores families start in the summer, when afternoons are free, and keep the same weekly hour when school returns in March. Others start in August, after the July break, so that a child has a full term of coding before the year ends.

Families arriving from a school year that begins in September face a different question: which Peruvian grade a child joins and what happens to the months in between. ## Does a Peruvian school teach coding as a subject?

No. The national curriculum, the CNEB, has no computing or programming area in its plans of study for primaria or secundaria. Digital skills are treated as something every subject shares.

### A competency without a lesson

The CNEB places its digital competency, number 28, among the competencies that cut across all areas rather than belonging to one. In practice a pupil may make a presentation in history or a spreadsheet in science, but no hour of the week is set aside for writing a program.

Bilingual and international schools in Miraflores and nearby districts may add their own computing courses, and Cambridge and IB schools teach computer science as an examined subject where they offer it. What a particular school teaches is for that school to say; we do not speak for any of them.

### Where our classes fit

For a child at a school without computing, our classes are the first real programming they meet. For a pupil already taking IGCSE or IB computer science, they are extra practice in the same language as the exam, with a mentor who explains the reasoning, not only the answer.

In both cases we teach alongside school, never instead of it. Schools set and mark coursework and exams; we make sure the learner understands what they are doing, and can explain it in English.

## Why take coding classes online from Miraflores instead of crossing Lima?

Because the hour you save is the hour your child spends coding. A live online class starts at your kitchen table, and the mentor is there on the screen from the first minute to the last.

### Time that goes into learning

We will not quote traffic figures we cannot source, and Lima families do not need them. Anyone who has driven from Miraflores towards the east of the city at five in the afternoon knows that a one-hour class somewhere else can take most of an evening once the journey both ways is counted.

With a live online class, the journey disappears. A teenager comes home, eats, and logs in; a younger child does the same with a parent nearby. For a family with two children at different ages, two classes at home replace two sets of journeys.

### What you need at home

A computer with a real keyboard, a camera, a microphone and a steady connection. In INEI's household survey for April to June 2026, 84.7% of homes in the Lima Metropolitana area, which for INEI includes Callao, had an internet service, and 53.3% owned one computer or more.

A phone works for watching a lesson but not for writing code, so if the household has one shared computer, plan the hour around it. Headphones help in a busy flat, and so does a quiet corner away from the television.

Online does not mean recorded. Every lesson is live: the mentor sees the learner's screen, asks questions, waits for the answer and corrects mistakes as they happen. That is the difference between a class and a video, and it is why we keep the same mentor with the same learner wherever possible.

## What does a lesson look like? A comma that breaks a file

We give this one to learners of roughly 12 upwards. It starts with the most ordinary file in the world: a spreadsheet saved as CSV. The habit it builds is simple and valuable. Before you trust a column, count the columns on every row.

### The task

We give the learner a short sign-up list, invented for the lesson: five learners, where they live and how old they are. Two of them wrote their home as "Miraflores, Lima", with a comma, so the spreadsheet put that field in double quotes when it saved the file.

The question is easy: how many learners come from each district, and what is their average age? Most beginners split each line at the commas, which is exactly what Version 1 does.

### The surprise

The average age comes out right, 10.8, because the age is the last item on every line. The district count looks right too, at first glance: Miraflores 2. Look again and the key is "Miraflores, with a stray quote mark, and the word Lima has vanished.

The last line gives the game away. Some rows split into 3 pieces and some into 4, so the file does not have the shape the program assumed. Any column after the home address would now be read from the wrong place.

**Version 1: split at every comma** (Python)
```python
lines = [
    'learner,home,age',
    'Ana,"Miraflores, Lima",9',
    'Bruno,San Isidro,12',
    'Carla,"Miraflores, Lima",15',
    'Diego,Barranco,10',
    'Elena,Surquillo,8',
]

count = {}
for line in lines[1:]:
    parts = line.split(",")
    count[parts[1]] = count.get(parts[1], 0) + 1
print(count)

ages = [int(line.split(",")[-1]) for line in lines[1:]]
print("Average age:", sum(ages) / len(ages))
print("Fields per row:", [len(line.split(",")) for line in lines])
```
It prints {'"Miraflores': 2, 'San Isidro': 1, 'Barranco': 1, 'Surquillo': 1}, then Average age: 10.8, then Fields per row: [3, 4, 3, 4, 3, 3].

The fix is not a cleverer split. A comma inside quotes is part of the data, and the CSV format has rules for that, so we let a tool that knows the rules do the reading. Python's csv module ships with every installation and reads quoted fields correctly.

**Version 2: let the csv module read the quotes** (Python)
```python
import csv

# the same six lines as Version 1
rows = list(csv.reader(lines))
print("Fields per row:", [len(row) for row in rows])

count = {}
for learner, home, age in rows[1:]:
    district = home.split(",")[0]
    count[district] = count.get(district, 0) + 1
print(count)
print("Ana lives in:", rows[1][1])
```
With the six lines from Version 1 above it, it prints Fields per row: [3, 3, 3, 3, 3, 3], then {'Miraflores': 2, 'San Isidro': 1, 'Barranco': 1, 'Surquillo': 1}, then Ana lives in: Miraflores, Lima.

Every row now has three fields, the quote marks are gone, and the full home address survives for anyone who needs it. Only then does the program cut the district out of the address. The order matters: read the file correctly first, then clean the values, never the other way round.

1. **Ages 6 to 8, on paper.** Write three friends' names and favourite fruits, separated by commas. Now add a friend whose favourite is "apples, pears". How would someone reading your list know where one item ends?
2. **Ages 9 to 11, in Scratch.** Build a list from a sentence by splitting it at spaces. Then try a sentence with a double space in it and count the items you get.
3. **Ages 12 to 14, in Python.** Predict what Version 1 prints before you run it. Then add a learner whose name contains a comma and see which version still copes.
4. **Ages 15 and over.** Add a check that stops the program with a clear message if any row does not have exactly three fields. Then write two sentences in English explaining why the average age hid the problem.

## Is English in a live class a help or a hurdle?

For most learners in Miraflores, a help. EF's English Proficiency Index 2025 gives the city of Lima a score of 555, in its "High" band, against a national figure of 519, which EF calls "Moderate".

### What the index does and does not say

EF tests adults who take its online test, so the figure describes grown-ups who chose to be tested, not children, and not Miraflores on its own. It is still a useful signal: in the capital, English is a working language for many adults, and plenty of children hear it at home or at school.

Our mentors do not assume fluency. They talk at an even pace, rephrase an instruction more simply when a learner hesitates, and demonstrate every step on screen before asking for it. A child who answers half in Spanish is understood, then given the English for what they meant.

### Why English and code go well together

Programming languages are built from English words: if, while, return, print. Error messages are in English, and so is almost every manual a learner will read later. Learning to code in English means learning the vocabulary once, in the form the learner will keep meeting.

For a bilingual pupil, the class is one more subject in a familiar language. For a Spanish-first child, the English has a job to do: the program only works once the instruction has been understood, which is a stronger motive than any worksheet.

## Coding classes in Miraflores: questions and answers

### Are the classes in Spanish?

No. Every class is live, online and taught in English only. Mentors speak clearly, slow down when a learner needs it and point to each step as they go, so a child whose English is still growing can follow. A learner may answer in Spanish now and then; the mentor understands and gives the English back.

### Do you have a centre in Miraflores?

No. We have no premises in Miraflores or anywhere else in Lima. Every lesson takes place over live video, with a mentor based in India, which is why a learner in Miraflores, Barranco or San Isidro gets the same class at the same hour.

### What age can my child start?

From 6. Young children begin with Scratch, where blocks snap together and little typing is needed. From about 9, children can move to typed Python. Teenagers and adults usually start directly with Python, or with Java if they are at university.

### When are the classes in Miraflores time?

Any day, any time, flexible: choose a batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at the hour that suits your family. Peru never changes its clocks and India is ten and a half hours ahead of it, so 08:00 in Miraflores is 18:30 in India.

### Do the classes stop during school holidays?

Only if you want them to. Our classes follow your household calendar, not a school calendar, so they run through the management weeks of the Peruvian school year and through the January to March summer break unless you ask us to pause.

### Can you help with IGCSE or IB computer science?

Yes. We teach Cambridge IGCSE Computer Science 0478 and IB Diploma Computer Science at SL and HL, as well as IGCSE and IB maths, alongside the pupil's school. The school, Cambridge or the IB sets and marks all assessed work; we help the pupil understand it.

### We are moving to Lima from abroad. Can classes start before we arrive?

Yes. Because the lessons are online, a child can start in the home country, keep the same mentor through the move, and carry on in Miraflores. When you book, you pick times on your current clock; once you arrive, you switch to Peru time.

### Are you connected with any school or the municipality?

No. We are an independent online school. We are not connected with the Municipalidad de Miraflores, MINEDU, Cambridge, the IB or any school in Lima, and we award no Peruvian qualification. Learners who complete a course receive our own certificate of completion.
