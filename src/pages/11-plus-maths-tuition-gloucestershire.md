# 11 plus maths tuition in Gloucestershire

If your child is in Year 5 this year, the Gloucestershire 11 plus has just changed under your feet. The seven grammar schools, known as the G7, have appointed a new provider, Future Stories Community Enterprise (FSCE), and moved the test "from September of Year 6 to the latter part of the summer term of Year 5". The date for this cohort is Saturday 3 July 2027. Registration runs from 8 February to 19 March 2027. The format changes too: three papers, and one of them, the Beacon Paper, asks for English and maths answered in "Short Written Answers". So a child has less time than older siblings had, must be ready before Year 5 ends, and will need to write maths answers exactly rather than pick them. This page sets out what the schools and FSCE publish, and then teaches a Year 5 topic where exact written answers matter most: decimals.

The reasons given: it "will reduce pressure on pupils beginning Year 6, and provide families with earlier results".

The county council notes that registration and administration are "all carried out by the Gloucestershire grammar schools themselves". Qualifying scores and admissions are for the schools; we do not advise on them.

## Where the maths will be, and how it is answered

Pate's lists the three FSCE papers for 2028 entry. Two of them contain maths, answered in two different ways.

### Adventure Paper

"English and Maths, with Multiple Choice Answer options." Maths here looks like most 11 plus maths: choose the right option and mark it.

### Beacon Paper

"English and Maths, where you respond with Short Written Answers." No options. The child writes the answer, so its exact form matters.

### Compass Paper

Any school subject, from art to science, "with Multiple Choice Answer options". Maths may appear, but it is not the focus.

The Beacon Paper is the real change for maths. On a multiple-choice paper, a child who works out 2.5 when the answer is 0.25 can often spot that 2.5 is not offered and look again. On a short-written-answer paper, 2.5 simply goes down, and it is wrong.

Short written answers reward exactness: the right number of decimal places, the right unit, the right form. Those are learnable habits, and they are most easily learned on Year 5 topics where the form of the answer carries meaning, of which decimals are the clearest.

The July date changes the timing of everything. FSCE says the content is what has been "taught up to the end of Year 5", but the test is sat before Year 5 ends. Topics that a school teaches late in the summer term may only just have been met. We plan to finish the Year 5 maths curriculum by Easter, leaving the summer term for practice in both answer styles.

Registration closes on 19 March 2027, months before the test. A family deciding in the spring still has time to prepare, but not to postpone the decision.

## Decimals to three places, written exactly

Year 5 is where decimals reach thousandths. The curriculum asks children to "read, write, order and compare numbers with up to 3 decimal places" and to round and scale them.

**Year 5 decimals, as short written answers (our own questions)**

| Skill | Our question | Answer to write |
|---|---|---|
| Order | Write 0.305, 0.35, 0.3 and 0.053 in order, smallest first. | 0.053, 0.3, 0.305, 0.35 |
| Round to 1 decimal place | Round 4.67 to one decimal place. | 4.7 |
| Round to a whole number | Round 4.67 to the nearest whole number. | 5 |
| Multiply by 100 | What is 3.45 × 100? | 345 |
| Divide by 1,000 | What is 72 ÷ 1,000? | 0.072 |
| Thousandths as fractions | Write 0.007 as a fraction. | 7/1000 |

The ordering question catches most children. 0.35 looks smaller than 0.305 because 35 is less than 305. Writing them all to three places, 0.350 and 0.305, shows the truth at once. We teach that step, pad with zeros and compare, until it is automatic.

Multiplying and dividing by 10, 100 and 1,000 is the other big source of written errors. Children who were taught to "add a zero" write 3.450 for 3.45 × 100. The idea that digits move and the decimal point stays still fixes it for good.

On a short-written-answer paper, the form of the answer is part of the answer. 0.072 and 0.72 differ by a factor of ten. 4.70 and 4.7 are the same number, but a question asking for one decimal place wants 4.7. We practise writing decimals exactly as asked, every time.

The curriculum links decimals to fractions throughout Year 5, including thousandths. A child who can move between 0.007 and 7/1000, or 0.25 and a quarter, has several routes to an answer and a way to check it.

## Earlier, and in both answer styles

FSCE says its test aims to remove any need for tutoring. We take that seriously; here is what we do and do not do.

We teach the Year 5 maths curriculum properly and early, because a July test needs it finished early. We practise answers in both styles, multiple choice for the Adventure Paper and short written answers for the Beacon Paper, on the same topics so that neither becomes a weakness.

We do not claim to know what FSCE will ask. Its guides say question types and content change year to year, and we have never seen an FSCE paper. Our questions are our own.

We do not teach English, or the other subjects the Compass Paper may draw on. Families should plan those separately, and FSCE's familiarisation guides describe what each subject looks for.

For the 2027 cycle, Pate's FAQ suggested that, beyond familiarisation material, preparing well means reading books and using mathematics regularly. That remains good advice under the new provider, and it is what our lessons are built around.

FSCE is blunter than most test providers. Its Gloucestershire FAQ asks "Should my child have a tutor to prepare for the test?" and answers "No", adding that it does not want any parent to have to pay a tutor, and that "There are NO commercial 11+ books or past papers endorsed by FSCE." We think families should hear that before they hear from us. What we offer is teaching of the Year 5 maths curriculum, which many families choose for its own sake; parents who can teach it themselves do not need us for this test. Older GL-style practice books will not match the new papers either way.
