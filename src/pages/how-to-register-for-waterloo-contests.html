How to register for Waterloo contests and the CCC

Parents who search for a sign-up page for the Waterloo contests do not find one, because there is none for students. CEMC, the University of Waterloo's Centre for Education in Mathematics and Computing, deals only with schools, and every one of its math and computing contests, the Canadian Computing Competition included, is entered through a school that orders it. This guide quotes what CEMC itself says on its how-contests-work page, its school application form and its ordering terms, then sets out the three routes that follow from those rules, with a timeline worked backwards from the 2027 deadlines.

What these rules add up to

Read together, the rules leave no gap for a direct sign-up. A family cannot open an account, because accounts are for educators. A student cannot use the school's account, because the password must not be shared with students or parents. And a student cannot write from home, because every contest is supervised in person at the school that ordered it.

What is left is simple, if not always easy: a school has to order the contest, and the student writes it there.

Why CEMC works this way

CEMC does not explain the policy in one sentence, but its rules point to supervision. Its standards require participants to be "supervised in-person during the entirety of the contest writing", and the school that orders a contest is answerable for that.

The same standards carry real weight. CEMC says that a participant who breaks contest rules can be disqualified and lose invitations, and that "any current or future offers of admission to the University of Waterloo may be revoked."

Three routes

The three ways a student ends up writing a CEMC contest

Every successful entry follows one of three paths. Work out which one applies before the ordering deadline, not after it. The path labels and order of checks are ours; every rule inside them is CEMC's.

  1. Ask the school first, in writing. Email the math or computer science department and name the contest and date, for example the Canadian Computing Competition on Wednesday 17 February 2027 or the Pascal Contest on 23 or 24 February 2027.
  2. If the answer is yes, follow route 1. For a math contest the teacher counts the student into the school's order. For the CCC the student also creates a CCC Online Grader account.
  3. If the school has no CEMC account, ask whether the principal or head of math would apply. Route 2 is the one that helps the most students, because the school can then order every year.
  4. If the school declines, move straight to route 3. Find one or two nearby schools known to take part and contact their head of math. A sample message is in the next section.
  5. If the student is home-schooled, contact CEMC through its contact form, as its application form says. Do this early in the school year.
  6. Whatever the route, confirm the order is placed before CEMC's deadline, and ask which room, which day and what time the student should arrive.

Route 1

Writing at your own school: what happens for math contests and for the CCC

When the school already takes part, entry is mostly the teacher's work. The steps differ a little between CEMC's math contests and the Canadian Computing Competition, because the CCC runs on its own online grader.

For a CEMC math contest

The contest teacher orders through CEMC's Contest Supervisor Portal using the school's nine-digit CEMC School Number, before that contest's deadline. The student does nothing online.

CEMC lets a student write a contest meant for a higher grade, but not a lower one. Its how-contests-work page says students working ahead "may choose to write the contest for the more advanced grade but cannot write a contest that is below their official grade level."

For the Canadian Computing Competition

CEMC's CCC page asks the supervisor to give each participant the school number, "but not the confidential password", so the student can create a CCC Online Grader account. The teacher then approves the student from a list of unverified users.

The CCC is unusually forgiving about timing. CEMC will still add a writer right up until the contest begins, as long as their grader accounts are authenticated, and the school is invoiced afterwards.

Route 2

When the school has never ordered: the CEMC school account

A school that has never taken part needs a CEMC school account before it can order anything. Parents cannot apply on its behalf. The application form itself asks the person filling it in to confirm "I confirm that I am not a student."

  1. The right person applies. CEMC says the principal or head of math submits the New School Application. School accounts belong to the school, not to a teacher: CEMC says "School accounts are not individual educator accounts."
  2. CEMC reviews it by hand. Approval is not automatic. CEMC gives about three weeks and warns that it "cannot guarantee that an application will be approved by the ordering deadline." It also "reserves the right to decline any school application."
  3. The school receives its credentials. On approval, CEMC sends a nine-digit CEMC School Number and a confidential password, used to order contests, see results and print certificates.
  4. The teacher orders. With the account open, the teacher orders each contest before its own deadline through the Contest Supervisor Portal.
  5. The account lasts. For schools already registered, CEMC emails the number and password to the head of math contact each September, so the school does not apply again next year.

How a parent can help without overstepping

Send the teacher the two links that matter, CEMC's How contests work page and the New School Application form, and the deadline for the contest your child wants. Most teachers who decline are worried about the paperwork, and three weeks of review is easier to accept when they see it months ahead.

Offer practical help with what is the family's to give: covering the student's contest fee, and being on time on the day. The supervision itself must be done by the school.

Fees, and what schools may charge

CEMC charges schools a per-participant fee for most contests and sets a cap on what reaches families. Its ordering terms say "The CEMC does not permit schools to charge participants a contest fee higher than the fees published on the CEMC website."

CEMC is just as firm about its free materials: "Under no circumstances shall anyone be permitted to charge fees for accessing past contests and other CEMC resources for practice and/or preparation." Our fees pay for live teaching, never for access to CEMC's papers.

Route 3

Writing at another local school: how to ask

CEMC names this route itself, on its how-contests-work page: a student whose school is not participating can look for another local school willing to let them join. CEMC does not set out how to ask, so the approach below is ours.

  1. Pick schools that already take part. A school with a contest teacher and a CEMC account can add one more writer far more easily than one starting from nothing. Schools that run CEMC contests often mention them on their websites or in newsletters.
  2. Write to the head of math, not the front office. Name the contest, the date and the student's grade, and offer to cover the student's contest fee.
  3. Ask early. At least a month before CEMC's ordering deadline gives the host school time to add the student without rushing.
  4. Accept the school's rules completely. CEMC's standards say a student may write only at the school that ordered the contest, under that school's supervision, so the host school's arrangements apply in full.
  5. Say thank you, then ask about next year. A good first sitting makes the second request easy.

A host school is doing a favour, and may decline for reasons of space, staff or timing. A polite no is not the end: there are usually several schools within reach in a Canadian town or city, and the request costs each of them only a few minutes to answer.

Home-schooled students

Home-schooled students and Waterloo contests: what CEMC says, and what it does not

We looked for CEMC's guidance for home-schooled students on its how-contests-work page, the contest pages, its ordering terms and its school application form. The only mention we found is one sentence on the New School Application form.

What a home-schooling family can do

Follow CEMC's own direction: contact CEMC for assistance, through the contact form on its website, and do it in September or October rather than a few weeks before a deadline.

The another-local-school route on How contests work is worded for any student whose own school is not participating. Whether CEMC treats a home-schooled student that way is for CEMC to say, so ask it in the same message.

What we will not tell you

We will not describe a home-school procedure that CEMC has not published, and we cannot arrange a sitting. An online class is not a school that has ordered the contest, and CEMC does not allow contests to be written remotely.

Other organisers work differently. The Canadian Mathematical Society's COMC has had in-person Open Writing Centres, "Only for students whose school is not participating", though registration for the 2026 COMC closed on 22 October 2026.

Sources: the CEMC New School Application form, CEMC contact page, and CMS, COMC 2026, read 22 October 2026.

Our arithmetic: 11 February 2027 minus 21 days is 21 January 2027. We suggest mid-December instead because CEMC's review is done by hand and comes with no promise of approval by a deadline; the extra weeks absorb the winter break. The same pattern works for every CEMC contest: find the deadline in CEMC's 2026/27 table, count back three weeks for the latest application, then add a margin.

A checklist for the day

Know the room and the arrival time the school gives you. CEMC asks schools to run contests close to the start of the school day, in the school's own time zone.

For the CCC, know the grader username and password, and have tried the Practice Contest. For paper contests, bring what the school says is allowed and nothing else.

After the contest

Results go to the school, not to the family. Ask the contest teacher when they arrive, and whether the school prints certificates.

CEMC asks participants to consent to their names appearing in public honour rolls if their score is high enough, unless they opt out. Ask the school how to opt out if you prefer that.

Questions from Canadian families

Registering for Waterloo contests: common questions

Can my child register for a Waterloo contest on their own?

No. CEMC rules out individual registration: every student takes part through a school. A teacher orders the contest, and the student writes it in that school.

How do I register for the CCC?

Through a school that orders it. The student opens a CCC Online Grader account using the school's CEMC number (never its password), and the teacher then approves it. CEMC's ordering deadline for the 2027 CCC is Thursday 11 February 2027.

What if my child's school does not take part?

CEMC points to one fallback: a nearby school that agrees to let the student join its writers. The sitting then happens there, under that school's supervision. The host school is free to decline.

Can a home-schooled student write a CEMC contest?

The only mention of home schools we found is on CEMC's New School Application form, which says parents, students, home schools and others should contact CEMC for assistance. We suggest doing that early in the school year, through CEMC's contact form.

Can a parent open a CEMC school account?

No. CEMC says only educators may request a school account, and that the principal or head of math submits the application. Parents are directed to contact CEMC instead.

How long does a new CEMC school account take?

A person at CEMC reviews each application, which can take as long as three weeks, and the form adds that approval by the ordering deadline cannot be guaranteed. A school should apply well before the deadline of the contest it wants.

Can my child write a CEMC contest online from home?

No. CEMC bans remote and unsupervised writing, and the sitting must happen at the ordering school. That holds for the online CCC as well as paper contests.

Can my child write a contest for a higher grade?

Yes. CEMC lets a student working ahead write the contest for a more advanced grade, but not one below their official grade. A student may write only one contest from each contest family in a school year.

Can Modern Age Coders register my child?

No. We are not connected with CEMC and cannot order contests or host sittings. We teach the preparation, and we will help you work out which entry route fits your situation.

When are your preparation classes for Canadian families?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. We agree a time in Eastern time, then give it to you in your own province's clock.
