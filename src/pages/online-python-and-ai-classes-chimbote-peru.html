Why Python

What is Python, and why do so many people start with it?

Python is a programming language that reads almost like short English sentences. It is used for websites, data, science, automation and most AI work, and it is the language Peru's education ministry chose for its own school programming pilot.

Short, readable, and everywhere

A first Python program can be one line long, and a beginner can usually guess what a line does before running it. That matters for a learner who is also following lessons in English: if temperature > 22: means what it says.

The same language then carries a learner a very long way. The programs on this page read a file of 14,316 sea readings, group them by month and compare averages in about twenty lines, using nothing that a teenager could not learn in a term.

What MINEDU chose for schools

Under the Fábrica de Programadores scheme MINEDU announced in 2025, 1,000 Lima Norte pupils in the last two years of secundaria (4° and 5°) learn Python, and those who finish both stages may sit PCEP, an entry-level Python certificate from the Python Institute.

It is a Lima pilot, and secundaria everywhere else, Chimbote included, still has no computing area in its 35-hour week. A Chimbote student who wants the same path takes it outside school, which is what our teen Python course and the PCEP preparation course offer.

Live, online

What happens in a live online Python class taken from Chimbote?

It is a real class at a set hour, with a mentor who sees your screen and hears you, not a recorded video. These are the steps from first message to a regular class.

  1. Send a message. Use the form on this page or WhatsApp, in English, and say who the classes are for and roughly when suits you.
  2. Pick a time in your own clock. The booking page shows times in Peru time; the mentor sees the same moment in India time.
  3. Join the free class. A link opens a video call in the browser. The mentor asks a few questions, gives a short puzzle and helps the learner write a first small program.
  4. Get a recommendation. After the class you hear which course fits, which group, and whether one to one would suit better.
  5. Start the regular classes. The same mentor, at the same hours each week. The learner shares their screen and writes the code; the mentor watches, asks and guides.
  6. Practise between classes. A short task after each class, checked at the start of the next one, so problems surface early.
  7. See the progress. Learners keep every project they make, and families of children under 13 get a short written update after classes.

What you need

A laptop or desktop computer with a keyboard, a connection that holds a video call for an hour, and headphones with a microphone. Python is free to install, and for the first weeks a browser-based editor is enough.

A phone or a tablet without a keyboard is not enough for Python. With 30.3 per cent of Áncash households owning a computer in 2025, we would rather say this before you book than after.

What a group class is like

A group has fifteen to twenty learners of similar age and level. The mentor explains a short idea, everyone builds it on their own machine, and learners share their screen when stuck so the whole group learns from the fix.

One-to-one classes follow the same rhythm with one learner, at hours agreed with the mentor, and suit adults with an irregular shift pattern or a teenager preparing for an exam.

AI lessons

What do the AI classes actually teach?

How AI works and how to use it with judgement, matched to age. We teach learners to understand and check AI, not to hand their thinking to it.

Children, 9 to 12

How a computer can learn from examples, shown with tiny Python programs the child writes: a guesser that improves, a sorter that makes mistakes. No chatbot accounts. Children learn that AI can be confidently wrong, and how to spot it.

Teenagers, 13 to 18

Machine learning with real data: training a model, testing it on data it has not seen, and measuring how often it is right. Teenagers may use AI coding tools only where the provider's own age rules allow, and many require a parent's permission under 18.

Adults

Generative AI, prompt design, automation with Python and AI agents, always with the human checking the output. Adults learn where AI saves time and where its answers have to be verified line by line.

Whatever the age, the rule in our classes is the same: if AI wrote it, you must be able to explain it. A learner who pastes code they cannot explain is asked to rebuild it step by step, and that is usually where the real learning happens. Our page on learning to think rather than copy explains the approach in more detail, and the AI tools age guide lists what each age may use.

Taught in English

Is English a problem for Python learners in Chimbote?

For some, at first. Every class is taught in English only, and we would rather be honest about who finds that easy and who needs time.

What EF's 2025 index says

EF's English Proficiency Index 2025 gives the Áncash region a score of 475, in its low band, and publishes no score for Chimbote itself. Peru as a whole scores 519 and ranks 52nd of 123 countries, in the moderate band; Lima city reaches 555, in the high band.

So a learner in Chimbote is more likely than one in Lima to find a class in English demanding at first. That is not a reason to give up; it is a reason to start with the free class and see.

Where Python makes it easier

Most of Python's vocabulary is a handful of plain English words such as if, for, while and print, and learners who code for a few weeks often find their technical English grows faster than their general English. Mentors keep instructions short, repeat them in simpler words, and type key terms in the chat.

Adults who read technical documentation in English, and teenagers at bilingual schools, usually follow from the first class. A learner with very little English is welcome to try, and the mentor will say honestly after the free class whether one to one at a slower pace would serve them better.

A Chimbote project

Is the sea off Chimbote warmer than normal in 2026?

IMARPE, the Instituto del Mar del Perú, measures the sea-surface temperature every day at the pier of its Chimbote laboratory and publishes the readings as open data. Our teenage and adult learners use it to learn one of the most useful ideas in data work: compare like with like.

The data

The dataset is IMARPE's Temperatura superficial del mar (TSM) registrada en los laboratorios costeros del IMARPE, released through datosabiertos.gob.pe with the ODC-By licence (Open Data Commons Attribution) and updated on 6 October 2026. IMARPE describes it as daily measurements, in degrees Celsius, taken at the piers of its coastal laboratories.

The file we downloaded on 10 November 2026, IMARPE_Temperatura_TSM.csv, has 135,234 readings from 11 laboratories. The Chimbote laboratory accounts for 14,316 of them, from 5 January 1976 to 31 July 2026.

The question

On the coast people often say the sea feels warm or cold this year. We ask learners: was the sea off Chimbote warmer than normal in 2026? The obvious method is to work out a normal temperature and see how far each month sits from it.

Version 1 does exactly that. It averages every Chimbote reading from 1991 to 2020, a common thirty-year baseline, and compares February and July 2026 with that single number.

Version 1 says both months were warm, February by almost two degrees. Then someone who has lived in Chimbote points out the problem: February is always warm. It is summer. Comparing a summer month with an average that includes the winter will make every summer look hot and every winter look cold, whatever the sea is doing.

Version 2 builds twelve normals, one for each calendar month, and compares every month of 2026 with its own. The story changes completely. February 2026 was not warm at all; it was a third of a degree cooler than a normal February. The months from March onwards were warmer than their own normals, and the gap grew month by month: July 2026, at 22.70 degrees, was 4.14 degrees above a normal July. Version 1 had the right instinct about July and the wrong answer about February, because its baseline ignored the seasons.

  1. Ages 9 to 12. Take the twelve monthly normals from the table and draw them as a bar chart with Python's turtle. Which month is the warmest, and which the coolest?
  2. Teenagers, 13 to 15. Run Version 1 and Version 2 and write two sentences a fisherman could understand about why their answers for February disagree.
  3. Ages 15 to 18. Change the baseline to 1981 to 2010. Do the 2026 differences grow or shrink, and what does that say about the choice of baseline?
  4. University and adults. Repeat the analysis for Callao and Paita from the same file. IMARPE also publishes a separate anomaly dataset on the same platform: compare your own differences with theirs and explain any gap.

At the hour you name. Chimbote never changes its clocks, and India stays 10 hours 30 minutes in front.

Time rules: Peru uses UTC minus five hours throughout the year and has had no clock change since 1994 according to the America/Lima zone of the tz database, with INACAL keeping the legal hour. India uses UTC plus five and a half hours with no seasonal change, so the difference never moves.

Adults in Chimbote often take a late-evening class after work; teenagers usually prefer late afternoon once secundaria is over; younger children learn more easily in the morning during the January to March holidays. You choose the hour in your own clock when you book, and it stays the same through the year because neither country changes its clocks.

Chimbote learners ask

Python and AI classes in Chimbote: questions and answers

Are the classes in Spanish?

No. They are taught only in English, live and online. Mentors keep sentences short, repeat instructions in simpler words and type key terms in the chat, and Python's own keywords are short English words that learners pick up quickly.

Can I learn Python online from Chimbote?

Yes. Learners in Chimbote and Nuevo Chimbote join live classes from home on a computer, with a mentor on video. Nothing is recorded in place of a teacher; every class happens at an agreed hour.

What age should someone start Python?

Around 9 for children who can type a little; younger children usually begin with Scratch blocks. Teenagers and adults can start from zero at any age.

Do the AI classes use chatbots with children?

No. Children learn how AI works by writing small programs themselves. Teenagers use AI tools only where the provider's age rules allow, and some require a parent's permission under 18. Adults learn generative AI and agents with the output always checked by a person.

When could I have class?

Any day, any time, flexible: choose a batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at the hour that suits your family. Chimbote and India are always 10 hours 30 minutes apart, so 19:00 in Chimbote is 05:30 the next day in India.

Do I need my own computer?

Yes, a computer with a real keyboard and a connection that holds a video call. A phone is not enough for Python. In 2025, 30.3 per cent of households in Áncash had a computer (INEI).

Can a secundaria student prepare for PCEP?

Yes. From about 13, learners can join our course for the two Python Institute exams, PCEP and PCAP. The exam itself is run and awarded by the Python Institute, not by us.

Will these classes help me get a job?

They teach real skills you can show, but we promise no job, salary or university place. What we can promise is work you have built yourself and can explain.

How do payments work?

Each month, in US dollars: that is the figure on this page and the currency your card is charged in, and your bank works out the soles. From India the page shows rupees. The first class costs nothing.

Is there a certificate?

Courses that end in a certificate give one we issue ourselves, recording the work completed. No Peruvian body recognises it as a qualification, and MINEDU, SUNEDU and the universities have no part in it.
