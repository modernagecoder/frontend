The papers

How are the Edexcel and AQA A level maths exams organised?

Both specifications set three papers of equal weight and cover the same national content. They differ in where the statistics and the mechanics sit, and that changes how a pupil should revise.

Where the AQA cell says "see the board's current rules", we did not find the rule in the pages we read, so we do not state it.

The practical difference is this. An Edexcel pupil meets statistics and mechanics together in one paper, Paper 3, after two papers of pure maths. An AQA pupil meets mechanics in Paper 2 and statistics in Paper 3, each next to further pure questions. Either way, pure maths is never finished: AQA says Papers 2 and 3 can test any content from Paper 1, and Edexcel's two pure papers can each cover the whole pure content.

Pearson grades the A level from A* to E on the total of the three papers, out of 300, and says individual papers are not graded. A weak Paper 3 can be balanced by strong pure papers, and the reverse. For revision, that means the mentor looks at where the next ten marks are cheapest to gain, not at which paper "went badly".

What is marked

What does A level maths actually reward?

Both boards mark against three assessment objectives. Their weights explain why practice must go beyond routine questions.

Three objectives

Pearson names them AO1, use and apply standard techniques; AO2, reason, interpret and communicate mathematically; and AO3, solve problems within mathematics and in other contexts.

In the Edexcel table for the whole A level, AO1 carries 48 to 52 %, and AO2 and AO3 each 23 to 27 %. Roughly half the marks, then, are not for standard technique at all.

What that means in practice

A pupil who can differentiate anything but cannot explain why a model is unrealistic, or set up a problem with no worked example to copy, is aiming at about half the paper.

In Paper 3 the problem-solving weight is higher still: the Edexcel table gives AO3 a range of 10.33 to 11.67 % of the A level in Paper 3, against 6.33 to 7.67 % in each pure paper.

The large data set is the clearest example. Pearson says its questions assume knowledge of the data set and are likely to give a material advantage to students who have studied it. Pupils who first open it in revision week lose those marks. We work through the data set from the start of Year 12, in Python and on paper, so that a question about it reads like a question about something familiar.

In Germany

Where do pupils in Germany take A levels?

Mostly at British and some international schools. There is no official count of A level candidates in Germany, so we quote only what schools publish about themselves through AGIS.

In the AGIS directory, International School Westpfalz lists Cambridge Advanced AS and A Levels among its qualifications, and Berlin International School lists the Cambridge examinations board. AGIS says every one of its members is an authorised IB school, and its directory covers members only, so this is context, not a complete list of A level schools. AGIS also states that its member schools use English as their common teaching language.

We are not connected with AGIS, Pearson, AQA, Cambridge or any school, and no school sends pupils to us. Whether a German university accepts a particular set of A levels is decided under German rules by the university and the Länder; we do not give admissions advice, and we would send a family to the university's own admissions office for that.

A pupil who sits A levels as a private candidate, for example after moving to Germany from the UK mid-course, needs a centre that accepts private candidates. Finding one is the family's task; what we provide is the teaching, to the same specification the pupil started.

A lesson

How do you set up a hypothesis test without fooling yourself?

Hypothesis testing appears in the statistics content of both specifications. Our own lesson teaches one rule: decide what would count as evidence before you look at the result.

Task (ours). A school canteen says 30 % of pupils choose the vegetarian meal. A student suspects the true share is lower. She will ask a random sample of 20 pupils. Test at the 5 % significance level. Set-up: let X be the number in the sample who choose it. H0: p = 0.3, H1: p < 0.3, a one-tailed test. Under H0, X ~ B(20, 0.3).

Before any pupil is asked, the rule is fixed: reject H0 if X ≤ 2. Three would not be enough, because P(X ≤ 3) = 0.1071 is above 5 %. The actual significance level of this test is 3.55 %, not 5 %, because a binomial variable jumps in whole steps and cannot hit 5 % exactly. Now the student asks her 20 pupils and 2 say yes. 2 is in the critical region, so there is evidence at the 5 % level that fewer than 30 % choose the vegetarian meal.

The mistake this prevents

A pupil who looks first and then decides is tempted to move the line: "2 out of 20 is 10 %, that feels low, so reject". Or, after seeing 3, to choose a two-tailed test or a 10 % level so that 3 also counts.

Choosing the rule after the data is the oldest error in statistics. Examiners test for it by asking for the critical region before giving the observed value.

How to write it in the exam

State both hypotheses with the parameter p, the distribution under H0, and the tail.

Give the critical region with the probability that justifies it, here P(X ≤ 2) = 0.0355 < 0.05.

Conclude in context, in words that do not overclaim: "evidence that", never "proof that".

  1. Write the hypotheses before the data. H0, H1, one or two tails, the significance level.
  2. Find the critical region. The largest c with P(X ≤ c) at or below the level, and say the actual level.
  3. Only then compare. The observed value is in the region or it is not; the conclusion follows, in context.

Online A level maths tutoring in Germany: questions

Can I find an A level maths tutor online in Germany?

Yes. Modern Age Coders teaches A level Mathematics live online in English to sixth formers across Germany, for Pearson Edexcel 9MA0 and AQA 7357, in a group or one to one.

Are the classes in German?

No. All classes are live and in English only, the language of the A level papers. Mentors match each pupil's pace and accent.

How many papers are there in A level maths?

Three for both Edexcel and AQA. Each lasts 2 hours, carries 100 marks and is worth a third of the A level.

What is the difference between Edexcel and AQA A level maths?

Edexcel sets two pure papers and a third paper with statistics in Section A and mechanics in Section B. AQA puts mechanics in Paper 2 and statistics in Paper 3, each alongside pure content from Paper 1.

How is the A level maths grade worked out?

Pearson says its A level is graded from A* to E on the combined marks of the three papers, out of 300, and that individual papers are not graded.

What is the large data set?

A set of real data each board names for the statistics content. Pearson says its questions assume familiarity with the data set, so pupils should study it in advance; AQA also requires familiarity with its large data set or sets.

Can a German university accept A levels?

That is decided by the university under German rules, and we do not give admissions advice. Ask the admissions office of the university you are interested in.

What times are possible?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. A lesson at 15:40 in Germany is at 20:10 for the mentor while Germany is on winter time.

Who are the tutors?

Mentors based in India, teaching A level maths live in English. One mentor stays with a pupil through the course.

What does it cost?
