## How many hours of Informatik does each Land teach?

The SWK asks for six compulsory hours in lower secondary by 2030 and four in the short term, by 2024/25. The Länder are a long way apart, and inside one Land the school type often matters as much as the Land.

### The three that meet six

Mecklenburg-Vorpommern teaches Informatik und Medienbildung in every school type from Klasse 5 to 10, at least one hour in each year. Saarland teaches two hours in Klasse 7 and 8 and one in Klasse 9 and 10 at Gemeinschaftsschulen and the nine-year Gymnasium.

Thüringen introduced Medienbildung und Informatik (MBI) in 2024/25: two hours across Klasse 5 and 6, two across 7 and 8, then one hour in Klasse 9 and one in 10. It is phasing in, and reaches Klasse 10 only in 2029/30.

### The four that meet four

Sachsen teaches one hour a week in each of Klasse 7 to 10 at both Oberschule and Gymnasium. Hamburg has required four hours at Stadtteilschulen and Gymnasien since 2025/26 and lets each school spread them over Klasse 7 to 10; Schleswig-Holstein does the same at every school type since 2024/25.

Baden-Württemberg started its compulsory subject Informatik und Medienbildung in 2025/26 in Klasse 5 and 6, with four hours in total at Werkreal-, Haupt-, Real- and Gemeinschaftsschulen, growing a year at a time.

Three Länder make Informatik compulsory but sit under four hours for all. Nordrhein-Westfalen teaches it to every pupil in Klasse 5 and 6, and Niedersachsen in Klasse 9 and 10 since 2024/25. Bayern is the most interesting case: its Mittelschule teaches one hour in every year from Klasse 5 to 10, its Realschule between seven and eleven hours of Informationstechnologie depending on the subject group, but its Gymnasium only two, one hour of Informatik focus inside Natur und Technik in Klasse 6 and in Klasse 7. The program in the next section shows why that one school type decides Bayern's place.

Behind all of this is a staffing problem. Every Land page of the monitor says the same: "Informatik ist in allen Bundesländern ein Mangelfach", a shortage subject everywhere, with too few teachers qualified to teach it. A timetable can promise an hour that a school struggles to staff.

## Why is Bayern not in the top group? The answer is a minimum

The monitor's bands read "at least six hours at all school types". A rule about all pupils is only as strong as the school type that offers least. Our short program adds up the hours the monitor gives for each school type and then compares the minimum with the maximum.

**For all pupils, or for the luckiest?** (Python)
```python
# Compulsory Informatik hours in Sekundarstufe I, added up over the grades,
# for each school type (Informatik-Monitor 2026/27, Land pages).
hours = {
    "Mecklenburg-Vorpommern": {"every school type": 1 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 1},
    "Saarland": {"Gemeinschaftsschule": 2 + 2 + 1 + 1, "Gymnasium (G9)": 2 + 2 + 1 + 1},
    "Thüringen": {"every school type": 2 + 2 + 1 + 1},   # 2 in 5/6, 2 in 7/8, 1 in 9, 1 in 10
    "Sachsen": {"Oberschule": 1 + 1 + 1 + 1, "Gymnasium": 1 + 1 + 1 + 1},
    "Hamburg": {"Stadtteilschule": 4, "Gymnasium": 4},
    "Schleswig-Holstein": {"every school type": 4},
    "Bayern": {"Mittelschule": 1 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 1, "Realschule": 7, "Gymnasium": 1 + 1},
    "Sachsen-Anhalt": {"Sekundarschule": 1 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 1, "Gymnasium": 0},
}

def band(h):
    if h >= 6: return "6 or more"
    if h >= 4: return "4 or more"
    if h >= 1: return "at least 1"
    return "not for all"

for land, by_type in hours.items():
    for_all = min(by_type.values())     # what every pupil is sure to get
    top = max(by_type.values())         # what the luckiest pupil gets
    print(f"{land:24} for all: {for_all}  {band(for_all):12} top case: {top}  {band(top)}")
```
Run with Python 3 on 3 November 2026, it prints eight lines. Two of them carry the lesson: Bayern for all: 2 at least 1 top case: 7 6 or more and Sachsen-Anhalt for all: 0 not for all top case: 5 4 or more. The other six give the same band both ways.

### What the minimum shows

Measured by the minimum, all eight Länder land exactly where the monitor puts them: Mecklenburg-Vorpommern, Saarland and Thüringen at six, Sachsen, Hamburg and Schleswig-Holstein at four, Bayern compulsory but under four, and Sachsen-Anhalt among the six with nothing for all. Our sums of the monitor's per-grade hours, in other words, reproduce its own verdicts.

Bayern's Realschule entry is the smallest of its four subject groups, seven hours; using any of the others would not change the result, because the Gymnasium decides it.

### What the maximum would have claimed

Swap min for max and Bayern jumps to "6 or more" on the strength of its Realschule, while Sachsen-Anhalt reaches "4 or more" because its Sekundarschule teaches five hours, even though its Gymnasium pupils get none as a compulsory subject.

The best school type is what a brochure quotes. The minimum is what a parent can count on. Choosing the right one of two built-in functions is the whole difference.

1. **Check the units first.** Thüringen says "two hours in the double year 5/6", Sachsen says "one hour in each of 7 to 10", Hamburg says "four hours in total". Before adding, turn every rule into the same unit: weekly hours, summed over the years.
2. **Then decide the question.** "Can a pupil get six hours here?" needs max. "Does every pupil get six hours?" needs min.
3. **Try it.** Add Baden-Württemberg with the four hours at its Werkreal-, Haupt-, Real- and Gemeinschaftsschulen. Which number would you need for its Gymnasium before you could trust the result?

## How many pupils take Informatik in the Oberstufe?

After lower secondary, Informatik becomes a choice almost everywhere, and the choices differ more than the rules do.

### From 7 to 43 percent

The Informatik-Monitor puts the national share of Oberstufe pupils taking Informatik in the Qualifikationsphase at about 15 percent. The Land pages for 2024/25 run from 7 percent in Baden-Württemberg and 9 percent in Niedersachsen to 40 percent in Mecklenburg-Vorpommern and 43 percent in Sachsen.

The high scorers are the Länder that teach it longest lower down. A pupil who has had Informatik for four or six years is far more likely to choose it at sixteen.

### What it means for a pupil

In a Land with low take-up, a school may not run an Informatik Leistungskurs at all, and a pupil who wants computer science may find no course to join. In Sachsen, for example, the monitor says a Leistungskurs is possible only at selected Gymnasien.

## What should a family do if their Land teaches little Informatik?

Moving between Länder, or arriving from abroad, can leave a child years ahead or years behind in Informatik. Three practical steps help.

1. **Find out what the school actually teaches.** Ask for the Stundentafel (timetable of hours) and the Informatik plan for your child's year. A Land rule may be phasing in, as in Thüringen and Baden-Württemberg.
2. **Fill the gap with real programming.** A child in Hessen or Berlin may meet no compulsory Informatik at all; one who moves from Mecklenburg-Vorpommern to Niedersachsen may already know more than the new class.
3. **Keep the German terms.** School work is in German even when a pupil thinks in English. Schleife, Verzweigung, Variable and Algorithmus are worth knowing next to loop, branch, variable and algorithm.

## Informatik in German schools: common questions

### Is computer science compulsory in German schools?

In ten of the sixteen Länder, Informatik is compulsory for all lower secondary pupils in 2026/27, according to the Gesellschaft für Informatik's Informatik-Monitor. In Berlin, Brandenburg, Bremen, Hessen, Rheinland-Pfalz and Sachsen-Anhalt it is not compulsory for all.

### Is Informatik compulsory in Bavaria?

Yes, but the amount depends on the school type. The Mittelschule teaches one hour a week in Klasse 5 to 10; the Gymnasium teaches an Informatik focus of one hour in Klasse 6 and in Klasse 7 inside Natur und Technik, and two compulsory hours in Klasse 11.

### Is Informatik compulsory in Niedersachsen?

Yes. Since 2024/25 Informatik has been a compulsory subject in Klasse 9 and 10 at every general school type in Niedersachsen, according to the Informatik-Monitor 2026/27.

### How many hours of Informatik are recommended?

The KMK's standing scientific commission (SWK) recommends six compulsory hours across lower secondary by 2030, and four as a short-term step. Mecklenburg-Vorpommern, Saarland and Thüringen meet six; Baden-Württemberg, Hamburg, Sachsen and Schleswig-Holstein meet four.

### Which Länder will add compulsory Informatik next?

Bremen from 2027/28, with two to four hours in total, and Rheinland-Pfalz from 2028/29, with four hours. The monitor knows of no binding plans in Berlin, Brandenburg or Hessen.
