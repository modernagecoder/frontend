## Duqm within Al Wusta

Oman's [Governorates System](https://bur.gov.om/assets/pdf/en/governorate-system.pdf) lists Haima, Mahout, Duqm and Al Jazir as Al Wusta's four wilayats. It identifies Haima, not Duqm, as the governorate centre.

The current [Al Wusta page on the unified government portal](https://gov.om/en/al-wusta-governorate) locates the governorate office in Haima and separately lists Duqm among the wali offices. It publishes a Sunday-through-Thursday office week.

Oman's [Foreign Ministry regional guide](https://www.fm.gov.om/en/about-oman/state/oman-by-region/) describes Al Wusta as an extensive central area linking the north of Oman with the south. Three of its wilayats overlook the Arabian Sea. Duqm city, Duqm wilayat and the Special Economic Zone at Duqm are related geographic concepts but not interchangeable boundaries.

That distinction matters for data. A figure published for Al Wusta cannot automatically describe the Duqm wilayat. A value for SEZAD may not describe all residents. A port-terminal measurement cannot stand for the whole zone. Every record needs an entity identifier, geographic level, source, unit and time basis.

## Special Economic Zone at Duqm

The [Public Authority for Special Economic Zones and Free Zones](https://www.opaz.gov.om/en/zones/special-economic-zone-at-duqm) says the Special Economic Zone at Duqm was established in 2011 and covers 2,000 square kilometres. [SEZAD's official homepage](https://duqm.gov.om/en) also states the 2,000-square-kilometre area and describes 90 kilometres of coastline.

The [official SEZAD profile](https://duqm.gov.om/en/sezad/inside-sezad/profile) names a multipurpose port, ship-repair dry dock, fishing port and regional airport, together with tourism, industrial and logistics development areas. These are different assets and functions. Their capacities should never be added merely because they belong to one development zone.

OPAZ describes the zone as overlooking the Arabian Sea and the Indian Ocean beyond, near global shipping lanes. It presents request submission, plot allocation and agreement signing as three top-level steps for starting a business in the zone. A student simulator does not represent those real administrative stages.

SEZAD's historical record says Royal Decree 5/2016 expanded the zone from 1,745 to 2,000 square kilometres to include oil-storage activities at Ras Markaz. A growth calculation between those values needs the dates and scope. It should not compare an older boundary with a later infrastructure subsection.

## The Duqm tiny-base growth project

The learner builds a synthetic logistics-simulator test dashboard. Route planner A begins with two passing checks and later has six. Six minus two equals four additional checks. Four divided by the starting value of two equals two, so the relative increase is 200%.

The percentage is mathematically correct. It can still create a misleading headline because the baseline is tiny and six passing checks may remain far below what the simulator needs.

| Prototype | Earlier passing checks | Later passing checks | Absolute change | Percentage change | Honest reading |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Route planner A | 2 | 6 | +4 | +200% | Tripled from a tiny baseline; six checks now pass |
| Manifest validator B | 200 | 240 | +40 | +20% | Forty additional checks pass |
| Alert parser C | 0 | 3 | +3 | Undefined | No percentage because the baseline is zero |
| Unit converter D | 48 | 48 | 0 | 0% | No change; current quality still needs context |

Route planner A has the largest defined percentage, but it adds only four passing checks. Manifest validator B adds forty, ten times the absolute increase. Ranking by percentage alone reverses the practical scale.

Percentage change divides absolute change by the starting value. When the denominator is close to zero, a small numerator can produce a dramatic percentage. A responsible report therefore shows the starting count, ending count and absolute difference beside the percentage.

Starting from zero is a separate case. Three minus zero equals three, but dividing by zero is undefined. The dashboard displays the absolute increase and states that no percentage change is available. It does not show infinity or invent a rate.

The measurement period also matters. A change over one hour and the same change over one year do not mean the same thing. Each comparison stores start time, end time and test-suite version.

If the later prototype has more total checks, passing counts may not be like-for-like. The student stores total attempted checks and coverage. A move from two of two passing to six of one hundred passing is not an improvement in pass rate.

| Required fact | Why it matters | Failure when omitted |
|---|---|---|
| Starting value | Supplies the denominator | Tiny base remains hidden |
| Ending value | Shows current scale | Reader cannot judge adequacy |
| Absolute change | Shows what was added or lost | Relative headline dominates |
| Measurement period | Defines the comparison window | Different durations look comparable |
| Metric and test version | Confirms both values mean the same thing | Changed definitions create false growth |
| Decision threshold | Connects movement to purpose | Growth is mistaken for readiness |

Rounding happens after calculation. Rounding a tiny baseline first can substantially change the reported percentage. The project retains full precision internally and uses a documented display rule.

If the metric comes from sampled runs, the learner reports sample size and variability. One successful trial cannot establish stable performance. A plain-language caption states that two became six, an increase of four or 200%, and then gives the number required for readiness.

A child can compare towers of blocks and count what changed. A middle-school learner can use fractions and variables. A teenager can build the dashboard in Python and test zero. An adult can design metric contracts, thresholds, versioning, audit logs and accessible captions.

Every prototype and count in this section is invented. It is not data from SEZAD, OPAZ, Port of Duqm, OQ8 or an Al Wusta government service.
