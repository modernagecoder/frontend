Families

How many Indian families are there in Germany, and what do they ask for?

One official figure sets the scale, and it is the only one we use on this page.

According to Destatis, the Federal Statistical Office, 311,130 Indian nationals were registered in Germany on 31 December 2025. The figure comes from the Ausländerzentralregister, in the Destatis table of the foreign population by nationality, last updated on 2 April 2026. It counts nationals only, not families or children, and children with German citizenship are not in it; we quote it for scale and draw nothing else from it.

What a family in this position wants is simpler than any statistic, and it is usually one of three things: "Help my child with maths at the German school, in English." "Keep my child's maths at the level I had at their age." And "Teach my child to code properly, not just to play with apps."

German and CBSE

The CBSE "Classes 1 to 8" row is our own summary of where the CBSE secondary curriculum begins.

The biggest difference for an Indian parent is the early sorting. In most of Germany, children leave Grundschule after Klasse 4 and go on to different school types, and the KMK says there is no exam at that point. The school's picture of the child's work over the years carries the decision. A parent used to judging progress by test marks may need a different lens here: steady understanding through Klasse 3 and 4 matters more than any single result.

The second difference is what a 10th-class exam is. In CBSE it is one national board exam with a fixed paper design. In Germany it is set by each Land, with its own dates and rules, and a pupil at a Gymnasium on the way to the Abitur may take a different route through Klasse 10 than a pupil at another school type.

Maths

Is German school maths easier than CBSE maths?

A common question, and a fair one. The honest answer is that the two systems spend their time differently.

The CBSE Class X maths syllabus for 2026-27 gives Algebra 20 of its 80 exam marks, Geometry 15, Trigonometry 12, Statistics and Probability 11, Mensuration 10, and Number Systems and Coordinate Geometry 6 each. It describes itself as "a deliberate shift from rote learning to competency-based education", and its new optional advanced course for Class IX adds sets, logarithms, relations and functions, combinatorics and progressions.

German school maths follows the KMK standards, which list communicating, problem solving and modelling among their process competences next to calculation, so a German Klassenarbeit often asks a pupil to explain in words why a method works. Neither system is simply easier; they are hard in different places. A child who is quick at calculation but has never been asked to justify a step in a sentence can struggle in a German Klassenarbeit for exactly that reason.

What we do for the German side

Follow the Klasse and the Land: the topic the class is on this month, in the order the school teaches it.

Practise the reasoning and the wording the German tests reward, in English first, then with the German terms.

What we do for the CBSE side

For families who want it, add the CBSE depth: longer algebra, proofs such as the irrationality of √2, and board-style problem sets.

Keep the two apart in the child's head, so that extra practice never confuses the German test.

Coding

Do German schools teach coding, and what should an Indian family add?

It depends on the Land. The Gesellschaft für Informatik tracks Informatik across the sixteen Länder in its Informatik-Monitor.

For 2026/27 the Informatik-Monitor reports that Nordrhein-Westfalen teaches compulsory Informatik in Klasse 5 and 6 at all general school types, while six Länder (Berlin, Brandenburg, Bremen, Hessen, Rheinland-Pfalz and Sachsen-Anhalt) have no Informatik that every pupil must take. So two Indian families a few hundred kilometres apart can have children with very different amounts of coding at school.

What we add is the same everywhere: a real programming path, taught in English. Children aged 6 to 9 start with Scratch and block coding; from about 8 or 9 they move to Python; teenagers go on to larger projects, data and, where the school has it, the Informatik syllabus itself. Under-18s use only tools suitable for their age; we do not put children on accounts that require an adult.

A lesson

How do you write 3,11,130 in Germany? A lesson in notation

Children in Indian families in Germany meet three ways of writing the same number: lakh and crore at home, commas every three digits in English books, and dots with a decimal comma at a German school. Our own lesson turns that into maths and code.

The Destatis figure above is printed by Destatis as 311 130, with a space. In English it is 311,130. A German maths book writes 311.130. And in the Indian system it is 3,11,130: three lakh, eleven thousand, one hundred and thirty. The value never changes; only the notation does. A child who understands that is ready for place value in any language.

Both are wrong, and asking the child why is the lesson. The Indian system is not "a comma every two digits". The last three digits stay together, the thousands, and only above that do the groups become two digits wide: lakh, then crore. Version 1 broke the number from the right in twos from the start. The fix is to cut off the last three digits first.

Practice set (ours)

1. Write 4,50,000 in German notation and in German words. Answer: 450.000, vierhundertfünfzigtausend.

2. One crore is how many millions? Answer: 1 crore = 1,00,00,000 = 10,000,000, so 10 million, in German 10 Millionen.

3. A German Milliarde is a thousand million. How many crore is that? Answer: 1,000,000,000 ÷ 10,000,000 = 100 crore.

4. At school the sheet says 2,75 + 1,5. What is the answer, written the German way? Answer: 4,25.

Why it matters in a German test

In a German maths test, 2,5 means two and a half, not two thousand five hundred, and 2.500 means two thousand five hundred. A child who mixes the two systems loses marks for an answer that was right in her head.

German also uses Milliarde for a thousand million, where English says billion; the German "Billion" is a million million. The same word, a thousand times apart.

We teach the German notation for school, keep lakh and crore for home, and make the child say which system she is using before she writes the number.

  1. Find the value first. How many thousands, how many lakh, how many millions: say it in words.
  2. Then choose the notation. Indian at home, English in an English book, German at school.
  3. Check with a round trip. Convert back and see if the same value comes out, as version 2 does for all three numbers.

Coding and maths for Indian families in Germany: questions

Are there maths and coding classes for Indian children in Germany?

Yes. Our mentors in India run live online lessons, in English, for children of Indian families attending German schools, from age 6 to 18. A child joins a group of fifteen to twenty or learns one to one.

Are the classes in German?

No, English is the only teaching language, and that is deliberate: it is the language the family and the mentor share. The mentor adjusts to how fast your child works and how she speaks, and adds the German maths and Informatik words the school uses.

Do you follow the German syllabus or CBSE?

The German school syllabus first, so the child does well in class. For families who want it, we add CBSE-level depth, such as the Class X Standard maths topics, kept clearly separate.

Is German school maths easier than CBSE?

Not easier, different. German maths standards put weight on explaining and modelling in words; CBSE Class X maths gives Algebra the largest share of its exam, 20 of 80 marks. Children used to one often find the other hard in new places.

Is there an exam at the end of Grundschule in Germany?

No. The KMK says no leaving exam is held at the end of Grundschule. Most children move on after Klasse 4, or Klasse 6 in Berlin and Brandenburg.

How many Indian nationals live in Germany?

Destatis reports 311,130 Indian nationals in Germany on 31 December 2025, from the Ausländerzentralregister. The figure does not count children with German citizenship.

What times are possible?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. India is four and a half hours ahead of Germany in winter and three and a half in summer, so German afternoons are Indian evenings.

Who are the mentors?

Teachers based in India who teach live online in English. Your child keeps one mentor, who comes to know both the German school and the family's expectations.

Can my child learn coding even if the school does not teach it?

Yes. In six Länder no Informatik is compulsory for every pupil, according to the Informatik-Monitor 2026/27. We teach coding from Scratch to Python regardless of what the school offers.

What does it cost?

The monthly fee is shown on this page in euro, using the European Central Bank reference rate. Payment is in US dollars, converted by your bank. The first class is free and needs no card.
