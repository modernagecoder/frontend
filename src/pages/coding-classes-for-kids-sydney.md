Signature project

## How do you fit Sydney Harbour onto a Scratch stage without squashing it?

Seventy-one ferry wharf entries from OpenStreetMap, turned from latitude and longitude into Scratch x and y, two different ways.

| Measure | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Ferry wharf entries in our harbour rectangle | 71 |
| Rectangle size on the ground | about 18,473 m wide and 10,008 m tall |
| Scratch stage size | 480 steps wide and 360 steps tall |
| Stretch to fill the stage | 38.5 m per step across, 27.8 m per step up and down |
| One shared scale instead | 38.5 m per step both ways, using about 260 of the 360 steps of height |
| Manly Wharf on the stage | (201, 138) stretched, (201, 50) with one scale |

### 1. Find the fraction

A wharf a quarter of the way across the rectangle should be a quarter of the way across the stage. Working out that fraction is the first step, for both directions.

### 2. Stretch, and see the problem

Using a different fraction for each direction fills the stage, but each step up covers 27.8 metres while each step across covers 38.5. The harbour comes out about 1.39 times too tall.

### 3. One scale keeps the shape

Using 38.5 metres per step in both directions keeps every distance true to the map. The price is a strip of empty stage above and below the harbour, and children decide that is worth it.

### When wharves land on the same spot

Circular Quay has 11 separate berth entries in the map data, all within a few hundred metres. Rounded to whole Scratch steps with one scale, they land on just 7 distinct points, and across all 71 entries only 66 points are different. Some wharves are closer together than one step on the stage. Children learn that a screen has a resolution, and that zooming in, not squeezing harder, is how to tell nearby things apart.

All positions are our calculations from an OpenStreetMap snapshot taken on 7 October 2026, using a flat-map approximation that is fine at harbour scale. OpenStreetMap is mapped by volunteers, the rectangle is our own choice, and some entries are individual berths rather than separate wharves. We make no claim about ferry routes or timetables.

## From grid paper to a Scratch map

Scaling a picture to fit a space is maths every child meets, and code makes it something they can see working.

| Year level | What happens in class | Concept |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kindergarten to Year 2 | Places harbour stickers on squared paper using left-right and up-down steps | Position, direction and counting squares |
| Years 3 to 6 | Moves a Scratch sprite to each wharf with go to x: y: blocks and draws the harbour outline | Coordinates, negative numbers and the stage grid |
| Years 7 and 8 | Writes Python that converts latitude and longitude to stage x and y, with and without a shared scale | Fractions, proportional reasoning and functions with parameters |

### The middle is zero

Scratch puts 0, 0 at the centre of the stage, not the corner. Children who forget this draw the whole harbour in one quarter of the screen, and learn to shift as well as scale.

### Up is positive

In Scratch, a bigger y is higher up. Latitudes in Sydney are negative, so children check that Manly, further north, really does end up above Circular Quay.

### Test with corners

Before plotting all 71 wharves, the child plots the four corners of the rectangle. If they land on the stage edges, the conversion is right.

Transport for NSW, Sydney Ferries, the Scratch team at MIT, OpenStreetMap and the ABS have no part in this project and have not reviewed or approved it. The census numbers are the ABS's; the stage positions are our calculations.

## Can an AI tool draw Sydney Harbour for a child, and would it be right?

It can produce a picture quickly, but whether the picture keeps the right shape depends on a scaling choice the tool makes without asking.

Ask an AI helper for code that plots the wharves on a Scratch stage and you may well get the stretch-to-fit version, which fills the screen nicely and quietly makes the harbour 1.39 times too tall. It looks finished. A child who has worked out the scale by hand spots the distortion at once, because Manly sits much too high above Circular Quay.

What we want young Sydney coders to learn is to look at a result and ask whether it is true, not only whether it is tidy. In our lessons AI is used only in ways suited to a child's age and always with a teacher present, and spotting a convincing but wrong picture is a skill we practise on purpose.

So yes, learning to code is still worth it in 2026, because it teaches children to check what a computer shows them. A Sydney child who has fitted the harbour onto the stage without squashing it already can. ## Questions from Sydney parents

What Sydney families usually want to know before starting.

### What age can a Sydney child start coding classes?

Our live classes begin at age 6, usually in Kindergarten or Year 1. The first lessons use blocks and pictures, and typing comes later.

### What is aspect ratio?

Aspect ratio is the width of a shape divided by its height. Our harbour rectangle is about 1.85 times as wide as it is tall, while the Scratch stage is 1.33 times as wide, so fitting one into the other means choosing between filling the stage and keeping the shape.

### What is the Sydney harbour project?

Children take 71 ferry wharf entries from OpenStreetMap and convert each latitude and longitude into Scratch x and y. Stretching to fill the stage makes the harbour 1.39 times too tall; using one scale of 38.5 metres per step keeps it true.

### Why do some wharves end up on the same spot?

Because one Scratch step stands for about 38.5 metres, and some berths, such as the 11 at Circular Quay, are closer together than that. They collapse onto 7 points, a lesson in screen resolution.

### Do you plan around the NSW school year?

Yes. We use NSW year names from Kindergarten to Year 8, and lessons are planned around NSW terms, holidays and development days.

### What time are lessons for Sydney children?

After school on weekdays or at weekends, set during the free lesson and always given in Sydney time.

### How are children kept safe in online lessons?

A teacher runs and watches each live session, the groups are small, no child is signed up to an adult AI service, and parents may sit in at any time.

### Is there a Modern Age Coders centre in Sydney?

No, and none is claimed. All teaching is live and online, with no premises anywhere in Australia. A learner needs a laptop or desktop, working sound and a reliable connection, and the contact number shown is an Indian one.

### What do coding classes for Sydney kids cost?

The first lesson is free. After that a group place is USD 100 a month for two live lessons a week, around eight a month, with fifteen to twenty learners, or USD 150 a month for the same pattern one to one. Course, format and time are agreed before anything is paid.

### How big are the groups?
