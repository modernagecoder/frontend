Q2. Part A and Unit 1

Class 10 AI MCQ with answers: Part A and the project cycle unit

Questions 1 to 10. Try each before opening the answer, then read the reason: it names the option most students wrongly pick.

Part A: employability skills (one per unit)

  1. During a group discussion, Arjun nods and keeps eye contact while a classmate speaks. This is
    (a) verbal communication
    (b) written communication
    (c) non-verbal communication
    (d) visual communication
    Answer (c) No words are used, only body language. Visual communication means charts, signs and pictures.
  2. In a SMART goal, the letter M stands for
    (a) Meaningful
    (b) Measurable
    (c) Motivating
    (d) Manageable
    Answer (b) You must be able to track progress. "Manageable" is the trap: being realistic is what the A, achievable, covers.
  3. Which of these protects a computer from viruses that arrive with downloads?
    (a) defragmenting the disk
    (b) installing and updating antivirus software
    (c) raising screen brightness
    (d) deleting temporary files
    Answer (b) The others are care and maintenance tasks; none of them stops harmful software.
  4. Meera sees that students near her school cannot buy healthy snacks, and starts a small stall. Which entrepreneurial quality does she show first?
    (a) spotting an opportunity
    (b) avoiding every risk
    (c) following instructions
    (d) waiting for a job offer
    Answer (a) An entrepreneur starts by noticing an unmet need. Starting a stall involves risk, so (b) contradicts the story.
  5. Using compost and crop rotation in place of chemical fertilisers is part of
    (a) organic farming
    (b) monoculture
    (c) deforestation
    (d) heavy pesticide use
    Answer (a) Organic farming keeps soil healthy without synthetic chemicals; monoculture repeats one crop and drains it.

Every Part A unit, with its short answers as well, is covered on Part A employability skills.

Unit 1: Revisiting AI Project Cycle and Ethical Frameworks for AI

  1. Which stage of the AI project cycle comes straight after Data Acquisition?
    (a) Modelling
    (b) Data Exploration
    (c) Problem Scoping
    (d) Evaluation
    Answer (b) Collected data is explored and visualised before a model is chosen. Jumping to (a) is the usual slip.
  2. In the 4Ws problem canvas, which W asks how solving the problem will benefit the people affected?
    (a) Who
    (b) What
    (c) Where
    (d) Why
    Answer (d) Why records the value of the solution. What describes the problem itself, which is why it tempts.
  3. A set of ethical rules written only for hospitals and medical AI is
    (a) a value-based framework
    (b) a sector-based framework
    (c) a rule-based model
    (d) a utility-based framework
    Answer (b) It is built for one sector, healthcare. Bioethics is the standard example of a sector-based framework.
  4. A city picks the traffic AI that cuts total travel time for the most people, even though a few roads get slower. Which approach is this?
    (a) rights-based
    (b) virtue-based
    (c) utility-based
    (d) sector-based
    Answer (c) It weighs the overall benefit against the harm. A rights-based approach would protect every road user first.
  5. Which of these is NOT one of the four principles of bioethics?
    (a) respect for autonomy
    (b) do not harm
    (c) ensure maximum benefit
    (d) maximise the hospital's income
    Answer (d) The fourth principle is justice, meaning fair treatment. Income appears nowhere in the four.

Q3. Units 2 and 3

Modeling and evaluating models: class 10 MCQ, including the numericals

Questions 11 to 22. Unit 3 carries the most objective items, and five of its seven below need a quick calculation.

Unit 2: Advanced Concepts of Modeling in AI

  1. A spam filter gets better every week as users mark new emails as spam. It follows
    (a) a rule-based approach
    (b) a learning-based approach
    (c) a fixed if-else checklist
    (d) no model at all
    Answer (b) It learns from fresh labelled data. A rule-based system changes only when a person rewrites the rules.
  2. A model predicts tomorrow's highest temperature in degrees Celsius. This is
    (a) classification
    (b) clustering
    (c) regression
    (d) association
    Answer (c) The output is a continuous number. Labels such as "hot" or "cold" would make it classification.
  3. An online shop groups its customers by buying habits, with no labels given. It uses
    (a) supervised learning, classification
    (b) unsupervised learning, clustering
    (c) supervised learning, regression
    (d) reinforcement learning
    Answer (b) No labels means unsupervised, and grouping means clustering. Association finds items bought together, not groups of people.
  4. A perceptron has inputs 1, 0 and 1, weights 0.4, 0.7 and 0.2, and a bias of -0.5. What is the weighted sum plus bias?
    (a) 0.6
    (b) 0.8
    (c) 0.1
    (d) 1.3
    Answer (c) 0.4 + 0 + 0.2 - 0.5 = 0.1. Option (a) forgets the bias; (b) treats the middle input as 1.
  5. Which deep learning network is designed for images, using small filters that slide across the picture?
    (a) ANN
    (b) CNN
    (c) a decision tree
    (d) a rule-based system
    Answer (b) A convolutional neural network. ANN is the general family, so it is close but not the specific answer.

Unit 3: Evaluating Models

  1. The precision of this model is
    (a) 0.60
    (b) 0.79
    (c) 0.90
    (d) 0.72
    Answer (c) TP / (TP + FP) = 54 / 60 = 0.90. Option (a) is the recall.
  2. The recall of this model is
    (a) 0.60
    (b) 0.90
    (c) 0.27
    (d) 0.79
    Answer (a) TP / (TP + FN) = 54 / 90 = 0.60. Option (c) divides TP by all 200 reports, which no metric does.
  3. The F1 score is
    (a) 0.75
    (b) 0.72
    (c) 0.79
    (d) 0.54
    Answer (b) 2 x 0.90 x 0.60 / (0.90 + 0.60) = 1.08 / 1.50 = 0.72. Option (a) is the plain average, the most common mistake.
  4. The accuracy, and the number of wrong predictions, are
    (a) 0.79 and 42
    (b) 0.90 and 20
    (c) 0.72 and 56
    (d) 0.79 and 36
    Answer (a) (54 + 104) / 200 = 0.79, and wrong = FP + FN = 6 + 36 = 42. Option (d) counts only the false negatives.
  5. For a disease test, which error is most dangerous, so which metric matters most?
    (a) false positives, precision
    (b) false negatives, recall
    (c) true negatives, accuracy
    (d) true positives, precision
    Answer (b) A missed patient goes untreated. For a spam filter the answer flips to precision.
  6. Of 100 patients, 5 have a rare disease. A lazy model calls everyone healthy. Its accuracy is
    (a) 5%
    (b) 50%
    (c) 95%
    (d) 100%
    Answer (c) 95 of 100 are right, yet its recall is 0. Accuracy flatters a model when one class is rare.
  7. Why is a model not tested on the same data it was trained on?
    (a) testing would be slower
    (b) the score would be over-optimistic and hide overfitting
    (c) training data has no labels
    (d) the data would be deleted
    Answer (b) A model can memorise its training data, so only unseen test data shows how it will really perform.

Q4. Units 5 and 6, then the practical units

Computer vision class 10 MCQ and NLP class 10 MCQ, with the counting questions

Questions 23 to 36. Pixel and token counts are where a careful student and a rushed one part ways.

Unit 5: Computer Vision

  1. A self-driving car's camera draws a box around every pedestrian and cyclist in a frame. Which task is this?
    (a) image classification
    (b) object detection
    (c) instance segmentation
    (d) image processing only
    Answer (b) It finds and locates many objects with boxes. Segmentation would trace each object's exact outline in pixels.
  2. How many pixels are there in an image of resolution 640 x 480?
    (a) 1,120
    (b) 307,200
    (c) 921,600
    (d) 30,720
    Answer (b) 640 x 480 = 307,200. Option (c) is three times that, the count of RGB values, not pixels.
  3. A colour (RGB) image is 200 x 100 pixels. How many numbers does it store?
    (a) 20,000
    (b) 300
    (c) 60,000
    (d) 600
    Answer (c) Each pixel holds a red, a green and a blue value: 200 x 100 x 3. Option (a) would be the grayscale version.
  4. In a grayscale byte image, a pixel value of 0 means
    (a) pure white
    (b) pure black
    (c) mid grey
    (d) a missing pixel
    Answer (b) 0 is no brightness and 255 is full brightness, white. Reversing the two is a frequent slip.
  5. Why does a byte image allow exactly 256 pixel values?
    (a) a byte has 8 bits, and 2 to the power 8 is 256
    (b) screens show only 256 colours
    (c) each pixel uses 256 bytes
    (d) cameras record 256 shades
    Answer (a) Eight bits give 256 patterns, which is why values run from 0 to 255.

Unit 6: Natural Language Processing

  1. How many tokens does the sentence "AI helps doctors, and doctors help patients." produce?
    (a) 7
    (b) 6
    (c) 9
    (d) 8
    Answer (c) Seven words plus the comma plus the full stop. Punctuation marks are tokens too.
  2. After lowercasing and dropping punctuation, how many unique words does that sentence give a Bag of Words dictionary?
    (a) 9
    (b) 7
    (c) 6
    (d) 5
    Answer (c) "doctors" repeats: ai, helps, doctors, and, help, patients. Option (d) wrongly merges "help" and "helps", which happens only after stemming.
  3. With the dictionary [rain, go, away, come], the vector for "rain rain go away" is
    (a) [1, 1, 1, 0]
    (b) [2, 1, 1, 0]
    (c) [2, 1, 1, 1]
    (d) [1, 2, 1, 0]
    Answer (b) Bag of Words counts occurrences in dictionary order. Option (a) only marks presence.
  4. "studies" becomes "studi" with one technique and "study" with another. Which pairing is right?
    (a) stemming gives studi, lemmatisation gives study
    (b) lemmatisation gives studi, stemming gives study
    (c) both give study
    (d) tokenisation gives studi
    Answer (a) Stemming chops the ending and may leave a non-word; lemmatisation returns a real dictionary word.
  5. A word appears in all 4 documents of a corpus. With IDF taken as log(N / documents containing the word), its IDF is
    (a) 4
    (b) 1
    (c) 0
    (d) 0.25
    Answer (c) log(4 / 4) = log 1 = 0, so its TFIDF is 0: a word found everywhere tells the documents apart not at all.
  6. "The chair sang a song" is grammatically correct but makes no sense. At which stage does it fail?
    (a) lexical analysis
    (b) syntactic analysis
    (c) semantic analysis
    (d) tokenisation
    Answer (c) The grammar passes the syntactic check; the meaning fails the semantic one.

Units 4 and 7: practical only, not in the theory paper

These units carry no Section A items, but the practical exam and viva ask students to predict what a program prints.

  1. For the data 9, 3, 10, 4, 4, the median is
    (a) 10
    (b) 6
    (c) 4
    (d) 3
    Answer (c) Sort first: 3, 4, 4, 9, 10, so the middle value is 4. Option (a) is the middle of the unsorted list and (b) is the mean.
  2. What does print(np.median([1, 4, 9, 7])) show?
    (a) 9
    (b) 5.5
    (c) 5.25
    (d) 4
    Answer (b) Sorted, the list is 1, 4, 7, 9; with an even count the median is (4 + 7) / 2. Option (c) is the mean.
  3. Which Matplotlib call draws a scatter chart of x against y?
    (a) plt.plot(x, y)
    (b) plt.scatter(x, y)
    (c) plt.bar(x, y)
    (d) plt.hist(x)
    Answer (b) plt.plot joins the points into a line chart by default.

Q5. How do students lose easy marks?

MCQ technique for 417, and the traps that cost the most

Objective marks are rarely lost on unstudied topics. They go to distractors built from half-remembered ideas. These are the patterns we see most when marking class sets.

TrapWhat it looks likeThe habit that beats it
Swapped cellsPrecision and recall both appear as options, from FP and FN reversedDraw the matrix with Reality across the top every time, even for a one-mark item
Average for F1An option equal to (P + R) / 2Write 2PR / (P + R) beside the question before choosing
Forgotten punctuationToken counts one or two shortCircle each comma and full stop before counting
Pixels versus valuesA figure three times too large or too smallAsk: does the question want pixels, or numbers stored?
Family versus memberANN when the answer is CNN; supervised when it is classificationPick the most specific option that is still true
Close definitionsClustering against association; stemming against lemmatisationLearn one example of each that cannot belong to the other
The NOT questionThree true statements and one odd one outUnderline NOT, then mark each option true or false on its own

Read all four first

It catches the option that is true but less specific than the one the examiner wants.

Eliminate, then decide

Strike the plainly wrong ones. With no negative marking, a choice between the last two is always worth making.

Choose your skips early

On a first read, mark the four AI items you are least sure of, answer the rest in order, then return if time allows.

Questions about Class 10 AI MCQs

What students and parents ask about class 10 AI MCQs

Where can I find class 10 AI MCQ with answers for 2026-27?

This page has 36 original MCQs for CBSE 417, sorted by the 2026-27 unit names: Part A employability skills, then Units 1, 2, 3, 5 and 6, plus three practical-only items for Units 4 and 7. Each has four options, the answer and a one-line reason.

How many MCQs are there in the CBSE Class 10 AI 417 paper?

In the 2025-26 sample paper, the objective section printed 30 one-mark items and students answered 24: any 4 of 6 from Part A and any 20 of 24 from the AI units. The 2026-27 sample paper for 417 is not out yet.

Is there negative marking in class 10 AI MCQs?

No. The 2025-26 sample paper has no negative marking in the objective section, so attempt every item, using elimination when unsure.

Which unit has the most MCQs in Class 10 AI?

Evaluating Models, with 6 of the 24 AI objective items in the 2025-26 blueprint, many of them confusion matrix calculations. Units 1 and 6 follow with 5 each, and Units 2 and 5 have 4 each.

Are MCQs asked from Statistical Data and Advance Python?

Not in the theory paper. Units 4 and 7 are assessed through the practical: the file, the practical exam and the viva. Examiners there ask what a short NumPy or Matplotlib program will show.

Are assertion reason questions asked in CBSE Class 10 AI?

We cannot confirm it for 417. Assertion and reason items are confirmed for the related IT 402 paper, but the 2026-27 sample paper for 417 is not out. We still practise judging whether a reason explains a claim.

How should I practise AI class 10 chapter wise MCQ?

One unit at a time first, timed at about a minute per item, then mixed sets once every unit is done. Read the reason for every option, and redo the weakest unit a week later with new numbers.

Are numerical MCQs asked in Class 10 AI?

Yes. Expect confusion matrix metrics (accuracy, precision, recall, F1 score), the perceptron weighted sum, pixel and RGB value counts, token counts and Bag of Words vectors.

How long should I spend on Section A?

Our mocks give the objective section about 45 to 50 minutes of the two hours, leaving the rest for written answers.

It is part of ordinary teaching: timed sets, live marking, re-drills and mocks are all covered by the monthly fee shown on this page, in your own currency, for the batch or one to one. There is no admission fee and the first class is a free demo.
