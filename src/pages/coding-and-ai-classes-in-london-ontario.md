The two chains meet in university admission. The University of Waterloo&rsquo;s computer science page, read on 22 October 2026, lists Advanced Functions, Calculus and Vectors, any Grade 12 U English and one other 4U course for Ontario applicants, and names Grade 11 U Introduction to Computer Science as recommended. ## Were 203 London park amenities really built in 2021? A project on the city&rsquo;s file

The City of London publishes a Park Amenities file: soccer fields, ball diamonds, play structures, courts and more, each with an owner and a year built. Our students chart the years and find a spike that needs explaining. All counts below are ours.

**Our count of the City of London Park Amenities file, downloaded 22 October 2026 (1,225 rows). Year built, by decade, for the 1,073 rows that give one**

| Decade | Rows | Note from our analysis |
|---|---|---|
| 1920s to 1950s | 9 in all (1, 1, 2, 5) | The oldest entry is 1927 |
| 1960s | 27 |  |
| 1970s | 12 |  |
| 1980s | 13 |  |
| 1990s | 181 | 141 of them in 1996 alone |
| 2000s | 279 |  |
| 2010s | 290 |  |
| 2020s | 262 | 203 of them carry the single year 2021 |

This script prints the decade chart as rows of hash marks, then breaks down the 2021 rows by owner. Run it with Python 3 next to the downloaded CSV:

```
import csv
from collections import Counter

decades = Counter()
by_owner_2021 = Counter()
no_year = Counter()
sizes = Counter()

with open("park-amenities.csv", encoding="utf-8-sig") as f:
    for row in csv.DictReader(f):
        year = row["YearConstructed"].strip()
        owner = row["Owner"] or "(blank)"
        if year.isdigit():
            decades[int(year) // 10 * 10] += 1
            if year == "2021":
                by_owner_2021[owner] += 1
        else:
            no_year[owner] += 1
        if row["AmenityType"]:
            sizes[row["AmenityType"].lower()] += 1

for decade in sorted(decades):
    print(decade, "#" * (decades[decade] // 10), decades[decade])
print("built in 2021, by owner:", dict(by_owner_2021))
print("no year, by owner:", dict(no_year))
print("peewee after lower():", sizes["peewee"])
```

### 1. Draw the decades

A text histogram, one hash mark per ten rows, shows the shape at a glance: almost nothing before 1990, then a broad plateau. Then one year inside the plateau stands out, with 203 rows dated 2021.

### 2. Cross the spike with owner

Split the 2021 rows by the Owner column and the spike changes character: 164 are school-owned, 32 city-owned and 7 private. Most are ball diamonds (124) and tennis courts (42).

### 3. Check the other side

The file lists 276 school-owned rows in total. Every one is dated 2021 or has no year at all (112). Not a single school row carries any other year.

### What we think the spike means, and why it is only a guess

Our reading, and it is ours, not the city&rsquo;s: 2021 is probably the year school fields were added to the city&rsquo;s records, not the year 164 diamonds and courts were built. The file cannot prove that. A good student writes &ldquo;dated 2021 in this file&rdquo; instead of &ldquo;built in 2021&rdquo;, and says why.

The last line of the script teaches a smaller lesson. The AmenityType column spells one size as Peewee 25 times and as PeeWee once; lowercasing first gives the honest count of 26. For a stronger student, the next step is mapping the soccer fields (254 rows) and ball diamonds (194) with the X and Y columns, which hold projected map coordinates rather than latitude and longitude. The data comes from the City of London under its [Terms of Use for City of London open data](https://london.maps.arcgis.com/sharing/rest/content/items/e31458fd0c7e41dd9f93144a9550781d/data), which allow use for any lawful purpose; the file is publicly available from the City under those terms ([dataset page](https://opendata.london.ca/datasets/716311972be047f39a5937247ed80a2c_1)). The City has not approved or endorsed this page or our classes.

## Will AI make ICS3U pointless for a London teen?

No. It makes understanding the code more valuable, not less.

Give an AI assistant the London amenities file and ask how many were built each year, and it may well plot 2021 as a building boom. The tool did the counting correctly and the interpretation wrongly. Spotting that the spike is almost all school rows, and that school rows have no other year, is a human judgment that comes from writing the cross-tab yourself.

That is how we teach: reason through the problem, write the code, then challenge the result. Teens meet AI tools as part of the work, within each tool&rsquo;s age rules, and use them to explain and check rather than to produce what they hand in.

ICS4U asks for programs that are fully documented, and documenting code well means understanding every line of it. The longer argument is in [why understanding beats generating, in our longer essay](/blog/is-coding-worth-learning-2026).
