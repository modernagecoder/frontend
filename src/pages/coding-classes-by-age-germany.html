Coding classes by age in Germany

A six year old in the first Grundschule year and a sixteen year old in the Einführungsphase both "learn to code", but they do very different things, with different tools, for different reasons. This page walks through every age from 6 to adult, sets each one against the stage of German school the learner is in, shows what the Land and the national competitions offer at that age, and follows a single problem as it gets deeper from one age to the next.

In short

In Germany, coding at 6 to 8 means Scratch and puzzles alongside the Grundschule; at 9 to 12 first typed Python, around the switch to a secondary school; at 13 to 15 programs that match the Land's Informatik; at 16 to 18 Java or Python for the Oberstufe; and for adults, Python, web and AI. Modern Age Coders teaches every stage live online in English, in a group or one to one.

Two features of German schooling shape this map more than any other. The first is the Länder: the same age can mean three compulsory Informatik hours a week in one Land and none in another. The second is the transition after Klasse 4, or after Klasse 6 in Berlin and Brandenburg, when children move to different secondary tracks. Both explain why we place every learner by a short check in the first class rather than by age alone.

Ages 6 to 9

Can a six year old in Germany learn to code?

Yes, if coding means giving precise instructions and seeing what happens, rather than typing. That is what it should mean at this age.

What the Grundschule does

According to the KMK, Grundschule runs from Klasse 1 to 4, and to Klasse 6 in Berlin and Brandenburg, and more than half of all lesson hours go to German, maths and Sachunterricht. In 2024 the KMK adopted a key-points paper on anchoring media education and informatics education in the Grundschule, but there is no national computing subject at this stage.

There is also no leaving exam at the end of Grundschule, which leaves room for children to explore without a test in view.

What coding looks like

At six and seven: arranging arrows to guide a toy across a grid, then the same idea in Scratch, where blocks snap together and nothing has to be spelled. At eight and nine: games with a loop, a score and a sprite that reacts to keys, and the first bug a child finds and fixes alone.

Reading is the limit at six, not thinking. A mentor reads instructions aloud and lets the child do the deciding. Parents of the youngest are welcome to sit nearby.

The first national competition opens at Klasse 3: the Informatik-Biber, where pupils in the Grundschule solve nine short tasks in 30 minutes, according to BWINF, and need no programming knowledge. Jugend forscht junior accepts children from Klasse 4, and the FIRST LEGO League in Germany addresses ages 5 to 16.

Age 10

What should a 10 year old in Germany learn in coding?

Ten is the age families ask about most, and for good reason: it is the year of the move to a secondary school in most Länder, and the age at which most children are ready to type real code.

Where a 10 year old is at school

In most Länder a ten year old is in Klasse 4 or has just started Klasse 5 at a Gymnasium, a Realschule or one of the multi-track school types that each Land names differently. In Berlin and Brandenburg the same child is still at Grundschule until Klasse 6.

The KMK notes that a change into a different track is still possible in Klasse 5 and 6, so these two years are a period of orientation. In Nordrhein-Westfalen, they are also the years in which every pupil at a general school now has compulsory Informatik.

What coding looks like at 10

Most ten year olds are ready to leave blocks behind. In Python they learn that a variable holds a value, that if makes a decision and that a loop repeats, and they build small games and quizzes that they can show at home. Typing slows them down at first, which is fine: it makes them think before they write.

At this age a child can also start to explain why a program works, out loud and in a sentence. That habit matters more than any particular language.

Ages 12 to 15

How should teenagers in Klasse 7 to 10 learn to code in Germany?

The middle years of Sekundarstufe I are where the Länder differ most, and where a learner either gets serious about programming or drifts away from it.

What the Länder teach

Hamburg requires four Informatik hours in total across Klasse 7 to 10 at Stadtteilschulen and Gymnasien, Schleswig-Holstein the same at all school types, and Sachsen one hour a week from Klasse 7 to 10. Niedersachsen makes Informatik compulsory in Klasse 9 and 10. Six Länder, according to the GI monitor, have no Informatik that every pupil must take.

In Nordrhein-Westfalen, Klasse 10 ends with the central ZP10 exams in German, maths and English, and Informatik is not one of them. In the busiest school year, a subject without an exam is easily squeezed.

What coding looks like at 12 to 15

Functions that do one job each, lists and dictionaries, reading a file of real data, and testing a program on its edge cases. At fourteen and fifteen, first projects with a purpose: a quiz app for a younger sibling, a chart of the class's survey results, a small game with levels.

The Jugendwettbewerb Informatik follows this progression with its Silber level, recommended for Klasse 7 to 9, and Gold from Klasse 10. Jugend forscht junior is open up to age 14, and Jugend forscht itself from 15.

Ages 16 to 18

What coding do 16 to 18 year olds need for the Oberstufe and the Abitur?

The gymnasiale Oberstufe has a one-year Einführungsphase and a two-year Qualifikationsphase, according to the KMK. Informatik there is a real exam subject, and it is examined differently from how most teenagers practise coding.

Informatik in the Oberstufe

In Nordrhein-Westfalen, Informatik can be taken as a Grundkurs of three hours or a Leistungskurs of five hours in the Qualifikationsphase. In Bayern every Gymnasium branch has two compulsory hours of informatics in Klasse 11. Nationally, the GI monitor puts the share of Oberstufe pupils who take Informatik at about 15 percent.

The written exam is on paper. In Bayern, large parts of the written Informatik Abitur are programming tasks on paper with no work at a computer; in Nordrhein-Westfalen, a computer is not intended for the written tasks at all.

What coding looks like at 16 to 18

Java or Python with classes and objects, lists, stacks and queues, recursion, and tracing a program by hand to predict its output, the skill the paper exam rewards. Alongside that, larger projects that never fit a school term: a web app, a data analysis, a small machine-learning model.

The national competitions open up fully: the Bundeswettbewerb Informatik, whose round 1 can be solved without deep informatics knowledge, and the BWKI for AI projects in Sekundarstufe II.

We teach alongside school and award no German grades; the school course and the Land's exam are the ones that count. Our Informatik Abitur course practises the paper-based exam habit, and the Informatik-Nachhilfe page, in German, explains each Land's rules.

Adults

Is it too late to learn coding as an adult in Germany?

No. Adults learn differently from children, not worse: they need fewer games and more reasons, and they usually have a project in mind from the first class.

After the Abitur

Young adults up to 21 who are not yet at university or in work can still enter the Bundeswettbewerb Informatik, according to BWINF. Many use the year to build a portfolio before a degree.

At work

Python for automating reports and spreadsheets, SQL for data, and web development. Teams in Germany often write code and documentation in English, so classes in English fit the job.

With AI

Adults can use the tools that are closed to teenagers, such as Claude, which its terms restrict to users aged 18 and over. We teach how to check what an AI tool writes, not only how to ask it.

Adults learn in the evening or at the weekend, in a group or one to one, and at whatever pace their week allows. Our AI and machine learning page for Germany describes the AI track in more detail.

One problem, five ages

How does the same coding problem change from age 6 to adult?

We give learners of every age the same staircase of blocks: one block in the first column, two in the second, three in the third. How many blocks does a staircase of 10 steps need? Watch what each age does with it.

  1. Ages 6 to 8, with real blocks. Build staircases of 1, 2, 3 and 4 steps and count the blocks: 1, 3, 6, 10. What do you add each time?
  2. Ages 8 to 10, Scratch. Make a sprite draw the staircase column by column and keep a counter variable that goes up by one for every block it stamps.
  3. Ages 10 to 12, Python. Write a loop that adds the column heights. The program below does that and tries a guess at the same time.
  4. Ages 12 to 15, test a shortcut. Guess a formula, then let the computer check it against the loop for a thousand staircases.
  5. Ages 16 and up, prove it. Explain why the right formula must be right, and why it is faster than the loop for a staircase of a million steps.

The guess looks almost right, and for 100 steps it is within one percent. A staircase does fill about half of a square. But "about half" is not half: every column pokes out above the square's diagonal, so a straight cut through the square slices the top block of each column in two. Rather than argue, the learner asks the computer to check the guess, and a better formula, against the loop for every staircase from 1 to 1000 steps.

What the older learner adds

Why does steps × (steps + 1) ÷ 2 work? Put two equal staircases together, one turned upside down, and they make a rectangle of steps by steps + 1 blocks. One staircase is half of that rectangle, exactly.

The loop does one addition per column, so a million steps means a million additions. The formula does one multiplication and one division, whatever the size. That difference, counting the work a program does, is where computer science begins.

What every age learns

A six year old learns that counting carefully beats guessing. A twelve year old learns that a guess must be tested before it is trusted, and that a computer is a fast, honest tester. A sixteen year old learns to prove what the test suggested.

The problem stays the same; the thinking gets deeper. That is how we teach across ages: the same habits, at the depth each learner is ready for.

Coding by age in Germany: common questions

What age should a child start coding in Germany?

Many children start at 6 or 7 with screen-free puzzles and Scratch, while they are in the first years of Grundschule. Typed Python usually follows from about 9. There is no wrong age to start; a late starter simply begins further along.

What should a 10 year old learn in coding?

Most ten year olds are ready for typed Python: variables, decisions with if, loops and small games. In Nordrhein-Westfalen they also meet compulsory Informatik in Klasse 5 and 6; in Berlin and Brandenburg they are still at Grundschule until Klasse 6.

Are the classes in German?

No. Every class is live and taught in English only, by a mentor who matches the learner's pace and accent. Younger children are given simple instructions and plenty of time, and many families value the English practice as much as the coding.

Do German schools teach coding?

Some do, depending on the Land and the Klasse. NRW teaches Informatik in Klasse 5 and 6, Niedersachsen in Klasse 9 and 10, and Hamburg four hours across Klasse 7 to 10, while six Länder have no Informatik compulsory for all pupils, according to the GI Informatik-Monitor 2026/27.

Can a six year old learn online?

Yes, in short, active steps with a mentor who reads instructions aloud. Six year olds work with blocks in Scratch and puzzles away from the screen; a parent nearby helps with the first log-in.

Which coding competitions are open at which age?

The Informatik-Biber is open from Klasse 3, the Jugendwettbewerb Informatik has levels for Klasse 5 and 6, 7 to 9 and from 10, and the Bundeswettbewerb Informatik is open to young people up to 21 who are not yet studying or working.

Can teenagers use ChatGPT in class?

From 13, OpenAI says, as long as a parent or guardian agrees while the user is under 18; Claude is for adults only. A teenager in our classes works only with tools that their age permits, and parents are asked first whenever the vendor requires it.

When are classes held for different ages?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Children usually come after school or on weekend mornings, adults in the evening.

Can siblings of different ages learn together?

They usually learn better in separate groups of their own age and level, which can be booked at times that suit the family. One-to-one classes are another option for each child.

Do you teach adults?

Yes. Adults learn Python, web development, data and AI with us, usually in the evening or at weekends, in a group or one to one. Many adults in Germany find classes in English useful because their workplace already uses English for code.
