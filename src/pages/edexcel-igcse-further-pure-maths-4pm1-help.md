# Edexcel IGCSE Further Pure Maths 4PM1 help

Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Further Pure Mathematics, code 4PM1, is a second maths qualification for pupils who already handle Higher tier maths comfortably. It brings calculus, logarithms, the binomial series and radian trigonometry into Years 10 and 11, with no statistics or easy questions to balance them. Pearson builds it on top of International GCSE Mathematics A at Higher tier. This page explains the two papers, the ten topics, the formulae that must be learned by heart, and how we teach 4PM1 live online.

## How is Edexcel IGCSE Further Pure Maths assessed?

Two long papers, one tier, one series. Any topic can appear on either paper, and questions often join two topics together.

The specification describes a short, demanding format. "This specification is offered through a single tier." Both papers draw on the whole content, and Pearson notes that "the solution of any questions may require knowledge of more than one section of the specification content." With "around 11 questions" in two hours, each question is long: a typical one carries several parts and asks for a chain of reasoning, not a single technique.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Source: Pearson Edexcel International GCSE in Further Pure Mathematics specification, Issue 1, January 2016, "Qualification at a glance", from the [Pearson 4PM1 qualification page](https://qualifications.pearson.com/en/qualifications/edexcel-international-gcses/international-gcse-further-pure-mathematics-2017.html).

### Grades on one tier

Every question aims at grades 9 down to 4, and a grade 3 can still be awarded. Roughly two fifths of the marks sit over grades 4 and 5, and three fifths over grades 6 to 9.

Below the minimum standard the result is an unclassified U. There is no Foundation option to fall back on.

### Status in the UK

The specification states: "This qualification is not accredited or regulated by any UK regulatory body." Pearson designs it as a lead-in to its AS and A levels in Mathematics, Further Mathematics or Pure Mathematics.

For a UK pupil that makes it a signal of ability and a head start on sixth-form maths, rather than a qualification that replaces anything.

## What is in the IGCSE Further Pure Maths syllabus?

Ten sections, all pure mathematics. Statistics and matrices are explicitly left out.

**The 4PM1 content, with the detail from Pearson's notes that changes how each is taught**

| # | Section | A detail from the specification notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Logarithmic functions and indices | Laws of logarithms, change of base, equations of the form ax = b, simple surds |
| 2 | The quadratic function | Completing the square, the discriminant, and functions of the roots of a quadratic |
| 3 | Identities and inequalities | Algebraic division, the factor and remainder theorems, quadratic inequalities and simple linear programming |
| 4 | Graphs | Polynomials and rational functions with asymptotes, and graphical solution of equations |
| 5 | Series | Arithmetic and geometric series and sum to infinity; "Proofs of the above are not required." |
| 6 | The binomial series | Positive integer n, and rational n for |x| < 1; "The validity condition for (ii) is expected." |
| 7 | Scalar and vector quantities | Vectors with i and j, position vectors, and dividing a line in a ratio |
| 8 | Rectangular Cartesian coordinates | Distance, gradient, dividing a line in a given ratio, parallel and perpendicular lines |
| 9 | Calculus | Product, quotient and chain rules, kinematics, areas, volumes of revolution, connected rates of change |
| 10 | Trigonometry | Radians, arc and sector, addition formulae, and equations on a given interval |

The calculus section is the one that surprises families. It includes differentiating products and quotients, finding volumes by rotating a curve about an axis ("The volumes will be obtained only by revolution about the coordinate axes"), and connected rates of change, where one quantity's rate is found from another's. For maxima and minima, "Justification of maxima and minima will be expected", so a pupil must show why a stationary point is a maximum, not just find it.

### What 4PM1 assumes

Pearson expects "a thorough knowledge of the content common to" International GCSE Mathematics A at Higher tier, or Mathematics B. Questions can lean on that content without teaching it again.

In practice a pupil needs secure Higher algebra, surds, trigonometry in triangles and straight-line geometry before starting 4PM1.

### What 4PM1 leaves out

The specification says "knowledge of statistics and matrices will not be required." There is no data handling and no probability to collect easier marks on.

For trigonometry, Pearson adds: "Long questions, explicitly involving excessive manipulation, will not be set." The difficulty is in combining ideas, not in pages of algebra.

## Which formulae must you memorise for 4PM1?

Pearson prints a formula sheet in the exam, and then publishes a separate list of formulae that will not be on it.

Appendix 5 of the specification is titled "Formulae to learn", and it "gives formulae that students are expected to remember and will not be included on the formula sheet provided in the examination papers." It is the single most useful page in the specification for revision, because it tells a pupil exactly what has to be carried into the exam room.

**Areas covered by the 4PM1 "Formulae to learn" list, with examples in our words**

| Area | Examples of what must be remembered |
|---|---|
| Logarithms and indices | loga xy = loga x + loga y, the quotient and power laws, loga a = 1 and loga 1 = 0 |
| Quadratic equations | The quadratic formula, and for roots &alpha; and &beta;: &alpha; + &beta; = &minus;b/a and &alpha;&beta; = c/a |
| Series | The nth term and the sum to n terms of arithmetic and geometric series |
| Coordinate geometry | Gradient, distance between two points, and the point dividing a line in the ratio m : n |
| Calculus | The standard derivatives of powers of x, sin ax, cos ax and eax |
| Trigonometry | Arc length s = r&theta;, sector area &frac12;r2&theta;, the area &frac12;ab sin C and cos2&theta; + sin2&theta; = 1 |

Some trigonometry is given rather than learned. The specification notes that the addition formulae for sin(A + B), cos(A + B) and tan(A + B) "will be on the formula sheet", and that tan &theta; = sin &theta; &divide; cos &theta; is provided too. The cosine rule is given; the sine rule is expected to be known. We give pupils a one-page list split into "on the sheet" and "learn it" in the first month, and test the second column every week.

## Three 4PM1 style problems, worked in full

We wrote these to show the three habits 4PM1 tests most: rates in calculus, the binomial series with its validity condition, and the roots of a quadratic.

**Our first problem, connected rates.** A spherical balloon is inflated so that its volume V cm3 increases at 50 cm3 per second. Given V = (4/3)&pi;r3, find the rate at which the radius is increasing when r = 5 cm.

1. Write down what is known and what is wanted: dV/dt = 50, and we want dr/dt when r = 5.
2. Differentiate the volume with respect to the radius: dV/dr = 4&pi;r2. At r = 5 this is 100&pi;.
3. Link the rates with the chain rule: dV/dt = (dV/dr) &times; (dr/dt), so 50 = 100&pi; &times; dr/dt.
4. Solve: dr/dt = 50 &divide; 100&pi; = 1/(2&pi;), which is about 0.159 cm per second. Leaving 1/(2&pi;) as the exact answer first, then rounding, protects the accuracy mark.

### Our second problem, the binomial series

Expand (1 + x)1/2 up to the term in x3, state the values of x for which it is valid, and use it to estimate &radic;1.02.

With n = 1/2: 1 + (1/2)x + [(1/2)(&minus;1/2)/2]x2 + [(1/2)(&minus;1/2)(&minus;3/2)/6]x3 = 1 + x/2 &minus; x2/8 + x3/16. Because n is not a positive integer, the series is valid only for |x| < 1. Putting x = 0.02 gives 1 + 0.01 &minus; 0.00005 + 0.0000005 = 1.0099505, which agrees with a calculator to seven decimal places.

### Our third problem, functions of the roots

The roots of 2x2 &minus; 5x + 1 = 0 are &alpha; and &beta;. Without solving the equation, find &alpha;2 + &beta;2.

From the formulae to learn, &alpha; + &beta; = 5/2 and &alpha;&beta; = 1/2. Then &alpha;2 + &beta;2 = (&alpha; + &beta;)2 &minus; 2&alpha;&beta; = 25/4 &minus; 1 = 21/4. The identity in the middle step is the whole question; pupils who try to find the roots first waste minutes on surds.

## Is IGCSE Further Pure Maths worth taking?

For a pupil heading towards A level maths and enjoying algebra, often yes. For a pupil still consolidating Higher 4MA1, not yet.

Pearson positions 4PM1 as "a solid basis for students wishing to progress to Edexcel AS and Advanced GCE Level, or equivalent qualifications." Much of its content (logarithms, the binomial expansion, radians, differentiation rules and integration) reappears in the first year of A level Mathematics. A pupil who has met it at fifteen arrives in the sixth form with a term or more of pure maths already familiar.

**A readiness check we use before recommending 4PM1 (our criteria, not Pearson's)**

| Sign | Ready for 4PM1 | Wait a term |
|---|---|---|
| Higher 4MA1 algebra | Rearranges and factorises without prompts | Still needs worked examples for each type |
| Surds and indices | Simplifies and rationalises reliably | Makes sign or index-law slips |
| Unfamiliar questions | Tries an approach and checks it | Stops when the method is not obvious |
| Time available | Can give 4PM1 its own weekly slot | Already stretched across other subjects |

One point from the 4MA1 specification is worth repeating here. Pearson warns that taking two qualifications "in the same subject" is very likely to be treated as one by colleges and universities. 4PM1 is a different subject from Mathematics A, so it adds a second maths qualification rather than duplicating the first.

## Four rungs through Further Pure

The order we teach follows dependency: algebra and logarithms first, calculus once those hold, mixed papers last.

**Milestones for a Further Pure candidate**

| Stage | When | Evidence of readiness |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Higher 4MA1 secure | Before starting | All Higher algebra, surds, trigonometry and coordinate work done reliably |
| 2. Algebraic topics | First two terms | Logarithms, quadratics and their roots, identities, inequalities, series and the binomial expansion |
| 3. Calculus, vectors, radians | Following two terms | All four rules of differentiation, integration with areas and volumes, rates, vectors and radian trigonometry |
| 4. Mixed long questions | Before the series | Two-hour papers where one question joins two or three sections, with every step written |

## What families ask about IGCSE Further Pure Maths

### How many papers are in IGCSE Further Pure Maths 4PM1?

Two, Paper 1 and Paper 2. Each is a two-hour written exam worth 100 marks, with about eleven questions, and both are sat in one series.

### Is there a Foundation tier for 4PM1?

No. The specification is offered through a single tier, aimed at grades 9 to 4, though a 3 can be awarded. A performance below that is reported as U, unclassified.

### Is a calculator allowed in IGCSE Further Pure?

Yes, on both papers. It needs ln x, e to the x and trigonometry in radians, and any model that does algebra symbolically, stores text or has a full typing keyboard is banned.

### What topics are in 4PM1?

Ten sections: logarithms and indices, the quadratic function, identities and inequalities, graphs, series, the binomial series, vectors, Cartesian coordinates, calculus and trigonometry. Statistics and matrices are not required.

### What formulae do I need to memorise for 4PM1?

Appendix 5 of the specification lists them. They include the laws of logarithms, the sum and product of the roots of a quadratic, arithmetic and geometric series, coordinate formulae and the standard derivatives.

### Is IGCSE Further Pure Maths hard?

It is harder than International GCSE Mathematics A, because every question is pure maths at a level close to the first year of A level, and questions often combine topics. It assumes the Higher tier 4MA1 content already.

### Should I take 4PM1 with 4MA1?

Most candidates do, because 4PM1 assumes the Higher 4MA1 content. They are different subjects, so 4PM1 adds a second maths qualification rather than repeating the first.

### Is IGCSE Further Pure Maths regulated in the UK?

No. Pearson's specification says no UK regulator accredits or oversees it. It exists to prepare pupils for sixth-form maths and further maths.
