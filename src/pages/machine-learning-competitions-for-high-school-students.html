Machine learning competitions for high school students

Machine learning competitions for teenagers come in two very different shapes. One is the olympiad: a proctored exam where a student derives a gradient by hand and trains a small model in Google Colab under a clock. The other is the project challenge: a team spends weeks applying a model to a real problem and explains it in writing and on video. A US high schooler can do both in 2026-27. This guide describes each route from its organiser’s own pages, checked on 17 October 2026, and then builds a tiny classifier from scratch to show the level of understanding the olympiad expects.

Olympiad or project?

The olympiad suits a student who likes exact answers: deriving an estimator, computing a backpropagation step by hand, or writing a training loop that runs on a CPU within the time. There is one score and it is individual.

Project contests suit a student who likes a messy real problem, can work in a team and can explain a model to a judge who is not a specialist. Accuracy still matters, but so do the data, the safety thinking and the presentation.

Why both are worth doing

Olympiad preparation forces the theory. Project work forces the judgment about when a model is good enough and when the data is the real problem.

The calendars barely clash: SI Challenge projects are due in December, and the olympiad’s first round is in February.

The AI olympiad

The USA-NA-AIO: how the 2027 AI olympiad works

The USA-North America AI Olympiad is run by USAAIO, which also organises Team USA for the international AI olympiads. Its 2027 rules were posted on September 9, 2026.

USA-NA-AIO 2027 key facts, from the USAAIO site (checked 17 October 2026)

EligibilityAge: under 20 years old by August 30 of the competition year. Status: a US or Canadian citizen or permanent resident, or a full-time middle or high school student studying in the US or Canada. Not a full-time university student
Format"It is an individual competition." Every round is taken onsite with a proctor

Where it leads

USAAIO says the USA-NA-AIO is the only pathway for students to qualify for Team USA at the international AI olympiads. IOAI 2027, the fourth International Olympiad in AI, is set for July 4 to 10, 2027 in Singapore.

USAAIO also sends a team to the International AI Olympiad (IAIO); its 2027 edition is listed for Vietnam from February 25 to March 2, 2027, with that team already chosen.

Practical points for families

Students register themselves, then find a proctor; the proctor’s details are only collected during the contest. A school teacher, or an approved site such as a library or testing center, can host.

Because the coding part is in Colab with recording software running, a student should rehearse the whole setup on the same laptop at least once before February.

Sources: USAAIO 2027 USA-NA-AIO, About USA-NA-AIO and home page; IOAI; checked 17 October 2026.

What is tested

The USA-NA-AIO syllabus, topic by topic

USAAIO publishes a syllabus for the whole competition and says which parts Round 1 covers. It is a useful map of what high school machine learning looks like at contest level, whether or not a student enters.

Two rules worth noticing

Deep learning answers "must be programmed with PyTorch rather than TensorFlow", in line with IOAI. A student who learned TensorFlow first should switch early.

USAAIO also expects theory and code together. Its own example is that knowing the theory without being able to build a PCA class from scratch in NumPy is not enough, and the reverse is not enough either.

How we read it for a grade 10 student

The maths is the long pole. Most of the Round 1 list is reachable for a strong grade 10 or 11 student, but eigenvalues and multivariable derivatives usually have to be taught ahead of the school curriculum.

In USAAIO’s words, the AI Olympiad "is neither a pure math Olympiad nor a pure coding Olympiad". Our plan below splits each week between the two for that reason.

The question that tests ML understanding

Among the questions the report must answer is how the team tested or verified the accuracy of any SI-generated content, outputs or predictions, and how accurate they were. That is a machine learning evaluation question in plain words.

A strong answer names the test data, explains why it was kept separate, gives a number, and says where the model fails. The kNN example below shows the smallest version of that habit.

Two other doors for an ML project

The Congressional App Challenge accepts apps built with AI, as long as every use of AI is declared in the entry. Our guide to that challenge covers the rest of its rules, and our Presidential AI Challenge guide goes deeper on the SI Challenge.

Science fairs are the research route. Regeneron ISEF 2026 used 22 categories, including Robotics and Intelligent Machines (ROBO), Software Design (SFTD) and Computational Biology and Bioinformatics (CBIO); each affiliated fair sets its own categories.

The program now called the Presidential SI Challenge was launched as the Presidential AI Challenge. Its official site explains that the federal term changed from "Artificial Intelligence" to "Super Intelligence" under a September 29, 2026 executive order, so search results may show either name. A model from scratch

Build a k-nearest-neighbours classifier in NumPy

Olympiad problems expect students to build simple models without a library doing the thinking. This example is ours: a complete kNN classifier in a few lines, on six made-up points, runnable on any laptop CPU.

Distances from the in-between point (4.0, 3.8) to each training point (ours, rounded)
Training pointLabelDistanceRank
(6.0, 5.5)12.621
(1.5, 2.0)03.082
(2.0, 1.2)03.283
(7.0, 6.0)13.724
(6.5, 7.0)14.065
(1.0, 1.0)04.106

What the example teaches

With k = 1, the single closest point has label 1, so the prediction is 1. With k = 3, the three closest are labels 1, 0 and 0, so the majority vote is 0. Same data, same code, different answer, because of a setting the student chose.

That is the core idea behind cross-validation, which is on the Round 1 syllabus: choose k by testing on data the model has not seen, not by what looks right.

Three extensions to try

Scale the second feature by 10 and run it again. Why do the predictions change, and what does that say about normalising features?

Replace the vote with a distance-weighted vote, then write the same classifier as a small PyTorch function instead of NumPy.

Hold out two of the six points, choose k on the other four, and report accuracy on the held-out two.

Where teens start

Four levels between using AI and competing in it

A teen new to ML competitions is usually at level 1 or 2.

From AI user to ML competitor (ours)
LevelIn practice
1. UserUses AI apps; has not trained a model
2. Library userCan call scikit-learn fit and predict on a dataset
3. BuilderCan write a simple model from scratch and explain the maths
4. EvaluatorCan show, with held-out data, how good a model is and where it fails

For parents

Level 2 to level 3 needs maths that many schools teach later. That is normal, and it is the part we spend most time on.

Ask your teen how they know their model works. A clear answer is a better sign than any accuracy number.

For students

Write one model from scratch before using a library for it. You will understand the library far better afterwards.

Keep a notebook of every experiment, including the ones that failed. Project judges ask about those.

ML competitions for teens: what families ask

Which ML contests are open to US high schoolers?

In 2026-27 the main ones are the USA-NA-AIO (the AI olympiad that selects Team USA for IOAI) and the Presidential SI Challenge for team projects. AI apps can also go to the Congressional App Challenge, and research projects to science fairs on the way to Regeneron ISEF.

When is the 2027 USA-NA-AIO?

USAAIO has scheduled Round 1 for Friday, February 19, 2027, from 12:00 p.m. to 3:00 p.m. U.S. Eastern Time. Registration runs until January 31, 2027, and Round 2 is in March or April 2027.

Who can take the USA-NA-AIO?

A US or Canadian citizen or permanent resident, or a full-time middle or high school student studying in the US or Canada, who is under 20 by August 30 of the competition year and is not a full-time university student.

Do I need a GPU for the AI olympiad?

Not for Round 1. USAAIO says all Round 1 code must run on CPUs and GPUs are neither required nor allowed. Some Round 2 problems may require GPUs.

Should I learn PyTorch or TensorFlow?

For the USA-NA-AIO, PyTorch. Its syllabus says deep learning problems must be programmed with PyTorch rather than TensorFlow, in line with IOAI.

Where is IOAI 2027?

The IOAI site lists the fourth International Olympiad in AI for July 4 to 10, 2027 in Singapore. US students reach it through the USA-NA-AIO.

Is the Presidential AI Challenge the same as the Presidential SI Challenge?

Yes. The official site says the name changed after a September 29, 2026 executive order. High school teams of one to four register by December 18, 2026 and submit by December 23, 2026.

What times do your classes run?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays.

What about Kaggle competitions?

Kaggle pages could not be read as plain text when we checked, so we do not describe their rules here. Each Kaggle competition has its own rules page; read the eligibility section with a parent before entering.

Are you part of USAAIO or the SI Challenge?

No. We have no link with any organiser. We teach the machine learning, maths and Python these competitions draw on; registration is always directly with the organiser.
