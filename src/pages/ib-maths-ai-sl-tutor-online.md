# IB Maths AI SL tutor online

IB Mathematics: applications and interpretation, usually shortened to AI, is one of the two maths subjects in the IB Diploma Programme, offered at standard level (SL) and higher level (HL). The IB describes it as a course that "emphasizes the meaning of mathematics in context", with technology allowed on every written paper. At SL it is 150 recommended teaching hours, two papers and an individual exploration. ## What is in the IB Maths AI SL syllabus?

Five topic areas and a block of time for inquiry, modelling and the exploration. The IB's subject brief gives the recommended teaching hours for each at SL and HL.

**Mathematics: applications and interpretation, recommended teaching hours (IB subject brief, first assessments 2021)**

| Syllabus component | SL hours | HL hours |
|---|---|---|
| Number and algebra | 16 | 29 |
| Functions | 31 | 42 |
| Geometry and trigonometry | 18 | 46 |
| Statistics and probability | 36 | 52 |
| Calculus | 19 | 41 |

### Where the hours go at SL

Statistics and probability has the largest share of SL hours, 36, and functions is next at 31. Between them they take more than half of the topic time, so a student weak in either will feel it across the course.

Number and algebra has the fewest SL hours, 16. That is not because algebra does not matter; it is used everywhere else, particularly in functions and calculus.

### Common ground with AA

The brief says the two maths subjects "share 60 hours of common content". The AA guide adds that each topic "begins with SL content which is common to both" courses, which is why the first months of AI and AA can look similar in class.

For a student who might switch subjects early in year one, that overlap is useful. After it, the two courses diverge in emphasis and in how technology is used; the brief is blunt about AI: "Students should expect to develop strong technology skills".

## How is IB Maths AI assessed at SL and HL?

At SL, two written papers and an internal exploration. At HL, the same two papers made longer, plus a third paper. Technology is allowed on every paper.

**Assessment at a glance, IB subject brief for Mathematics: applications and interpretation**

| Component | Format | SL time | SL weight | HL time | HL weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 (external) | Technology allowed; compulsory short-response questions on the syllabus | 1.5 hours | 40% | 2 hours | 30% |
| Paper 2 (external) | Technology allowed; compulsory extended-response questions on the syllabus | 1.5 hours | 40% | 2 hours | 30% |
| Paper 3 (external, HL only) | Technology allowed. Two compulsory extended-response problem-solving questions |  |  | 1 hour | 20% |
| Exploration (internal) | An individual exploration of an area of mathematics | 15 hours | 20% | 15 hours | 20% |

### What "technology allowed" changes

When a calculator does the arithmetic, marks move to setting the problem up, choosing the right tool, and saying what the output means. The brief lists technology as one of six assessment objectives: using it "accurately, appropriately and efficiently both to explore new ideas and to solve problems."

A student who can drive a graphic display calculator but cannot explain what a correlation coefficient says about the data is only halfway there.

### The six assessment objectives

The brief says the objectives are common to both maths subjects: knowledge and understanding; problem solving; communication and interpretation; technology; reasoning; and inquiry approaches.

Communication and interpretation is the one AI students most often under-practise. It asks students to "transform common realistic contexts into mathematics" and to "comment on the context", which is exactly the habit we teach below.

### The exploration

The brief calls it "an integral part of the course and its assessment", compulsory at SL and HL, written "without the time limitations" of an exam.

## How do you choose and judge a model in IB Maths AI?

Fit more than one model, compare them against the data and the context, then say what each predicts and where it breaks. The data and questions below are ours, invented for teaching; they are not IB questions.

**Our data: the temperature of a cup of tea above room temperature, every 5 minutes**

| Time (minutes) | Excess temperature (degrees C) |
|---|---|
| 0 | 60 |
| 5 | 45 |
| 10 | 34 |
| 15 | 25 |
| 20 | 19 |
| 25 | 14 |

1. Look before fitting. The drops are 15, 11, 9, 6, 5: getting smaller. A straight line assumes equal drops, so a linear model is already suspect.
2. Try the linear model. A calculator's least-squares line through this data falls by roughly 1.8 degrees a minute. Extended to 40 minutes it predicts a negative excess: the tea colder than the room, which is impossible for a cooling cup.
3. Try an exponential model. Each 5-minute ratio is close to 0.75 (45/60 = 0.75, 34/45 = 0.76, 25/34 = 0.74). A model of the form T = 60 x 0.75 to the power t/5 fits every row to within about one degree.
4. Interpret the parameters. 60 is the starting excess; 0.75 means the excess falls by about a quarter every 5 minutes, whatever its size. That matches the physical idea that hotter things lose heat faster.
5. Predict and judge. At 40 minutes the exponential model gives 60 x 0.75 to the power 8, about 6 degrees above room temperature. It never reaches zero, which is reasonable here. Say also what the model ignores: a lid, stirring, a draught.

Our practice set. (1) For our tea data, use the exponential model to find when the excess first falls below 10 degrees; check with a table of values. (2) A plant's height in our invented data is 4, 7, 10, 13, 16 cm in weeks 0 to 4. Which model fits, and why might it fail by week 30? (3) Our invented phone survey gives a correlation coefficient of 0.92 between hours of revision and test mark. Write two sentences on what that does and does not show. Answers, ours: (1) between 30 and 35 minutes, since the model gives about 10.7 at 30 and about 8.0 at 35; (2) linear, adding 3 cm a week, but no plant grows without limit, so a model that levels off would be needed later; (3) a strong positive linear association in this sample, which does not show that revision causes the higher marks or that the pattern holds for other groups.

### Why this lesson family

Functions and statistics hold the most SL hours, and modelling ties them together. The brief's objectives ask students to transform realistic contexts into mathematics and comment on the context; choosing between models is where both happen at once.

It also uses technology the way the course intends: the calculator fits the curves, and the student decides which curve is believable.

### Mistakes we see most

Choosing a model by the highest r or R squared alone, without asking whether its predictions make sense. The linear tea model has a respectable fit over 25 minutes and is still wrong.

Copying calculator output to many decimal places with no units and no sentence. In an AI paper, "about 6 degrees above room temperature after 40 minutes" is the answer; the number alone is half of it.

## What students ask about IB Maths AI

### What is the IB Maths AI SL syllabus?

The IB subject brief lists five areas: number and algebra, functions, geometry and trigonometry, statistics and probability, and calculus, with 30 hours for modelling skills and the exploration. Statistics and probability has the most SL hours, 36.

### How many papers are there in IB Maths AI SL?

Two. The brief gives Paper 1, short-response questions, and Paper 2, extended-response questions, each 1.5 hours and 40 per cent at SL, with technology allowed on both.

### What does AI HL add?

More teaching hours, 240 instead of 150, longer papers of 2 hours each, and Paper 3: two compulsory extended-response problem-solving questions in 1 hour, worth 20 per cent.

### How much is the IB Maths AI exploration worth?

The brief gives the internal exploration 20 per cent at both SL and HL, with 15 recommended hours. It is compulsory for every student.

### Can I use a calculator in IB Maths AI?

Yes. The IB subject brief marks every AI paper, at SL and HL, as technology allowed. Ask your school which calculator models it uses.

### Is IB Maths AI SL easier than AA SL?

The IB presents them as different courses for different interests rather than easier and harder. AI suits students who enjoy maths in practical contexts with technology; AA suits students who enjoy problem solving and generalisation.

### Where can I find IB Maths AI SL past papers?

Past papers are IB publications and we do not reproduce them. Your school will tell you which ones it uses and how to get them; our lessons use questions we write.

### Will you help with my IB Maths AI exploration?

We teach the mathematics a student might use and how mathematical writing works, using other examples. We do not read, plan, write or edit any part of an exploration.

### How much do AI and AA have in common?

The IB subject brief says the two maths subjects share 60 hours of common content. Each topic opens with SL material common to both courses.
