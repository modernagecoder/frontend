# Transfer test maths tuition in Belfast

A P7 child in Belfast sits exactly the same transfer test as a child in Enniskillen or Coleraine: the SEAG Entrance Assessment, two papers on two Saturdays in November, set and marked by GL Assessment. What differs by area is what happens next. SEAG says plainly that it has no say in how its outcomes are used; each grammar school's Board of Governors writes its own Year 8 admissions criteria, and the application itself goes through the Education Authority in the spring. Belfast has more SEAG schools than anywhere else in Northern Ireland, so Belfast parents face more sets of criteria to read. This page explains the outcome, how schools may use it, what to do with the fortnight left before Paper 1, and how our lessons help.

### Where your child sits the test does not matter

Parents choose an Assessment Centre from the 63 when they register. SEAG is emphatic that the choice has no bearing on admissions: "There is no connection between where a pupil sits the SEAG Entrance Assessment and the Post Primary Admissions Process."

A Belfast child can sit the test in one school and apply to any SEAG school afterwards.

### One test where there used to be two

For 14 years before SEAG, SEAG says, P7 children needed "two different types of Entrance Assessment on four consecutive Saturdays to cover all 63 potential schools". Parents still search for the old names, AQE and PPTC.

Now there is one assessment over two Saturdays. By the end of 2025, SEAG says, around 40,000 pupils had sat it.

## What transfer test score is needed for a Belfast grammar school?

There is no single answer, and SEAG does not publish one. Each school's Board of Governors decides whether its criteria use the total score, the band, both, or another method, and publishes those criteria during the 2026 to 2027 school year, before the application window opens.

SEAG's words leave no room for doubt: it provides the test and the Statement of Outcomes, but "it has no role or involvement in decisions as to how each SEAG school uses those Outcomes." The choice of "whether to use Total Standardised Age Score, Band, a combination of both or by some other method" is "entirely a matter for the Board of Governors of each SEAG school". Any figure a parent hears at the school gate about a required score is, at best, about one school in one year.

**The SEAG outcomes most likely to be used in admissions (SEAG FAQs, 2026 Entrance Assessment)**

| Outcome | How SEAG defines it |
|---|---|
| Total Standardised Age Score (TSAS) | English (or Gaeilge) SAS plus Maths SAS, across both papers; range 138 to 282, mean 200 |
| Band 1 | Cohort percentile 60 and above, so the highest-scoring two fifths of the year's cohort |
| Band 2 | Cohort percentile 50 to 59 |
| Band 3 | Cohort percentile 40 to 49 |
| Band 4 | Cohort percentile 30 to 39 |
| Band 5 | Cohort percentile 20 to 29 |
| Band 6 | Cohort percentile below 20 |

Two consequences follow for maths. First, the Maths SAS is half of the total, on a scale from 69 to 141 with a mean of 100, so the maths section carries exactly the same weight as the English. Second, the bands are relative: SEAG says "Bands 2-5 will each contain 10% of the total cohort", and the cohort is around 13,000 children a year. A band describes where a child stands among those who sat that year's test, not a fixed standard of maths.

### Reading a school's criteria

Look for which outcome is used (TSAS, band or both), how ties are broken, and whether criteria such as siblings or feeder primaries come before or after the score.

SEAG's own advice: "It is up to the Parent/Guardian to read and familiarise themselves with the Year 8 Post Primary Schools Admissions Criteria for any schools which they are considering for their child."

### Special circumstances

If a child is unwell on a test day, SEAG cannot change the marking. Instead, schools' criteria usually include "Special Circumstances", claimed with evidence during the transfer application, which SEAG says begins around February 2027 and is run by the Education Authority.

In SEAG's words, "This process is completely outside the remit of SEAG."

Source: SEAG, [FAQs](https://seagni.co.uk/guidance/faqs), read on 31 October 2026. We could not read the Education Authority's admissions pages for this build (the site refused automated access), so the page quotes only what SEAG says about the EA process.

## How should a P7 child prepare in the last two weeks before the transfer test?

With familiar material, not new topics. On 31 October 2026 there are two weeks until Paper 1 on 14 November, and the most useful work now is two timed runs of SEAG's own free practice papers, short daily number practice, and calm routines for the day.

1. **Use the official papers.** SEAG publishes four practice papers with answer sheets, in English and in Gaeilge, plus a Parents' Guide with answer keys revised in September 2026, all free. SEAG adds: "No, SEAG do not sell any practice papers." Anything sold under that name is someone else's material.
2. **One full timed paper a week.** Two runs before 14 November is plenty. Afterwards, go through the maths questions the child got wrong and sort them: slip, misread, or genuinely unknown.
3. **Ten minutes of number a day.** Times tables, fractions of amounts, decimals and percentages. Short, daily and finished while the child still feels good about it.
4. **Fix only what is quick to fix.** Reading a scale, converting units, or answer-sheet slips can improve in days. A large topic gap is better left than crammed now.
5. **Plan the two Saturdays.** SEAG notes there is "no alternative date for individual pupils who have missed one of the two Papers", so treat both days as fixed: a normal breakfast, an early arrival, nothing new the night before.

Children in P6 have more time. For them, the NI-wide page on [transfer test maths practice](/transfer-test-maths-practice-northern-ireland) sets out SEAG's content list, the three sections of each paper and the shared hour in detail; this page does not repeat it. Families elsewhere in the UK comparing systems can see [11 plus maths tuition](/11-plus-maths-tuition) for England's tests.

## Four transfer test style maths examples, worked through

Written by us at P7 level, not taken from any SEAG paper. The first three are five-option multiple choice, where ruling out wrong answers is a skill in itself; the last is free response.

### 1. Estimate, then eliminate

**Question (ours).** 398 &times; 21 = ? (A) 2786 (B) 6358 (C) 8358 (D) 8538 (E) 83 580

**Estimate.** About 400 &times; 20 = 8000, so A, B and E are far out.

**Last digit.** 8 &times; 1 ends in 8, which C and D both do, so be exact: 398 &times; 20 = 7960, plus 398 = 8358.

**Answer.** C. Estimating removed three options in seconds.

### 2. A function machine backwards

**Question (ours).** A machine multiplies by 4 and then subtracts 7. The output is 45. What was the input? (A) 9 (B) 11 (C) 13 (D) 15 (E) 173

**Undo in reverse.** Add 7: 45 + 7 = 52. Divide by 4: 52 &divide; 4 = 13.

**Check.** 13 &times; 4 = 52, and 52 &minus; 7 = 45.

**Answer.** C. Option E is what you get by running the machine forwards on 45, a common trap.

### 3. Fractions, decimals and percentages

**Question (ours).** Which is largest? (A) 3/5 (B) 0.58 (C) 62% (D) 0.6 (E) 7/12

**Make them alike.** As percentages: A is 60, B is 58, C is 62, D is 60, E is a little over 58 (7 &divide; 12 = 0.583&hellip;).

**Answer.** C. Converting everything to one form is faster than comparing pairs.

### 4. Free response: a measures problem

**Question (ours).** A jug holds 1.5 litres. Ciara pours out 4 glasses of 250 ml. How many millilitres are left?

**Same units.** 1.5 litres is 1500 ml.

**Poured.** 4 &times; 250 = 1000 ml.

**Answer.** 1500 &minus; 1000 = 500 ml. In free response there are no options to check against, so the unit conversion has to be right first time.

**A short practice set (ours, with answers).** (1) What is 15 per cent of 80? (2) A dice is rolled once. What is the probability of a number greater than 4? (3) The first four terms of a sequence are 3, 7, 11, 15. What is the 10th term? (4) A rectangle has perimeter 30 cm and length 9 cm. What is its area? (5) Round 4.648 to one decimal place. (6) A film starts at 18:40 and lasts 1 hour 35 minutes. When does it end? **Answers:** (1) 12; (2) 2/6, which is 1/3; (3) 39; (4) width 6 cm, so 54 square centimetres; (5) 4.6; (6) 20:15.

## What Belfast parents ask about the transfer test

### Which transfer test do Belfast grammar schools use?

The SEAG Entrance Assessment. SEAG is open to all 63 post-primary schools in Northern Ireland that use academic selection, and 18 of those on its member list have a Belfast address.

### What score do you need for a grammar school in Belfast?

No single score exists. SEAG says each school's Board of Governors decides whether to use the total score, the band, both or another method, and publishes its criteria during the 2026 to 2027 school year.

### When is the transfer test in 2026?

SEAG's key dates put the first paper on Saturday 14 November 2026 and the second a week later, on Saturday 21 November. Statements of Outcomes follow on Saturday 23 January 2027.

### Does the test centre my child chooses affect which school they get?

No. SEAG says there is no connection between where a pupil sits the test and the admissions process, and a child may apply to any SEAG school.

### What do the transfer test bands mean?

They are cohort percentile groups. Band 1 covers percentile 60 upwards, roughly two children in five; Bands 2 to 5 each hold a tenth of the cohort; Band 6 lies below the 20th percentile.

### Is the AQE or PPTC test still used?

Not as separate tests. SEAG says that for 14 years before it was formed, children sat two different assessments on four Saturdays; now one SEAG assessment over two Saturdays serves all 63 schools.

### Where can I get free transfer test practice papers?

SEAG publishes four practice papers with answer sheets, in English and Gaeilge, and a Parents' Guide with answer keys, free on its website. It does not sell any papers.

### What if my child is ill on the day?

There is no alternative date. Schools' criteria usually allow a claim for special circumstances, made with evidence during the transfer application that the Education Authority runs from around February 2027.

### When are lessons?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Every time is quoted in UK time.

### Are you connected with SEAG or any Belfast school?

No. We teach independently online and have no tie to SEAG, GL Assessment, the Education Authority or any Belfast school.
