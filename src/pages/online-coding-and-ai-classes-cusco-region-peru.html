The region in figures

How big is the Cusco region beyond Cusco city?

Much bigger than the city. INEI's 2025 census counted 1,379,251 people in the department of Cusco, across 13 provinces and 71,986.5 square kilometres, and only a little over a third of them live in the province of Cusco itself.

Cusco city and the towns of 2017

The 2017 census, INEI's most recent source with city totals, put the city of Cusco at 428,450 people. That city spreads over several districts; the district called Cusco had 102,111 people in 2025 on its own.

INEI's 2017 directory of centros poblados lists the pieces separately, each with its altitude: Cusco with 111,930 people at 3,439 metres, San Sebastián with 109,915 at 3,578, Santiago with 92,729 at 3,464, Sicuani with 46,102 at 3,593, Yauri in Espinar with 28,888 at 3,976, and Quillabamba with 23,985 at only 1,086 metres, on the way down to the forest. Each is a single place, never a city total.

Young people in the 2025 census

The census counted 131,405 people aged 6 to 11 in the department and 138,470 aged 12 to 17, the two age bands that match primaria and secundaria most closely.

Those age figures are people counted in person; INEI publishes the department's headline total of 1,379,251 with an allowance for people the census missed, so the two are not directly comparable.

Getting online

Is home internet in Cusco good enough for live classes?

In many homes, yes, but fewer than in the rest of Peru. INEI's household survey found an internet service in 40.0% of Cusco households in 2025, against 56.8% nationally, and the share of people using the internet fell from 70.0% in 2024 to 65.7% in 2025.

The region's own answer

The Gerencia Regional de Educación Cusco knows the gap. On 27 May 2026 it announced CREE, a regional centre for specialised educational resources serving more than 360,000 students, with a virtual platform called PLADEC built, in the office's words, to overcome the connectivity gaps that affect rural communities.

GEREDU Cusco describes PLADEC as offline-first, with artificial intelligence built in as an assistant for teachers. That is a school-system tool; our classes are something different, live lessons for families who already have a usable connection at home.

A practical checklist

For a live class you need a connection that holds a video call for about an hour, a laptop or desktop for anything typed, and a quiet corner. A tablet with a keyboard is enough for younger children.

If your signal drops in the evening, choose a morning slot, when Cusco mornings line up with the Indian evening. If the connection is shared with siblings doing homework, one to one at an agreed hour is often easier than a group. We would rather tell you honestly at the free class than sell a course your connection cannot carry.

School and results

Do Cusco schools teach coding, and how do pupils do in maths?

They do not teach coding as a subject: Cusco follows the national curriculum, which has no computing area. In maths, though, Cusco's pupils finished above the national average on the 2025 national assessment, in both grades tested.

Where coding is missing

The national curriculum, the CNEB, puts digital skills in competency 28, "Se desenvuelve en entornos virtuales generados por las TIC", which is transversal: every subject is meant to use it, but no subject owns it. The regular secundaria week gives Matemática 4 hours and Inglés 3, and has no computing or programming area at all.

MINEDU's Python pilot, Fábrica de Programadores, began in July 2025 with 1,000 pupils of 4° and 5° de secundaria in Lima Norte. It has not reached Cusco, so a Cusco teenager who wants the same path learns it outside school.

The size of the school system

In 2025 Cusco had 121.3 thousand pupils in public primaria and 99.9 thousand in public secundaria, with 27,832 teachers across all levels and sectors. Across the country, 6,225,000 students and 53,631 schools started the year on 16 March 2026.

Primaria lasts six years and secundaria five, under Law 28044, and MINEDU places children in a grade by their age on 31 March, so a six year old by that date starts 1° de primaria.

Strong maths results are a good reason to add coding, not to skip it. A pupil who is already comfortable with fractions and proportion is exactly the pupil who enjoys turning them into programs, and who is ready for the national contests: the ONEM for maths, whose 2026 school stage ran on 8 July 2026 with ten questions in two hours, and the OPI for programming, which UTEC runs free for every secundaria student and whose 2025 final banned generative AI. Students enter both through the organisers' own routes; we are not connected with either. For an English-language view of school maths, see our maths teachers in English for Peru.

Taught in English

Are the classes in Spanish?

No. All our teaching is in English, live and online. Mentors are based in India; they speak clearly, slow down when asked, and adapt to each learner.

Cusco's English in figures

EF's English Proficiency Index 2025 scores Cusco city 520, in its moderate band. Among the Peruvian cities EF lists, only Lima, at 555, scores higher; Trujillo follows with 519 and Arequipa with 512. EF's score for the Cusco region as a whole is 504, also moderate.

A moderate score means a learner can usually follow a clear explanation and needs practice producing English, not just hearing it. EF also finds speaking is Peru's weakest skill, so mentors leave plenty of room for learners to talk.

How the lessons work in English

Each lesson is mostly building on a shared screen. The mentor explains a step in short sentences, the learner tries it, and the conversation is about what the program did. Words like loop, list and average become familiar quickly because they are used, not memorised.

If a learner answers in Spanish, the mentor understands and replies with the English phrase. Families who want a Spanish summary of the approach can share our page on clases de programación para niños en inglés.

A real Cusco project

What is the average quinoa yield in the Cusco region?

The regional agriculture office publishes its crop figures, district by district, as open data. Our students use the quinoa rows to learn something that catches out adults too: the average of 84 districts' yields is not the yield of the region.

The data

The file is "Producción Agrícola periodos 2023 al 2025 en la Región de Cusco", released by the Gerencia Regional de Agricultura Cusco (GERAGRI) on the Plataforma Nacional de Datos Abiertos. Its licence is ODC-By, the Open Data Commons Attribution Licence, which allows reuse with credit. We downloaded it on 9 November 2026.

It has 3,672 rows covering 75 crops in two farming seasons, 2023-2024 and 2024-2025. Each row gives a district, a crop, the hectares sown and harvested, the tonnes produced, and a yield column, Rendimiento, in kilograms per hectare.

The question

Quinoa is grown in 84 districts in the 2023-2024 season. A student wants one number: how many kilograms does a hectare of quinoa give in the Cusco region?

The first idea is natural. There is already a yield column, so take the 84 yields and average them.

That average treats every district as equal. A district that harvested two hectares counts exactly as much as one that harvested five hundred. But a hectare in a big quinoa district is just as much a hectare as one in a small district, so the honest regional yield is all the quinoa produced divided by all the land it came from.

The answer rises from 1,494.2 to 1,873.0 kilograms per hectare, a difference of almost 380. The reason is in the last line. Colquemarca, in the province of Chumbivilcas, harvested 525 of the region's 2,469 hectares, more than a fifth, at a yield of 3,230 kilograms per hectare. In Version 1 it was one district in 84; in Version 2 it carries the weight of its land. Neither number is a lie. They answer different questions: "what does a typical district get?" and "what does a typical hectare give?"

The sums and ratios are ours, from the 2023-2024 quinoa rows. The file is Latin-1 encoded with semicolons between fields, so it must be opened with encoding="latin-1" and delimiter=";".

There is a second lesson in the same file, and it is the reason we only used 2023-2024. Yield should equal production divided by area, so a careful student checks it on every row before trusting the column.

In 2023-2024 the columns agree on every harvested row. In 2024-2025 they disagree on every single one, by more than 1%. We do not know why, and the page does not guess: the data dictionary may describe a different unit, or the columns may have shifted. Until the publisher explains it, the 2024-2025 yields are left out of anything we teach as a result. Checking that the numbers agree with each other is part of the job, not an extra.

  1. Under 9, with beans. One bag holds 2 handfuls of beans from 1 small pot; another holds 30 handfuls from 10 pots. What is the "average per pot" if you average the bags, and if you count all the pots together?
  2. Scratch, around 9 to 11. Store three districts' hectares and tonnes in two lists, then show both averages side by side on the stage.
  3. First Python, around 11 to 13. Run Version 1 and Version 2 and write one sentence for each saying what question it answers.
  4. Ages 13 and over. Repeat both versions for potatoes or maize amiláceo in 2023-2024. Which crop shows the biggest gap between the two averages, and why?
  5. Going further. Read the GERAGRI data dictionary and propose an explanation for the 2024-2025 yields, then test it against the rows before believing it.

When could a Cusco learner have classes?

At any hour that suits the family. Cusco runs on Peru time, five hours behind UTC throughout the year, and the mentor adjusts.

Peru: UTC minus 5, no daylight saving since 1994 (tz database, America/Lima). India: UTC plus 5:30, no daylight saving. The difference is 10 hours 30 minutes every day of the year.

Because the gap never shifts, a time chosen once stays the same through the Peruvian summer break and the school year that follows. You book in Cusco time; the mentor sees the India time on their side. Mornings suit families whose connection is busier in the evening; afternoons suit teenagers after colegio. Cusco families ask

Coding and AI classes in the Cusco region: questions and answers

Are the classes in Spanish?

No. Every lesson is in English, live and online. Mentors speak plainly, slow down on request and get used to each learner's accent; a learner who answers in Spanish is understood and given the English words.

Can we take classes from outside Cusco city?

Yes. A learner in Sicuani, Urubamba, Quillabamba, Yauri or any other town joins exactly as one in Cusco city does, provided the home connection can hold a video call for about an hour.

Is our internet fast enough?

Try it in the free class. In 2025 INEI found an internet service in 40.0% of Cusco households, so it varies a lot by area. A laptop or desktop is best for typed coding; if evenings are slow, choose a morning slot.

What age can my child start?

Six, with block coding such as Scratch or Minecraft, which needs no typing. Typed Python usually starts around nine. Teenagers and adults can start from zero at any point.

Do schools in Cusco teach programming?

Not as a subject. The national curriculum treats digital skills as part of every area, and the secundaria timetable has no computing subject, though Cusco pupils scored above the national average in maths in 2025.

What time are classes?

Any day, any time, flexible: choose a batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at the hour that suits your family. Peru is 10 hours 30 minutes behind India all year, so 09:30 in Cusco is 20:00 for the mentor.

Can UNSAAC students and working adults join?

Yes. University students and adults have their own groups, usually starting with data analysis or Python and moving on to automation or generative AI, at hours around lectures or work.

Do you prepare students for the ONEM or the OPI?

We build the reasoning both contests reward, with problems we write ourselves and never the organisers' papers. Students enter through their school for the ONEM and through UTEC for the OPI. We are not connected with MINEDU, UTEC or either contest.

How much does it cost, and in what currency?

Plans are monthly and shown here in US dollars, charged by card in dollars; your bank converts them to soles. Visitors browsing from India see rupees. The first lesson is free.

Is there a certificate?

Some courses end with our own certificate of completion. It is not a Peruvian school or university qualification and is not issued or endorsed by MINEDU, a UGEL, GEREDU Cusco or any school.

What is the quinoa project?

An original Python lesson on the GERAGRI Cusco crop file: averaging 84 district yields gives 1,494.2 kg per hectare, while total production over total area gives 1,873.0, because one district, Colquemarca, harvests over a fifth of the land.
