The shortage

## Informatica has one of the largest teacher shortages of any secondary subject

The figures below come from the national report "Personeelstekorten voortgezet onderwijs, peildatum 1 oktober 2024", published in December 2024 by Hendri Adriaens, Benjamin Feher, Suzan Elshout and Maartje Elshout for the education ministry's labour market reporting.

| Measure | Figure |
| --- | --- |
| Shortage across all subjects in vmbo, havo and vwo | 4.8 percent |
| NASK | 10.3 percent, the largest by share |
| Informatica | 10.0 percent, the second largest by share |
| Nederlands | 8.1 percent |
| Techniek | 8.0 percent |
| Informatica nationally, in full-time equivalents | about 433 fte of employment, with a shortage estimated at 39 fte |
| Of informatica's 10.0 percent, hidden shortage | 8.5 percent |
| Second-degree informatica teachers | 11.6 percent, the highest of any subject |
| First-degree informatica teachers | 7.7 percent |

The headline is the second row of subjects: after NASK, informatica has the largest shortage as a share of the teaching it needs. In absolute numbers it is small, because informatica is a small subject; the largest shortages in full-time equivalents are in Nederlands, about 550 fte, and wiskunde, about 400.

For a pupil, though, the percentage is what counts. A small subject with a large shortage is a subject where many schools struggle to find or keep a qualified teacher.

The report separates open vacancies from hidden shortage, such as lessons given by someone not fully qualified for the subject. For informatica, 8.5 of the 10.0 percentage points are hidden: the lessons happen, but not always as the subject intends. The report also finds the shortage of second-degree informatica teachers, at 11.6 percent, to be the largest of any subject.

How many schools offer informatica at all, and how many pupils take it, is set out on the [national academy page](/ai-and-python-academy-netherlands); it is not repeated here.

Beside the subject

## What classes beside informatica can do, and what they should not try to do

Outside classes cannot fix a staffing shortage, and they should not compete with the school. They can add depth where a pupil wants it and a specialist where a school cannot always provide one.

### Add depth in programming

Informatica covers programming among many other topics. A pupil who wants more can learn Python thoroughly with us, writing larger programs and reading their own errors, so that school assignments feel like practice rather than first contact.

### Support a profielwerkstuk

Every havo and vwo pupil writes a profielwerkstuk. A pupil with a technical topic can build the software or data analysis with a specialist teacher alongside. We teach the skills; the research and the writing remain the pupil's own, as school rules require.

### Stand in where the subject is missing

For a pupil whose school does not offer informatica, or cannot run it this year, our classes cover the programming, data and algorithms a future technical student will need. They do not give school credit, but they give real ability.

| Topic | What we add | Course |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Programming | Depth, larger programs, debugging on their own | Python for Teens |
| Databases | SQL, design, queries on real data | MySQL for Teens |
| Algorithms | Searching, sorting and their cost, reasoned out | AI and Machine Learning for Teens, then data structures |
| Data | Cleaning, plotting and a first model | Data Science for Teens |
| Web | Building a real site with a front and back end | Full Stack Web Development for Teens |

A common pattern runs like this. A pupil in havo 4 or vwo 4 chooses informatica because they have enjoyed a little programming, and finds that the subject spends a term on something else entirely: how computers represent data, or how organisations use information. That breadth is the point of the subject, and it is valuable. But the pupil who came for programming can feel the programming arrive in small doses.

Classes beside the subject let that pupil keep programming every week while the school covers the rest, so that when programming returns in the school programme they are already comfortable with it.

The opposite pattern matters too. Some pupils take informatica without much prior experience and find the programming parts hard to follow at the pace of a full class. An hour or two a week with a specialist, going over the same ideas slowly and with more practice, often turns the subject around.

In both cases our teacher asks what the school is covering this term and fits the lessons around it, without doing the pupil's graded work for them.

This section is our teaching plan. It makes no claim about any school's informatica provision and does not replace the school's programme or assessment.

### Why depth before breadth

Informatica is broad by design. Pupils who want to become strong programmers need one area, usually programming itself, taken deep. Everything else becomes easier after that.

Our [coding roadmap](/coding-roadmap) shows how the programming, data and algorithms rungs connect to everything that follows them.

### Around toetsweken and the eindexamen

Lessons usually pause during school test weeks and ease off in the exam year. The profielwerkstuk year is often the busiest, and one-to-one lessons fit it best.

## What informatica pupils and their parents ask

### Is there really a shortage of informatica teachers?

Yes. In the national measurement for 1 October 2024, informatica had a shortage of 10.0 percent of its teaching, second only to NASK, and 8.5 of those percentage points were hidden shortage rather than open vacancies.

### Do your classes replace informatica?

No. They sit beside the school subject and do not give school credit or replace its assessment. They add depth and a specialist teacher.

### My school does not offer informatica. Is this useful?

Very. Our classes cover the programming, data and algorithms a pupil would otherwise miss, and they prepare a pupil well for a technical degree.

### Can you help with a profielwerkstuk?

We teach the programming and data skills a technical profielwerkstuk needs. The research, conclusions and writing remain the pupil's own, as school rules require.

### Which language should an informatica pupil learn?

Python for most, taught in depth. Java suits pupils heading for computing degrees who want a second, stricter language.

### Is it suitable for havo as well as vwo?

Yes. Pupils are placed by ability, not by school type.

### Will this help with informatica grades?

It often does, because a pupil who programs every week finds the programming parts of the subject easier. But we teach skills, not the school's assessments, and we never complete graded work for a pupil.

### Are the lessons in English?

Yes, with Dutch school terms kept. Programming is written in English anyway.

### What does it cost?

After a free trial lesson, a place in a small group is USD 100 per month and a private teacher USD 150 per month. Payment is in US dollars; you can stop at the end of any month, and there is no registration charge.

### When are lessons held?

After school, in the early evening or at the weekend, Dutch time. The teacher is four and a half hours ahead in winter and three and a half in summer.

### What happens after we send the form?

A member of our team rings the parent back, at a time that suits a Dutch evening, and fixes the trial lesson. You pay nothing unless the pupil carries on afterwards.
