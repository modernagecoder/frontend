Free coding programs for high school students

Free coding programs for high school students are real and some are excellent, but "free" means different things on different sites. One is free to study and free to certify; another is free to study, with credit only through your own school; a third is a free curriculum your teacher has to choose. We read five of them from their own pages: Harvard’s CS50x, freeCodeCamp, USACO, Code.org’s Computer Science Principles and MIT OpenCourseWare. Here is exactly what each one gives at no cost, what the fine print says about certificates and school credit, a sixteen-week plan that uses only free material, and a short Python practice set built on CS50’s own grading rule.

Three kinds of free

Free to study and free to certify: freeCodeCamp says its certifications cost nothing, and CS50x offers a free CS50 Certificate of its own beside a paid verified certificate from edX.

Free to study, with credit elsewhere: CS50’s FAQ says its certificates are "not accredited academic offerings", and that a high schooler wanting academic credit needs their own school to grant it.

A free curriculum is not a free class

Code.org’s CS Principles is a curriculum written for teachers. A student usually meets it through a school that has chosen it, so the practical question is whether your school offers it.

The programming units use JavaScript, and Code.org lists a version built with CMU CS Academy whose programming units use Python instead.

Sources: CS50x and its FAQs, freeCodeCamp About, USACO, Code.org CSP, MIT OpenCourseWare About, each checked on 17 October 2026. Modern Age Coders has no link with any of these organisations.

CS50x rules

CS50x for a high schooler: ages, deadlines, certificates and credit

CS50x is the free program families ask about most. Its FAQ answers nearly every practical question, and a few answers surprise people.

The deadline, carefully read

The FAQ says that a few months before 30 June 2027 the team re-evaluates the course and may extend it by six or twelve months. Until then, plan as if 30 June 2027 is final.

Work completed with 70% or better during the current term carries forward if a new term begins. Work submitted before 1 January 2026 does not.

Converting the deadline to US time

The deadline is 11:59 PM UTC. Our conversion: on 30 June, US Eastern Daylight Time is four hours behind UTC, so the deadline falls at 7:59 PM Eastern, 6:59 PM Central, 5:59 PM Mountain and 4:59 PM Pacific that same evening.

Time-zone arithmetic is an easy place to slip, so our advice is simple: submit a day early and the conversion never matters.

The score in code

CS50’s grading rule as a Python program, with a practice set

The FAQ explains how each problem set is scored: "weighted 75% for correctness and 25% for style", with 70% needed to count as complete. Turning a rule like that into code is a skill in itself.

  1. Practice 1. With 5 of 8 tests passing, what is the lowest style score that still completes? Our answer: 0.70 minus 0.46875 is 0.23125, divided by 0.25 is 0.925.
  2. Practice 2. Change the program to print the minimum style needed for any number of passing tests, and the word impossible when it is above 1.0.
  3. Practice 3. Explain in one sentence why the weights 0.75 and 0.25 must add up to 1. Then break the rule on purpose and see what the program prints for a perfect submission.

This is the lesson family we teach here: weighted averages and conditionals. The same pattern sits behind a school GPA, a game’s damage formula and the AP CSP Create task’s procedures, which is why we use it early.

USACO

USACO: the free contest route, and how a season runs

The USA Computing Olympiad is free to enter online and open to everyone, with one limit on who can be selected as a finalist.

USACO format and eligibility, from usaco.org (read 17 October 2026)
PointWhat USACO publishes
Who can enter"All are welcome to participate in online USACO contests and training."
Finalists"Only pre-college students in the USA are eligible for selection as finalists" for the training camp and the international teams
DivisionsBronze, silver, gold and platinum; every new participant starts in bronze
A contestTypically "3 or 4 problems", solved in C, C++, Java or Python
Time"4..5 contiguous hours", started whenever the student chooses inside the contest window
2025 to 2026 seasonThree online contests in early 2026, then a proctored invitational US Open; finalists invited to a training camp in late May

Promotion, briefly

A perfect score earns an in-contest promotion to the next division. Otherwise, promotions follow each contest for everyone above that contest’s threshold.

Once promoted, a student never needs to take the lower division again, so progress is permanent.

Is bronze realistic in a first year?

Our view: a student who writes loops, arrays and functions comfortably in one language is ready to attempt bronze problems, and the free training pages are the place to begin.

Our dedicated USACO coaching page goes division by division.

Going further

MIT OpenCourseWare and other free next steps

After a first course, the question changes from "where do I start" to "what next". Two free sources answer it well.

MIT OpenCourseWare

OCW has shared MIT course material since 2001. Its About page says there is no enrollment, no start or end date and no account to create, and that materials can be downloaded, modified and reused as long as MIT OpenCourseWare is cited as the source.

It is material, not a class: lecture notes, problem sets and sometimes video. A strong 11th or 12th grader can use it to go deeper into a topic first met in CS50.

A free online prerequisite

MIT’s Beaver Works Summer Institute runs online prerequisite courses for its summer program, and its FAQ says they are free for students. They open each February.

For paid and funded summer options, see our summer research programs page.

Sixteen weeks

Our sixteen-week plan using only free material

This is our plan for a high schooler starting from zero, using CS50x and USACO training and nothing paid. It assumes five hours a week, so 80 hours in total.

Why the C weeks get more time

CS50 teaches memory and pointers in C before moving to Python. For most teenagers those weeks are the hardest, so our plan gives them six weeks out of sixteen.

At five hours a week, weeks 3 to 8 are 30 hours, more than a third of the 80-hour total. That is deliberate.

What free cannot easily give

Free courses are complete as material. What a self-learner often lacks is someone who reads their own code and asks why. That is our view and the part we offer.

Online forums help with errors; a regular teacher helps with habits, which matter more over sixteen weeks.

For parents

The jump from stage 1 to stage 2 is the hardest. Watching is easy; submitting is where the learning starts.

Ask to see the problem set, not the video. Code on the screen is the honest progress report.

For students

Pick one free course and finish it before starting another.

When stuck for an hour, write down exactly what you expected and what happened. That sentence often solves it.

Questions from families

Free coding programs for high school students: common questions

Is CS50x really free?

Yes. Anyone may take it free through OpenCourseWare and earn a free CS50 Certificate with at least 70% on every problem set and the final project. A verified certificate from edX is a separate paid option.

Can a high school student get credit for CS50x?

Only through their own school. The CS50 FAQ says its certificates are not accredited academic offerings and that only official school representatives can grant academic credit.

What is the CS50x deadline?

The FAQ gives end of day UTC on 30 June 2027 for the term that began on 1 January 2026. There are no deadlines for individual assignments.

How old do you need to be for CS50x?

The FAQ points to learners aged 12 and up, and says younger learners may need a parent to help them sign up for the free third-party services the course uses.

Is freeCodeCamp free, including certifications?

Its About page says every aspect of freeCodeCamp is 100% free, including the courses, the projects and the certifications. It describes itself as a 501(c)(3) public charity.

Who can enter USACO?

USACO says all are welcome to participate in its online contests and training. Only pre-college students in the USA can be selected as finalists for its training camp.

Which languages does USACO accept?

C, C++, Java and Python. A contest typically has 3 or 4 problems and lasts four to five contiguous hours within a longer window.

Is Code.org CS Principles a free class my teen can join?

It is a free year-long curriculum for grades 9 to 12 that schools and teachers use. A student usually takes it through a school that has chosen to offer it.

When are your classes?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays.

Why pay for classes when good courses are free?

You may not need to. Many students do well alone. A live teacher helps when a student stalls, wants feedback on their own code, or needs a steady weekly rhythm.
