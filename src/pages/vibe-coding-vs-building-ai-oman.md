> Vibe coding is real and we teach it. Here is exactly what it gives a student, what it does not, and a ten-minute test you can run at home tonight in Oman.

In short

Vibe coding is describing what you want and letting an AI tool produce the code. It is powerful, it is permanent, and Modern Age Coders teaches it. What it does not by itself give a learner is the ability to reason about the code when the tool stops being able to fix it, and that ability is what this academy exists to build. We teach both, in that order, for learners in Oman aged six to sixty-seven. Group teaching is USD 100 a month and private teaching USD 150, and the first class is free.

Starting from an honest position

## Vibe coding works, we teach it, and it is not the same skill

We should declare an interest before arguing: we sell vibe coding courses. So this page is not going to tell you the practice is worthless. It is going to tell you precisely what it gives a learner and precisely what it does not, because the two are easy to confuse and expensive to confuse.

Vibe coding is describing what you want and letting an AI tool produce the code, then steering it by describing what is wrong. It is genuinely powerful. A thirteen year old can now put a working web application in front of their family in an evening, which even five years ago took months of study to reach. Anybody who dismisses that has not watched a child do it.

It also teaches real things. Specifying what you want clearly is a skill, and most people are bad at it. Reading code you did not write is a skill, and it is one traditional teaching neglects badly. Judging whether an output meets a requirement is a skill. Those are not nothing.

Here is the part that gets left out of the marketing. Vibe coding is an *operating* skill: it makes you effective with a tool. Building is a *constructing* skill: it makes you able to reason about the thing the tool produced. They feel similar from the outside, because both end with working software on a screen, and they diverge completely the moment something goes wrong in a way the tool cannot fix by being asked again.

The honest way to put it to a parent: after a year of vibe coding a student can build more than they can explain. After a year of this track they can explain more than they can build. The second gap closes on its own; the first one does not.

|   | Vibe coding gives you | Building gives you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Speed to a working thing | Very high, immediately | Low at first, high later |
| When it works | You have software | You have software |
| When it breaks | Re-describe and hope | Read it, find it, fix it |
| Ceiling | Whatever the tool can do | Whatever you can reason about |
| Transfers to | The next version of that tool | Any language, any tool, any decade |
| Time to competence | Weeks | Years |

The test

## One question that separates the two, and you can run it tonight

Forget the argument and try this instead. It takes about ten minutes and it will tell you more about where your child actually is than any syllabus we could publish.

1. #### Ask them to show you something they made with an AI tool

  Anything: a page, a game, a small application. Let them enjoy showing it, because they should. This is not a trap and it should not feel like one.
2. #### Ask them to change one specific small thing

  Not something the tool would obviously handle. Something narrow: make this button only work once, or stop this from happening when the list is empty. Watch what they do first. Reaching for the code is one answer; reaching for the chat box is another.
3. #### Now break it yourself, gently, and ask why

  Change a value, remove a line, rename something. Then ask: what is wrong, and where. The question is not whether they can fix it. It is whether they can locate it and describe what that part was for.
4. #### Ask what one line does

  Point at any line and ask what it is doing and what would happen without it. This is the single most diagnostic question of the four and it takes about twenty seconds.

### What the answers mean

If the student can find the line, explain what it does, and say why it is wrong, they are building. The tool is an accelerator and they are the engineer, which is exactly where you want them.

If they can only re-describe the problem to the tool and hope, they are operating. That is a real capability and it has a ceiling: it works until the tool cannot fix the thing, and then it stops completely. Every student we have met who was stuck at that ceiling was stuck at rungs one and two, and both are recoverable in a term.

### What we do about it

Teach both, in an order. Building first, so the student has something to judge with. Vibe coding on top, so they are not slower than their peers at producing things. That order is not moral, it is practical: a learner who can read code gets far more out of an AI tool than one who cannot, because they can tell when it has quietly done something odd.

What to do about it, by age

## The answer is different at nine, at thirteen and at sixteen

Having run the test, most parents want to know what follows. It depends less on the result than on how much time is left before the student needs the underlying skill for something that matters.

### Around nine to eleven

Let them use the tools, with somebody nearby, and do not worry about the gap yet. At this age the valuable thing is that making software feels possible and enjoyable, and AI tools deliver that faster than anything before them. Start typed Python alongside, in small amounts, so that reading code becomes familiar before it becomes necessary. There is no urgency and plenty of runway.

### Around twelve to fourteen

This is the window where the order matters most. A student who builds the reading and debugging habit here will spend the rest of their education using AI tools as an amplifier. A student who does not tends to arrive at sixteen able to produce impressive things and unable to progress past whatever the tool can do unaided, which is a frustrating place to be stuck and takes longer to fix later.

### Fifteen and upward

Move quickly and go straight at the gap. Older students close it far faster than younger ones: the abstractions land in a term rather than a year, and the motivation is usually already there because they have hit the ceiling themselves. Pair it with a published project from the start, because at this age accumulated evidence of work matters more than accumulated hours of study.

## Why this matters more here than it would in a bigger market

Oman is close to fully connected. At the start of 2025, 95.3 per cent of the population was online, 5.14 million people out of 5.40 million. Which means every AI tool that exists is already in every Omani teenager's hand, at the same moment it reaches a teenager in London or Singapore. There is no lag to exploit and no advantage in access.

When the tools are universal, the differentiator stops being who has them and becomes who can do something with them that the tools cannot do alone. That is not a comfortable thought and it is the accurate one.

It also cuts the other way, which is the optimistic half. A student in Salalah or Sohar has exactly the same tools as a student anywhere, and the thing that used to separate them, which was access to teaching, is now a video call. The remaining question is entirely what they do with the time.

## What parents in Oman ask about AI-assisted coding

### Should I stop my child using AI tools to write code?

No, and trying would be both futile and counterproductive. These tools are how software is written now, and a student kept away from them is disadvantaged rather than protected. What is worth doing is making sure they can read what comes back. A learner who can review the output gets far more from an AI tool than one who cannot, because they notice when it has quietly done something strange.

### Is vibe coding going to make traditional programming obsolete?

Some of it, and not the part that matters here. Typing out boilerplate is already largely gone and nobody should mourn it. What has not become obsolete, and shows no sign of it, is deciding what should be built, judging whether it works, and diagnosing why it does not. Those are the parts this track teaches, and they have become more valuable rather than less as the typing got cheaper.

### My child built a whole app but cannot explain it. Is that bad?

It is extremely normal and it is fixable, usually within a term. It means they are operating rather than constructing, which is where nearly everybody starts now. The route out is rungs one and two: Python written by hand and data handled honestly. Neither is glamorous and both go faster than parents expect, because the student is already motivated by things they have made.

### Do you actually teach vibe coding, or is this page selling against it?

We teach it and sell it, which is why the page opens by declaring that. There is a vibe coding course for kids and one for teens, and a coding agents course that works with Codex and Claude Code. The argument here is about sequence rather than about worth: build the judgement first, then the tools amplify it instead of substituting for it.

### How much does it cost?

A group batch of five to eight students is USD 100 a month and one to one teaching is USD 150 a month, billed in US dollars, the same flat figures everywhere outside India. There is no enrolment fee, no annual contract, and the first class is free without a card.

### At what age can a child start with AI tools?

Around nine or ten in our experience, and with an adult nearby. The vibe coding course for kids is built around exactly that: the child directs a tool, gets something back, and then has to work out which parts are broken and repair them. Issuing the instruction takes an afternoon to learn. Evaluating what returns is where the whole course goes.

### Will using AI tools stop my child learning to think?

It can, and whether it does depends almost entirely on whether anybody ever asks them to explain what came back. A student who is never asked will not develop the habit. A student who is asked in every session develops it quickly, and then the tool accelerates a mind that is working rather than replacing one that is not. That question is most of what a teacher does here.

### What if my child only wants to do the AI tool part?

That is common and it is workable. We start where the motivation is and let the gaps surface naturally, because they always do: within a few weeks something breaks that the tool cannot fix, and at that moment a student who has been resisting Python usually wants it. Teaching that when it is wanted is far easier than insisting on it beforehand.

### Does any of this help at school?

Not directly, and nothing here is on an Omani school certificate. What tends to transfer is the habit of specifying a problem precisely before attacking it, which shows up in open-ended assignments across subjects. Several parents have reported that as the first visible change, well before anything technical.
