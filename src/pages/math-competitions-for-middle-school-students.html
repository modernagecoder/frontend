Math competitions for middle school students

Most US middle schoolers who enjoy math will meet three competitions: MATHCOUNTS, which runs from a school round up to a national final; Math Kangaroo, a single 75-minute multiple-choice test each March; and MOEMS, five short monthly contests taken at school. They feel very different on the day, mostly because of how much time each problem gets. This page sets them side by side from each organiser’s own pages, checked on 17 October 2026, works out the time per problem for every round, and ends with seven practice problems our teachers wrote.

Which suits which student

MATHCOUNTS rewards speed and teamwork across a whole season, and a strong student can keep advancing from February to May. It is the most involved of the three for families, because it runs through the school.

Math Kangaroo is one morning of careful reasoning with no penalty for a wrong answer. MOEMS is the lightest: five short contests in class, good practice for students who have never competed.

Doing more than one

They fit together well. MOEMS runs monthly from November, MATHCOUNTS chapter rounds are in February, and Math Kangaroo is in March, so a single year of preparation serves all three.

Our deeper guides cover two of them on their own: MATHCOUNTS preparation and Math Kangaroo preparation.

MATHCOUNTS

MATHCOUNTS: four rounds, four levels, one school year

The MATHCOUNTS Foundation, a non-profit, runs this program for sixth, seventh and eighth graders. Here is the 2026-27 season as the Foundation published it.

Rules families ask about

Students must be enrolled in grades 6 to 8, and MATHCOUNTS states that "Participation in MATHCOUNTS competitions is limited to 3 years for each student". A school can send up to 14 students to its chapter competition: a team of 4 plus 10 individuals.

Public, private, charter, virtual and home schools can all register; home schools add a participation form. Tutoring and enrichment centers that are not a student’s official school of record cannot.

If the school does not take part

A student may register as a non-school competitor only if their school is not registering and will not support participation. Online sign-up by a parent opens on November 5, 2026, and there is a limit of 14 NSCs per school.

MATHCOUNTS also runs a free National Math Club all school year, which needs no competition registration and is a gentler start for a school with no team.

Sources: Competition Series, Competition Rules and FAQ and home page, checked 17 October 2026.

Math Kangaroo

Math Kangaroo USA: one 75-minute test in March

Math Kangaroo is the US arm of an international contest. For 2027 it marks its 30th annual competition, and registration opened on September 15, 2026.

Math Kangaroo test format and scoring, from the official FAQ and scoring pages (checked 17 October 2026)
ItemAs Math Kangaroo USA publishes it
Length"a 75 minute multiple-choice test for students in grades 1-12"
Questions24 questions in grades 1 to 4; 30 questions in grades 5 and higher
PointsQuestions come in three equal groups worth 3, 4 and 5 points
Wrong answers"There are no points taken away for answering questions incorrectly."
Top score120 from grade 5 up; 96 for grades 1 to 4
Date ruleThe contest day is the 3rd Thursday in March each year
RegistrationRegular from September 15 to December 31; late from January 1 to February 1
RankingsThe twenty highest scores in each grade nationally, and the three highest in each grade in each state

What the numbers mean for a seventh grader

In grades 5 to 12 there are 30 questions, so ten are worth 3 points, ten worth 4 and ten worth 5. By our arithmetic that is 30 + 40 + 50 = 120, which matches the published maximum.

Because nothing is taken away for a wrong answer, an unanswered question can only cost points. Our advice in class is to mark an answer for every question in the last two minutes, after a real attempt at the ones that can be reasoned out.

The 2027 date

For the 2027 individual contest the organiser gives the month, March 2027, and its standing rule of the third Thursday of March. By our calendar calculation that is Thursday, March 18, 2027; confirm with your center, which sets the exact arrangements.

Math Kangaroo also runs a newer in-person Team Competition in the fall for teams of 2 to 4 students. The questions themselves are selected by the International Math Kangaroo Committee, AKSF, not by the US organiser.

Sources: Math Kangaroo USA About the Test, Scoring, Team Competition and home page, checked 17 October 2026.

MOEMS in brief

In MOEMS’s words: "Five Olympiad Contests, each consisting of five questions, and limited to 30 minutes are held monthly from November through March." Division M covers sixth to eighth grade, and a team can have up to 35 students, on paper or online.

MOEMS was established in 1979. Groups can also host a one-day tournament with an individual round and a team round, any time from April through December.

Why the AMC 8 has no details here

The AMC 8 is run by the Mathematical Association of America. When we tried to read the MAA’s competition pages on 17 October 2026, the site returned an access-denied error, and we do not work around blocks like that.

So this page prints no AMC dates or rules. Check the MAA site directly, and see our AMC and math olympiad tutoring page for how we prepare students.

Reading the table

At the fast end, Countdown and Sprint reward instant recall: squares, primes, common fractions as decimals, and quick estimates. At the slow end, MOEMS gives six minutes a question, so it rewards trying several approaches and checking.

Math Kangaroo sits in the middle at two and a half minutes, but its later questions are worth more and are harder, so in practice a student should move faster through the early ones.

How we practise for each

For Sprint we run short timed sets of ten problems in about thirteen minutes, the same pace as the real round. For Target and MOEMS we slow down and ask students to write a full solution, then find a second method.

Team rounds get their own practice: four students, ten problems, and a rule that nobody works alone for more than five minutes.

Practice set

Seven practice problems in the style of middle school contests

These problems are ours, written for this page and not taken from any competition. They sit at roughly school and chapter level. Try them with a timer set to 80 seconds each.

  1. What is the sum of the first 20 positive odd integers?
  2. A rectangle’s length is 3 more than its width, and its perimeter is 46. What is its area?
  3. How many positive divisors does 72 have?
  4. The mean of five numbers is 18. Four of them are 12, 15, 20 and 25. What is the fifth?
  5. How many integers from 1 to 100 are divisible by 3 or by 5?
  6. A bag holds 4 red and 6 blue marbles. Two are drawn without replacement. What is the probability that both are red? Give a common fraction.
  7. What is the units digit of 7 to the power 2026?
Answers and the key idea (ours; each answer checked in Python)
ProblemAnswerKey idea
1400The first n odd numbers add to n squared, and 20 x 20 = 400
2130Width plus length is 23, so the width is 10 and the length 13; 10 x 13 = 130
31272 = 2 cubed x 3 squared, so (3 + 1) x (2 + 1) = 12 divisors
418The five numbers total 5 x 18 = 90; 90 minus 72 leaves 18
54733 multiples of 3 plus 20 multiples of 5, minus 6 multiples of 15 counted twice
62/154/10 x 3/9 = 12/90 = 2/15
79Units digits of powers of 7 cycle 7, 9, 3, 1; 2026 leaves remainder 2 when divided by 4

What these problems practise

Problems 1, 3 and 7 reward knowing a pattern so well that the answer takes seconds, which is what Sprint and Countdown need. Problems 2, 4 and 6 are short algebra and probability, the bread and butter of every contest here.

Problem 5 is the classic overlap trap: adding 33 and 20 gives 53, which counts the multiples of 15 twice.

Making more of your own

Change one number and solve again: the first 25 odd integers, divisors of 96, a perimeter of 58. If the method still works, the student has learned the idea, not the answer.

Then ask the harder version. Problem 5 becomes "divisible by 3, 5 or 7", which needs the full inclusion and exclusion idea.

Questions from parents

Middle school math competitions: what parents ask

What are the main math competitions for middle school in the US?

MATHCOUNTS (grades 6 to 8, from school to national level), Math Kangaroo USA (a 75-minute multiple-choice test for grades 1 to 12), MOEMS Division M (five monthly contests for grades 6 to 8) and the AMC 8, which is run by the Mathematical Association of America.

When is MATHCOUNTS in 2026-27?

School competitions run from November 2026 to January 2027, chapter competitions February 1 to 28, 2027, state competitions March 1 to 31, 2027, and the National Competition on May 9 to 10, 2027 in Washington, DC.

Can a homeschooled student do MATHCOUNTS?

Yes. Home schools can register for the Competition Series and submit a separate Home School Participation Form. Home school students should not register as non-school competitors.

What if my child's school does not do MATHCOUNTS?

If the school will not register or support participation, the parent may sign the student up online as an NSC (non-school competitor) once registration opens on November 5, 2026. There is a limit of 14 NSCs per school.

How is Math Kangaroo scored?

Each question carries 3, 4 or 5 points, split evenly across the paper, and a wrong answer costs nothing. From grade 5 up the maximum is 120; in grades 1 to 4 it is 96.

When is Math Kangaroo 2027?

The organiser lists March 2027, and its rule puts the contest on the third Thursday of March. Counting the calendar ourselves, that falls on March 18, 2027; your center confirms the details.

How many years can a student compete in MATHCOUNTS?

MATHCOUNTS limits competition participation to 3 years per student, though there is no limit on years of school-based coaching.

Why does this page not give AMC 8 dates?

The MAA website refused our request when we checked on 17 October 2026, and we only print competition facts read from the organiser. Please check the MAA site directly for AMC 8 dates and rules.

Are you affiliated with MATHCOUNTS or Math Kangaroo?

No. Modern Age Coders runs its own classes and has no tie to MATHCOUNTS, Math Kangaroo USA, MOEMS or the MAA. Families register with each competition themselves.

Which days and times can my child take classes?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays.
