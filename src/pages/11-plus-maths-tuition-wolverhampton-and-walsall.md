In short

West Midlands Grammar Schools says any child wanting a place at a selective grammar school in Birmingham, Shropshire, Walsall, Wolverhampton or Warwickshire must sit its entrance test. Locally that means Wolverhampton Girls' High School, Queen Mary's Grammar School and Queen Mary's High School. Last cycle, for 2027 entry, families registered between Tuesday 5 May and 4pm on Friday 26 June 2026, and children sat the test on Saturday 12 September. We teach the maths, live online. According to the partnership, a child faces two papers of roughly fifty to sixty minutes including instructions, each cut into separately timed blocks that alternate between English, verbal reasoning, mathematics and non-verbal or spatial questions, every one of them multiple choice. Mathematics carries a quarter of the weighting, non-verbal a quarter, English and verbal reasoning together a half.

Queen Mary's Grammar School adds that "Candidates MUST write answers on the separate answer sheets provided", and that each paper is normally about 50 minutes, with around an hour of administration.

Queen Mary's High School says children are allocated the test centre closest to their home address and cannot choose one. The test is the same everywhere in the partnership, so a child in Bilston sits the same paper as a child in Stratford-upon-Avon.

Both Queen Mary's schools say places depend first on reaching the Minimum Qualifying Score and then on each school's admission rules. We keep out of those rules entirely.

Two names, one section

## "Mathematics" and "Numerical Reasoning" are the same thing here

A small wording difference between the partnership and one of its schools can send families looking for the wrong kind of practice.

### What each source says

West Midlands Grammar Schools lists the four section types as "English Comprehension, Verbal Reasoning, Mathematics and Non-Verbal/Spatial Reasoning", and weights "25% for mathematics".

Queen Mary's Grammar School, describing the same test, says it assesses "Verbal Reasoning, Non-Verbal Reasoning and Numerical Reasoning". Same provider, same papers, same day: the "Numerical Reasoning" there is the partnership's mathematics.

### Why it matters

Families who read "numerical reasoning" sometimes buy books of number puzzles and code-breaking that sit outside the school curriculum. The familiarisation guide published for this very test puts it plainly: the maths draws on classroom learning, stretched by a few harder items.

So the preparation is the primary maths curriculum, and in particular the facts that let a child answer short questions quickly inside a timed section. That is where we spend the time.

On scoring, the Queen Mary's schools explain that results across the partnership use "an age-weighted standardisation around a median score of 200": half of children score above 200 and half below, after allowing for age. The Minimum Qualifying Score for 2027 entry is set by governors in November 2026, after the results. We never guess it, and we would be wary of anyone who did.

## Fractions, decimals and percentages that match at a glance

The Year 5 curriculum asks children to know "percentage and decimal equivalents of 1/2, 1/4, 1/5, 2/5, 4/5 and those fractions with a denominator of a multiple of 10 or 25". In a timed section, knowing them is much faster than working them out.

| Fact | Our question | Answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1/4 = 0.25 = 25% | What is 25% of 64? | 16: a quarter of 64 |
| 1/5 = 0.2 = 20% | Which is larger, 0.2 or 1/4? | 1/4, which is 0.25 |
| 2/5 = 0.4 = 40% | What is 40% of 35? | 14: two fifths of 35 |
| 4/5 = 0.8 = 80% | A test has 25 questions. A child gets 80% right. How many is that? | 20 |
| 7/10 = 0.7 = 70% | Put 0.65, 7/10 and 68% in order, smallest first. | 0.65, 68%, 7/10 |
| 9/25 = 0.36 = 36% | Write 9/25 as a percentage. | 36%: multiply top and bottom by 4 |

The curriculum chooses these fractions carefully: halves, quarters, fifths, and anything over 10 or 25, because each converts to hundredths in one step. A fraction over 25 becomes a percentage by multiplying top and bottom by 4; a fraction over 10, by multiplying by 10.

In a timed section of a multiple-choice paper, questions like "which is larger, 0.2 or 1/4?" appear in many disguises. A child who has to convert each time spends the section converting. A child who simply knows that 1/4 is 0.25 answers and moves on.

Ordering mixed forms, as in the fifth row, is where the facts pay off most. Turning everything into hundredths, 65, 70 and 68, makes the order obvious. Children who try to compare 0.65 with 7/10 directly often get it wrong.

Percentages of amounts follow straight from the facts: 40% of 35 is two fifths of 35, which is 14. We teach the fact first, then the calculation, so the calculation is short.

## What local families ask us

### Which schools use the West Midlands test here?

Wolverhampton Girls' High School, Queen Mary's Grammar School and Queen Mary's High School, as part of the Shropshire, Wolverhampton and Walsall consortium within West Midlands Grammar Schools.

### Is it the same test as Birmingham's?

Yes. West Midlands Grammar Schools says children applying to selective grammar schools in Birmingham, Shropshire, Walsall, Wolverhampton and Warwickshire sit its entrance test.

### What is "Numerical Reasoning"?

It is how Queen Mary's Grammar School describes the maths sections of the same GL test that the partnership calls "Mathematics". The preparation is primary maths.

### How much does maths count?

The partnership publishes the weightings as 50% English and verbal reasoning, 25% non-verbal reasoning and 25% mathematics.

### What score does my child need?

The Queen Mary's schools say the Minimum Qualifying Score is set by governors after the results; for 2027 entry it was subject to confirmation in November 2026. Scores are standardised around a median of 200. We do not predict scores.

### When is the next test?

On 26 September 2026 neither Queen Mary's school showed 2028-entry dates yet. Last cycle's pattern was registration in May and June of Year 5 and a Saturday test in mid-September of Year 6.

### Can we choose the test centre?

Queen Mary's High School says children are allocated the test centre closest to their home address and cannot select one.

### Do you teach English or reasoning?

No. Three quarters of the weighting is English and reasoning, and we teach neither; nor do we comment on school choices or places.

### Do you use GL papers?

No. We write our own questions.

### What do lessons cost?

The first lesson is free. After that, USD 100 a month in a small group or USD 150 a month one to one.
