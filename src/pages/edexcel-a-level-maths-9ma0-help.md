# Edexcel A level Maths 9MA0 help

Pearson Edexcel A level Mathematics, code 9MA0, is examined in three papers at the end of Year 13. Two of them are entirely pure maths. The third, Statistics and Mechanics, is divided into two sections and is where the large data set, modelling and most of the problem-solving marks sit. ## How many papers are in Edexcel A level Maths?

Three, equal in time and marks. Two are pure; one is applied.

The specification describes 9MA0 as consisting "of three externally-examined papers", and is firm about timing: "Students must complete all assessment in May/June in any single year." There is no route to sit one paper in Year 12 and the others later. Each paper is a 2-hour written exam out of 100, and the grade comes from "the total combined marks (out of 300) for the three compulsory papers. Individual papers are not graded."

**Pearson Edexcel A level Mathematics 9MA0: the three papers**

| Paper | Code | Content | Time | Marks | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1: Pure Mathematics 1 | 9MA0/01 | Any pure topic, 1 to 10 | 2 hours | 100 | 33.33 per cent |
| Paper 2: Pure Mathematics 2 | 9MA0/02 | Any pure topic, 1 to 10 | 2 hours | 100 | 33.33 per cent |
| Paper 3: Statistics and Mechanics | 9MA0/03 | Section A statistics topics 1 to 5; Section B mechanics topics 6 to 9 | 2 hours | 100 | 33.33 per cent |

Source: Pearson Edexcel Level 3 Advanced GCE in Mathematics (9MA0) specification, Issue 4, February 2020, "Qualification at a glance", from the [Pearson A level Mathematics page](https://qualifications.pearson.com/en/qualifications/edexcel-a-levels/mathematics-2017.html).

### The ten pure topics

Proof; algebra and functions; coordinate geometry in the (x, y) plane; sequences and series; trigonometry; exponentials and logarithms; differentiation; integration; numerical methods; vectors.

Either pure paper "may contain questions on any topics from the Pure Mathematics content", so there is no topic that belongs to Paper 1 only. Revision for both papers is the same revision.

### The applied topics

Statistics: sampling, data presentation and interpretation, probability, statistical distributions, hypothesis testing. Mechanics: quantities and units, kinematics including projectiles, forces and Newton's laws including friction, and moments.

Pure skills still matter in Paper 3: kinematics with variable acceleration uses calculus, and resolving forces uses trigonometry.

## What is special about Edexcel A level Maths Paper 3?

It is the only applied paper, it assumes the large data set, and it gives problem solving more weight than either pure paper.

Pearson publishes the assessment-objective split paper by paper, and it shows a real difference. On each pure paper, AO3 (solving problems and modelling) is worth 6.33 to 7.67 per cent of the A level. On Paper 3 it is worth 10.33 to 11.67 per cent, while AO2 (reasoning and communicating) falls to 5.66 to 7.00 per cent. Paper 3 is where the specification puts the most modelling.

**Assessment objectives by paper, as a percentage of the whole A level (Pearson's figures, rounded by Pearson)**

| Paper | AO1 technique | AO2 reasoning | AO3 problem solving |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1: Pure Mathematics 1 | 16.00 to 17.33 | 8.66 to 10 | 6.33 to 7.67 |
| Paper 2: Pure Mathematics 2 | 16.00 to 17.33 | 8.66 to 10 | 6.33 to 7.67 |
| Paper 3: Statistics and Mechanics | 16.00 to 17.33 | 5.66 to 7.00 | 10.33 to 11.67 |
| Whole A level | 48 to 52 | 23 to 27 | 23 to 27 |

Then there is the data set. The specification says: "Students are required to become familiar with the data set in advance of the final assessment." Questions "assume knowledge and understanding of the data set", and Pearson is explicit that they "should be likely to give a material advantage to students who have studied and are familiar with the data set." At the same time, students "will not be required to have copies of the data set in the examination, nor will they be required to have detailed knowledge of the actual data".

### What familiarity means

Knowing the variables, their units and how they are recorded, what any abbreviations stand for, and where values are missing or unusual.

Being able to read a summary statistic or chart from the data set and interpret it in context, without needing the setting explained.

### What it does not mean

Memorising individual values. Pearson rules that out in writing.

Using a different year's data. The specification says Pearson reviews the data set annually and will notify centres of any change "before the beginning of the two-year course", so download the version for your cohort.

## Do you get a formula sheet in Edexcel A level Maths?

Yes: the Mathematical Formulae and Statistical Tables booklet. But Appendix 1 lists formulae that are deliberately left out of it.

Every 9MA0 paper is sat with "The booklet 'Mathematical Formulae and Statistical Tables'". The specification adds a list of formulae that "students are expected to know for A Level Mathematics" which "will not appear in the booklet". These run from the quadratic formula and laws of indices to the sine and cosine rules, standard derivatives and integrals, standardising a normal variable, and the friction model.

## Four problems in the style of the three papers

Two pure, two applied. All written by us to show the method and the written reasoning the papers reward.

**Our first problem, pure: integration by parts.** Find the exact value of the integral of x ln x from x = 1 to x = e.

1. Choose u = ln x, because its derivative 1/x is simpler, and dv/dx = x, so v = x2/2.
2. Apply the formula: the integral of x ln x is (x2/2) ln x &minus; the integral of (x2/2)(1/x), which is (x2/2) ln x &minus; x2/4.
3. At x = e: e2/2 &minus; e2/4 = e2/4. At x = 1: 0 &minus; 1/4 = &minus;1/4.
4. Subtract: e2/4 + 1/4 = (e2 + 1)/4, about 2.0973. The question asked for an exact value, so (e2 + 1)/4 is the answer and the decimal is only a check.

### Our second problem, pure: proof by contradiction

Prove that there are no positive integers a and b with a2 &minus; b2 = 2.

Assume there are. Then (a &minus; b)(a + b) = 2. Since a + b is a positive integer, a &minus; b is positive too, and a + b > a &minus; b, so a &minus; b = 1 and a + b = 2. Adding gives 2a = 3, so a = 1.5, which is not an integer. This contradicts the assumption, so no such integers exist.

### Our third problem, mechanics: a projectile

A ball is projected from level ground at 20 m s&minus;1, 30&deg; above the horizontal. Taking g = 9.8 m s&minus;2 and modelling the ball as a particle with no air resistance, find its range.

Vertically, 0 = (20 sin 30&deg;)t &minus; 4.9t2, so t = 10 &divide; 4.9 = 2.04 s. Horizontally, the range is (20 cos 30&deg;) &times; 2.041 = 35.3 m. The modelling assumptions are part of the answer: Paper 3 can ask which one to refine.

**Our fourth problem, statistics.** Events A and B have P(A) = 0.4, P(B) = 0.5 and P(A &cup; B) = 0.7. Are A and B independent? Using P(A &cup; B) = P(A) + P(B) &minus; P(A &cap; B), we get P(A &cap; B) = 0.2. Since P(A) &times; P(B) = 0.2 as well, the events are independent, and so P(A | B) = 0.4, equal to P(A). The final sentence, stating what the equality shows, is where the reasoning mark sits.

## What students and parents ask about Edexcel A level Maths

### How many papers are in Edexcel A level Maths 9MA0?

Three: Pure Mathematics 1, Pure Mathematics 2, and Statistics and Mechanics. Each is a 2-hour written paper out of 100 marks and worth a third of the A level.

### What is on Edexcel A level Maths Paper 3?

Section A is statistics: sampling, data presentation, probability, distributions and hypothesis testing. Section B is mechanics: units, kinematics including projectiles, forces and Newton's laws, and moments.

### Is there a formula sheet for Edexcel A level Maths?

Yes. The booklet Mathematical Formulae and Statistical Tables is provided in every paper. Appendix 1 of the specification lists formulae students must know because they are not in the booklet.

### What is the Edexcel large data set?

A set of real data that Pearson publishes and that students must become familiar with before Paper 3. Questions assume knowledge of its context, but students do not need copies in the exam or to memorise its values.

### Can you use a calculator in all Edexcel A level Maths papers?

Yes. Calculators are allowed in all three papers and must have an iterative function and be able to compute summary statistics and probabilities from standard distributions.

### Can I sit one Edexcel A level Maths paper in Year 12?

No. The specification says all assessment must be completed in May and June of a single year, so the three papers are sat together.

### How is Edexcel A level Maths graded?

On a six-grade scale from A* to E, using the combined total out of 300. Individual papers are not graded, and a result below E is an unclassified U.

### What do I need before starting 9MA0?

Pearson sets no formal prior learning requirement. In our experience a strong Higher GCSE or Extended IGCSE in maths makes Year 12 far more manageable, and sixth forms set their own entry grades.

### Are you part of Pearson Edexcel?

No. We are an independent teaching company with no link to Pearson, and our practice questions are our own.
