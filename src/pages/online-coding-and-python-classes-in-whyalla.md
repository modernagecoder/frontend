## How far did Whyalla fall from its peak?

Twenty-five yearly estimates, one running maximum, and a gap that a simple subtraction misses.

**Estimated resident population, statistical area "Whyalla", selected years (ABS Data API, read 10 October 2026)**

| Year | Estimate | Running peak so far | Below the peak by |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2001 | 22,003 | 22,003 | 0 |
| 2005 | 21,898 | 22,003 | 105 |
| 2009 | 22,299 | 22,299 | 0 |
| 2014 | 22,624 | 22,624 | 0 |
| 2017 | 22,162 | 22,624 | 462 |
| 2021 | 21,862 | 22,624 | 762 |
| 2025 | 21,641 | 22,624 | 983 |

**Three ways to describe the same 25 numbers (our calculation)**

| Summary | Result |
|---|---|
| First year minus last year | a fall of 362 (1.65%) |
| Largest fall from any earlier peak | 983 below the 2014 peak (4.34%) |
| Year-on-year changes | 11 rises and 13 falls |
| Biggest single-year fall | 270, from 2016 to 2017 |
| Biggest single-year rise | 169, from 2006 to 2007 |

### Keep a running peak

itertools.accumulate(values, max) walks the list once and returns, for every year, the highest estimate seen up to that year.

### Measure the gap

Subtract each year from its running peak. The largest of those gaps is the maximum drawdown, here 983 people in 2025.

### One pass, not two loops

Comparing every year with every earlier year takes 300 comparisons for 25 years; the running maximum needs one pass of 25 steps.

### Endpoints can hide the story

Measured from 2001, Whyalla looks almost flat. Measured from 2014, it has lost close to a thousand residents. Neither number is wrong, but only the running maximum shows both at once, which is why we teach learners to compute it before writing a one-line summary of any series.

These are the bureau's estimated resident population figures for the statistical area named Whyalla, which does not have exactly the same boundary as the urban area used in the Census table above, so the two sets of numbers should not be compared directly. The neighbouring statistical area "Whyalla - North" shows zero residents in every year. All differences, peaks and gaps are our own calculation from the published figures.

## What the project teaches about Python

The finished program is short. Getting there exercises most of the language a beginner needs.

Learners start with a plain for loop: keep a variable called peak, update it whenever a bigger value turns up, and print the gap each year. Once that works, they meet itertools.accumulate, which does the same job in one line, and they check that both versions agree. Then they reshape the output into a small table, format the numbers with thousands separators, and write a test that feeds in a series with a known drop so the function proves itself before it touches real data.

### Teenagers

Write the running maximum by hand, then with accumulate, and draw a text chart of the series with one row per year.

### University students

Generalise to the longest losing streak and the slowest recovery time, and compare the cost of the naive and single-pass versions.

### Adults

Apply the same check to a work series such as monthly sales or stock levels, and report the drawdown alongside the headline change.

## An AI assistant can summarise a series in seconds. Why learn to code the summary?

Because a quick summary usually compares the first and last values, and in Whyalla that hides a fall almost three times larger.

Ask a chatbot whether a town is growing and it will often take the earliest and latest figures and report the difference. For Whyalla that answer is a drop of 362 people since 2001, which is true but leaves out the climb to 2014 and the long slide since. Without the yearly series in front of you, there is no way to tell how much the tidy answer has left out.

Learners who have written the running maximum know to ask for the peak, the drawdown and the direction of recent years, and they can check an assistant's answer against the data in a few lines of Python. That is the difference between accepting a summary and understanding a trend.

In 2026 the useful skill is not producing a summary but knowing which summary the data actually supports. The longer argument is in [why teenagers should still learn to code in 2026](/blog/is-coding-worth-learning-2026).

## Whyalla Python class questions

### What is a running maximum?

A running maximum is a list where each position holds the largest value seen so far. In Python, itertools.accumulate(values, max) produces it in a single pass over the data.

### What is a maximum drawdown?

A maximum drawdown is the largest fall from an earlier peak to a later value. For the Whyalla estimates it is 983 people, from 22,624 in 2014 to 21,641 in 2025.

### How many people live in Whyalla?

Census night 2021 counted 21,154 people in the Whyalla urban area, with a median age of 41.

### Why do the Census figure and the yearly estimate differ?

They measure different things over different boundaries: the Census counts people in the urban area on one night, while the estimated resident population covers a statistical area and is adjusted for people missed or away.
