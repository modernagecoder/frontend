KMK rules

Which IB maths course does Germany accept for university entrance?

The KMK, the standing conference of the Länder education ministers, first agreed in 1986 how an IB Diploma is recognised as a German university entrance qualification. The current version dates from 15 June 2023. These are its conditions, with the maths ones marked.

Up to and including exam year 2024, the version of 24 March 2022 applies. Up to 2020 the agreement named the older courses, Mathematics SL or HL.

Read the table from the bottom up and one thing is clear: in Germany, maths is not just one of six subjects. It is the only subject the agreement names twice, once as a required subject and again in a footnote on its level. A pupil who chooses Standard Level maths in the first Diploma year is making a decision that the KMK text treats as consequential for what she can study later.

This is not legal or admissions advice, and we give none. The agreement is applied by universities and the Länder, and its text names an advisory committee of the KMK's Central Office for Foreign Education (ZAB) to review whether the IB still meets the criteria. If a choice of course matters for a particular degree, ask the university, and the school's IB coordinator, before the subject choice is fixed.

SL or HL

Is IB Maths SL enough to study in Germany?

The footnote is short and easy to miss. Here it is in plain English, then what it means for a pupil choosing courses.

What the footnote says

In the German text, footnote 3 says that graduates who present Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches SL or Mathematics: Applications and Interpretation SL from the 2021 exam session onward can be granted only a subject-restricted entrance qualification (fachgebundener Hochschulzugang), for Fachhochschulen and universities and only for subjects outside the mathematical, scientific and technical field.

The exception is graduates of the schools listed in the agreement's two annexes: for them the maths requirement counts as met and the Diploma opens general university entrance.

What the annexes are

Annex 1 lists international schools that have a separate agreement with the IBO; in the current German file it is dated 26 March 2026 and has a section for schools in Germany (Deutschland). Annex 2 lists schools abroad with the bilingual German IB.

Whether your school is on the list is a question for your school, which will know. We do not reproduce the list here, because it changes and only the KMK's current file is authoritative.

For a pupil the practical upshot is a three-way choice. AA HL keeps the most options open and is the hardest course. AI HL is also Higher Level and suits a pupil whose strength is modelling and statistics rather than proof. A Standard Level course is a sound choice for many pupils, especially at a listed school or with a clear plan outside the sciences, but it is a choice to make knowingly, not by default.

AA HL

Algebra, proof and calculus in depth. The heaviest load and the widest set of doors.

AI HL

Modelling, statistics and technology-led problem solving, still at Higher Level.

AA SL or AI SL

A lighter load. Check the footnote and your school's status before you settle on it.

German grade

How is an IB score turned into a German grade?

German universities rank applicants by a single average grade, from 1.0 (the top) to 4.0. The KMK agreement gives a formula to turn an IB total into that grade.

The formula is N = 1 + 3 · (Pmax − P) / (Pmax − Pmin), where P is the IB total, Pmax = 42 and Pmin = 24. Bonus points are added into P, and a pupil with 42, 43, 44 or 45 points is given the top grade, 1.0. The agreement adds that the average is calculated in the country where the Diploma is issued. Put the numbers in and the formula simplifies to N = 1 + (42 − P) / 6: each extra IB point improves the German average by 1/6, about 0.17.

That is a straight line, and a straight line can be read in both directions. Most pupils ask "what grade do my points give?" An IB maths pupil can ask the more useful question, "how many points do I need for the grade I want?", by inverting the function: P = 42 − 6 · (N − 1).

The inverted line has a trap, and it is the lesson. For a target of 2.3 the formula gives P = 42 − 6 · 1.3 = 34.2. IB totals are whole numbers, and a higher total gives a better (smaller) grade, so the pupil needs at least 35, not 34: 34 points give 2.333, just outside the target. Rounding the wrong way is the mistake; the check is to put the answer back into the original formula.

IB in Germany

Where in Germany can pupils take the IB Diploma?

We cannot give a national count: the IBO's school finder refused our request, so this page prints no total. What we can quote is AGIS (Arbeitsgemeinschaft der Internationalen Schulen in Deutschland) on its own members.

In AGIS's own words, every member is an IB school with IBO authorisation running one or more of the four IB programmes; it adds that English is the shared teaching language, and that the IB Diploma, with the prescribed subject combinations, is equivalent to the German Abitur. Its directory pages list the IB Diploma Programme, or the IB generally, for member schools in Berlin, Bonn, Dresden, Düsseldorf, Frankfurt, Hamburg, Hannover and Stuttgart, among other places. Some German Gymnasien also offer the IB Diploma alongside the Abitur, according to the same directory.

We mention these places only as context. We are not connected with AGIS, the IBO or any school, and no school sends pupils to us. A pupil at any IB school in Germany, or at a school abroad, learns the same course with us, because the IB syllabus is the same everywhere.

Practice

How do you work backwards in IB maths? Three practice tasks

Our own tasks in the style of IB papers, not taken from any IB exam. Each one starts from the target and works back to the input, then checks the answer in the original condition, as the grade example above did.

AA style: an inverse and a log equation

Task 1. f(x) = (3x − 1) / 2. Find f−1(x) and the value of x for which f(x) = 7. Working back: write y = (3x − 1) / 2, so 2y = 3x − 1 and x = (2y + 1) / 3. Hence f−1(x) = (2x + 1) / 3 and f−1(7) = 15 / 3 = 5. Check: f(5) = 14 / 2 = 7.

Task 2. Solve log2x + log2(x − 2) = 3. Working back: log2(x(x − 2)) = 3, so x(x − 2) = 8, x² − 2x − 8 = 0, (x − 4)(x + 2) = 0. Check: x = 4 gives log24 + log22 = 2 + 1 = 3. x = −2 is not in the domain, because log2(−2) is undefined. Only x = 4 survives.

AI style: a model read in reverse

Task 3. A phone battery at 18 % charges at a steady 2.5 percentage points per minute, so C(t) = 18 + 2.5t. When does it reach 80 %? Working back: 2.5t = 62, t = 24.8 minutes. Check: 18 + 2.5 · 24.8 = 80.

Then question the model. Real batteries slow down near full, so the straight line is honest only for part of the range. In an AI paper, saying where a model stops being reasonable earns marks of its own, and the internal assessment rewards it too.

Why "check" is a step. In task 2 the algebra produced two answers and only one was true. Working backwards finds candidates; the original condition decides.

  1. Write the target first. f(x) = 7, a grade of 2.3, 80 % charge: name what you want before you rearrange.
  2. Undo the steps in reverse order. The last operation done to x is the first one you undo.
  3. Put the answer back in. Into the original equation, not the rearranged one, and reject what fails the domain or the context.

IB maths tutoring in Germany: common questions

Can I get an IB maths tutor in Germany who teaches in English?

Yes. Modern Age Coders teaches IB Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches and Applications and Interpretation, at SL and HL, live online in English to Diploma pupils in Germany, in a group or one to one.

Are the classes in German?

No. Every class is live and taught in English only, which is also the language of the IB papers. Mentors adapt to each pupil's pace and accent.

Which IB maths course does Germany require?

The KMK agreement on recognising the IB Diploma, as amended on 15 June 2023, requires maths as Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches or Mathematics: Applications and Interpretation among the six subjects.

Is IB Maths SL accepted for German universities?

The KMK text says that from the 2021 exams, AA SL or AI SL alone gives only a subject-restricted entrance qualification, for subjects outside maths, science and technology, unless the school is listed in the agreement's annexes. Ask your school and the university about your own case; we do not give admissions advice.

How is my IB total converted into a German grade?

The KMK formula is N = 1 + 3(42 - P) / (42 - 24), where P includes bonus points and totals of 42 or more give 1.0. By our arithmetic, 36 points give 2.0 and 30 points give 3.0.

Do I need a minimum grade in maths?

The agreement asks for at least IB grade 4 in each of the six subjects. One grade 3 can be balanced by a grade 5 in another subject of at least the same level, with at least 24 points overall.

Do you help with the internal assessment?

Yes, with choosing a topic, planning the maths and reviewing drafts against the criteria. The exploration must be the pupil's own work, and we teach it that way.

What times are possible?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. German afternoons fall in the Indian evening, by four and a half hours in winter and three and a half in summer.

Who teaches the classes?

Mentors based in India who teach IB maths live online in English. A pupil works with the same mentor throughout.

What does it cost?

Monthly fees are shown in euro on this page using the European Central Bank reference rate; the payment runs in US dollars and your bank converts it. The first class costs nothing and needs no card.
