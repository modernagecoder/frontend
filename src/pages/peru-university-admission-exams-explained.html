Peru's university admission exams, explained in English

Getting into a Peruvian university usually means sitting that university's own entrance exam, in Spanish, often more than once. For a family who reads English more comfortably, or who is new to Peru, the system can look like a maze of acronyms. This page lays it out from the universities' own pages: how admission works, what UNI, PUCP, the Universidad de Lima and San Marcos ask for, the dates of the current cycle, and what an IB or English-medium student can do.

The short version

Peruvian universities admit students through their own competitive entrance exams, held in Spanish, usually twice a year for intakes starting around March and August; most also run a pre-university centre and special routes such as one for IB Diploma holders. This page explains UNI, PUCP, Universidad de Lima and San Marcos from their own pages. Modern Age Coders does not prepare students for these exams as an academy: we teach the mathematics and reasoning beneath them, live online and in English, in a group or one to one, at flexible hours.

How admission works

How does university admission work in Peru?

Each university runs its own competition for a fixed number of places. There is no single national exam like the SAT, and a place at one university says nothing about another.

The legal frame

PUCP quotes Peru's University Law, Ley 30220, in its admission guide: admission is by public competition for places defined in advance, at most once per academic cycle, and the competition consists of a knowledge exam as the main compulsory process, with an optional assessment of aptitudes and attitudes alongside.

In practice that means each university publishes its own rules, its own subjects and its own calendar. A student applying to three universities may sit three different exams.

Two intakes a year, and many routes

Universities name their intakes by year and half: PUCP's 2027-1 starts in March 2027, UNMSM's 2026 process had two stages, 2026-I and 2026-II, and UNI's 2026-2 ordinary exam ran in August 2026.

Alongside the ordinary exam, most universities offer other routes: an exam for students still in 5° de secundaria, the university's own pre-university centre (CEPRE-UNI, CeprePUCP, CEPUSM at San Marcos, the Centro de Estudios Preuniversitarios at the Universidad de Lima), and routes for IB Diploma holders or top school students.

Peru also has a large private industry of academias preuniversitarias, independent cram schools that prepare students for these exams. We are not one, and nothing here is advice to choose any route. We describe what the universities publish and teach mathematics in English. For a full picture of a 5° de secundaria year, including school and coding, our Spanish page for 5to de secundaria may help.

Two of UNI's three papers are mathematics or physical sciences, which fits an engineering university. A student who is fluent in algebra and comfortable with physics problems starts with an advantage that cannot be crammed in a month.

PUCP and U de Lima: reading and maths

Both put reasoning with numbers and reasoning with text side by side. At the Universidad de Lima the humanities part explicitly assesses Spanish spelling and grammar, which matters for a student educated mostly in English.

San Marcos: a public university with five areas

UNMSM's 2027-I schedule groups its programmes into engineering, basic sciences, humanities and social sciences, economics and management, and health sciences. Its engineering list includes software engineering, computer science and artificial intelligence engineering.

Language

Are Peru's admission exams in Spanish, and can classes in English help?

They are in Spanish. Classes in English help with the mathematics and the reasoning, which are the same in any language, but they are not a substitute for reading Spanish fluently in an exam.

Where English helps

Mathematics is a large share of every exam above: a whole paper at UNI, a stated emphasis at PUCP, a full reasoning area at the Universidad de Lima. A student who understands algebra and geometry deeply, in whatever language, carries that into a Spanish paper.

Students at English-medium, Cambridge and IB schools in Peru often learn their maths in English already. For them, strengthening it in English is natural, and the university route via the IB Diploma, below, may matter more than the ordinary exam.

Where it does not

The humanities and reading sections test Spanish itself. The Universidad de Lima's guide says its reading area assesses spelling and the grammatical rules of Spanish, and PUCP emphasises reading comprehension. Those need Spanish practice, which we do not provide.

We say this plainly because the alternative, implying that English classes prepare a student for a Spanish exam, would be untrue. We teach mathematics in English; a student's Spanish reading needs its own work.

Most exams also ban calculators: the Universidad de Lima lists them among prohibited items, and PUCP does not let candidates bring them onto campus. A student used to doing arithmetic on a calculator at school should practise by hand well before the exam. That is one area where our teaching transfers directly.

Should you guess?

When wrong answers lose points, should a student guess? A lesson in expected value

The Universidad de Lima's guide says each correct answer earns points, each wrong answer subtracts points, and a blank changes nothing. Its guide does not say how many points a wrong answer costs, so the numbers below are invented for the lesson. The reasoning is what matters.

The question (ours)

A test question has five options, one correct. A correct answer earns 1 mark. Suppose a wrong answer loses some fraction of a mark: nothing, a quarter, or a half. If you have no idea, should you guess? And what changes if you can rule out one or two options?

The tool is expected value: the average mark a guess would earn if you made the same guess many times. If it is positive, guessing gains marks on average; if negative, it loses them.

The working

With k options left, a blind guess is right with probability 1/k and wrong with probability (k − 1)/k. So the expected mark is 1/k × 1 − (k − 1)/k × penalty.

With five options and a penalty of a quarter, that is 1/5 − 4/5 × 1/4 = 0. A blind guess neither gains nor loses on average. Eliminate one option and it becomes 1/4 − 3/4 × 1/4 = 1/16, a small gain.

The lesson students should take away is not "always guess" or "never guess". It is that the size of the penalty decides how much you must know before a guess is worth it. With a heavy penalty, a guess only breaks even once two options are ruled out; with no penalty at all, as in ONEM's early stages, you should always answer. Before any exam, find out the scoring rule from the official guide and do this calculation once, calmly, instead of in the exam hall.

We use Fraction rather than decimals for a reason we show students. Rewrite expected with ordinary floats (p = 1 / options_left) and the break-even case with three options and a penalty of a half comes out as -5.551115123125783e-17, a hair below zero. Version 2 then decides three options are not enough and prints 2 instead of 3: wrong advice from a rounding error. Exactness is part of the mathematics, not decoration.

Questions families ask

Peru's university admission exams: questions and answers

Are the classes in Spanish?

No. Our classes are live, online and taught only in English. The admission exams themselves are in Spanish, so a student also needs Spanish reading practice, which we do not teach. What we teach is the mathematics and reasoning, which are the same in either language.

Is there one national university entrance exam in Peru?

No. Each university runs its own admission competition. PUCP's guide quotes Ley 30220: admission is by public competition, at most once per cycle, with a knowledge exam as the main compulsory part. A student applying to several universities usually sits several exams.

What does the UNI exam cover?

UNI's ordinary exam is three in-person papers on a Monday, Wednesday and Friday: academic aptitude and humanities, then mathematics, then physics and chemistry. Its 2026-2 exam was held on 10, 12 and 14 August 2026. Applicants must have finished secundaria.

Does the Universidad de Lima take marks off for wrong answers?

Its 2027-1(A) guide says each correct answer earns points and each wrong answer subtracts them, while unanswered questions neither add nor subtract, and marking more than one option counts as wrong. The guide does not state the size of the penalty.

Can an IB Diploma help with admission?

At some universities. The Universidad de Lima has a route for IB Diploma holders who apply within three years, and PUCP has a Diploma de Bachillerato route and can recognise Bachillerato courses for up to 23 credits. Each university decides; check its current guide.

When are the San Marcos exams?

UNMSM's 2027-I exam had already been held by November 2026; its admission letters were handed out from 2 to 5 November 2026. Its 2027-I rules can only be downloaded after a bank payment, so we could not read them. Check the university's admission office for the next process.

Do your classes prepare students for a specific university?

No. We are not an academia preuniversitaria and we are not connected with any university. We teach the mathematics that every maths section relies on, in English, and we never promise admission.

When are classes in Peru time?

Any day, any time, flexible: choose a batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at the hour that suits your family. Peru is on UTC minus 5 all year and India on UTC plus 5:30, so 17:00 in Lima is 03:30 the next morning in India.

Can calculators be used in these exams?

Not in the ones we could check. The Universidad de Lima lists calculators among prohibited items and PUCP does not allow them onto campus for written evaluations. Practising arithmetic by hand is therefore part of good preparation.

How are fees paid?

By card, in US dollars; your bank converts each charge to soles, so the amount can shift a little from month to month. The first class is free and needs no card details.
