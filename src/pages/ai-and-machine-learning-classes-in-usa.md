## Why does a kilowatt-hour cost four times more in Hawaii than in Idaho?

Every market we teach gets a first project on public data from that country, and the United States has the richest federal open data anywhere. The source is the Energy Information Administration's Electric Power Monthly, Table 5.6.A, average price of electricity to ultimate customers by state, residential sector, from Form EIA-861M. The edition read here is labelled "Data for May 2026, Release Date: July 23, 2026", and its own footnote says the values are preliminary estimates based on a cutoff model sample. Students meet that footnote on day one, because a dataset honest about how provisional it is deserves quoting with the warning attached.

The two price columns are published by the EIA. The year on year column is computed from them. The May 2026 column is preliminary and will be revised in a later edition, which is stated here for the same reason it is stated at the source.

Divide Hawaii by Idaho and you get 4.21. A twelve year old can hold that ratio in their head and a seventeen year old can turn it into a modelling problem. Why would the same commodity, delivered the same way, cost four times as much in one state as in another?

The target is not the price. It is the year on year percentage change in a state's residential price, and that switch is the most important decision in the project. Prices did not simply move upward everywhere either: Connecticut fell 13.4 percent while Illinois rose 28.4 percent, so any story beginning "energy got more expensive" is too coarse to use.

Features come from the EIA State Electricity Profiles, 2024 data released 10 November 2025, which publishes net summer capacity, net generation and total retail sales for every state. Students derive ratios: generation over retail sales says whether a state exports or imports power, and generation over capacity times hours gives a rough capacity factor.

A trap sits inside that second table and it is the likeliest way this project fails in the real world. The State Electricity Profiles also carry an average retail price column whose national figure is 12.68 cents per kilowatt-hour, because it averages across all sectors. The target here is the residential price, national figure 18.44 cents. A student who joins the two tables on a column called "price" has silently merged two metrics, and no error message will say so.

1. #### Transcribe the table, then prove the parse is right

  Pull Table 5.6.A for all fifty states plus the District of Columbia, residential sector, current month and year-ago column. Type the twenty rows above in by hand first, then check the parse against the printed national figures of 18.44 and 17.37. If the totals do not reproduce, the parse is wrong. The data is not.
2. #### Plot this year against last year before writing a model

  Put May 2026 on one axis and May 2025 on the other. The points sit almost exactly on the forty five degree line, and that chart kills the obvious framing before any modelling starts. That framing is to predict the May 2026 price with last year's price as a feature, watch R-squared land near 0.97 and declare victory. Nothing has been learned except that prices are sticky, because the answer was handed to the model in its own input.
3. #### Redefine the target, then build a baseline dumb enough to be honest

  Switch to year on year percent change. Predict that every state moved by the national average of positive 6.2 percent and record that baseline's mean absolute error. Any model that cannot beat the number is worthless, and most first attempts do not. Writing it down first stops a student quietly moving the goalposts later.
4. #### Join the generation features using ratios, never raw totals

  Derive ratios rather than merging raw numbers. Raw net generation only encodes how large a state is, and Texas will dominate every coefficient for no interesting reason. Check the price column trap before the merge, not after the results look strange.
5. #### Validate leave-one-out, because fifty one rows demand it

  Fit ordinary least squares and validate leave-one-out. Do not use an eighty twenty split: with fifty one rows a ten row test set makes the error swing wildly with the random seed, and a student who reruns it twice gets two different stories. Report the leave-one-out mean absolute error against the step three baseline.
6. #### Remove Hawaii, refit, and compare the coefficient tables

  Hawaii is a leverage point. Its island grid burns imported fuel oil and it sits nearly nineteen cents above the next highest state, so with at most fifty one rows that one observation can anchor a least squares fit by itself. Refit without it and record which coefficients changed sign and which changed magnitude. That comparison, not the accuracy score, is the deliverable.
7. #### Write the conclusion, and name what the features cannot reach

  American retail electricity prices are set by state public utility commissions through rate cases. No feature built from generation mix can see a regulatory decision, so part of the variation is structurally invisible here. Say so, and note that the May 2026 column is preliminary. Being able to write "here is the part my data cannot explain" is the skill this exercise exists to teach.

#### Building AI

- Find real data, reconcile it against a published total, account for every row you dropped
- Pick a model knowing which assumption it is quietly making about your data
- Train it, then report the error in a unit a neighbour could argue with
- Separate a bad result into its causes: the sample, the features, or the model itself
- Put it in front of inputs nobody anticipated and watch which part gives way
- Built on probability, matrix algebra and programming, none of which get deprecated

Why now, in the United States

## A federal order to teach AI, and fifty state systems that mostly do not

There is no funded national AI education programme here in the sense a ministry would recognise. There is a sequence of executive actions, and reading them in order explains the market. Executive Order 14179, "Removing Barriers to American Leadership in Artificial Intelligence", was signed in January 2025 and commissioned the action plan that followed.

The order that matters to a school is Executive Order 14277, "Advancing Artificial Intelligence Education for American Youth", signed 23 April 2025 and published in the Federal Register on 28 April 2025. It establishes the White House Task Force on AI Education, and directs it to produce plans for a Presidential Artificial Intelligence Challenge within 90 days, with agencies holding the Challenge no later than 12 months after those plans are submitted. The stated purpose is to encourage and highlight student and educator achievements in AI, and to bring government, academia, philanthropy and industry together. One qualification belongs beside it: the order carries no appropriated dollar figure. It is a directive instrument, and a directive does not by itself put a machine learning teacher in a class.

"Winning the Race: America's AI Action Plan" arrived on 23 July 2025 with more than 90 federal policy actions across three pillars: accelerating innovation, building American AI infrastructure, and leading in international diplomacy and security. It is led by the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy with the AI and Crypto Czar's office, and three further executive orders on infrastructure and technology export were signed the same day. It states no dollar figures, and its named high-demand occupations are electricians and HVAC technicians. That is a plan to staff data-centre construction, not to teach a fourteen year old how a gradient descends. The children's mandate lives in Executive Order 14277 and nowhere else.

Now the delivery side. Code.org's 2025 State of AI and CS Education report, data finalised 1 December 2025 and released 10 December 2025, found that 60 percent of US public high schools offer foundational computer science, that only 4 of the 50 states explicitly emphasise AI within their computer science standards, and that only 5 of the 50 fund AI and computer science professional development for teachers. No state requires both AI and computer science for graduation.

The population is large. The National Center for Education Statistics puts public school enrolment from prekindergarten through grade 12 at 49.6 million in fall 2022, projected to fall to 46.9 million by fall 2031, about 5 percent. That indicator was last updated in May 2024, so read it as scale rather than a current-year count. We print no market-size dollar estimate for American online AI or coding education, because every figure available traced back to a vendor press release rather than a statistical agency.

The Bureau of Labor Statistics Occupational Outlook Handbook is employment evidence we will stand behind. Data scientists had a median annual wage of $112,590 as of May 2024, with employment projected to grow 34 percent from 2024 to 2034 and about 23,400 openings each year across the decade. Computer and information research scientists had a median of $140,910 as of May 2024, with 20 percent projected growth and about 3,200 annual openings.

The infrastructure story is real and frequently oversold, so here is the careful version. The Stargate Project was announced at the White House in January 2025 by OpenAI, SoftBank and Oracle with Abu Dhabi's MGX, at $100 billion initial funding ramping toward $500 billion over four years, the flagship campus in Abilene, Texas on Oracle Cloud Infrastructure. Five further sites were announced on 23 and 24 September 2025: Shackelford County in Texas, Dona Ana County in New Mexico, Lordstown in Ohio, Milam County in Texas, and a Wisconsin site developed by Oracle with Vantage. Stargate is data-centre construction, and its direct hiring is electricians, welders and site operators. It shows where American AI compute is being built and why the industry is spreading inland. It is not a hiring pipeline for a teenager learning gradient descent. The BLS rows above are.

School and university

## How this sits beside AP, IB and whatever your state calls computer science

### There is no American exam board

Families arriving from other systems keep looking for one. The United States has no national curriculum and no national exam board. Education is controlled by the fifty states and roughly 13,000 local school districts, so any provider implying an American equivalent of CBSE or Cambridge is describing something that does not exist. What exists is a shared reference document, the CSTA K-12 Computer Science Standards, which states adapt. Twelve states require a computer science course to graduate: Alabama, Arkansas, Indiana, Louisiana, Nebraska, Nevada, North Carolina, North Dakota, Rhode Island, South Carolina, Tennessee and West Virginia, the last being the most recent addition.

The country codes programmes rather than exams. The National Center for Education Statistics maintains the Classification of Instructional Programs, in its fifth revision as CIP 2020, where 11.0101 is Computer and Information Sciences, General and 11.0102 is Artificial Intelligence, with secondary courses additionally coded under the NCES School Codes for the Exchange of Data. AI having its own CIP code is worth knowing: the federal statistical system already treats it as a distinct discipline where state standards do not.

### Advanced Placement, and its limits

Advanced Placement, run by the College Board, is the closest thing to a national qualification in this subject. AP Computer Science A is a programming course in Java. AP Computer Science Principles is broader and includes a through-course Create performance task submitted online rather than sat entirely as a written paper. Neither is a machine learning course, and both read better as prerequisites in spirit than as destinations.

For reference, the 2026 administration ran 4 to 8 May and 11 to 15 May 2026, with AP Computer Science Principles on Thursday 14 May 2026 and AP Computer Science A on Friday 15 May 2026, both at 12pm local, and the Create performance task deadline of 30 April 2026 at 11:59pm Eastern. The College Board has published 30 April 2027 for the next Create deadline, but had not published the full per-subject 2027 grid as of July 2026, typically releasing it around the preceding autumn, so we print no specific 2027 subject dates. The International Baccalaureate Diploma Programme, including Computer Science at Standard and Higher Level, is offered at many American high schools alongside or instead of AP, and our teenage track maps onto it the same way.

### Carnegie Mellon, and what an AI degree asks for

Carnegie Mellon University's Bachelor of Science in Artificial Intelligence, run from its School of Computer Science, is the reference undergraduate AI degree in the country. Its own admission page lists the required high school coursework: 4 years of English; 4 years of mathematics, which should include at least algebra, geometry, trigonometry, analytic geometry and elementary functions and preferably calculus, with advanced mathematics encouraged; 1 year of physics; 2 years of chemistry, biology or computer science; 2 years of a foreign language; and 3 electives.

Notice the shape. That is a coursework profile, not a threshold. The page publishes no minimum grade point average, no test-score cut-off and no acceptance rate for the major, so any page quoting one has invented it. What it does say is that four years of mathematics, ending as close to calculus as your school allows, is the thing being checked.

### The affordable online graduate routes

Two programmes come up in almost every conversation with an American adult. The University of Texas at Austin runs an online Master of Science in Artificial Intelligence of 30 credit hours, 3 hours of required courses plus 27 hours of electives across 10 courses, described by the university in its own words as an affordable, advanced degree priced at $10,000 plus fees, with international student fees and late registration fees possible. Fall applications open 15 December, priority 15 March, final 15 April; spring open 1 June, priority 1 August, final 1 September.

Georgia Tech's Online Master of Science in Computer Science carries a machine learning specialisation, is also 30 credit hours and typically takes three years. Its prospective-student FAQ states the GRE is not required and that the preferred qualification is an undergraduate degree in computer science or a related field, typically mathematics, computer engineering or electrical engineering, with a cumulative grade point average of 3.0 or higher, others evaluated case by case. Materials include a bachelor's degree from a regionally accredited institution, transcripts, three recommendation letters submitted online by the recommenders themselves, English proficiency documentation for international applicants, and a misconduct-history disclosure. Tuition is per credit hour at University System of Georgia rates, and because the FAQ prints no total, we do not quote one.

The teaching implication is the same at both ends of that ladder. Whether a student aims at Carnegie Mellon out of high school or at a graduate programme ten years later, the binding constraint is mathematical depth, meaning calculus and linear algebra, not the number of AI products they have used. That is why our teenage track teaches the mathematics inside the machine learning instead of parking it in a separate subject to be survived.

## Class times we can genuinely staff, and the ones we will not pretend to

The contiguous United States spans Eastern to Pacific, and India does not observe daylight saving, so the gap between us moves twice a year. American daylight saving runs from the second Sunday in March to the first Sunday in November. In July 2026 Eastern Time is UTC-4 and India is 9 hours 30 minutes ahead of it; Pacific Time is UTC-7 and India is 12 hours 30 minutes ahead. From November to March those become 10 hours 30 and 13 hours 30. Timetables are rebuilt on both Sundays, or every American class silently slides by an hour.

### What a session is

Ninety minutes on live video with a teacher who remembers what went wrong last week and opens with it. Both screens are shared, so an error gets interrupted while the student is still making it rather than annotated three days later. Batches hold five to eight students, or one. Between one week and the next there is a build rather than a worksheet, and questions in the gap go to that same teacher instead of a ticket queue staffed by someone who has never met the child.

The honest limit is worth stating once. A weekday evening class at 6:00pm to 8:00pm Eastern falls between 3:30am and 5:30am in India. No teacher on a normal schedule holds that slot week after week, so we do not sell it. We serve the United States fully at weekends, and on weekdays we serve the Pacific coast.

### The American school year, and the two weeks in May

There is no national school calendar. Start and end dates are set by roughly 13,000 local districts, so they vary by weeks. New York City Public Schools, the largest district in the country, begins the 2026 to 2027 school year on Thursday 10 September 2026. Most districts run from mid-August or early September to late May or June. Thanksgiving is a fixed federal anchor on the fourth Thursday of November, which is 26 November 2026, and most districts close the Thursday and Friday. Winter break runs from shortly before Christmas to shortly after New Year.

AP season dominates late April into mid May, when attendance dips and demand for exam-focused help spikes. Spring break is a single week in March or April and is not synchronised nationally, which is why an American cohort loses different students in different weeks that month and why we do not pause a batch for it. September and January are the natural intake points, and June to August is the deepest window, because school is out and a weekday morning slot becomes workable.

Enrolment does not run in cohorts with a closing date. A student is placed into the next batch that fits their level, in whichever week they arrive, and the free first class doubles as the placement check that decides which batch that is.

### Do American universities want AI projects or AP scores?

Read what a strong programme publishes. Carnegie Mellon University's admission page for its Bachelor of Science in Artificial Intelligence asks for 4 years of English, 4 years of mathematics including at least algebra, geometry, trigonometry, analytic geometry and elementary functions and preferably calculus, 1 year of physics, 2 years of chemistry, biology or computer science, 2 years of a foreign language and 3 electives. That is a coursework profile, not a cut-off score, and the page publishes no minimum grade point average, no test threshold and no acceptance rate for the major. Mathematical depth is the binding constraint. Projects matter alongside it because an admissions reader can open a link and watch the mathematics being used.

### Is a $10,000 online master's degree in AI a real thing?

Yes, and two are worth knowing about years before anyone applies. The University of Texas at Austin runs an online Master of Science in Artificial Intelligence of 30 credit hours, 3 hours of required courses plus 27 hours of electives across 10 courses, described by the university itself as an affordable, advanced degree priced at $10,000 plus fees, with international student fees and late registration fees possible. Fall applications open 15 December, priority 15 March, final 15 April; spring open 1 June, priority 1 August, final 1 September. Georgia Tech's Online Master of Science in Computer Science carries a machine learning specialisation, is also 30 credit hours, typically takes three years, requires no GRE, and prefers a related undergraduate degree with a cumulative grade point average of 3.0 or higher. Both screen on an undergraduate record.

### Will AI take the jobs you are training my child for?

The honest answer uses federal statistics rather than a vendor forecast. The Bureau of Labor Statistics Occupational Outlook Handbook puts the median annual wage for data scientists at $112,590 as of May 2024, with employment projected to grow 34 percent between 2024 and 2034, which the agency calls much faster than average, and about 23,400 openings projected each year across the decade. Computer and information research scientists sit at a median of $140,910 as of May 2024, with 20 percent projected growth and about 3,200 openings a year. Those are federal projections, and they describe the people who build these systems rather than the people who type at them.

### Does my state require computer science to graduate from high school?

Twelve states do, according to Code.org's 2025 report: Alabama, Arkansas, Indiana, Louisiana, Nebraska, Nevada, North Carolina, North Dakota, Rhode Island, South Carolina, Tennessee and West Virginia, the last being the most recent addition. No state requires both artificial intelligence and computer science for graduation, and only 5 of the 50 fund AI and computer science professional development for teachers: Arkansas, Indiana, Louisiana, New Jersey and Ohio. In the other 38 states computer science is an elective your child may or may not be offered, and anything past it is arranged outside the school day.

### How do you schedule around AP season, spring break and the summer?

AP exams are the one genuinely national fixture, administered in schools across two weeks in May. In 2026 that was 4 to 8 May and 11 to 15 May, with AP Computer Science Principles on Thursday 14 May 2026 and AP Computer Science A on Friday 15 May 2026, both at 12pm local time, and the CSP Create performance task due 30 April 2026 at 11:59pm Eastern. College Board has published 30 April 2027 for the next Create deadline but had not released the full 2027 subject grid as of July 2026, so we treat May 2027 as two weeks in May with dates to be confirmed. Late April into mid May is a crunch and we lighten the load through it. Spring break is a single week in March or April and is not synchronised nationally, so we do not pause for it. June to August is our deepest window.
