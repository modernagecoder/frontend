## Your first hour, scripted

Minute 0: pick a real problem, tiny and yours. Packing lists, splitting bills, watering reminders. Minute 5: open an AI assistant you are allowed to use at your age, one tab, nothing else. Minute 8: type what the tool is, who uses it, and the three things it must do, and ask for one single HTML file. Minute 12: save it as tool.html and double-click it; it opens in your browser and runs. Minute 15: use it honestly for five minutes and write down the three most annoying faults. Minute 25 onward: fix them one at a time, one request then one test, never two changes at once. Minute 55: send it to one person who likes you. Minute 60: you are a person who builds tools.

The single-file trick matters because every classic beginner detour lives in the setup you just skipped. Choosing a problem from your own life makes you the world expert on the requirements. The one-request-one-test rhythm is professional incremental change, installed without jargon.

When it goes wrong, four moments are normal: the blank page (say exactly what you see), the change that broke a working thing (tell the model the last thing that changed), the model apologising in a loop (stop prompting, read the code slowly), and feeling stupid (everyone does; it passes).

## The thirty-day arc

- Day 1: the first-hour script, done.
- Days 2 to 7: the same tool, five improvements, one tested change at a time.
- Days 8 to 14: a second tool for somebody else, learning that their requirements are not yours.
- Days 15 to 21: the first hard bug, survived by reading rather than re-prompting.
- Days 22 to 29: a third tool, chosen and deliberately scoped small.
- Day 30: one tool put where other people can reach it.

## What you genuinely do not need

A powerful computer: everything runs in a browser tab, and an eight year old laptop is fine. Mathematics: a packing-list app contains no algebra, and the maths only becomes real if you later choose machine learning proper. Youth: adults past forty are the fastest-growing group in our batches. Perfect English: short plain sentences outperform elaborate ones. A grand project idea: the graveyard of abandoned learning is full of social networks. Certainty about the destination: the first month is identical whether this becomes a career, a portfolio or a hobby.

## Where a live class changes the odds

And knowing when you have outgrown the beginner rung, which someone who has watched you for a term can name precisely.

## Frequently asked questions

**Am I too old in my forties?** Our oldest current students are in their sixties. Adults arrive knowing exactly which boring problem they want solved and describe things precisely, which is most of the skill.

**Do I need maths?** No. Logic is trained by the work, not demanded upfront. Real maths appears only if you later choose machine learning itself.

**What computer?** Anything from the last eight years that runs a modern browser. Nothing to install, nothing to buy in the beginner months.

**How long to something real?** Running: one hour. Showable: two to three weeks. Usable by a stranger: about a month of steady evenings.

**Do I need paid AI subscriptions?** Not in the beginner months; free tiers cover the first course. Later agent-tool courses that need subscriptions say so on their own pages.

**Free videos instead?** Videos teach the happy path. The product here is a person who watches your specific project fail in its specific way, and beginners meet that moment in week one.
