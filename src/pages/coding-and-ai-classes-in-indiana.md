# Coding and AI classes in Indiana

**Do most Indiana high schools offer computer science?** Yes. Code.org (2025) reports 368 of 414 Indiana high schools, 88.9%, offering foundational computer science in 2023 to 2024, but only 5.57% of high school students enrolled in it. Modern Age Coders closes that gap from the other side: live online coding and AI lessons for grades 3 to 12, on Eastern or Central time.

Indiana is a state where the course is usually there and the students mostly are not. Close to nine in ten of its high schools offered computer science in 2023 to 2024, well above the 60% national figure in the same Code.org sheet, yet barely one high school student in eighteen was enrolled in it. Indiana is also, by the account of the people who maintain the world&rsquo;s time zone database, the most complicated state in the country to keep a clock in. This page sets out the published access and enrollment figures, explains Indiana&rsquo;s two time zones the way the database records them, and walks through a project on Indianapolis greenway data that teaches one of the cleanest ideas in programming: sets, and what they share.

## Built, planned, or both? Indianapolis greenways as Python sets

The City of Indianapolis and Marion County publish their greenway network as open data. Every segment carries a name and a status, and the two statuses make two sets.

**Indianapolis Greenways layer, 105 segments downloaded 18 October 2026: names compared as sets (our calculations)**

| Set operation | Python | Our result |
|---|---|---|
| Names on Existing segments (50 segments) | existing | 19 names |
| Names on Proposed-MDCAdpt segments (55 segments, one unnamed) | proposed | 25 names |
| Built in part and planned to grow | existing & proposed | 11 names, including Fall Creek GW, Eagle Creek GW and Central Canal |
| Built, with no planned extension listed | existing - proposed | 8 names, including Monon Trail and Pennsy Trail |
| Planned only, nothing built under that name | proposed - existing | 14 names, including Nickel Plate and Interurban Tr |
| Every named greenway | existing | proposed | 33 names |

### 1. Build the sets

Students read each segment&rsquo;s FULL_NAME and STATUS and add the name to one of two Python sets. Duplicates vanish on their own: Fall Creek GW has eight existing segments but is one member of the set.

### 2. Ask set questions

Intersection, difference and union each answer a plain-English question about the network. Checking that 19 plus 25 minus 11 equals 33 is a first lesson in testing your own code.

### 3. Spot the traps

Monon Trail is existing and Monon Rail is proposed; Vandalia Tr and Vandalia Trail both appear. Sets match exact strings, so students must decide whether those are the same path before trusting the answer.

### Credit, and what the numbers are

Data from the City of Indianapolis/Marion County, IN, whose terms ask that derived data acknowledge them and which provides it as is, without warranty. The counts above are our own from one download. A name appearing in both sets suggests a greenway that is partly built, but only the city can say what is actually planned.

Once the idea clicks, students reuse it on Indiana&rsquo;s clocks. The time zone database quotes the federal rule that moved eight counties, Starke, Pulaski, Knox, Daviess, Martin, Pike, Dubois and Perry, from Eastern to Central time on April 2, 2006, and then records six of them returning to Eastern in 2007. The difference of those two sets is Perry and Starke, exactly the two Indiana counties the database still lists on Central time with their own zones.

## Indiana&rsquo;s clocks, as the time zone database records them

Most of Indiana is on Eastern time, while a group of western counties and two others keep Central time. The IANA database explains the details.

**Indiana in the IANA tzdb northamerica file (quoted county lists; the India offsets in the last column are our arithmetic)**

| Area | Zone in the database | India ahead by (summer, winter) |
|---|---|---|
| Most of Indiana | America/Indiana/Indianapolis, Eastern | 9 h 30 min, 10 h 30 min |
| Gibson, Jasper, Lake, LaPorte, Newton, Porter, Posey, Spencer, Vanderburgh and Warrick counties | Like America/Chicago, Central | 10 h 30 min, 11 h 30 min |
| Perry and Starke counties | America/Indiana/Tell_City and America/Indiana/Knox, Central | 10 h 30 min, 11 h 30 min |
| Dearborn and Ohio counties | Like America/New_York, Eastern | 9 h 30 min, 10 h 30 min |
| Clark, Floyd and Harrison counties | Like America/Kentucky/Louisville, Eastern | 9 h 30 min, 10 h 30 min |

### 345 regions

The database records that an earlier time zone reference partitioned Indiana into 345 regions, each with its own time history. Its maintainers note that most of that complexity happened before 1970.

### Daylight saving arrived late

The America/Indiana/Indianapolis entry stays on Eastern Standard Time all year until 2006 and only then follows the US daylight saving rule. Separately, Switzerland County did not observe DST from 1973 through 2005.

## An AI model can write the set code. Can it tell you what Monon Rail is?

Writing three lines of Python is easy now; deciding whether the answer is right is still the job.

Ask an assistant which Indianapolis greenways are both built and planned and it will produce correct set code in seconds. It will not stop to ask whether Monon Rail and Monon Trail are one path, or whether a blank name hides a real segment. Those questions only occur to someone who has handled the data.

That is the case for learning to code even when AI writes much of it. A student who understands intersection and difference can read generated code, test it on a small example and spot when the data, not the code, is the problem.

Indiana already offers the course in most high schools; the students who take it with real practice behind them are the ones who will use AI well. The longer argument is in [why we still teach students to write code themselves](/blog/is-coding-worth-learning-2026).
