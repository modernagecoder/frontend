# Coding and AI classes in Utah

**What computer science standards does Utah use?** Utah has its own K-5 Computer Science Standards (2019) and 6-12 Computer Science Standards (November 8, 2019) from the Utah State Board of Education, built on strands such as Data and Analysis and Impacts of Computing. Code.org (2025) counts 175 of 213 Utah high schools (82.2%) with a computer science course in 2024 to 2025. Utah approached computer science as a planned project rather than a single law. A state taskforce set priorities in 2018, standards for kindergarten through fifth grade and for sixth through twelfth grade followed in 2019, and a master plan aimed at access for every student by 2022. The published figures since then put Utah well above the national share of high schools offering computer science. This page sets out what the standards ask at each level, what the graduation rules say about credit, the access and participation numbers, and a mapping project on the state&rsquo;s own trailhead data that teaches one of the most practical skills in geographic coding: drawing a box and counting what falls inside it.

## Utah&rsquo;s two sets of computer science standards, both from 2019

The Utah State Board of Education (USBE) publishes separate K-5 and 6-12 computer science standards. Both documents open with the same history, quoted here as written.

**Utah computer science standards at a glance, from the USBE documents**

| Item | K-5 standards | 6-12 standards |
|---|---|---|
| Date on the document | 2019, developed with educators in March 2019 | November 8, 2019 |
| Organized by | Strands, each containing standards | Strands, each containing standards |
| Example standard | 2.DA.1: demonstrate how to store, copy, search, retrieve, modify and delete information using a computing device | 6.NI.1: explain potential security threats and practice practical measures to reduce them |
| Stated aim | Start early by engaging students at the elementary level | Each student in secondary public schools will have access to robust and varied computer science courses by 2022 |

### The core concepts

The K-5 document lists Computing Systems, Networks and the Internet, Data and Analysis, Algorithms and Processing, Impacts of Computing, and Computational Thinking. A standard code such as 2.DA.1 names the grade and the strand.

### Elementary groundwork

The 6-12 standards say secondary readiness begins in elementary school with keyboarding, appropriate and responsible use of technology, and basic coding principles. That is a useful checklist for parents of younger children.

### Thirty-plus courses

Both documents note more than thirty computer science and IT courses offered in Utah, naming Computer Programming 1, Computer Science Principles, Exploring Computer Science, Web Development 1 and Game Development as popular.

The standards trace back to a USBE Computer Science Taskforce, whose strategic priorities were presented to and accepted by the Board in June 2018. One of those priorities was to ensure students can access a majority of the state&rsquo;s computer science and IT courses regardless of location, which is the same problem online classes solve from the family side.

## A master plan for access, and a diploma built on 24 credits

Utah&rsquo;s Computer Science Education Master Plan summary, dated July 2019, and the USBE graduation requirements page, read on 18 October 2026.

### The goal

The master plan is subtitled To give every student access to robust computer science education by 2022, and records a goal set by the governor at the time of computer science taught in every school by 2022.

### Where Utah started

Writing in 2019, the plan stated that only 16 percent of Utah&rsquo;s high schools offered intermediate and advanced computer science. That figure is the plan&rsquo;s own, dated 2019, and measured something narrower than the access figures below.

### Funding for teachers

The plan names HB227, the Utah Computer Science Grant Act, as the route for grants to districts, including professional development and more teacher endorsements in computer science.

**Utah graduation credit rules, as the USBE graduation requirements page states them**

| Rule | What USBE says |
|---|---|
| Minimum credit | A student in grades 9 to 12 must earn a minimum of 24 units of credit to graduate |
| Two routes | Credit through course completion or through competency assessment consistent with R277-705 |
| Earlier credit | An LEA shall recognize high school credits earned before grade 9 |
| Local additions | Districts may add additional credit requirements |
| Detail | High school requirements are set out in R277-700-6 |

The graduation page itself does not list a separate computer science credit, and we have not read R277-700-6 in full, so we make no claim about whether or how a computer science course counts toward a specific Utah credit area. A school counselor can answer that for a particular course.

## Which Utah trailheads fall inside a box? Mapping with code

The Utah Geospatial Resource Center (UGRC) publishes a statewide trailhead layer under a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 licence. Students download every point with its latitude and longitude, then filter by drawing boxes.

**Bounding-box counts on the UGRC Utah Trailheads layer (our boxes and counts, 18 October 2026)**

| Box, our choice | Trailheads inside |
|---|---|
| Whole layer | 568 |
| Latitude 40.5 to 40.9, longitude -112.1 to -111.7, roughly the Salt Lake Valley | 50 |
| North of latitude 40.0 | 317 |
| South of latitude 38.0 | 136 |
| Outside a rough Utah box of latitude 37 to 42 | 1 |

### Four comparisons

A point is inside a box when its latitude sits between the south and north edges and its longitude between the west and east edges. Students write that test once, as a function, and reuse it for every box.

### The edge case

One trailhead sits at latitude 36.99999, a hair south of 37, so a box that starts exactly at 37 misses it. Is that point really outside Utah, or is the box edge too tidy? Students decide and say why.

### Empty columns

The layer has fields for maintenance, seasonal restrictions and features, but all 568 records leave them empty, and 559 name OrbitalView as the data source. Checking for empty fields first saves an afternoon of puzzled charts.

In Python the core test is one line: inside = [p for p in points if s &lt;= p["lat"] &lt;= n and w &lt;= p["lon"] &lt;= e]. Students then change the inequality from &lt;= to &lt; and watch the count change for points that lie exactly on an edge. UGRC&rsquo;s own description says the layer features many of Utah&rsquo;s trailheads but is not yet complete, so a count from it is a count of this layer, never of every trailhead in the state.

### Our boxes, our counts

The boxes were chosen by us for teaching and the counts are ours, from one download. UGRC provides the data as is, with no guarantees of completeness or accuracy, under CC BY 4.0, so these numbers credit UGRC and describe the layer, not Utah&rsquo;s official trail network.

## Utah keeps Mountain Time, and so do our Utah timetables

In the IANA time zone database, Utah is in the US Mountain list, served by the America/Denver zone, described as Mountain for most areas.

### An afternoon class

A 4:00 p.m. class in Salt Lake City starts at 3:30 a.m. the next morning in India under daylight saving time, and at 4:30 a.m. once Mountain Standard Time returns.

### The gap in hours

India is UTC plus 5:30 all year. Utah is UTC minus 6 in summer and minus 7 in winter, so India runs 11 hours 30 minutes or 12 hours 30 minutes ahead.

### Changeover Sundays

The US rule moves Utah&rsquo;s clocks back on November 1, 2026 and forward on March 14, 2027. We send every family the class time in Mountain Time before each change.

**A boundary problem in time, too** Like the trailhead at latitude 36.99999, a class at 2:30 a.m. on March 14, 2027 does not exist on a Utah clock, because the hour is skipped. Students who test their scheduler on that date with the America/Denver zone meet the same kind of edge case in time.

## An AI can write the box filter. Can it choose the box?

The code takes a minute. The decisions around it are the real work.

Ask an AI assistant for code that counts trailheads in the Salt Lake Valley and it will produce a neat filter in seconds. It will also quietly choose the edges of the valley, decide whether points on the line count, and ignore the trailhead at 36.99999. None of those choices will be mentioned unless the student asks.

Students who have written the filter themselves know which questions to put to the assistant and to their own code. That is the Impacts of Computing and Data and Analysis thinking Utah&rsquo;s standards describe, practised on a real map, with AI as a fast helper rather than the decision maker.

Utah set out to give every student access to computer science; the habit of questioning a tidy answer is what turns access into skill. The longer argument is in [whether coding is worth learning in 2026](/blog/is-coding-worth-learning-2026).
