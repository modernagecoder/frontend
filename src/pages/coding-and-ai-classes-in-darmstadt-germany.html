Hessen schools

Does a Darmstadt school teach Informatik?

Some do, as an option. None has to. Everything in this section was checked against the GI monitor and the KMK calendar on 2 November 2026.

Optional, not required

The Informatik-Monitor 2026/27 states for Hessen that there is no compulsory Informatik across school types. At a Gymnasium, Informatik can be chosen as an elective from grade 7.

Looking at all sixteen Länder, the monitor puts Hessen among six without Informatik that is compulsory for all, and one of three, with Berlin and Brandenburg, where no binding plans to introduce it are known.

What that means at home

A Darmstadt child can finish school without ever having had a programming lesson, unless the school offers one and the child chooses it. Whether that happens depends on the school, the timetable and luck.

Our classes do not replace school Informatik and award no marks. They give a learner who wants to code a regular, structured place to do it, whatever the school offers.

A real Darmstadt project

Does a model trained on Darmstadt's past still fit today? A lesson in data drift

The Wissenschaftsstadt Darmstadt publishes its average temperature for every month since January 1961. Teenage and adult learners work through it to see one of the commonest ways AI goes wrong: the model is fine, but the world it learnt from has moved on.

The data

The city's dataset Monatliche Durchschnittstemperatur ab 1961 gives the monthly mean temperature in degrees Celsius, based on measurements by the city and the Deutscher Wetterdienst. Reuse is allowed with credit under the Datenlizenz Deutschland Namensnennung, version 2.0. Our copy, fetched on 3 November 2026, holds 781 months from January 1961 to January 2026.

The city notes that the station moved several times, from the Botanischer Garten in the early 1960s to the Dieburger Straße since August 1995. Moves like that are a second, quieter kind of drift, and we tell learners about it.

The model

The simplest temperature model there is: for each month, predict the average of that month over a training period. Train it on 1961 to 1990 and it knows what a normal Darmstadt January or July looked like then.

We measure two things on test years. The average size of the error, and the bias: whether the errors lean one way. A good model has a bias near zero.

On its own training years, the model has no bias at all, which is guaranteed: an average has no lean on the data it came from. On 2016 to 2025 the bias is 1.13. On average every month of the last ten years was 1.13 degrees warmer than the 1961 to 1990 model expected. The code did not change. The world did.

Error and bias on 2016 to 2025, in degrees C (our calculation)
ModelAverage errorBias
Normals from 1961 to 19901.55+1.13
Normals from 1986 to 20151.35+0.78
Retrained every year on the previous 30 years1.31+0.66

Training on more recent years helps, and retraining every year helps a little more, but the bias never reaches zero, because the warming continued even inside each training window. Averaging the city's own annual values gives 9.99 degrees for 1961 to 1990 and 11.09 for 2016 to 2025. The warmest year in the file is 2023 at 11.7 degrees, the coldest 1963 at 8.0.

Temperatures: Wissenschaftsstadt Darmstadt on opendata.darmstadt.de (Monatliche Durchschnittstemperatur ab 1961), credited under Datenlizenz Deutschland Namensnennung 2.0, file of 3 November 2026. Training periods, errors, biases and averages are our own. The project is about how models age, not a climate analysis; the station moves listed by the city mean the series is not a single site.

  1. Children, 9 to 12. Write down what you wore to school last November. Would last year's clothes list work for this November? That is a model and its test.
  2. Teenagers. Change the test years to 1991 to 2000 and then 2006 to 2015. Plot the bias of the 1961 to 1990 model against the decade.
  3. Adults. Try training windows of 10, 20 and 30 years with yearly retraining. Which window gives the smallest error, and why might a short window be noisier?
  4. Everyone. Name one AI system you use whose world could drift. What would you test it on?

Questions from Darmstadt

Coding and AI classes in Darmstadt: frequent questions

Are the classes in German?

No. All classes are live and taught in English only, by mentors based in India. They match each learner's pace and accent, explain things a second way when needed, and keep language simple for children.

Is Informatik compulsory in Darmstadt schools?

No. The Gesellschaft für Informatik reports that Hessen has no compulsory Informatik across school types; at a Gymnasium it can be an elective from grade 7. No binding plans to change this are known.

From what age can my child use ChatGPT or Gemini?

According to the providers' terms, ChatGPT is from 13 with a parent's permission under 18, Gemini with a personal account from 16 in Germany, Microsoft Copilot from 18 in Germany, and Claude from 18. Our classes never require a child to open any of these accounts.

When are classes in Darmstadt time?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Afternoons suit pupils; early evenings and weekends suit most adults.

What is data drift, and why do you teach it?

It is when the data a model learnt from no longer matches the world it is used in. Our Darmstadt project shows it with 65 years of temperatures. Real AI systems drift too, so learners must know to test on recent data and retrain.

Can my child start AI without coding?

Young children start with AI literacy and Scratch, which need no typing. Real AI work needs Python, which most children start from about nine and teenagers can learn quickly.

Is the temperature project real Darmstadt data?

Yes. It uses the Wissenschaftsstadt Darmstadt dataset Monatliche Durchschnittstemperatur ab 1961, under the Datenlizenz Deutschland Namensnennung 2.0, downloaded on 3 November 2026. The models and figures are ours.

Do you teach adults who already code?

Yes. Developers and researchers often go straight to the AI and ML Masterclass, Data Science or Generative AI, usually one to one so the projects can use their own data.

How big is a class?

A class holds fifteen to twenty people. Children are put together by age and older learners by how far they have got. Private learners get two lessons a week with the mentor alone.

What currency will I pay in?

US dollars. We display the monthly fee in euro, worked out from the ECB reference rate; when the dollar charge reaches your account, your bank converts it, so a little variation is normal. You pay nothing for a first class.
