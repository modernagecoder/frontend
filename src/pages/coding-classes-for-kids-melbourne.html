Coding classes for kids in Melbourne

What are the best coding classes for kids in Melbourne?

For a Melbourne child in Prep to Year 8, look for a live teacher, a small group at the same level and projects the child can explain afterwards. Greater Melbourne had 307,523 children aged 5 to 9 at the 2021 Census. Our classes start at age 6, run online after school, and the first lesson costs nothing.

Ask a Melbourne eight-year-old which playground is closest to the State Library and you will get a confident answer and an argument. We settled it with data. OpenStreetMap lists 234 playgrounds in a rectangle we drew around the inner city, and a child can find the nearest one with a single idea: keep a note of the smallest distance seen so far, and cross it out only when a smaller one turns up. That one variable is the heart of countless programs. Our young Melbourne coders run it in Scratch, then in Python, and then discover two things: in a list of 234 playgrounds the record only changed six times, and starting the record at the wrong number gives a confident wrong answer.

Signature project

Which playground is nearest the State Library, and how often does the answer change?

Every playground OpenStreetMap records in our inner-Melbourne rectangle, measured from the State Library Victoria building, then searched with one variable.

1. Start with the first

The first playground in the list was 4,341.8 metres away. With nothing to compare it to, it becomes the record: the smallest distance so far.

2. Compare, and maybe replace

Each new playground is compared with the record. Bigger or equal, ignore it. Smaller, write it down instead. A Scratch sprite can do this with one variable and one if block.

3. Notice how rarely it moves

Across all 234 playgrounds the record changed only six times: at the 1st, 3rd, 5th, 22nd, 138th and 143rd playground. After that, nothing beat 805.5 metres.

What if the record starts at the wrong number?

Beginners often start the record at a round number instead of the first distance. Start it at 0 and nothing is ever smaller, so the program reports a playground 0 metres away. Start it at 500 and the program reports 500 metres, because no playground in the list is that close: the nearest is 805.5 metres. Start it with the first playground's distance and the answer is right every time. One starting value, three different answers, and only one of them is true.

These figures are ours, measured on an OpenStreetMap snapshot from 7 October 2026. OpenStreetMap is mapped by volunteers, so a playground may be missing or one that has gone may remain; the rectangle is our own choice and not a council boundary. Distances are straight lines from the library building, not walking routes, and the nearest playground happens to have no name in the data, which children find funny and which is a useful lesson in itself.

How children learn it

From a pile of cards to a working search

The same idea at three ages, so a Prep child and a Year 8 student can each meet it at the right size.

Years 3 to 6Builds a Scratch sprite that walks past playground signs and updates a variable called nearestVariables, if blocks and loops working together

A bug worth making

Children often start the record at zero. Then nothing is ever smaller and the program proudly reports a playground zero metres away. Fixing it by starting with the first item is a lesson they remember.

Ties and equal values

What if two playgrounds are the same distance? Using smaller rather than smaller-or-equal keeps the first one found. Children decide which rule they want and say why.

Checking the answer

Older learners sort the whole list as a second opinion and confirm both methods agree on 805.5 metres before they trust either.

We are not connected with the State Library Victoria, OpenStreetMap or the Australian Bureau of Statistics, and none of them has endorsed this project. The Census figures are the Bureau's own; the playground distances and record counts are our calculations.

Coding and AI

Should a Melbourne child still learn to code when AI can write programs?

Yes, because an AI helper can start the record at zero just as easily as a beginner can, and only a child who understands the idea will notice.

Ask an AI tool for a program that finds the nearest playground and it will usually produce something tidy. Sometimes it sets the starting record to zero or to a fixed number such as 1,000, which quietly breaks when every playground is further away than that. Nothing on the screen says so. A child who has built the search by hand knows to look at that first line and ask what the record starts as.

That habit of reading a program and asking one sharp question is what we are really teaching young Melbourne coders. It applies to code from a friend, a book or an AI tool, and it is far easier to build at nine than to learn at nineteen. Children use AI helpers only in age-appropriate ways in our classes, with a teacher present.

Learning to code in 2026 is not about racing a computer. It is about knowing which line to check, and a child who has watched a record change six times in 234 playgrounds knows exactly where to look. The longer argument is in our longer case for children learning to code.

FAQ

Questions from Melbourne parents

What Melbourne families tend to ask before the first lesson.

What age can my child start coding classes in Melbourne?

Our live classes start at age 6, usually Prep or Year 1. The youngest children begin with picture-based coding and simple loops before they type anything.

What is a running minimum in coding?

A running minimum is a variable that holds the smallest value seen so far while a program looks through a list. Each new value is compared with it and replaces it only if it is smaller, so one pass through the list finds the minimum.

What is the Melbourne playground project?

Children take the 234 playgrounds OpenStreetMap records in an inner-Melbourne rectangle, measure each one's distance from the State Library Victoria building and find the nearest, 805.5 metres away, by keeping a smallest-so-far record and counting how often it changes.

Does my child need to be good at maths first?

No. Coding and maths grow together. Comparing distances, counting and spotting patterns are part of the lessons, and many children find maths easier once they have used it to make something work.

Do you follow the Victorian school year?

Yes. We use Victorian year names from Prep to Year 8 and plan breaks around the Victorian school terms, which in 2027 start on Wednesday 27 January for schools.

When are lessons for Melbourne children?

After school on weekdays or on weekends, chosen during the free lesson and always stated in Melbourne time. Exact times depend on which groups suit your child's level.

Is it safe for my child to learn online?

Lessons are live with a teacher in a small group, children are not asked to create accounts on adult AI tools, and parents are welcome to sit in, especially for the first few sessions.

Is there a Modern Age Coders centre in Melbourne?

No, and none is claimed. All teaching is live and online, with no premises anywhere in Australia. A learner needs a laptop or desktop, working sound and a reliable connection, and the contact number shown is an Indian one.

What do coding classes for Melbourne kids cost?

The first lesson is free. After that a group place is USD 100 a month for two live lessons a week, around eight a month, with fifteen to twenty learners, or USD 150 a month for the same pattern one to one. Course, format and time are agreed before anything is paid.

How big are the groups?

Fifteen to twenty learners, matched on level, pace and what they want to build rather than on age or suburb. If no suitable group meets at a workable time, one-to-one lessons are offered instead.
