Who asks

Who looks for an IGCSE maths tutor in Peru?

Mostly families whose child studies maths in English at a school that enters students for Cambridge exams, and a smaller group who are moving into or out of that system. The reasons differ; the exam does not.

Students already at a Cambridge school

They meet the syllabus in class every week but want someone to go deeper, check working line by line, and push them from a safe C to the Extended grades. Often the gap is not knowledge but method: answers that are right but not shown, or rounded too soon.

Students who changed system

A child who moved from a Spanish-medium colegio into an IGCSE class at 13 or 14 meets English mathematical language and new topics at once. Vectors, surds or set notation may never have come up in their old school, and the class does not wait.

Families planning ahead

Parents of a 12 or 13 year old who will start IGCSE next year, or a family returning to Peru from abroad who want the maths to stay in English. Starting a term early with the algebra base is usually worth more than cramming in the final months.

The 0580 papers

What does the IGCSE Mathematics 0580 exam look like in 2025 to 2027?

Two written papers per candidate, one without a calculator and one with, at either Core or Extended tier. Cambridge's syllabus for exams in 2025, 2026 and 2027 sets it out as below, and the June and November series are the ones open to schools outside India.

Source: Cambridge International, Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027 (Version 3), assessment overview, read on 9 November 2026. Core candidates take Papers 1 and 3 and can be awarded grades C to G; Extended candidates take Papers 2 and 4 and can be awarded grades A* to E. Exams run in the June and November series, plus a March series for schools in India only.

Core or Extended: a choice made early

Cambridge writes that the Core content is meant for students aiming at grades C to G and the Extended content for students aiming at A* to C. Extended contains everything in Core plus more, such as exponential growth and decay, surds and harder algebra.

Because the tier caps the grade, the decision matters. A Core candidate cannot be awarded higher than C, however well they do. A student who may be capable of Extended but is not yet secure needs the extra content taught in time for the school's entry decision, not after it.

A non-calculator paper at each tier

For this syllabus Cambridge has made one paper at each tier a dedicated non-calculator paper. Half of the grade therefore rests on arithmetic, fractions, standard form and algebra done by hand, with clear working.

Students who grew used to doing everything on a calculator feel this first. The skills are not hard, but they have to be fast and reliable under time pressure: two hours for 100 marks on the Extended paper leaves little room for slow long division.

Cambridge also states that the syllabus and its assessment materials exist in English only, and it designs each IGCSE subject around roughly 130 guided learning hours, a figure it calls guidance rather than a rule. Private candidates may enter for 0580 through a centre that accepts them, and a candidate can retake the whole qualification as often as they wish. One administrative trap is worth a parent's attention: once the entries deadline passes, an entry on the A* to G grading scale cannot be switched to the 9 to 1 scale.

Schools decide tier, entry and series; we do not enter candidates, and we are not a Cambridge school or exam centre. Our job is to make sure the student is ready for whichever papers the school chooses.

What the national timetable gives

The CNEB, MINEDU's national curriculum, gives secundaria 4 periods of maths a week on the regular school day and 6 on the full school day, each period lasting 45 minutes. By our multiplication that is 180 or 270 minutes a week.

Schools that teach Cambridge courses set their own timetables, so this is the national reference, not a description of any one school. It still explains why a student who moves between systems can find the pace so different.

An accuracy lesson

Why does rounding too early cost IGCSE marks? A lesson in Python

Cambridge asks for non-exact answers to 3 significant figures, and in the same breath tells candidates to "avoid rounding figures until they have their final answer". Here is a lesson we teach Extended students that shows, with real output, how much that second rule matters.

The problem (ours)

A machine is worth 2,500 when new and loses 13% of its value every year. In which year does its value first fall below half of the original? This is exponential decay, which the 0580 syllabus lists for Extended candidates with depreciation as its example.

Logarithms are not on the syllabus, so a student answers by trial: multiply by 0.87 again and again until the value drops under 1,250. Many students write each year's value to 3 significant figures as they go, which feels tidy and exam-like.

What happens

The tidy way gives year 6. The correct answer is year 5. At the end of year 5 the true value is 1,246.05, which is below 1,250, but rounded to 3 significant figures it becomes exactly 1250 and looks as if it has not crossed the line.

So the student who rounded every year compared a rounded number with the threshold, and lost a whole year. Nothing in the method was wrong except when the rounding happened.

The lesson we want students to keep is short: compare the unrounded value, then round only what you write down. On paper that means keeping the full calculator display, or the exact expression 2500 × 0.875, until the last line. In Python it means rounding inside the print, never inside the loop.

We then turn it round. Students change the rate to 12% and predict, before running anything, whether the two versions will still disagree. They also explain in two English sentences why the loop condition says >= and what would change with >. Reasoning about the program is the point; Python simply makes the error impossible to argue with.

  1. Core warm-up, no calculator. A price rises by 10% and then falls by 10%. Is it back where it started? Show it with 200 as the starting value.
  2. Core, calculator. A population of 8,000 grows by 5% a year. Find it after 3 years to 3 significant figures, keeping full accuracy in between.
  3. Extended, by trial. Starting at 2,500 and losing 13% a year, find the first whole year in which the value is below 1,000. Write each trial with the full calculator display.
  4. Extended, in Python. Edit Version 2 to find the first year below 1,000, then add a line that prints the value to 3 significant figures only after the loop ends.

All four problems are ours, written for this page; none comes from a Cambridge paper. Answers: no, 198; 9,260; year 7 (the value then is about 943); the program agrees, year 7.

Maths in English

Where do Spanish-speaking students lose marks in English maths?

Rarely in the mathematics itself. Most lost marks we see come from notation and wording that differ between the maths a child learnt in Spanish and the maths Cambridge writes in English.

Our own notes from teaching. Peruvian official documents write decimals with a comma (INEI, for example, writes 84,7% for internet access in Lima Metropolitana); the 0580 syllabus writes them with a point and asks for 3 significant figures unless a question says otherwise.

Reading the question

The 0580 syllabus prints a table of command words with fixed meanings. "Show (that)" asks for "structured evidence that leads to a given result", so the answer is already known and the steps are the marks. "Write down" means "give an answer without significant working". "Explain" means say why or how, with evidence.

A student reading in a second language tends to skim these words, and then writes a bare answer where working was the mark, or pages of working where a single line was enough.

Writing the answer

We ask students to say their method aloud in English before writing it. If they cannot explain why a step is there, the examiner will not see why either. It is slow at first and then saves more time than any shortcut.

Mentors speak clearly and at the student's pace, and repeat an idea in simpler words where needed. Students who are still building their English usually find the mathematics is the easier half.

Understanding, not past papers alone

Will a tutor just drill past papers?

Past papers come at the end, not the start. A student who has only practised question types is stuck the first time Cambridge words something differently, and the Extended papers do exactly that.

Why before how

Every method is taught from a reason first: why the angle in a semicircle is a right angle, why 0.87 is the multiplier for a 13% fall. A student who knows the reason can rebuild a method they have half forgotten.

Two papers, two skills

Non-calculator sessions build speed with fractions, indices and algebra by hand. Calculator sessions train full-accuracy working and checking. Both get regular timed practice before exam season.

Weighting for analysis

Cambridge weights the Extended papers 50 to 60% towards analysing, interpreting and communicating mathematics, against 30 to 40% on Core. Higher grades depend on explaining, so explaining is what we practise.

We do not hand students answers to copy, from a tutor or from an AI tool. Younger students do not use adult chatbot accounts in class, and older students may use AI only to question their own reasoning after they have solved a problem. Cambridge gives teachers at registered schools access to its own large resource bank, which we do not have and do not claim; we work from the public syllabus and from problems we write ourselves. We are not connected with Cambridge International.

Families in Peru ask

IGCSE maths tutoring in Peru: questions and answers

Are the classes in Spanish?

No. Every lesson is live, online and taught in English, which is also the only language Cambridge offers 0580 in. Mentors speak clearly, adjust their pace and accent to the student, and write key terms on screen, so a student whose English is still growing can follow the mathematics.

Which syllabus do you teach?

Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580, in the version for exams in 2025, 2026 and 2027, at Core and Extended level. Our IGCSE course also covers Additional Mathematics. If a school uses another board's IGCSE, tell us at the free lesson and we will say honestly how much overlaps.

Should my child take Core or Extended?

The school decides. Cambridge intends Core for students aiming at grades C to G and Extended for A* to C, and a Core candidate cannot get above C. We can show you where your child stands on the Extended content so that the conversation with the school is based on evidence.

What does the non-calculator paper involve?

One paper at each tier is taken without a calculator: Paper 1 for Core, 1 hour 30 minutes and 80 marks, and Paper 2 for Extended, 2 hours and 100 marks. Each counts for half of the grade, so mental and written arithmetic matter as much as calculator skill.

When are lessons in Peru time?

Any day, any time, flexible: choose a batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at the hour that suits your family. Peru is on UTC minus 5 all year and India on UTC plus 5:30, so 18:00 in Lima is 04:30 the next morning in India.

Can you enter my child for the exam?

No. We are not a Cambridge school or exam centre, and we are not connected with Cambridge International. Entries go through the school, or for a private candidate through a centre that accepts private candidates. We prepare the student for the papers.

Do you give grades or certificates?

We do not award Cambridge or Peruvian grades; Cambridge marks the exam and the school reports on its own work. A student who completes one of our courses receives our own certificate of completion.

My child moved from a Spanish-medium school. Is it too late?

Usually not, if you start early enough before the exam. We first rebuild algebra and number in English, then the IGCSE topics in order of need. The free lesson shows how large the gap is, and one-to-one lessons close it faster than a group.

Do you use past papers?

Yes, near the end and under time limits, after the topics are understood. Most of our teaching uses problems we write ourselves, so that a student learns to handle unfamiliar wording rather than memorising familiar questions.

How are fees paid?

By card, in US dollars. Your bank converts each charge into soles, so the amount on your statement can change a little from month to month. The first lesson is free and no card is needed to book it.
