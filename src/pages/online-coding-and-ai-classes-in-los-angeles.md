## How big is a typical Los Angeles park? Measuring 566 parks from their coordinates

The City publishes every park boundary as a polygon of map coordinates, with no acreage column. Students calculate the area themselves, then discover that &ldquo;typical&rdquo; depends on which average they pick.

**Park areas computed from the City&rsquo;s Parks polygons, dataset 4jt3-efvk, downloaded 18 October 2026 (our calculation, approximate, not official acreage)**

| Measure | Our result |
|---|---|
| Park polygons in the dataset | 566 |
| Mean area | 33.77 acres |
| Median area | 3.46 acres |
| Polygons under one acre | 166 |
| Polygons over 100 acres | 31 |
| Largest: Griffith Park | about 4,574 acres |

### From points to area

Each park arrives as a list of corner points. The shoelace formula multiplies neighboring coordinates crosswise and halves the total, which gives the area inside the outline. Holes, such as a lake inside a park, are subtracted.

### From feet to acres

The coordinates are large numbers that behave like feet on a state plane grid. Dividing square feet by 43,560 gives acres. Students check the method by running it on a square they draw themselves first.

### Mean or median?

Our mean lands near 34 acres, the median near three and a half. One average says a typical park is a sizeable green space; the other says it is closer to a large lot. Both are calculated correctly.

Why the gap? A handful of giants pull the mean upward. By our calculation Griffith Park alone covers about 4,574 acres, followed by the Sepulveda Basin Recreation Area at about 1,539 and the Hansen Dam Recreation Area at about 1,450. Remove those three and the mean drops sharply, while the median barely moves. That is the whole lesson in one sentence: when a few values are huge, the median describes the middle and the mean describes the total spread thin.

A second dataset on the same portal adds context. The Department of Recreation and Parks&rsquo; facility list, last updated in 2015, holds 986 entries, among them 387 parks, 156 recreation centers and 76 public computer centers. Students who join the two lists by name meet the next real problem, that the same place is spelled differently in two files.

### Read these numbers carefully

Every area here is ours. The dataset has no acreage field and its metadata does not state the projection, so our acres assume the coordinates are in feet and will differ from the City&rsquo;s official figures. The data is released under a public domain dedication and refreshed quarterly, so a later download may change the results.

## Why learn to code in LA when AI writes the code?

Because someone still has to know whether the answer is right.

Ask an AI assistant for the average size of a Los Angeles park and it may produce one number with great confidence. Which average? Measured how? A student who has computed 566 areas and seen a mean of about 34 acres beside a median of about three and a half knows that the honest answer starts with a question.

Our AI courses keep that habit. Students write the code, check it against a case they can work out by hand, and only then use AI tools, within each provider&rsquo;s age rules, to go faster. The skill being taught is judgment, and judgment does not come from the tool.

An AI that writes a shoelace function in seconds is useful only to someone who can tell when it got the units wrong. The longer argument is in [is learning to code still worth it](/blog/is-coding-worth-learning-2026).
