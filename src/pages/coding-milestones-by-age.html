In short: milestones for 8 to 10 year olds

Coding milestones for 8 to 10 year olds: build a Scratch game that uses events, loops, a condition and a score variable; plan it first on paper; find and fix their own bugs; explain that two different programs can solve the same problem; and, by about 10, type a short Python program. These match the K-12 CS Framework endpoint for the end of grade 5. Modern Age Coders teaches this live online for US students, in a batch or one to one, and the first class is free.

Ages 8 to 10

Ten coding milestones for 8, 9 and 10 year olds

This is the age range parents ask us about most. The checklist below is ours, written so you can check each point at home by watching or asking, without knowing how to code yourself.

A ten-point milestone checklist for ages 8 to 10, with a way to check each one (ours)
#MilestoneHow a parent can check
1Gives exact instructions and fixes the one that is wrongAsk them to direct you to draw a house, step by step
2Uses a repeat or loop instead of copying the same blockAsk where in their project something happens more than once
3Makes something happen when an event occursClick, press a key, or touch a sprite in their game
4Uses a condition to decide what happensAsk: what makes the player win or lose?
5Keeps a score or timer in a variableWatch the number change while they play
6Plans a project before building itLook for a sketch, a list or a storyboard
7Breaks a project into parts and builds one at a timeAsk which part they are working on this week
8Finds and fixes their own bugs, at least sometimesAsk about the last thing that went wrong
9Knows two programs can solve the same problem differentlyAsk if there was another way to do it
10Around 10: types and changes a short Python programAsk them to change one number and predict the result

Eight, nine and ten are not the same

Our own plan: milestones 1 to 5 at 8, usually in block code; planning and splitting work into parts at 9; and at 10, aiming for milestones 8 and 9 to become reliable, with typed Python for children who are ready.

Reading and typing set the pace more than raw age. A strong reader of 8 can be ahead of a reluctant reader of 10, and that is fine; the order of the milestones matters more than the speed.

If your child is not there yet

None of these is a deadline. A child who starts coding at 9 can move through the first five milestones quickly, because reading and concentration are already in place.

If a child has been coding for a while and seems stuck, a common sticking point is milestone 6, planning. Children who jump straight into building tend to make the same half-finished project repeatedly. A teacher who insists on a two-minute sketch first often unblocks them.

Where milestones come from

The framework milestone for ages 8 to 10: the end of grade 5

The K-12 Computer Science Framework, which its authors say informs state- and district-level decisions, describes its concept statements as "conceptual milestones at different grade-band endpoints: Grades 2, 5, 8, and 12." For a child of 8 to 10, the endpoint ahead is the end of fifth grade.

End of grade 5, programming strand, in the framework’s exact words (k12cs.org, accessed 17 October 2026)
AreaWhat the framework says by the end of grade 5Matching checklist point
Algorithms"Different algorithms can achieve the same result. Some algorithms are more appropriate for a specific context than others."9
Variables"Programming languages provide variables, which are used to store and modify data."5
Control"Conditionals selectively execute or skip instructions under different conditions."3 and 4
Modularity"Programs can be broken down into smaller parts to facilitate their design, implementation, and review."7
Program development"People develop programs using an iterative process involving design, implementation, and review."6 and 8

What is not expected yet

The framework also includes boundary statements, which it says "clarify what is not expected to be learned at that grade level." For variables at grade 5, it says "understanding how to use variables is sufficient, without a fuller understanding of the technical aspects of variables (such as identifiers and memory locations)."

That is reassuring for parents. A 9 year old who keeps a score in Scratch has met the milestone, even if they could not explain what a variable is in technical terms.

Algorithms without a computer

The grade 5 description notes that algorithms "can be expressed in noncomputer languages, including natural language, flowcharts, and pseudocode", using examples such as choosing a route home. A child who can explain two routes and why one is faster is practising milestone 9.

The framework is careful to say it "is not a set of standards"; states may use it to develop their own. Treat these as directions of travel, not a test.

Sources, all on k12cs.org and read 17 October 2026: Navigating the Concepts, statements by grade band and the FAQ. The checklist matching is ours. The framework is licensed CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; we quote short extracts and have no connection with its authors.

Ages 6 to 18

Coding milestones at every age from 6 to 18

The same thread runs from first grade to senior year: exact steps, then patterns, then organisation, then judgement. This table is our summary, with the nearest framework endpoint for each age.

Coding milestones by age, with the nearest framework endpoint (ours)

7Repeats a pattern with a loop; makes a sprite react to a clickBlock codingEnd of grade 2

Reading the table

The ages are typical, not targets. A teenager who starts at 14 will pass through the early milestones in weeks, because reading, typing and patience are already in place.

What does not change is the order. In our teaching, functions and parameters always come after loops and conditions feel comfortable, never before.

Ages and US grades

Turning an age into a US grade

US schools and the framework talk in grades, while parents search by age. The link between the two depends on each state’s kindergarten entrance age, which NCES tracks state by state.

Kindergarten entrance age in selected states, NCES State Education Practices, Table 1.3, 2020
StateKindergarten entrance age as listed
California5 on or before 9/1
Texas5 on or before 9/1
Florida5 on or before 9/1
Connecticut5 on or before 1/1
New YorkLEA option (set by the local education agency)

Our arithmetic

Where a child must be 5 by September 1, they start kindergarten at 5, first grade at 6, third grade at 8 and fifth grade at 10. So, by our reckoning, 8 to 10 year olds are mostly in grades 3 to 5, and the end of grade 5 is the framework milestone ahead of them.

In a state with a later cut-off, such as Connecticut’s January 1 in the 2020 table, some children are a few months younger for their grade. Where the local district decides, as in New York, it varies by district.

A cross-check from school courses

Code.org’s course catalogue lines up with the same mapping: its CS Fundamentals series runs from kindergarten to grade 5, CS Discoveries is listed for grades 6 to 10, and CS Principles for grades 9 to 12.

Those are curriculum choices, not rules, but they show the same broad shape as the milestone table: blocks through fifth grade, typed code from middle school, and formal computer science in high school.

Sources: NCES Table 1.3, kindergarten entrance and attendance, by state: 2020 (which itself draws on the Education Commission of the States) and the Code.org curriculum catalogue, both read 17 October 2026. Check your own district for current rules. We are not connected with NCES or Code.org.

One task, five ages

The same task at five ages: drawing a square

The clearest way to see milestones is to give children of different ages the same job. Here is what drawing a square looks like as children grow, from our own classes. Each step is the previous one with one more idea added.

Drawing a square at five ages (ours)
AgeWhat the child producesThe new idea
6Four spoken steps: forward, turn, forward, turn, forward, turn, forward, turnSequence
8A Scratch script: repeat 4 times, move 100 steps, turn 90 degreesLoop
10Typed Python: a for loop that repeats forward and turn four timesTyped syntax
13A function square(size) that draws a square of any sizeParameters
16A function polygon(sides, size) that turns 360 / sides degrees each time, tested for 3, 4 and 6 sidesGeneralising and testing

Why this works as a check

Ask your child to tell you how to draw a square, and listen for which version you get. A 9 year old who says "repeat four times" without prompting has made the jump from sequence to loop.

The 16 year old version is worth a second look. Turning 360 / sides degrees works for any regular polygon, so a single idea replaces endless special cases. That move, from one answer to a general rule, is what the grade 12 framework statements are about.

Try the next step up

Whatever version your child produces, ask for the next one. A 6 year old can try to spot the repeat. An 8 year old can try changing the 4 and the 90 to make a triangle (3 and 120).

Students enjoy discovering that 3 sides need 120 degrees, 6 sides need 60, and that the numbers always multiply to 360. That is a maths milestone hiding inside a coding one.

A milestone program

What the age 10 milestone looks like in typed code

This is a program a 10 year old in our Python class can write, change and explain after a few weeks. It pulls milestones 3 to 5 together in typed form: a loop, a condition and a variable that keeps the score.

The variable

score starts at 0 and goes up by one for each correct answer: milestone 5.

The loop

for question in range(5) asks exactly five questions: milestone 2, in typed form.

The condition

if answer == str(a * b) decides between two replies: milestone 4.

Changes a 10 year old can make

Ask ten questions instead of five, use numbers up to 12, or print a different message for a perfect score. Each change is one or two lines, and each one shows whether the child understands the line they are changing.

The test we use: before running a change, the child predicts what will happen. A correct prediction is the milestone; the running program only confirms it.

The same program at 8

An 8 year old builds the same quiz in Scratch with an ask block, a repeat block, an if block and a score variable. It is the same thinking without the typing.

That is why we do not rush children into Python. The milestone is the idea, and blocks let younger children reach it while their typing catches up.

Four big steps

The four big shifts between 6 and 18

Underneath the age-by-age detail, children make four big changes in how they think about code.

Four shifts in coding thinking (ours)
ShiftTypical ageFrom, to
1. Steps to patterns7 to 8From listing every step to noticing repeats
2. Doing to planning9 to 10From building straight away to sketching first
3. One program to reusable parts12 to 14From one long script to functions with parameters
4. Working code to good code15 to 18From "it runs" to "it is the right design, and here is why"

For parents

Shifts 1 and 2 are the ones to watch for between 8 and 10. Both show up in conversation before they show up in code.

Praise the plan, not just the finished game. Planning is the milestone most children skip.

For kids

If you are copying the same blocks again and again, there is probably a loop hiding in there.

Draw your game on paper before you build it. Two minutes of planning saves twenty minutes of fixing.

Questions from parents

Coding milestones: common questions

What coding milestones should an 8 to 10 year old reach?

By 8, a block-coded game with events, a loop, a condition and a score. By 9, planning a project first and building it in parts. By 10, debugging alone, comparing two solutions and typing a short Python program. These match the K-12 CS Framework endpoint for the end of grade 5.

Can an 8 year old learn to code?

Yes. Most 8 year olds can build Scratch games with events, loops and a score. Typed languages usually come a little later, around 9 or 10, once reading and typing are comfortable.

What grade is a 9 year old in?

Usually third or fourth grade. Where a child must be 5 by September 1 to start kindergarten, as NCES lists for California, Texas and Florida in 2020, children are 8 when third grade starts and turn 9 during it, so a child who is already 9 in September is usually starting fourth grade.

What are the K-12 CS Framework milestones?

The framework describes its concept statements as conceptual milestones at four grade-band endpoints: the ends of grades 2, 5, 8 and 12. States may use it to develop their own standards, so it is a guide rather than a test.

At what age can a child learn Python?

Around 9 or 10 for most children, after they have built a few projects in block code. Teenagers starting from scratch can begin with Python straight away.

Is my child behind if they cannot code at 10?

No. Children who start later often move quickly through the early milestones because they read and concentrate well. The order of the milestones matters more than the age they are reached.

How can I check my child's coding level at home?

Ask them to tell you how to draw a square. Four separate steps, a repeat four times, a typed loop, or a function with a size are each a different milestone. The ten-point checklist on this page gives more ways to check.

What should a 6 year old be able to do in coding?

Follow and give exact step-by-step instructions, use simple picture blocks, and fix a wrong step when it is pointed out. Many 6 year olds also enjoy making a character move with a click.

What times are classes for US families?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible. Choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays.

How much do coding classes cost?

The first lesson, which includes a milestone check, is free. After that, classes are paid monthly and the fees section on this page gives the amount in US dollars.
