Q2. What are the stages of the AI project cycle?

The six stages of the AI project cycle, on one worked example

The AI project cycle has six stages: problem scoping, data acquisition, data exploration, modelling, evaluation and deployment. It is called a cycle because what you learn at evaluation and after deployment sends you back to improve earlier stages.

One example makes the stages stick better than six definitions. Suppose a school canteen throws away trays of unsold lunch every week and wants an AI that predicts tomorrow's number of meals.

StageThe question it answersIn the canteen project
1. Problem scopingWhat exactly are we solving, for whom, and why?Cooks over-prepare; the goal is to predict meals needed per day so less food is wasted
2. Data acquisitionWhat data do we need, and from which reliable source?Two terms of daily meal counts, the day of the week, exam days, holidays, weather
3. Data explorationWhat patterns does the data show once we visualise it?A bar chart shows Fridays and rainy days sell fewer meals; two days have impossible counts and are fixed
4. ModellingWhich model suits the problem, and which performs best?The output is a number, so a regression model; two are tried
5. EvaluationHow well does the model do on data it has never seen?Tested on the last three weeks, held back from training; the better model is kept
6. DeploymentHow is it put to real use, and kept useful?The cook sees each morning's prediction on a phone; counts keep flowing back so the model is retrained

The 4Ws canvas and problem statement template

Problem scoping is done with the 4Ws: Who has the problem (the stakeholders), What the problem is, Where or when it arises (the context), and Why solving it matters (the benefit). The problem statement template then puts all four into one sentence you can return to later.

AI project cycle class 10 questions with answers

  1. A team draws scatter plots of the collected data to look for trends before choosing a model. Name the stage. (1)
    Answer Data exploration. Choosing the model comes next, in modelling.
  2. At which stage is the model tested on newly fetched data it was not trained on? (1)
    Answer Evaluation. Students who write "modelling" have mixed up building with checking.
  3. In the 4Ws canvas, which block records the stakeholders? (1)
    Answer Who. A two-statement item in an earlier board paper placed stakeholders under "When" to catch this.
  4. Explain the second and third stages of the AI project cycle. (2)
    Answer Data acquisition [1]: collecting relevant data from reliable, authentic sources; it is the base the whole project rests on. Data exploration [1]: arranging the data into graphs, charts or tables so that patterns, trends and errors become visible and the right model can be chosen.
  5. Why is the AI project cycle drawn as a cycle and not a straight line? (2)
    Answer Evaluation can show the model is not good enough, so the team returns to collect more data or try another model [1]. After deployment, new data and feedback keep arriving, so the model is improved and redeployed [1].
  6. What is deployment, and why does it matter? (2)
    Answer Deployment is putting the evaluated model into real use, inside an app, a website or a device, where people rely on it [1]. Without it the project helps nobody, and only real use shows problems that testing missed [1].

The same six stages are what the project report and its viva are built on; how to write each one up is on the project and practical file page.

Q3. Which AI domain is it?

The three AI domains: Statistical Data, Computer Vision and NLP

AI is grouped into domains by the type of data the model is fed. Numbers and tables mean Statistical Data, images and video mean Computer Vision, and spoken or written human language means Natural Language Processing.

DomainThe input it works onClue words in a questionAn example to quote
Statistical DataRecords, numbers, tablespredict a price, sales figures, records, compare, forecastPrice comparison websites
Computer VisionPhotos, video, camera feeds, scanscamera, image, scan, detect, recognise faces, count vehiclesCrop monitoring from drone images
Natural Language ProcessingSpoken or written wordsreviews, essays, translate, voice command, chat, captionsSpam filters, machine translation

Note the curriculum's name: the 2026-27 handbook calls the first domain Statistical Data. Older books say Data Science, and the 2026 board paper used Statistical Data as the answer to a price-comparison item.

Six scenarios: name the domain

  1. A railway app answers "When does the next train to Pune leave?" typed in Marathi.
    Answer NLP: the input is written human language.
  2. A dairy forecasts next month's milk demand from three years of daily sales.
    Answer Statistical Data: past numbers predict a future number.
  3. A toll gate reads number plates from its camera.
    Answer Computer Vision: the input is an image of the plate.
  4. An app writes subtitles for a lecture video as the teacher speaks.
    Answer NLP: it converts speech to text. The word "video" is the trap; the AI works on the audio.
  5. A tablet app checks a child's handwritten digits on a worksheet photo.
    Answer Computer Vision: it reads shapes from an image.

A four-mark domain answer, marked

Q. Identify the AI domain for each, with a justification. (a) A hospital app reads patients' typed symptoms and suggests which department to visit. (b) A dam's cameras watch the spillway and raise an alarm when debris blocks it. (2 + 2)

(a) Natural Language Processing [1]. The app must understand sentences that patients type in their own words, which is the core of NLP [1].

(b) Computer Vision [1]. The system interprets images from cameras to spot debris, so it processes visual data [1].

The 2026 marking scheme split its two-scenario item the same way: one mark for the domain, one for a relevant justification. A bare domain name earns half the question.

Q4. What is an ethical framework for AI?

Ethical frameworks for AI, class 10: meaning, need and types

An ethical framework is a step-by-step guide that helps us make sure the choices we make, and the choices our AI makes, do not cause unintended harm. A framework on its own is any structured set of steps for solving a problem; adding ethics points those steps at right and wrong.

Why AI needs one

AI now decides or influences who gets a loan, a job interview or a hospital bed. If it learns from biased data, it repeats that bias at scale. A framework applied while building the system catches the harm before it happens, not after.

What sways our choices

The curriculum's "My Goodness" activity asks students to donate to charity and then shows what swayed them: who the recipient was, where they lived, a pull towards relatives, whether hidden details were revealed. Culture, religion, intuition and values shape decisions without our noticing.

The two types

Sector-based frameworks are written for one field, such as bioethics for healthcare, or finance, education and transport. Value-based frameworks rest on moral principles that apply anywhere: rights-based, utility-based and virtue-based.

The one-question test for any framework

Ask: is it written for a particular industry? If yes, it is sector-based. If it is a moral principle you could carry into any field, it is value-based, and then ask which value leads:

  • Protect each person's rights and dignity first: rights-based.
  • Do the most good for the most people: utility-based.
  • Act as an honest, caring person would: virtue-based.

Side-by-side difference tables for these, with the two-mark answer, are on CBSE Class 10 AI difference between questions.

Sort these, then check

  1. A banking regulator's code for fair AI credit scoring.
    Answer Sector-based: written for finance only.
  2. A city refuses a face-recognition rollout because no citizen consented, though it would cut crime.
    Answer Rights-based: consent and privacy come before the overall gain.
  3. A flood app sends rescue boats where the most people can be reached first.
    Answer Utility-based: the greatest benefit for the greatest number.
  4. A developer reports a flaw in her own team's model though nobody asked her to.
    Answer Virtue-based: honesty and integrity of the person acting.
  5. Assertion: Bioethics is a value-based framework. Reason: Bioethics deals with ethical issues in health, medicine and the life sciences.
    Answer Assertion false, reason true. Because it belongs to one field, bioethics is sector-based. The 2026 board paper set this same trap.
  6. Assertion: Ethical frameworks help an AI avoid unintended outcomes. Reason: They are applied while the solution is being built.
    Answer Both true, and the reason explains the assertion: harm is caught before deployment.

More items in this two-statement shape are on CBSE Class 10 AI assertion reason questions.

Q5. How do I answer the bioethics case study?

Bioethics class 10 AI: the four principles and the case study, in exam format

Bioethics is the sector-based framework used in healthcare and the life sciences. Its four principles are respect for autonomy, do not harm, ensure maximum benefit for all, and give justice. A case answer names a principle, then shows how it applies to the story.

PrincipleFormal nameIn plain wordsWhat it asks of an AI system
Respect for autonomyAutonomyPeople decide for themselves, knowing the factsTell users how it works; make its data and decisions open to checking
Do not harmNon-maleficenceAvoid harm to anyone, human or not; if every path harms, take the least harmfulTrain on data that spreads risk fairly, not only for some groups
Ensure maximum benefitBeneficenceGo beyond avoiding harm and actively do goodMeet clinical standards, and help every group of patients
Give justiceJusticeShare benefits and burdens fairly, whatever a person's backgroundUnderstand the social causes of bias and work against them

The handbook case, in brief

The CBSE handbook's case is a hospital tool built to flag high-risk patients so that extra care went to those who needed it most. It learned from past healthcare spending, treating money spent on a patient as a stand-in for how ill they were. In the place it was built, less had historically been spent on patients from one group, which the handbook calls the western region. So two patients with the same risk score were not equally ill: the patient from that region was usually sicker, and the tool would have sent care away from them.

The lesson: the data looked neutral, but the measure it used carried an old inequality into the model.

Exam question on this case (4 marks)

(a) Give two reasons the algorithm's risk scores were unfair. (2)
(b) Name two bioethics principles the designers should have applied, and explain how each would change the system. (2)

(a) It measured illness through money spent, not through the patient's actual health [1]. Less money had been spent on patients from one region, so the model learned to under-rate their needs even when they were sicker [1].

(b) Do not harm [½]: the tool would have denied care to the sickest group, so it must be trained on data such as diagnoses and test results that reflect real illness for everyone [½]. Justice [½]: care must be shared fairly whatever a patient's background, so designers should check scores group by group before use [½].

This split, one mark per reason and half a mark each for the principle and its explanation, follows the scheme for the four-mark biased-selection case in the 2025-26 sample paper.

Practise on a fresh case

A state health mission screens diabetic patients' eyes with an AI that reads photographs of the retina. It was trained only on sharp images from an expensive camera used in city hospitals. In village camps, cheaper cameras produce softer images and the AI misses far more early disease. Patients are not told an AI made the call.

  1. Which stage of the AI project cycle should have caught the problem before rollout? (1)
    Answer Evaluation: testing on images from the village cameras would have shown the drop. Data acquisition is also a fair answer if explained, since the training data left those cameras out.
  2. Name the bioethics principle broken by not telling patients, and give a fix. (2)
    Answer Respect for autonomy [1]. Tell each patient that an AI screened the photo, what it can miss, and how to ask for a doctor's reading [1].
  3. Explain two more principles the screening breaks and how to repair the system. (4)
    Answer Do not harm [½]: missed disease can lead to lost sight [½]; retrain with images from the cheaper cameras and send unclear images to a doctor [1]. Justice [½]: village patients get a worse service than city patients for the same disease [½]; report accuracy separately for camp and hospital images and deploy only when both meet the same standard [1].

Questions about Unit 1

What students ask about the AI project cycle and ethical frameworks

What are the 6 stages of the AI project cycle in class 10?

Problem scoping, data acquisition, data exploration, modelling, evaluation and deployment. Problem scoping sets the goal with the 4Ws, acquisition collects reliable data, exploration visualises it to find patterns, modelling chooses and trains a model, evaluation tests it on unseen data, and deployment puts it into real use. Learn them in this order; one-mark items often describe a stage and ask for its name.

How many marks is Unit 1 in CBSE Class 10 AI?

Unit 1, Revisiting AI Project Cycle and Ethical Frameworks for AI, carries 7 of the 50 theory marks in the 2026-27 CBSE 417 curriculum. Expect a mix of one-mark objective items, two-mark short answers such as sector-based versus value-based frameworks, and sometimes a four-mark case or scenario answer.

What is an ethical framework in AI class 10?

An ethical framework is a step-by-step guide that helps make sure the choices we make, including those built into an AI system, do not cause unintended harm. AI needs one because it is used to make or influence decisions, and a model trained on biased data can repeat that bias for thousands of people.

What is the difference between sector based and value based frameworks?

A sector-based framework is written for the ethical problems of one industry, for example bioethics for healthcare. A value-based framework rests on general moral principles that apply in any field: rights-based, utility-based or virtue-based. In the 2026 marking scheme, this two-mark answer earned one mark for a correct difference and half a mark for each example.

What are the four principles of bioethics in class 10 AI?

Respect for autonomy (people decide with full knowledge), do not harm or non-maleficence, ensure maximum benefit or beneficence, and give justice (benefits and burdens shared fairly). In a case answer, name the principle and then explain how it applies to the story; the name and the explanation usually carry half a mark each.

Is bioethics a value based or sector based framework?

Sector-based. Bioethics is written for one field, healthcare and the life sciences. The 2026 CBSE Class 10 AI paper tested this with an assertion that called bioethics value-based: the assertion was false and the reason, that bioethics deals with health and medicine, was true.

What is the bioethics case study in class 10 AI?

The CBSE handbook describes a hospital tool that flagged high-risk patients using past healthcare spending as a stand-in for illness. Because less had been spent on one group of patients, the tool rated them as less at risk even when they were sicker. Applying the bioethics principles, especially do not harm and justice, would have caught this before use.

What are the three domains of AI in class 10?

Statistical Data (numbers and records, such as price comparison websites), Computer Vision (images and video, such as crop monitoring from drones) and Natural Language Processing (spoken and written language, such as spam filters and translation). The 2026-27 handbook uses the name Statistical Data where older books said Data Science.
