AI and Python classes for teens in Denver

Where can a Denver teenager learn AI and Python with a live teacher?

Modern Age Coders teaches Python, data science and machine learning live online to Denver students in grades 7 to 12, in level-matched groups or one to one. Colorado’s computer science standards, adopted May 9, 2024, list Artificial Intelligence as one of six strands; our teen courses build that AI on real Python and real Denver data.

Colorado rewrote its computer science standards in 2024 and did something few states have done: it gave artificial intelligence a strand of its own, beside programming and cybersecurity. For a Denver teenager that is a signal about where the subject is heading, but standards are not a course, and in Colorado they are not even compulsory for districts. This page sets out what the state actually asked for, how many Colorado high schools offer computer science according to the published figures, and how our live online classes take a teen from first Python scripts to machine learning. The worked example is Denver’s own bikeway inventory, a dataset where the word "mile" means two different things and the length column is not in miles at all.

Adopted in 2024

CDE records that on May 9, 2024 the State Board adopted revised K-12 standards, adding K-8 standards for the first time and new content on AI, cybersecurity and digital citizenship.

First taught 2026 to 2027

The document says the 2026-27 school year is the first year of implementation for the revised standards, so this year’s Denver ninth graders are the first to meet them in class.

Voluntary by law

Under House Bill 16-1198 local education providers may choose whether to implement them. CDE notes this voluntary status is different from every other set of Colorado academic standards.

House Bill 16-1198 also ties the high school standards to courses that can qualify as a graduation requirement in either mathematics or science, so in some districts a computer science course can fill a math or science slot. Whether it does is a local decision, which is why a Denver family should ask the school directly rather than rely on a statewide rule.

Signature project

How many miles of bikeway does Denver have? It depends which mile you mean

The City and County of Denver publishes its bicycle facilities, existing and planned, as open data built for the Denver Moves: Bikes update. Teens query it, add up lengths by facility type, and discover that the dataset holds three different measures of length.

1. Respect the cap

A count request returned 2,025 records, more than the 2,000 the service hands back in one page. Rather than paging, students ask the server to group and sum for them with outStatistics, a real API skill.

2. Two kinds of mile

CENTERLINE_MILES measures the street once. BIKE_LANE_MILES counts each side that has a lane, and MILEAGE_MULTIPLIER is 2 on 1,833 records and 1 on 192. That is why lane miles are close to double for trails but not for protected lanes.

3. The hidden unit

Shape__Length looks like a third length, but the layer uses a Colorado state plane system measured in feet. The Trail rows sum to 604,328; divide by 5,280 and you get back 114.46 miles.

A one-record puzzle

Group by display status and you find 822 existing bikeway records; group by existing facility type and you find 823. The odd one out is marked Future Bikeway yet already carries an existing type of Protected Bike Lane. A good analyst reports both numbers and the reason, rather than quietly picking one.

Every figure in this section is ours, computed from the city’s layer on 18 October 2026. The City and County of Denver supplies the data as is, without warranty and not for engineering purposes, so our sums are a classroom result, not an official network length. The status field adds the forward-looking part of the story: 1,388 records are Unfunded and 500 Completed, with 60 in design or construction, which makes a good second question about what share of the plan already exists.

The Python is short: a requests call with groupByFieldsForStatistics, a loop that divides feet by 5,280, and a bar chart of centerline and lane miles side by side. The habit it builds is longer lasting. Before adding any column, find out its unit and its definition, and write both into the chart’s caption.

Coding and AI

Colorado put AI in the standards. Why still learn Python first?

Because the AI strand is about understanding systems, and understanding comes from building them.

Hand the bikeway question to a chatbot and it may well sum the length column and call the answer miles. It would be off by a factor of 5,280 and nobody would notice unless the person asking knew where the number came from. Teens who wrote the query themselves catch it at once.

Our machine learning lessons follow the same rule. Students prepare the data, train a model, measure it on examples it has never seen, and then explain in plain words where it fails. AI assistants are allowed as helpers for drafting code, and every suggestion is read and tested before it is kept.

The Colorado AI strand asks teens to understand how computers learn; the reliable way to understand it is to make one learn something and check its work. The longer argument is in is coding still worth learning in 2026.

FAQ

Questions from Denver families

Asked by Denver parents and teens before a first class.

Do Colorado computer science standards include AI?

Yes. The 2024 Colorado Academic Standards for computer science are organized into six strands, and Artificial Intelligence is one of them, alongside Cybersecurity and Digital Citizenship.

When do the new Colorado CS standards start?

CDE says the State Board adopted them on May 9, 2024 and that the 2026-27 school year is the first year of implementation.

Must Denver schools follow the computer science standards?

No. Under House Bill 16-1198 local education providers may choose to implement them, which CDE notes makes them unlike the state's other academic standards.

How many Colorado high schools offer computer science?

Code.org (2025) reports 344 of 519 Colorado high schools, or 66.3%, offering foundational computer science in 2023 to 2024.

Is this page good for a teen who has never coded?

Yes. Python for teens starts from zero, and the free class finds the right group, whether that is beginners or students already writing functions.

What is the Denver bikeway project?

Teens query the City and County of Denver bike facilities data, sum lengths by facility type, and learn why centerline miles, lane miles and a length in feet are different numbers.

What time are classes for Denver teens?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Slots are confirmed in Mountain Time.

Do class times move when the clocks change?

We reconfirm every Denver class before November 1, 2026 and March 14, 2027, because India does not change its clocks and the gap moves by one hour.

Do you have a center in Denver?

No building, no campus: lessons stream live from our teachers in India to the teen at home, so a microphone and decent internet are the only kit required.

Which AI tools do teens use in class?

Only tools whose own terms allow the student's age. We never ask under-18 students to use adult-only services, and most work is plain Python they write themselves.
