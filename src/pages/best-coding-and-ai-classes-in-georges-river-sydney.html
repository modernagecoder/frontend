Signature project

How lopsided are train entries across Georges River?

Transport for NSW releases monthly Opal entries for each station. We placed every station point inside the Georges River boundary using the statistics bureau's council map, kept the ten that fall inside, and measured how concentrated their August 2026 entries are.

1. Shares

Divide each station's entries by the ten-station sum. In Python that is one list comprehension, and the shares must add to 1 apart from rounding.

2. Square and add

Squaring rewards big shares: Hurstville's 39.57% contributes 1,566 points to the index on its own, while Oatley's 3.64% contributes 13.

3. Read it backwards

10,000 divided by 2,193 is 4.56, so the area behaves as if its entries were split evenly across fewer than five stations, not ten.

Why the index beats a headline

Saying Hurstville handles 40% of the entries hides what happens to the other 60%. The index weighs every station, so it moves if Kogarah grows or if four small stations shrink at once. Across the 23 months in the file it stayed between 2,105.5 (February 2026) and 2,203.7 (January 2026), which tells a learner the pattern is stable rather than a one-month quirk.

Entries count Opal taps at the gates, not individual people, and one person can tap in many times in a month. Narwee station sits just outside the council boundary on the bureau's map, so it is not one of the ten. The ten-station sum is our own arithmetic for working out shares, not a published Georges River total, and Transport for NSW has no part in the calculation.

Coding and AI

An AI can name the busiest station. Why should a Georges River learner compute the index?

Because the useful answer is rarely the single biggest number, and AI tools tend to stop there.

Ask a chatbot which station in the area is busiest and it will usually say Hurstville, which is true and not very informative. Ask how evenly the area's traffic is spread and the answer depends on a measure, a time period and a list of stations, and an AI that skips those steps can sound confident while comparing the wrong things. Building the index by hand shows exactly which choices go into it.

A learner who has squared and summed ten station shares checks what an AI tool did before trusting its summary: which stations it included, whether it used entries or exits, and whether the month was typical. That habit carries from Opal data to any dashboard an AI produces later.

Summaries are cheap in 2026; the learner who knows how one is built is the one who can tell a fair summary from a misleading one. The longer argument is in why teenagers should still learn to code in 2026.

How many people live in the Georges River council area?

The statistics bureau's QuickStats for the Georges River local government area records 152,274 residents on Census night 2021, with a median age of 38.

What is the Herfindahl-Hirschman index?

It is a measure of concentration: square each participant's percentage share and add the squares. Ten equal shares give 1,000, a single participant with everything gives 10,000, and higher numbers mean the total is dominated by fewer participants.

What does the Georges River station project show?

Using August 2026 Opal entries for the ten stations inside the council area, the index comes to 2,193, so the area behaves like 4.56 equal stations. Hurstville takes 39.57% of the ten-station sum on its own.

The stations of Hurstville, Penshurst, Mortdale, Oatley, Allawah and Carlton all sit inside the council boundary on the statistics bureau's map. We have not printed a full suburb list, because several suburbs on the edges, such as Kogarah and Kingsgrove, are shared with neighbouring councils.
