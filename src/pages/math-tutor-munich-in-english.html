Read 2 and 3 November 2026. According to the IQB, VERA produces no marks and no ranking of schools or Länder.

The ministry's page on assessment at the Gymnasium explains the newest change. From the school year 2026/27, a central digital test of Grundkompetenzen, the basic skills, is provided for grade 11. Pupils take it in a digital exam environment in the second half of the year, it counts as a small assessment, a kleiner Leistungsnachweis, and in the first step schools may choose whether to take part. The grade 8 and grade 10 Jahrgangsstufentests in maths carry on as before.

The same page also allows modular maths systems, MMS, as a digital aid in assessments from grade 8. For a pupil who learned maths in English with a graphing app, that is good news, but it also means the written working around the tool is what earns the marks. We practise both: using the tool and explaining in words what it showed.

School types

How does maths differ between Mittelschule, Realschule and Gymnasium in Bavaria?

The topics overlap far more than the school names suggest. What changes is the pace, the exam at the end, and how much written reasoning is expected.

Mittelschule

Runs to grade 9, or 10 in the M-Zug. The ministry describes a qualifizierender Abschluss for strong results and the option of the mittlerer Schulabschluss in grade 10 through the M-Zug. Maths help here is about secure arithmetic, percentages and everyday modelling.

Realschule

Ends after grade 10 with the Abschlussprüfung in German, English, maths and the profile subject of the chosen branch. Help focuses on algebra, functions and geometry, and on writing a solution that a marker can follow step by step.

Gymnasium

Nine years again, with the Oberstufe from grade 11 to 13 and the Abitur after Q13. Maths moves fastest here; the Schulaufgaben, the two Jahrgangsstufentests and the new grade 11 test are where gaps first show.

Across all three, the KMK's national standards set the same big ideas. The standards for the Abitur name five Leitideen, among them Funktionaler Zusammenhang, functions and how one quantity depends on another, and Daten und Zufall, data and chance. The trend-line task further down sits right where those two meet.

A pupil who moved to Munich from an English-language school often knows the mathematics and loses marks on the wording. "Begründe" asks for a reason, "berechne" for a calculation, "skizziere" for a sketch, and a correct number without the requested reason earns only part of the marks. Our mentors teach in English and show the German instruction word next to each task, so the pupil learns to read the question the way the teacher wrote it.

A Munich data task

Is Munich getting warmer? Fitting a trend line by hand

The Deutscher Wetterdienst publishes one mean temperature per year for its station München-Stadt, from 1955 to 2025, as open data. With it, a pupil in grade 10 or the Oberstufe can do a least-squares regression from the formula, then check the answer against the DWD's own figures.

The data

The annual file for station 3379 has 71 years, each with a mean temperature in degrees Celsius; no year is missing. We give the pupil munich_years.csv with three columns: the year, the mean temperature and the year's rain.

One warning sits in the metadata, not in the numbers: the station stood at 520 metres until 7 July 1997 and has been at a site at 515 metres since 8 July 1997. A good analysis mentions that, because a move can shift readings a little.

The method

For a line y = mx + c through points (x, y), least squares gives the slope m as the sum of (x minus the mean of x) times (y minus the mean of y), divided by the sum of (x minus the mean of x) squared. The line then passes through the point of means, so c is the mean of y minus m times the mean of x.

Pupils first do it by hand for five years to see how it works, then let Python do all 71.

The slope is about 0.034 degrees a year, so the fitted line rises by roughly 0.34 degrees per decade and by more than two degrees between 1960 and 2025. That is a description of the past 71 years at one station. It is not a forecast: nothing in the method knows about the future, and a pupil who extends the line to 2100 has only drawn a longer ruler.

Version 2 asks the question in another way. Instead of one line, it compares two thirty-year averages, the kind of figure meteorologists call a climate normal.

The regression, the period means and the differences are our own calculations. The DWD marks its 1961 to 1990 figure as referred to the current station site.

  1. Klasse 5 and 6. The means for 2023, 2024 and 2025 were 11.57, 11.58 and 10.66 °C. Find their average. (Answer: 11.27 °C.)
  2. Klasse 7. Rain fell from 1,126.8 mm in 2024 to 738.5 mm in 2025. Find the percentage change to one decimal place. (Answer: a fall of 34.5 percent.)
  3. Klasse 8 to 10. The line gives 10.84 °C for 2025, the station measured 10.66 °C. Find the residual and say what its sign means. (Answer: minus 0.18; 2025 was a little cooler than the trend.)
  4. Klasse 10 and Oberstufe. Our program finds the largest residuals in 1994, 1.41 above the line, and 1996, 1.56 below it. Explain why one cool year next to one warm year does not change the slope much.
  5. Oberstufe. Write two sentences for a report: one that the data supports, one that it does not. Use the words Ausgleichsgerade and Prognose correctly.

Practice set written by Modern Age Coders; the answers come from our own runs on the DWD annual file.

Maths tutoring in Munich in English: common questions

Are the classes in German?

No. Every lesson is live and in English only. The mentor adapts to your child's pace and accent and writes the German maths terms next to the English ones, so your child can read the Schulaufgabe in German and still follow the explanation in English.

Can you help with the Übertritt in Klasse 4?

We help with the maths behind it: place value, the four operations, measuring and word problems. The Übertrittszeugnis itself is based on the school's marks in German, maths and Heimat- und Sachunterricht, so we work alongside the class teacher, never instead of the school.

Do you prepare for the Realschule final exam in maths?

Yes. The Bavarian Realschule ends after grade 10 with an Abschlussprüfung in German, English, maths and the profile subject. We cover the maths with practice questions we write ourselves, timed, and we teach how to set out each step.

What is the new grade 11 test at the Gymnasium?

From 2026/27 the Bavarian ministry provides a central digital test of basic skills in maths for grade 11. It is taken in the second half-year in a digital exam environment and counts as a small assessment; at first, schools may choose whether to use it.

Can you help with the Abitur in Bavaria?

Yes, alongside school. The Bavarian Abitur has three written and two oral exams across five subjects, after Q12 and Q13. We teach Analysis, Geometrie and Stochastik in English. The school course and the exam are what count; we do not award grades.

My child is at an international school in Munich. Can you help?

Yes. The international schools near Munich listed by the AGIS teach IB programmes, and we run separate courses for IB and IGCSE maths. The mentor first checks which programme and level your child follows, then plans from their next assessment.

When are lessons in Munich time?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Most Munich pupils pick a slot between about 16:45 and 19:00, or a Sunday morning.

Who are the tutors?

Our mentors are based in India and teach live over video in English. Your child keeps the same mentor, who learns where they tend to slip and how they like to work.

How big are the groups?

A batch has fifteen to twenty pupils at a similar level. For exam preparation or a large gap, most Munich families choose one to one, with two private lessons a week.

How are fees paid?

Monthly. We show the fee in euro at the European Central Bank reference rate; the payment is made in US dollars and your bank converts it, so the amount on your statement may differ a little. The first lesson is free and needs no card.
