Informatik in Baden-Württemberg

Is Informatik a compulsory subject in Baden-Württemberg?

Yes, since the 2025/26 school year, but it arrives one year group at a time, and the grades above the wave still learn under the old rules. Every fact below names its source.

A subject that grows upwards

The Gesellschaft für Informatik (GI) compiles every Land's rules in its Informatik-Monitor 2026/27. For Baden-Württemberg it records that the compulsory subject Informatik und Medienbildung was introduced across all school types of Sekundarstufe I from 2025/26, starting in grades 5 and 6 and then growing year by year. The same page says the subject is eventually planned for grades 5 to 11.

At the Werkrealschule, Hauptschule, Realschule and Gemeinschaftsschule the monitor gives the subject four weekly hours in total across the lower secondary years. The GI's national summary therefore counts Baden-Württemberg among four Länder that meet the short-term recommendation of four compulsory hours made by the KMK's Standing Scientific Commission.

The plan comes later

One detail matters for parents. The monitor states that no Bildungsplan yet exists for the new subject: the consultation is planned for the 2026/27 school year and the plan is to come into force in August 2027. Until then teachers in grades 5 to 7 can lean on older plans for the former Aufbaukurs Informatik and Basiskurs Medienbildung, with a reading aid (Lesehilfe) from the ministry.

Pupils in grade 7 in 2026/27 therefore still take the Aufbaukurs Informatik, which the monitor says is taught from 2026/27 together with the new reading aid. A new profile subject, Naturwissenschaft, Informatik und Technik (NIT), is due at Gemeinschaftsschulen and Gymnasien from 2027/28, while the older profile subject IMP is being phased out.

Who takes it at the top

Few pupils keep Informatik to the end. The monitor gives 7 % as the share of upper secondary pupils taking Informatik in Baden-Württemberg in 2024/25, from KMK figures, and its national page names Baden-Württemberg among the three Länder where only about one pupil in twelve takes the subject in the Oberstufe. The national average it quotes is about 15 %.

Who teaches it

For 2025/26 the monitor reports 717 Informatik teachers at the school types it counts, 2 % of all teachers, and 26 new Informatik teachers hired in the year, 25 of them through a teaching degree and one through a lateral entry route. Its figure leaves out Werkreal and Hauptschulen, Gemeinschaftsschulen and special schools, so read it as a floor.

What this means in practice: a ten year old in Stuttgart, Karlsruhe or Ulm now meets computing at school, which is new, but the teaching is still being built, and the step from grade 10 to a Kursstufe course in Informatik is one most pupils do not take. Our classes are not a replacement for that subject. They give a pupil enough programming that the school lesson feels like practice rather than a first encounter, and they keep a teenager programming in the years the timetable does not.

School types

Which school types are there in Baden-Württemberg?

Five secondary school types serve a Land of 11,245,898 people, 612,663 of them in the capital, Stuttgart (Destatis, 31 December 2024). The Gymnasium is still mostly on the eight-year route.

Four years of Grundschule

Children spend grades 1 to 4 in the Grundschule, as in most Länder. The KMK states that there is no leaving exam at the end of it.

Five secondary types

The monitor lists the Werkrealschule, Hauptschule, Realschule, Gemeinschaftsschule and Gymnasium for grades 5 to 10. The KMK names the Gemeinschaftsschule in Baden-Württemberg among the schools that offer all three tracks under one roof.

Two routes to the Abitur

At the Gymnasium the Abitur mostly comes after grade 12. At a Gemeinschaftsschule with its own Oberstufe it comes after grade 13. Berufliche Gymnasien are a third door, with their own exam schedule.

Families moving into the Land, often for work in the Stuttgart region, ask whether an English-speaking child should start at a Gymnasium or a Gemeinschaftsschule. That is a school decision, not ours, and we do not advise on placement. What we can say is that both school types now teach Informatik und Medienbildung in grades 5 and 6, so the computing a pupil meets in the first two secondary years is the same subject whichever door they walk through.

The Informatik Abitur

How is the Informatik Abitur set in Baden-Württemberg?

Centrally for the Leistungsfach, on paper, with very little allowed on the desk. The facts come from the Informatik-Monitor and the Kultusministerium.

Written, central, without a computer

According to the GI's Baden-Württemberg page, a written Abitur exam is taken in the Leistungsfach Informatik and it is organised centrally, the same across the Land. The only permitted aids are a German spelling dictionary and a scientific calculator. Programming tasks may be answered in the language used in the course, but a programming environment is not allowed, and practical parts at a computer are neither planned nor permitted.

In the Wahlfach and the Basisfach an oral Abitur exam is possible instead. The monitor adds that Informatik is not assigned to any of the Abitur subject fields and so is not treated as equal to the sciences when pupils choose exam subjects.

What that asks of a pupil

A pupil who has only ever written code with an editor that completes brackets and flags errors will find a paper exam hard, even if they program well. The skill being tested is reading and writing correct code without help: tracing a loop by hand, writing a class from memory, explaining why an algorithm stops.

That is why our Oberstufe course mixes live programming with written practice on paper, timed and marked line by line. The school course and its teachers are what counts for the Abitur; we teach alongside them and do not set or mark school work.

Taught in English

Every class is live and in English. Our mentors are based in India and adapt to each learner's speed and accent.

English, said up front

We teach only in English, never in German. For a pupil at an international or bilingual school in the Land this matches the classroom; for a pupil at a German school it adds a second language to the coding at no extra cost.

Matched to the learner

Mentors slow down, rephrase and check understanding. They get used to how a pupil speaks, and a teenager who mixes in a German technical word is answered in plain English rather than corrected for it.

German terms explained

School Informatik in the Land uses German terms such as Algorithmus, Schleife or Datenbank. We name the German word next to the English one, so a pupil can use both in class.

A Stuttgart data project

Which Stuttgart library branch is the busiest? A project in three versions

The Landeshauptstadt Stuttgart publishes the stock and the loans of every branch of its city library as open data. Our pupils use the 2024 figures to learn a habit that matters well beyond libraries: a total and a rate answer different questions, and a rate is only fair across rows that are alike.

The data

The file is stadtbibliotheken_stuttgart_bis-2024.csv from the city's statistics office, published under the CC BY 4.0 licence, which allows reuse including commercial reuse with credit. Each row gives a year, a library, a branch, a measure (Medienbestand for items held, Entleihungen for loans) and a value. We downloaded it on 3 November 2026.

For 2024 it lists 20 branches under Stadtteilbibliotheken, from large ones such as Vaihingen and Bad Cannstatt to two pop-up branches and a tiny collection in the Rathauspassage.

The question

Which branch is the busiest? A first-time programmer sorts by loans and is done. A better question follows at once: busy compared with what? A branch with three times as many books will lend more simply because it is bigger.

So the project has three versions, and each one is right about the question it answers.

Vaihingen lent 274,027 items in 2024. True, and useful if the question is where most books leave the shelf. But Vaihingen also holds 50,715 items, the largest stock of the 20. Version 2 divides loans by stock, which librarians call turnover: how many times, on average, each item went out in the year.

Stuttgart-West, with 25,587 items and 164,462 loans, works its stock hardest: each item went out more than six times. The bottom of the list is stranger. Popup Hedelfingen held 4,106 items but recorded only 10 loans. Before anyone calls it a failure, a good programmer asks whether it is comparable at all, and the data can answer that: Hedelfingen appears in the file only in 2024.

  1. Ages 8 to 10, no screen. Two classes collect books for a fair. Class A brings 60 books for 30 children, class B brings 40 for 10. Which class gave more? Then: which class gave more per child?
  2. Ages 10 to 12, Scratch. Store loans and stock for five branches in two lists, find the largest loan count, then the largest loans divided by stock, and see whether the winner changes.
  3. Ages 13 to 16, Python. Run Version 1, predict the result of Version 2 before you run it, then write Version 3 yourself and explain why the three answers are all correct.
  4. Going further. The tiny Rathauspassage collection has 57 items and a turnover of 4.75. Write a rule that only ranks branches above a minimum stock, and argue for the threshold you chose.

A first contest

Can pupils in Baden-Württemberg enter the Informatik-Biber in 2026?

Yes, through their school. It is free and needs no programming.

Bundesweite Informatikwettbewerbe (BWINF) runs the Informatik-Biber for pupils from grade 3 up to grade 13, and nobody needs to have programmed before. This year the Biber is held from 9 to 20 November 2026; schools register their pupils and run the quiz in a lesson. For a pupil in grade 5 or 6 who has just met Informatik und Medienbildung, it is a short, friendly test of the same thinking. We do not reuse or copy any real Biber task; in our competitions course every puzzle is written by us, and families who want the originals get a link to the free archive the organiser keeps. We are not connected to BWINF.

Coding classes in Baden-Württemberg: common questions

Are the classes in German?

No, never. We teach in English alone, live, and the mentors (who are based in India) tune their speed and accent to the learner. Where school uses a German word such as Schleife, the mentor names it and explains it in English.

Is Informatik compulsory in Baden-Württemberg?

Yes. According to the Gesellschaft für Informatik's Informatik-Monitor 2026/27, the compulsory subject Informatik und Medienbildung started in grades 5 and 6 in 2025/26 at all secondary school types and grows a year at a time.

Does the new subject already have a curriculum?

Not yet. The Informatik-Monitor states that the consultation on the new Bildungsplan is planned for the 2026/27 school year and that it should come into force in August 2027. Until then teachers use older plans with a ministry reading aid.

Can my child use a computer in the written Informatik Abitur?

No. The Informatik-Monitor says the written Leistungsfach exam is set centrally, a programming environment is not allowed and there are no practical parts at a computer. The aids are a spelling dictionary and a scientific calculator.

Do you prepare pupils for the Abitur?

We teach alongside school, with programming and written practice on paper for the Kursstufe. The school course is what counts for the Abitur; we do not set, mark or award any school grade.

When are classes in German time?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Afternoons from about a quarter past three are popular across the Land.

What happens in the long summer holidays?

Classes continue. The KMK dates for Baden-Württemberg run from 29 July to 11 September 2027, and many families keep a lighter rhythm or switch to one to one for a few weeks.

How many learners share a class?

A batch brings together fifteen to twenty learners at a similar level. Choose one to one if your child would rather have the mentor alone, with lessons built around their own questions.

Why is the fee shown in euro but charged in dollars?

We display each monthly fee in euro, worked out from the ECB reference rate, so families in Germany can compare easily. The card payment itself runs in US dollars, which means your bank converts it on the day and a cent or two of difference is normal. Trying a first lesson costs nothing.

Are you connected to the Kultusministerium or a school?

No. We are an independent online school. We are not connected to the Kultusministerium, the KMK, BWINF, any competition or any school in Baden-Württemberg.
