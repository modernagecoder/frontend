## Where it does fit, grade by grade

| Stage | Examination position | What we would do |
|---|---|---|
| Grades 4 to 6 | None nationally | Start. Typed Python from about nine, gently, alongside blocks |
| Grades 7 and 8 | No national certificate at the end | The best window in the system. Run the full twelve months |
| Grade 9 | BJC at the end of the year | Reduce to one narrow session a week, or pause |
| Grades 10 and 11 | Building toward BGCSE, not sitting it | The second window, and the portfolio one |
| Grade 12 | BGCSE at the end of the year | Reduce or pause, and come back after |

There is a planning consequence worth stating plainly. A family beginning in Grade 7 gets a complete twelve months, a portfolio started and a habit formed before the BJC year arrives, and can then put it down for a year at no cost. A family beginning in Grade 9 is buying a year that will be interrupted at the worst possible moment. Same programme, same fee, materially different outcome, decided entirely by which year it was purchased in.

## Advising a pause, which costs us the month

In Grade 9 and Grade 12 our recommendation is usually to reduce or stop. A fixed national examination outranks a voluntary programme, and a student trying to do both properly generally does neither well.

We can give that advice in good conscience for a boring commercial reason: fees are monthly with no minimum term, so pausing costs a family nothing beyond the month they are in. A provider on an annual contract cannot afford to tell you this.

While a student is paused we hold the individual progress record, so resuming starts from where they actually were rather than from a re-diagnosis.

## What transfers, and what does not

**Transfers:** the habit of asking what a number is compared to, which applies to a laboratory result as readily as a model. Breaking a vague task into checkable steps, which shows up first in open-ended school assignments. Writing down what you cannot do, which is unusual in school work at any level.

**Does not transfer:** marks. We are not going to claim a grade effect we cannot demonstrate, and any provider promising improved BGCSE results from a coding course is making a claim about examinations from a course that is not on the examination syllabus.

**Also does not transfer:** a qualification. Our completion certificate is ours. The portfolio is the thing with external value, and a repository with a year of dated commits is something a reader can open and assess.
