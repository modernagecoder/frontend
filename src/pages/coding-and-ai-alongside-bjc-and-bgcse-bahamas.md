In short

Bahamian students sit the Bahamas Junior Certificate at the end of Grade 9 and the Bahamas General Certificate of Secondary Education generally at the end of Grade 12. This twelve-month coding and AI programme is not preparation for either, does not follow either specification, and awards nothing a school or university will treat as a qualification. What it offers instead is a deliberate sequence around those dates: the strongest windows are Grades 7 and 8 and Grades 10 and 11, and in the two examination years our advice is to reduce the load or pause, which we give even though it costs us the month.

Read the table for the windows rather than the examinations. Grades 7 and 8 carry no national certificate at the end of them, and neither do Grades 10 and 11 in the same way that Grade 12 does. Those are the years with room in them, and they are where a twelve-month programme does its best work.

There is a planning consequence in that which is worth stating plainly. A family who begins in Grade 7 gets a complete twelve months, a portfolio started and a habit formed before the BJC year arrives, and can then put the whole thing down for a year at no cost. A family who begins in Grade 9 is buying a year that will be interrupted at the worst possible moment. Same programme, same fee, materially different outcome, decided entirely by which year it was purchased in. That is the single most useful thing on this page.

Being blunt about this

## This is not examination preparation, and we will not sell it as such

There is a version of this page that would imply otherwise and get more enquiries. Here is the accurate version.

Nothing in this track appears on a BJC or a BGCSE syllabus. We do not teach to either specification, we do not award anything a Bahamian school or university will recognise as a qualification, and a student who takes it will not get marks for having done so.

If what you need is preparation for a specific Bahamian examination paper, the right people to ask are your child's school and a tutor who works to that specification daily. We would be guessing, and guessing about somebody's Grade 12 year is not a service.

What we will claim is narrower and, we think, more useful. Students who have spent months being made to say what a number is compared to, to write down what their project cannot do, and to break a vague objective into checkable steps tend to handle open-ended school work differently. That is a real benefit and it is not a marks guarantee, and the distance between those two statements is exactly the distance we are trying to keep. Any provider closing that gap for you, and several will, is making a claim about examination results from a course that is not on the examination syllabus.

Year by year

## What we would actually advise, grade by grade

Including the two years where our advice is to do less of this, which costs us money and is still the right advice.

1. #### Grades 4 to 6: start, and keep it light

  Ninety minutes a week is plenty and there is no external pressure anywhere in these years. The aim is that making a computer do something feels ordinary by the time it starts to matter, and that a blank file is not frightening. Typed Python from around nine, in small amounts, alongside blocks rather than replacing them.
2. #### Grades 7 and 8: run the whole track

  These two years carry no national certificate and they are the most valuable window in the Bahamian system for this. A student who does the full twelve months here arrives in Grade 9 with a portfolio already started and can afford to put it down for a year without losing it.
3. #### Grade 9: reduce, because the BJC is at the end of it

  Our advice is one session a week on a narrow goal, or a deliberate pause. Not because coding is unimportant but because a fixed national examination outranks a voluntary programme, and a student trying to do both properly usually does neither. This costs us revenue and it is still what we would tell a friend.
4. #### Grades 10 and 11: the second window, and the portfolio one

  The best years for the machine learning quarter and for building something publishable, because there is real time and the BGCSE is not yet immediate. A student who finishes two documented projects here is in an unusually strong position for applications a year later.
5. #### Grade 12: pause, and come back

  BGCSE year. If a student insists on continuing, one session a week and nothing more, with the project frozen rather than extended. Most families we advise honestly choose to stop, and we hold the record so that resuming afterwards starts from where they were rather than from a guess.

What genuinely transfers

## Three habits that show up in school work, and one that does not

### Asking what a number is compared to

The single most transferable habit in the programme. A student who has been made to produce a baseline before believing their own model tends to apply the same question to a laboratory result or a statistic in a comprehension passage.

### Breaking a vague task into checkable steps

Open-ended school assignments are where this shows up first. Several parents have reported it as the earliest visible change, well before anything recognisably technical.

### Writing down what you cannot do

Stating a limitation reads as confidence rather than weakness, and it is unusual in school work at any level. Students find it uncomfortable for about a term and then find it useful permanently.

### What does not transfer: marks

We are not going to claim a grade effect we cannot demonstrate. Nothing here is on either syllabus, and any provider promising you improved BGCSE results from a coding course is selling something they cannot deliver.

### What also does not transfer: a qualification

The completion certificate is ours, not a national award. It is honest about what it is, and no Bahamian school or university treats it as a credential. The portfolio is the thing with external value.

### What we hold while you pause

The individual progress record. It exists so that a student returning after a Grade 12 pause resumes at the right point rather than being re-diagnosed from scratch, which is the main practical benefit of a written record.

## What Bahamian families ask about examinations

### Will this help my child's BGCSE results?

We are not going to claim that, because we cannot demonstrate it and nothing here is on the syllabus. What tends to transfer is the habit of asking what a number is compared to and of breaking an open-ended task into checkable steps, which several parents have reported showing up in school work. That is a real effect and it is not a marks guarantee, and we would rather state both halves.

### Should my child in Grade 9 be doing this at all?

Reduced, or paused. The BJC is at the end of that year and a national examination outranks a voluntary programme, so our advice is one session a week on a narrow target at most. A great many families choose to stop for the year, and we hold the progress record so that resuming afterwards starts from where they actually were.

### What about Grade 12?

Pause, in almost every case. If a student genuinely wants to continue, one session a week with the project frozen rather than extended, and nothing more. Advising a pause costs us the month and it is still the right advice: a student trying to do BGCSE year and a twelve-month build programme properly usually does neither well.

### How much does it cost?

A group batch of five to eight students is USD 100 a month and one to one teaching is USD 150 a month, billed in US dollars, the same flat figures everywhere outside India. There is no enrolment fee and no minimum term, so pausing for an examination year costs you nothing beyond the month you are in.

### Do you teach to the BJC or BGCSE computer syllabus?

No. We do not follow either specification and we would be guessing if we tried. For preparation against a specific Bahamian paper, your child's school and a tutor who works to that specification every day are the right people, and we will say so rather than take the work.

### Which grades are actually the best time to start?

Grades 7 and 8, clearly. They carry no national certificate at the end, they have the most room in the whole system, and a student who completes the twelve months there arrives at the BJC year with a portfolio already begun and can afford to set it down. Grades 10 and 11 are the second-best window and the best one for building something publishable.

### Is the certificate you award worth anything?

It is ours, and it is honest about that. No Bahamian school or university treats it as a credential and we would not want them to. The thing with genuine external value is the portfolio: a repository with a year of dated commits, a stated error figure and an explicit limitation, which a reader can open and assess directly.

### My child is in Grade 12 and wants to apply abroad. Is it too late to start?

For a twelve-month track, effectively yes, and we would rather say so. What can be worth doing in that position is a short one to one block aimed at making existing work presentable: adding the baseline, the error figure and the limitation paragraph to something already built. That is a few sessions rather than a year and it often doubles what the existing work is worth to a reader.

### Can this replace a school computing subject?

No, and it is not trying to. It runs alongside school, awards nothing recognised, and covers things a school syllabus mostly does not: real data, measured models, version control and publishing. Where a school offers a computing subject, taking both is the strongest combination, and where the timetable will not allow it the school subject wins.

A mentor telephones you at a Bahamian hour to arrange the free session. Nothing is charged and no card is requested. Tell us the grade your child is in on that call, because for Grade 9 and Grade 12 the honest recommendation is frequently to wait, and it is better to hear that at the start than after a term.
