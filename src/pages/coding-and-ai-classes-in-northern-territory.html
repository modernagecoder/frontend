Coding and AI classes in the Northern Territory

How can a Northern Territory learner get the best coding and AI teaching from far away?

Through live online lessons that ignore distance and use the Territory's own geography as the maths. The 2021 Census counted 232,605 people in the NT. We teach ages 6 to 67 live, from Scratch to Python, geometry and AI, in a small group at one level or one to one, on the same clock all year, and the first lesson costs nothing.

Draw a triangle on a map between Darwin, Nhulunbuy on the Gove Peninsula and Alice Springs, and measure its three angles. At school you learn they must add up to 180 degrees. On the curved surface of the Earth they add up to 180.577. The extra bit, called the spherical excess, is not a measuring error: it is exactly proportional to the triangle's area. Our learners compute the distances and angles from map coordinates, find the excess, use it to work out the area of the triangle, and discover that treating the Territory as flat loses about 1,600 square kilometres.

Signature project

Why do the angles of a Territory triangle add up to more than 180 degrees?

Darwin, Nhulunbuy and Alice Springs as points on a sphere, with every side and angle computed from map coordinates. Our calculation, 7 October 2026.

The Darwin, Nhulunbuy, Alice Springs triangle on a spherical Earth (our calculation)
MeasureValue
Darwin to Nhulunbuy645.5 km
Nhulunbuy to Alice Springs1,316.7 km
Darwin to Alice Springs1,290.1 km
Angles at Darwin, Nhulunbuy, Alice Springs78.1546°, 73.5804°, 28.8420°
Sum of the three angles180.577°
Spherical excess0.577°, or 2,077 arcseconds
Area from the excess (Girard's theorem)408,809 km²
Area of a flat triangle with the same sides407,207 km²

1. Points as arrows

Each town's latitude and longitude becomes an arrow from the Earth's centre. The angle between two arrows, multiplied by the Earth's radius, is the great-circle distance between the towns.

2. Angles between planes

Each side of the triangle lies on a plane through the Earth's centre. The angle at a corner is the angle between two of those planes, found with a cross product and a dot product.

3. The excess is the area

Girard's theorem says the excess, measured in radians and multiplied by the radius squared, is the triangle's area. Here that gives 408,809 km², about 1,600 km² more than a flat triangle with the same sides.

Bigger triangles bend more

Swap Nhulunbuy for Katherine, close to the line between Darwin and Alice Springs, and the triangle becomes long and thin. Its excess drops to just 0.0864 degrees, 311 arcseconds, because its area is so much smaller. Learners see that the curve of the Earth only shows up when a shape covers a lot of ground, which is why surveyors of small blocks can treat the land as flat and surveyors of the Territory cannot.

All figures are our calculations from OpenStreetMap coordinates on a perfectly round Earth of radius 6,371.0088 km. The real Earth is slightly flattened, and each town's coordinate marks one mapped point, so official surveyed distances will differ a little.

Geometry in code

Five habits for any program that works on a curved Earth

Learned on Territory towns, then applied to flight paths, shipping routes, satellite data and any map bigger than a city.

How to stop flat-Earth assumptions creeping into code
HabitIn practiceWhat it prevents
Say which EarthWrite down the radius or model usedTwo programs that disagree for no visible reason

The fourth habit is the one Territory learners find most useful. Seeing a 1,600 km² gap on a big triangle and almost nothing on a thin one teaches them when the extra effort of spherical geometry earns its keep.

Coding and AI

An AI tool can measure the distance from Darwin to Alice Springs. Why should a Territory student understand how?

Because a formula that assumes a flat map can be confidently wrong over Territory distances.

Ask an assistant for the area of a triangle joining three Territory towns and it may use the school formula for a flat triangle, give a precise-looking number and not mention the curve of the Earth. Ask it to write a distance function and it may treat degrees of longitude as if they were the same width everywhere. The answers look reasonable on a small scale and drift as the distances grow.

A student who has computed the spherical excess of a real Territory triangle knows what to ask of any generated answer involving the globe: flat or curved, which radius, and how big is the difference at this scale? Those questions matter for aviation, mining, defence and remote logistics, all of which deal in long distances.

That is why a Territory student should still learn to code in 2026: to know when an AI tool's numbers have quietly flattened the Earth. The longer argument is in why teenagers should still learn to code in 2026.

FAQ

Northern Territory coding class questions

What Territory families tend to ask first.

How many people live in the Northern Territory?

The 2021 Census counted 232,605 people in the Northern Territory, with a median age of 33, and 139,902 of them in Greater Darwin.

What is spherical excess?

Spherical excess is the amount by which the angles of a triangle drawn on a sphere add up to more than 180 degrees. By Girard's theorem it is proportional to the triangle's area, so larger triangles have larger excess.

What is the Territory triangle project?

Learners compute the triangle joining Darwin, Nhulunbuy and Alice Springs on a spherical Earth from map coordinates. Its angles add up to 180.577 degrees, and the excess gives an area of 408,809 square kilometres.

Does the Northern Territory have daylight saving?

No. The IANA time zone database lists Darwin at nine and a half hours ahead of universal time with no daylight saving, so the Territory keeps one time all year, four hours ahead of India.

Why don't you list NT school term dates?

The Territory's school pages declined our automated check, and we only print dates we have read at the source ourselves. Families tell the teacher their school calendar during the trial.

Do you help senior students in the NT?

Yes, with the programming, maths and data skills senior subjects draw on. We could not read the department's senior secondary pages automatically, so we do not describe those subjects; assessed work always stays the student's own.

Can students in remote communities join?

Yes, if they have a laptop or desktop, sound and a connection reliable enough for a video call. They join exactly the same live lessons as learners in Darwin.

Is there a Modern Age Coders centre in the Northern Territory?

Fifteen to twenty, grouped by level, pace and goals rather than age or town. If no group meets at a suitable hour, the learner is offered one-to-one lessons.
