IOI 2027 in Potsdam: a guide for families

In September 2027 the International Olympiad in Informatics comes to Germany for the first time since 1992. About 400 contestants from up to 100 countries will spend two days solving algorithm problems at the Hasso-Plattner-Institut in Potsdam. This page explains what the olympiad is, how BWINF chooses the four pupils who represent Germany, who is eligible, and what a family with a strong young programmer can sensibly do now. It is a guide, not a route in: no course, ours included, can place anyone on the team.

In short

The IOI 2027 is the 39th International Olympiad in Informatics, held from 12 to 19 September 2027 at the Hasso-Plattner-Institut in Potsdam. Germany sends four pupils chosen by BWINF from the strongest entrants of the Bundeswettbewerb Informatik. Modern Age Coders is not part of that selection; we teach algorithms and C++ live online in English, to groups of fifteen to twenty or to a single pupil, at each teenager's pace and accent.

The olympiad

What contestants actually do

BWINF describes the format: on two contest days, contestants spend five hours each solving three algorithmic problems at university level, and their programs are checked automatically against test data.

Nobody marks an essay. A solution scores only if it produces correct output, fast enough, on data the contestant never sees.

How medals work

The IOI regulations say that, ties aside, half of all contestants receive a medal: one twelfth gold, one sixth silver and one quarter bronze.

If exactly 400 compete, that is about 33 gold, 67 silver and 100 bronze medals (our arithmetic). At IOI 2026 in Tashkent, 375 pupils from 92 countries took part and all four German team members won a medal, two silver and two bronze, BWINF reports.

The IOI's own site is direct about entry: participation is not open to individuals, and each country sends a team of four chosen through a national competition. In Germany that competition is the Bundeswettbewerb Informatik, and BWINF has run the German selection since 1989.

Team selection

How is the German IOI team chosen?

Over about a year, in stages, starting from the Bundeswettbewerb Informatik. BWINF's pages describe each step.

  1. The Bundeswettbewerb Informatik. Entering it is a requirement, BWINF's FAQ says. Finalists and other very strong round 2 entrants are invited to the IOI selection if they meet the IOI's rules.
  2. Online pre-selection. For the 2027 team it began on 5 September 2026 with 72 participants, invited from the 44th Bundeswettbewerb. Training runs online on Saturdays, with about 15 coaches, all former participants, each pupil with a personal coach. The language is C++.
  3. Two qualification contests in November. They decide who is invited to the in-person selection.
  4. Selection camps. BWINF's pages describe three Lehrgänge, each with two selection contests, together with talks on algorithms and problem-solving strategies. After the second camp about six or seven pupils go on to the third.
  5. The team of four. Places in the selection contests decide the teams for the Baltic, Central European and International olympiads.

Eligibility

Who is eligible for Germany's IOI team: age, school and nationality?

BWINF states three conditions. One of them, the age limit, is worded differently on two of its pages, so check with BWINF for a borderline birthday.

School and nationality

A pupil must still have been at school in the last school year that ends before the IOI, BWINF's Teilnehmen page says.

Nationality does not matter for pupils at a school in Germany: BWINF's FAQ says every pupil attending a school in Germany can become a team member. Only pupils at school abroad need German citizenship or a recognised German school abroad.

Age, in BWINF's two wordings

BWINF's FAQ says IOI contestants must not have reached their 20th birthday on 1 July of the IOI year. Its Teilnehmen page says they may not be older than 20 on the opening day.

For most pupils both give the same answer. For a birthday close to the limit, ask BWINF before planning; we do not interpret the rule for anyone.

BWINF adds that many Bundeswettbewerb finalists are too old for the IOI by the time the olympiad comes round, because the team competes in the year after the Bundeswettbewerb final. In practice that rewards an early start: the pupils who reach the selection usually first met contest problems several years before.

A lesson of our own

How are IOI solutions judged, and how can a pupil test like the grader?

IOI programs are run against hidden test data, so a solution that looks right can still fail. The habit that protects against this is the stress test. The problem and code below are our own, written for this page.

The problem (ours)

A tram records the number of passengers at each stop. Find the length of the longest stretch of stops where the count rises at every stop. For 3, 5, 7, 2, 8 the answer is 3: the stops with 3, 5 and 7.

A brute force checks every stretch, which is slow but almost impossible to get wrong. A fast solution walks once along the list and keeps the current run.

The stress test

Write the brute force and the fast version, then generate a thousand small random lists and compare the answers. The first list where they disagree is a gift: a tiny, concrete case that shows the bug.

Small lists matter. Six numbers from 1 to 5 are enough to reach the corner cases, and short enough to check by hand.

The first try looks reasonable and passes the example 3, 5, 7, 2, 8. Its bug only shows when the longest run is at the very end of the list: it updates the longest run found so far only when a run breaks, so a run that is still going when the list ends is never counted. On [4, 4, 2, 4] the final run 2, 4 has length 2, and the first try answers 1. The fix updates that record at every step. The random tester found the bug on its seventh list, in a fraction of a second; a grader with hidden data would have found it too, and taken the points.

  1. Reproduce it. Run the code. Then trace fast_first_try on [4, 4, 2, 4] by hand and find the moment it goes wrong.
  2. Break it again. Change the fixed version so the run continues when two counts are equal. Does the stress test catch your new bug, and on which list?
  3. Port it. Write the brute force and the fixed solution in C++, the language of BWINF's pre-selection, and compare them with the same random lists.
  4. Going further. Change the generator to produce lists of length 1. Which of the three functions has a problem with an empty list, and should the task allow one?

What we teach

We do not teach the live tasks of any running round, and we never write or submit work for a contest on anyone's behalf.

What we never claim

We are not connected with BWINF, the HPI or the IOI, and we have no role in choosing the German team. No pupil reaches the selection, a camp or a medal because of us; they get there through their own results in BWINF's contests.

What we can promise is narrower and real: patient teaching of hard material, at the pupil's pace.

IOI 2027 in Potsdam: common questions

When and where is the IOI 2027?

BWINF says the 39th International Olympiad in Informatics takes place from 12 to 19 September 2027 at the Hasso-Plattner-Institut in Potsdam, hosted by the HPI together with BWINF. Germany last hosted in 1992, in Bonn.

Can my child still get into the German team for Potsdam?

Only through BWINF's selection, which for 2027 began on 5 September 2026 with pupils invited from the 44th Bundeswettbewerb Informatik. A pupil starting now would aim at the 45th Bundeswettbewerb, which BWINF names as the entry to the selection for the IOI 2028 in Japan.

Does my child need German citizenship?

Not if they attend a school in Germany: BWINF's FAQ says nationality is then irrelevant. Pupils at school abroad need German citizenship or a recognised German school abroad.

How old can an IOI contestant be?

BWINF's FAQ says contestants must not have turned 20 by 1 July of the IOI year; another BWINF page says not older than 20 on opening day. For a birthday near the limit, ask BWINF directly.

Can you get my child into the IOI selection?

No. We are not connected with BWINF, the HPI or the IOI and have no part in the selection. The IOI says participation is not open to individuals; teams are chosen through national competitions. We teach the skills, and pupils enter the contests themselves.

Which programming language should my teenager learn?

C++. BWINF's pre-selection trains in C++, and the IOI names it as the most common language among contestants. Python is a good first language and fine for the Bundeswettbewerb.

Are the classes in German?

No. We teach live and in English only, and the mentor adapts to the pupil's pace and accent. Olympiad problems and code are international, and English is their common language.

When are classes in German time?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Teenagers usually choose an early evening or a weekend slot.

How are fees charged?

We show fees in euro using the European Central Bank's reference rate and charge them in US dollars, so your bank does the conversion and its euro amount may differ a little. The first class is free.
