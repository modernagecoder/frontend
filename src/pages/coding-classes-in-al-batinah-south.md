## The ten-day month that looked like a collapse

Synthetic event counts expose a comparison over unequal observation windows.

### 1. Read the wrong headline

June has 120 events while May has 341. A raw month-to-month calculation reports a fall of about 64.8 percent.

### 2. Find the cutoff

The data was extracted after day ten of June. May contains 31 days, so the two totals have radically different exposure.

### 3. Compare equal windows

The first ten days contain 100 events in April, 110 in May and 120 in June. The observed daily rate rises rather than collapses.

| Period | Observed days | Observed events | Events per observed day | First ten days | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| April | 30 | 300 | 10.0 | 100 | Complete |
| May | 31 | 341 | 11.0 | 110 | Complete |
| June as of day 10 | 10 | 120 | 12.0 | 120 | Partial |

### The running total is valid and the comparison is not

The value 120 correctly describes the synthetic feed through the cutoff. Calling it a monthly collapse is wrong because the previous total had 21 more observation days. The dashboard changes the comparison, not the raw event count.

## Eight checks before a monthly change is displayed

The metric retains its cutoff, exposure, timezone, completion state and revision status.

| Check | Question | Failure if skipped | Safer action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Window start | When did counting begin? | Periods cover different starts | Store inclusive start |
| Observation cutoff | Through what instant is data loaded? | Partial looks complete | Display cutoff |
| Expected close | When will the period finish? | Remaining exposure is hidden | Mark partial status |
| Timezone | Which local day owns the event? | Midnight shifts records | Store zone and UTC time |
| Elapsed exposure | How many eligible days or hours? | Totals are compared directly | Calculate rate |
| Equal-window view | What happened through the same day? | Prior months get more time | Compare matched elapsed days |
| Late-arrival policy | Can closed totals change? | History updates silently | Version preliminary and final |
| Forecast label | Is the value observed or projected? | Assumption looks like fact | Separate views |

### Do not divide blindly by calendar day

If only business days or operating hours are eligible, the exposure denominator must follow that definition.

### Compare equal elapsed windows

Day ten this month can be compared with day ten in prior months using the same timezone and inclusion rules.

### Keep the raw total

The partial total remains useful when clearly labelled. It should not be erased merely because the comparison changes.

### Separate forecasting

A forecast may use trend, schedule or season assumptions. It never replaces the observed partial count.

### Record revisions

Late-arriving records can create preliminary and final versions. The interface shows which one is displayed.

### Test month boundaries

Automated cases cover 28, 29, 30 and 31-day months, leap years, timezone cutoffs and empty periods.

### What is the Al Batinah South partial-month project?

The learner audits a synthetic dashboard that compares ten elapsed days of June with all 31 days of May and reports a collapse in the monthly total. The repair exposes the observation cutoff, compares equal elapsed windows, calculates a per-day rate and keeps the unfinished month visibly marked as partial.

### Why does an unfinished month look worse than a complete month?

A running total has had fewer days in which to accumulate events. Comparing it with a complete prior month mixes different exposure windows. The current total may be lower even when the per-day rate is higher. Equal elapsed days or a clearly defined exposure rate are needed for a fair comparison.

### Should the learner simply forecast the rest of the month?

Not automatically. A forecast adds assumptions about future activity and may be inappropriate for operational reporting. The dashboard first reports the observed partial total, elapsed days and rate, then compares the same first ten days in prior months. Any forecast is optional, method-labelled and kept separate from observed data.
