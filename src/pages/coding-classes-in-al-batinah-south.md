## The Al Batinah South partial-month project

The learner opens a synthetic dashboard on 10 June. It shows 120 events for June and 341 for May. A raw change calculation reports that the monthly total fell by about 64.8 percent.

The arithmetic compares 120 with 341 correctly. The comparison is invalid because June has only ten observed days while May has all 31.

| Period | Observed days | Observed events | Events per observed day | First ten days | Status |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| April | 30 | 300 | 10.0 | 100 | Complete |
| May | 31 | 341 | 11.0 | 110 | Complete |
| June as of day 10 | 10 | 120 | 12.0 | 120 | Partial |

The running total of 120 is valid through the cutoff. It is not a complete-month total. The fix does not delete the value. It changes the label and comparison.

An equal-elapsed view compares the first ten days in every month. April has 100, May 110 and June 120 synthetic events. The per-observed-day rates are 10, 11 and 12. Both compatible views point upward.

This trap is about unequal exposure windows. It is not a seasonality exercise. The learner does not use a moving average or infer a recurring calendar pattern. The first repair is simply to compare like amounts of elapsed time.

The exposure denominator may be calendar days, business days, operating hours, eligible sessions or another documented unit. Dividing blindly by the calendar day number is wrong if activity cannot occur every day.

| Required field | Question answered | Failure prevented |
|---|---|---|
| Window start | When did counting begin? | Periods cover different starts |
| Observation cutoff | Through what instant is the feed loaded? | Partial looks complete |
| Expected close | When will the period finish? | Remaining exposure is hidden |
| Timezone | Which local day owns an event? | Midnight moves records |
| Elapsed exposure | How many eligible days or hours occurred? | Raw totals are compared |
| Equal-window cutoff | Which elapsed point matches prior periods? | Earlier months get more time |
| Revision status | Is the total partial, preliminary or final? | History changes silently |
| Value kind | Observed or forecast? | Assumption looks like fact |

The dashboard stores timestamps in a precise format and records the display timezone. Automated tests cover 28, 29, 30 and 31-day months, leap years, midnight boundaries and periods with no events.

A forecast is optional and separate. Projecting 120 events across the rest of June assumes a future daily pattern. That may be useful for planning, but it is not observed data. The interface labels the method, assumptions and uncertainty.

Late-arriving records create another issue. A total saved on the first day after month end may be preliminary. If it changes later, the data service retains the earlier version, revision time and final status.

The plain-language caption states that June has 120 observed events through day ten and cannot yet be compared as a complete month with May. It then gives the equal-day or exposure-rate comparison.
