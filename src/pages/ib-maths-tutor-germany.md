## Which IB maths course does Germany accept for university entrance?

The KMK, the standing conference of the Länder education ministers, first agreed in 1986 how an IB Diploma is recognised as a German university entrance qualification. The current version dates from 15 June 2023. These are its conditions, with the maths ones marked.

**Conditions for recognising the IB Diploma in Germany, KMK agreement as amended on 15 June 2023**

| Condition | What the agreement says | Maths? |
|---|---|---|
| Schooling | At least twelve years of full-time education in ascending year groups |  |
| Six subjects | Two languages, one natural science, one social science, a sixth from a listed set, and maths | Yes |
| Which maths | Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches or Mathematics: Applications and Interpretation | Yes |
| Higher Level | At least one of the three HL subjects must be a language, Mathematics, or Biology, Chemistry or Physics (from exam year 2025) | Can be |
| Continuity | Every subject studied for the two years of the Diploma Programme | Yes |
| Grades | At least IB grade 4 in all six subjects; one grade 3 can be balanced by a 5 in a subject of at least the same level, with 24 points in total | Yes |
| Maths at SL | From the 2021 exams, AA SL or AI SL alone opens only a restricted entrance, outside maths, science and technology subjects, unless the school is listed in Annex 1 or 2 | Yes |

Source: KMK, Vereinbarung über die Anerkennung des "International Baccalaureate Diploma/Diplôme du Baccalauréat International", Beschluss vom 10.03.1986 i.d.F. vom 15.06.2023, current German text with Anlage 1 as at 26 March 2026 and Anlage 2 as at 22 May 2026, read alongside the KMK's unofficial English translation, downloaded from kmk.org on 3 November 2026. Up to and including exam year 2024, the version of 24 March 2022 applies. Up to 2020 the agreement named the older courses, Mathematics SL or HL.

Read the table from the bottom up and one thing is clear: in Germany, maths is not just one of six subjects. It is the only subject the agreement names twice, once as a required subject and again in a footnote on its level. A pupil who chooses Standard Level maths in the first Diploma year is making a decision that the KMK text treats as consequential for what she can study later.

This is not legal or admissions advice, and we give none. The agreement is applied by universities and the Länder, and its text names an advisory committee of the KMK's Central Office for Foreign Education (ZAB) to review whether the IB still meets the criteria. If a choice of course matters for a particular degree, ask the university, and the school's IB coordinator, before the subject choice is fixed.

## Is IB Maths SL enough to study in Germany?

The footnote is short and easy to miss. Here it is in plain English, then what it means for a pupil choosing courses.

### What the footnote says

In the German text, footnote 3 says that graduates who present Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches SL or Mathematics: Applications and Interpretation SL from the 2021 exam session onward can be granted only a subject-restricted entrance qualification (fachgebundener Hochschulzugang), for Fachhochschulen and universities and only for subjects outside the mathematical, scientific and technical field.

The exception is graduates of the schools listed in the agreement's two annexes: for them the maths requirement counts as met and the Diploma opens general university entrance.

### What the annexes are

Annex 1 lists international schools that have a separate agreement with the IBO; in the current German file it is dated 26 March 2026 and has a section for schools in Germany (Deutschland). Annex 2 lists schools abroad with the bilingual German IB.

Whether your school is on the list is a question for your school, which will know. We do not reproduce the list here, because it changes and only the KMK's current file is authoritative.

For a pupil the practical upshot is a three-way choice. AA HL keeps the most options open and is the hardest course. AI HL is also Higher Level and suits a pupil whose strength is modelling and statistics rather than proof. A Standard Level course is a sound choice for many pupils, especially at a listed school or with a clear plan outside the sciences, but it is a choice to make knowingly, not by default.

### AA HL

Algebra, proof and calculus in depth. The heaviest load and the widest set of doors.

### AI HL

Modelling, statistics and technology-led problem solving, still at Higher Level.

### AA SL or AI SL

A lighter load. Check the footnote and your school's status before you settle on it.

## How is an IB score turned into a German grade?

German universities rank applicants by a single average grade, from 1.0 (the top) to 4.0. The KMK agreement gives a formula to turn an IB total into that grade.

The formula is N = 1 + 3 · (Pmax &minus; P) / (Pmax &minus; Pmin), where P is the IB total, Pmax = 42 and Pmin = 24. Bonus points are added into P, and a pupil with 42, 43, 44 or 45 points is given the top grade, 1.0. The agreement adds that the average is calculated in the country where the Diploma is issued. Put the numbers in and the formula simplifies to N = 1 + (42 &minus; P) / 6: each extra IB point improves the German average by 1/6, about 0.17.

That is a straight line, and a straight line can be read in both directions. Most pupils ask "what grade do my points give?" An IB maths pupil can ask the more useful question, "how many points do I need for the grade I want?", by inverting the function: P = 42 &minus; 6 · (N &minus; 1).

**Points to grade, and grade back to points** (Python)
```python
import math

def grade(points):
    if points >= 42:
        return 1.0
    return 1 + 3 * (42 - points) / (42 - 24)

def points_needed(target):
    return math.ceil(42 - 6 * (target - 1))

for p in [24, 30, 35, 36, 39, 44]:
    print(p, "points ->", round(grade(p), 3))
for n in [3.0, 2.3, 1.5]:
    p = points_needed(n)
    print("for", n, "or better: at least", p, "points, giving", round(grade(p), 3))
```
It prints 24 points -> 4.0, 30 points -> 3.0, 35 points -> 2.167, 36 points -> 2.0, 39 points -> 1.5, 44 points -> 1.0, then for 3.0 or better: at least 30 points, giving 3.0, for 2.3 or better: at least 35 points, giving 2.167 and for 1.5 or better: at least 39 points, giving 1.5.

**IB totals and the German average from the KMK formula (our calculation, unrounded)**

| IB total (with bonus) | Average grade N |
|---|---|
| 24 | 4.0 |
| 30 | 3.0 |
| 35 | 2.167 |
| 36 | 2.0 |
| 39 | 1.5 |
| 42 to 45 | 1.0 |

Formula and constants from the KMK IB agreement, as amended on 15 June 2023. The results are our own arithmetic. The agreement itself does not say how the result is rounded; the general KMK rules on overall grades for foreign qualifications apply, and we do not restate them here.

The inverted line has a trap, and it is the lesson. For a target of 2.3 the formula gives P = 42 &minus; 6 · 1.3 = 34.2. IB totals are whole numbers, and a higher total gives a better (smaller) grade, so the pupil needs at least 35, not 34: 34 points give 2.333, just outside the target. Rounding the wrong way is the mistake; the check is to put the answer back into the original formula.

## How do you work backwards in IB maths? Three practice tasks

Our own tasks in the style of IB papers, not taken from any IB exam. Each one starts from the target and works back to the input, then checks the answer in the original condition, as the grade example above did.

### AA style: an inverse and a log equation

**Task 1.** f(x) = (3x &minus; 1) / 2. Find f&minus;1(x) and the value of x for which f(x) = 7. Working back: write y = (3x &minus; 1) / 2, so 2y = 3x &minus; 1 and x = (2y + 1) / 3. Hence f&minus;1(x) = (2x + 1) / 3 and f&minus;1(7) = 15 / 3 = 5. Check: f(5) = 14 / 2 = 7.

**Task 2.** Solve log2x + log2(x &minus; 2) = 3. Working back: log2(x(x &minus; 2)) = 3, so x(x &minus; 2) = 8, x&sup2; &minus; 2x &minus; 8 = 0, (x &minus; 4)(x + 2) = 0. Check: x = 4 gives log24 + log22 = 2 + 1 = 3. x = &minus;2 is not in the domain, because log2(&minus;2) is undefined. Only x = 4 survives.

### AI style: a model read in reverse

**Task 3.** A phone battery at 18 % charges at a steady 2.5 percentage points per minute, so C(t) = 18 + 2.5t. When does it reach 80 %? Working back: 2.5t = 62, t = 24.8 minutes. Check: 18 + 2.5 · 24.8 = 80.

**Then question the model.** Real batteries slow down near full, so the straight line is honest only for part of the range. In an AI paper, saying where a model stops being reasonable earns marks of its own, and the internal assessment rewards it too.

**Why "check" is a step.** In task 2 the algebra produced two answers and only one was true. Working backwards finds candidates; the original condition decides.

1. **Write the target first.** f(x) = 7, a grade of 2.3, 80 % charge: name what you want before you rearrange.
2. **Undo the steps in reverse order.** The last operation done to x is the first one you undo.
3. **Put the answer back in.** Into the original equation, not the rearranged one, and reject what fails the domain or the context.
