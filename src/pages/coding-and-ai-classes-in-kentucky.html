Coding and AI classes in Kentucky

Is computer science required to graduate in Kentucky?

Not as a course. Under 704 KAR 3:305, every Kentucky graduate needs at least 22 credits and must demonstrate performance-based competency in technology, but computer science is not one of the ten foundational credits; it is an elective with its own state standards. Modern Age Coders teaches Kentucky students live online, from block coding to Python and AP Computer Science.

Kentucky draws a line that many states blur. Technology is a graduation requirement: a separate regulation says no student leaves a Kentucky public high school without meeting the state’s technology standards. Computer science, the part where students write programs, is optional, though any school that offers it must teach to the state’s computer science elective standards. This page walks through both regulations as the Legislative Research Commission publishes them, the latest access and enrollment counts, the state’s split between Eastern and Central Time, and a Python project that counts the words in the names of 110 Lexington parks.

The regulations

Technology is required for every Kentucky graduate; computer science is an elective

Kentucky’s graduation rules live in the Kentucky Administrative Regulations. We read them on the Legislative Research Commission site on 17 October 2026.

Three Kentucky regulations that shape computing in high school
RegulationWhat it saysIn force
704 KAR 3:305, graduationAt least twenty-two (22) credits, including ten foundational and twelve personalized credits, plus technology competency, civics and financial literacyLatest amendment effective July 6, 2026
704 KAR 8:090, technologyBefore graduating, a student shall meet the minimum content requirements of the Kentucky Academic Standards for Technology (August 2020 edition)Effective March 2, 2021
704 KAR 8:010, computer sciencePublic schools offering a computer science course or program shall meet the Kentucky Academic Standards for Computer Science Elective (October 2018 edition)Effective February 1, 2019

The ten foundational credits in 704 KAR 3:305 are English and language arts, social studies, mathematics (Algebra I and Geometry), science, health, physical education, and visual and performing arts. Computer science is not on that list. A student can still earn credit for it through the twelve personalized credits, which include six credits of academic and career interest learning experiences tied to the student’s individual learning plan.

Technology, not coding

The regulation asks each student to “successfully demonstrate performance-based competency in technology”. That is a demonstration of skill, not a set course, and it does not require writing code.

The elective standard

Kentucky does not order schools to offer computer science through these regulations. It says that if a school does offer it, the course must meet the state’s elective standards, so quality has a floor wherever the course exists.

A note on our sources

The Kentucky Department of Education site returned an access error to us on 17 October 2026. We quote only the regulation text published by the Legislative Research Commission and have not read the standards documents themselves.

Signature project

What word do Lexington park names share? Counting words in Python

Lexington-Fayette Urban County Government publishes its park inventory as open data. Students take the NAME column, split every name into words and count them.

Word counts in the names of Lexington’s 110 park records (our calculation, data downloaded 17 October 2026)
What we countedOur result
Park records, all with a name110
Words after splitting on anything that is not a letter284 words, 168 different
Names containing the word PARK91 of 110
Next most common wordsHILL 5, RUN 4
Names containing the letters h-i-l-l anywhere8
Names containing HILL as a whole word5

Split and count

Lower-case each name, cut it into words with a regular expression, and feed the words to a Counter. In five lines a student has a frequency table for a whole city’s park system.

Substring is not a word

Searching for “hill” as text finds 8 names, because it also catches SHILLITO PARK, WOODHILL PARK and MASTERSON HILLS PARK. Matching whole words finds 5. Students decide which question they were really asking.

Apostrophes bite

A naive split turns CLAY’S SPRING PARK into clay, s, spring and park, so a lone “s” shows up 3 times. Keeping the apostrophe inside the word fixes that; then SPRING and SPRINGS still count as two different words.

Whose numbers these are

Every count here is ours, from one download of the Lexington-Fayette Urban County Government park layer. The government’s terms of use say the data comes as is and may contain errors, and the inventory changes over time. A school report that used these figures would need to say so, and so do we.

The same file has a park-type column, which turns the project into a comparison. Our tally gives 45 neighborhood parks, 18 community parks, 11 special use sites and 11 natural areas. Does PARK appear less often in the names of natural areas? Answering that means grouping before counting, the step that turns a word list into an analysis.

Class times

Eastern Time in the east, Central Time in the west

The IANA time zone database places eastern Kentucky in US eastern time and western Kentucky in US central time, and gives the state two zones of its own.

Your schedule, your choice

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays.

Kentucky’s own zones

The database names America/Kentucky/Louisville for the Louisville area and America/Kentucky/Monticello for Wayne County, which it records moving from Central to Eastern Time in October 2000.

India time, worked out

By our own count, noon on a summer day in Louisville is 9:30 p.m. in India, and noon in a western Kentucky town on Central Time is 10:30 p.m. there. US clocks change on November 1, 2026 and March 14, 2027.

Why one county gets its own zone

If Wayne County had always been on Eastern Time it would not need a separate entry. Because it changed in 2000, a program that converts old timestamps there must know the history. That is the reason the tz database keeps a zone for a single Kentucky county, and a neat lesson in why programmers never hard-code offsets.

Coding and AI

Kentucky requires technology skill. Is AI skill the same thing?

Using a tool and understanding how it reaches an answer are two different abilities.

Ask an AI model how many Lexington park names contain the word hill and it might say 8 or 5, and both can be defended. Which one is right depends on whether you meant a substring or a whole word. A student who has written the counting code sees the ambiguity at once; a student who has only used the tool may never notice it exists.

Kentucky’s technology requirement makes sure graduates can use digital tools. Programming adds the layer underneath: knowing what the tool actually did. With that, AI becomes a fast assistant whose answers a student can check, instead of an authority whose answers they must accept.

The technology standard gets every Kentucky student to the door; writing code is how they look inside the machine. The longer argument is in the case for learning to code in 2026.

FAQ

Kentucky families ask

Answers grounded in Kentucky regulations and published data.

How many credits does a Kentucky diploma need?

At least twenty-two (22), under 704 KAR 3:305: ten foundational credits and twelve personalized credits, plus the technology, civics and financial literacy requirements.

Is computer science one of the Kentucky foundational credits?

No. The ten foundational credits cover English and language arts, social studies, mathematics, science, health, physical education, and visual and performing arts. Computer science is not among them.

What is the Kentucky technology requirement?

A graduate must demonstrate performance-based competency in technology, and 704 KAR 8:090 requires meeting the Kentucky Academic Standards for Technology before graduating.

Does Kentucky have computer science standards?

Yes. 704 KAR 8:010 incorporates the Kentucky Academic Standards for Computer Science Elective, October 2018, and public schools offering a computer science course must meet them.

How many Kentucky high schools offer computer science?

The Code.org 2025 sheet counts 270 of 358 Kentucky public high schools in 2024 to 2025, or 75.4%. That year 17,236 high school students were enrolled.

Which time zone do you use for Kentucky classes?

Eastern or Central, matching your part of the state. The tz database puts eastern Kentucky on Eastern Time and western Kentucky on Central Time.

What days and times can my student attend?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays.

Will your classes count toward a Kentucky diploma?

Not directly. Credit is a school and district decision. We teach the skills and projects, and anything graded at school is the student's own work.

Are you based in Kentucky?

No. Our teachers work from India and every lesson happens live over the internet; there are no Kentucky premises.
