## Why 13 is the age everything changes

The line was drawn by the tool providers, in their own terms of service. ChatGPT admits users from 13 with parental permission to 17. Google Gemini admits most 13 year olds, with higher floors in parts of Europe. Microsoft Copilot admits a 13 year old in the UK or US and refuses the same teenager in India, where its floor is 18. Claude and Replit sit at 18 everywhere. Before 13, a child cannot lawfully hold these accounts, which is why the under-13 track at Modern Age Coders uses Scratch and teacher-operated tools instead. The full sourced audit is at /ai-tools-age-guide.

From 13 the question changes from "is my child allowed" to "who will teach them to use this well". Untaught, the pattern is predictable: five half-working projects in a month, then abandonment at the first bug the model cannot fix from a vague description, usually on the fourth project, the first one the teenager actually cared about.

## The prompt on day one, and the spec in week six

Progress is visible in what the student types before the model writes anything. Day one: "make me a website where you can track your football team and it shows cool stats and looks good." A wish, answered with guesses. Week six, same student: one page, a match is date, opponent, goals and scorers, stored in localStorage, table newest first, totals above it, deleting a match updates the totals, refresh loses nothing. A specification, answered correctly on the first draft, because nothing was left to guess. Teaching a teenager to make those decisions on purpose is teaching software design.

## The twelve-week build map

- Weeks 1 to 2: a working quiz or converter, built live. Learning the loop itself.
- Weeks 3 to 4: a tracker that survives refresh. State and storage, the first place vibes fail.
- Weeks 5 to 6: a dashboard fed by a live public API. Data you do not control.
- Weeks 7 to 8: a polished rebuild of the weakest project. Reading your own old code.
- Weeks 9 to 11: the chosen project, scoped down hard by the teacher.
- Week 12: demo day to the batch, families welcome.

## The two-track week

Track one is the live AI-assisted session, where the teacher steers: three prompts deep in a loop, the student is asked to open the file and find the line that disagrees with what they asked for. Track two is build work with the AI switched off, small Python and JavaScript exercises, because reading fluency comes from writing and the whole method depends on reading. This is the vaccination against the seventy-per-cent plateau, where every project stalls at the part the model cannot navigate alone.

## Teen-specific traps the course handles

Midnight doom-prompting, cured with the three-failed-prompts rule. The influencer comparison, cured by real demo days with real timelines. Homework laundering, cured with a clean line: coursework is unassisted, AI is for projects school never asked for. Account roulette, cured by naming exactly which accounts a 13 to 17 year old may hold. The unread deploy with a visible secret key, cured by extracting a key from a deliberately broken demo app in week five. Quitting at the first stall, cured by competence rather than motivation.

## Frequently asked questions

**My teenager already uses ChatGPT constantly. Why a class?** Heavy use and skilled use are different; the gap shows the first time a project fights back. The class teaches what usage never does: reading, testing, versioning, and when to stop prompting and start thinking.

**Will it help with school CS?** It runs alongside school. Build work keeps a deliberate no-AI track because exams assess handwritten code.

**What exists after one term?** Three small finished builds plus one chosen project shipped and demoed. Recent examples: a football stats dashboard, a revision timetable generator, a recipe scaler.

**What does a parent consent to?** Enrolment, and tool accounts created per each provider's terms with your knowledge. No account is ever created behind a parent's back.

**Group or one to one?** Groups of five to eight suit most teens; one to one suits deadlines, outliers and quiet students. The free class settles it.
