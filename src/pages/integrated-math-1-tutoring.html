Integrated Math 1 tutoring: help with Integrated Math I

Integrated Math I is the first course of the integrated high school pathway. Instead of a whole year of algebra, a ninth grader meets linear and exponential functions, a unit of data and regression, and a unit of geometric congruence in the same course. Families who moved from a district that teaches Algebra 1 often find the order unfamiliar, and textbook help is harder to find. This page sets out what the course contains, in the states' own words, and teaches its central idea.

In short

Integrated Math I is the first course of the Common Core integrated sequence (Mathematics 1, 2 and 3), taught in states such as Utah, as Secondary Mathematics I, and North Carolina, as NC Math 1. What it is

What is Integrated Math 1, and how is it different from Algebra 1?

It is the same Common Core high school content as the traditional pathway, cut into courses a different way. Algebra 1 keeps geometry for a separate year; Integrated Math I mixes a share of it in from the start.

Two sequences, one set of standards

The Common Core's note on courses says its high school standards "do not mandate the sequence of high school courses." It names two model sequences: "a traditional course sequence (Algebra I, Geometry, and Algebra II)" and "an integrated course sequence (Mathematics 1, Mathematics 2, Mathematics 3)."

The same note says "The standards themselves do not dictate curriculum, pedagogy, or delivery of content." That is why course names and textbooks differ from state to state while the underlying standards match.

What the integrated order changes

A student in Algebra 1 spends most of grade 9 on equations, functions and quadratics. A student in Integrated Math I stops after linear and exponential functions, then studies data and congruence before quadratics arrive in Integrated Math II.

Neither order is easier. Moving between them mid-sequence is what causes trouble, because a transferring student can miss a topic or meet one twice.

Test makers follow the integrated names too. NWEA's MAP Growth features page says "Every license of MAP Growth includes tests for Algebra 1 and 2, Integrated Math 1, 2 and 3, and Geometry", so a district can measure growth inside the course itself.

The units

What topics are in Integrated Math 1?

Utah's core standards set the course out in six critical areas. They give a clear picture of a typical Integrated Math I year, even outside Utah.

Utah opens its course description with the purpose: "THE FUNDAMENTAL PURPOSE OF SECONDARY MATHEMATICS I is to formalize and extend the mathematics that students learned in the middle grades." It deepens linear relationships "in part by contrasting them with exponential phenomena", and "The final unit in the course ties together the algebraic and geometric ideas studied."

Functions as objects

In Area 2, Utah says students "move beyond viewing functions as processes that take inputs and yield outputs, and start viewing functions as objects in their own right."

That is the step that trips up many ninth graders. A student can plug numbers into f(x) = 3x + 2 yet still struggle to compare two functions given as a table and a graph.

Sequences hide inside functions

Utah's wording is that students "interpret arithmetic sequences as linear functions and geometric sequences as exponential functions."

Once a student sees that 7, 11, 15, 19 is a line sampled at whole numbers, half of the course's sequence questions become questions they already know how to answer.

How do you tell linear growth from exponential growth?

Look at what happens from one step to the next. Linear quantities change by the same amount; exponential quantities change by the same factor. These examples are ours, written for this page.

Integrated or traditional

Is Integrated Math 1 the same as Algebra 1?

Not quite. It covers much of Algebra 1 plus some geometry and statistics, and leaves quadratics for the next year. Utah's pathways chart lists what each integrated course carries.

Moving from Algebra 1 into Integrated Math

A student arriving after Algebra 1 usually knows quadratics already but may have missed congruence and descriptive statistics. Those units are where we start.

Utah notes that "Taking all three Extended courses is equivalent to taking Pre-Calculus", which matters for students aiming at calculus in grade 12.

Moving from Integrated Math I into Geometry

A student leaving after Integrated Math I has seen congruence early but not quadratics. A short bridge on factoring and graphing parabolas usually closes the gap.

Ask the new school for its course map before the first week, so the bridge can start straight away.

Integrated Math 1: common questions

What is taught in Integrated Math 1?

Integrated Math I usually covers expressions and equations, linear and exponential functions, systems, linear models of data with residuals, triangle congruence through rigid motions, and coordinate geometry. Utah's Secondary Mathematics I sets these out in six critical areas.

Is Integrated Math 1 the same as Algebra 1?

No, though they overlap a great deal. Integrated Math I shares most of the Algebra 1 work on linear and exponential functions but adds geometry and statistics units, and leaves quadratics for Integrated Math II.

Is Integrated Math 1 harder than Algebra 1?

Not by design. Both follow the same Common Core high school standards in a different order. Students sometimes find it harder because the course switches between algebra and geometry within one year.

What grade takes Integrated Math 1?

Most students take it in grade 9. Some take it in grade 8 as an accelerated course, and others begin in grade 10, depending on district placement.

Which states use Integrated Math?

Utah (Secondary Mathematics I to III) and North Carolina (NC Math 1 to 3) are two states whose documents we checked. Many districts in other states also offer an integrated pathway alongside or instead of Algebra 1, Geometry and Algebra 2.

Where can I find an Integrated Math 1 practice test?

Your child's textbook and teacher are the first place, since school tests follow them. This page has twelve practice problems we wrote, with answers. We do not reproduce state test items or publisher answer keys.

My child moved from Algebra 1 to Integrated Math. What will they have missed?

Usually congruence through transformations and descriptive statistics, and sometimes coordinate proofs. A few focused lessons on those units usually close the gap.

Can you check my child's Integrated Math homework answers?

We go through the thinking with the student and check that their method is right, but we do not hand over answers to graded work. The aim is that they can do the next problem alone.

When are your Integrated Math 1 classes?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays.

Are you connected with any state or textbook publisher?

No. Modern Age Coders is an independent online school, not affiliated with any state education department, district or publisher. We teach from the published standards and your school's course.
