# Ontario high school credits explained: what the OSSD asks for

The Ontario Secondary School Diploma, the OSSD, is built from credits, and the rules for which credits count depend on one fact: the year a student started Grade 9. This page sets out both rule sets from the province&rsquo;s own graduation requirements page, explains the STEM-related course group and the technological education credit that arrived for newer students, and shows where computer studies fits. It is information, not advice about any one student; the school&rsquo;s guidance office confirms individual plans. ## Two sets of rules, decided by the year Grade 9 began

The province&rsquo;s page opens with the sentence that matters most: &ldquo;What you need to earn an Ontario Secondary School Diploma depends on the year you started Grade 9.&rdquo; Students who started in 2023 or earlier follow one set of rules; students who started in 2024 or later follow another. Here they are side by side.

**OSSD requirements by the year a student started Grade 9, from ontario.ca (page updated September 02, 2026; read 22 October 2026)**

| Requirement | Started Grade 9 in 2023 or earlier | Started Grade 9 in 2024 or later |
|---|---|---|
| Compulsory credits | 18 | 17 |
| Optional credits | 12 | 13 |
| Literacy requirement | Yes | Yes, the provincial secondary school literacy requirement |
| Financial literacy requirement | Not listed for this group | Yes, the provincial financial literacy graduation requirement |
| Online learning credits | At least 2 | At least 2 |
| Community involvement | 40 hours | 40 hours |
| How the choice credits work | One credit from each of Groups 1, 2 and 3 | One credit from the STEM-related course group |
| Technological education | Group 3 option only | 1 compulsory credit, Grade 9 or Grade 10 |

### Reading the numbers

For the newer group, the page says: &ldquo;You need the following 17 compulsory credits to get your&rdquo; diploma, and &ldquo;You must earn 13 optional credits by successfully completing courses from your school&rsquo;s program and course calendar.&rdquo; We report the two parts as the page gives them.

For students who started in 2023 or earlier, the same page lists 18 compulsory and 12 optional credits. In the newer rules, the three group credits of the earlier ones give way to two lines, one technological education credit and one credit from the STEM-related group, which is why the compulsory figure is one lower and the optional figure one higher.

### What the change means for a family

A student who started Grade 9 in September 2024 or later must take a technological education credit in Grade 9 or 10, and one credit from the STEM-related course group at some point. An older sibling may have followed the earlier rules, so the two can have different checklists.

Guidance counsellors work from the student&rsquo;s own record. This page is a map of the rules, not a ruling on any one transcript.

## The 17 compulsory credits, one line at a time

The province lists the compulsory credits for the newer group in eleven lines plus the STEM-related group. The middle column is quoted from the page; the last column is our note.

### Two notes on English

English language learners may count up to 3 credits in English as a second language or English Literacy Development toward the 4 English credits; the page adds that the fourth credit must be a Grade 12 compulsory English course.

The same page lists particular English and arts courses that can meet those compulsory lines too. The school course calendar shows which of them a school offers.

### A note on half credits

Career studies and civics are worth half a credit each, which is why they appear as 0.5. Other courses can sometimes be offered as half credits too; the 2008 Computer Studies document sets at least 55 scheduled hours for each half-credit course in that subject.

Two half credits in the same subject make one credit. Our tally program further down handles half credits for exactly that reason.

## The STEM-related course group, and where computer studies fits

For the newer group, one compulsory credit is a choice from a list. The page puts it this way: &ldquo;Of the 17 compulsory credits, you must complete 1 from the following group:&rdquo;. The list is short, and three of its lines carry an important condition.

**The STEM-related course group, as listed on ontario.ca (read 22 October 2026)**

| Option | Condition on the page | Note (ours) |
|---|---|---|
| Business studies | None |  |
| Computer studies | None | ICD2O, ICS3U, ICS3C, ICS4U and ICS4C are the computer studies courses on the current Ontario curriculum website |
| Cooperative education | None |  |
| Mathematics | &ldquo;in addition to the 3 compulsory credits currently required&rdquo; | A fourth math credit |
| Science | &ldquo;in addition to the 2 compulsory credits currently required&rdquo; | A third science credit |
| Technological education | &ldquo;in addition to the 1 compulsory credit required&rdquo; | A second tech ed credit |

### What &ldquo;in addition to&rdquo; means

A math, science or technological education credit only counts toward the STEM-related group if it is extra: beyond the subject&rsquo;s own compulsory credits. The third math credit fills the math line; a fourth can fill the STEM-related line.

Business studies, computer studies and cooperative education have no such condition, because none of them has a compulsory line of its own. Any one full credit in computer studies can count toward the group.

### Computer studies and technological education are different subjects

The new compulsory technological education credit and the computer studies option in the STEM-related group are separate lines on the page. On the Ontario curriculum website, the five computer studies courses all carry codes beginning IC.

We found no source saying a computer studies course such as ICD2O meets the technological education credit, so we do not say it. If a family is unsure which subject a course belongs to, the school course calendar or the counsellor will know.

## The earlier rules: Groups 1, 2 and 3

Students who started Grade 9 in 2023 or earlier need 18 compulsory credits, and three of them come from three groups: &ldquo;Of the 18 compulsory credits, you must complete 1 from each of the following groups:&rdquo;. Computer studies sits in Group 3.

| Group 3 option | Note (ours) |
|---|---|
| Science (Grade 11 or 12) | The 2008 footnote calls it an additional course in science |
| Technological education | An option here, not a compulsory line |
| French as a second language | Counted under a limit across the groups |
| Computer studies | Any computer studies course |
| Cooperative education | Counted under a limit across the groups |
| American Sign Language as a second language |  |

### Limits across the three groups

The page sets two limits. French as a second language can count as a compulsory group credit at most twice, one from Group 1 and one from Group 2 or 3. Cooperative education also counts at most twice: &ldquo;You can count a maximum of 2 credits in cooperative education as compulsory credits from any of the above 3 groups.&rdquo;

The 2008 Computer Studies document described the same Group 3 option when it was written: &ldquo;To meet the Group 3 additional compulsory credit requirement, students have the choice of earning one credit for a course in computer studies (Grades 10 to 12) or technological education (Grades 9 to 12), or one credit for an additional course in science (Grade 11 or 12), or one credit for a cooperative education course.&rdquo;

### Math under the earlier rules

The earlier rules ask for &ldquo;3 credits in mathematics (at least 1 credit in Grade 11 or 12)&rdquo;. The newer rules are more specific, naming Grade 9 and Grade 10 math as well.

Under both sets of rules, students aiming at university computer science plan more math than the minimum, because programs such as Waterloo&rsquo;s and U of T&rsquo;s computer science list Grade 12 math courses by name. ### Two framings of financial literacy

The province&rsquo;s page describes financial literacy in two places. A summary list includes it for students who started Grade 9 in 2024 or later. A sentence further down speaks of students who began Grade 9 in the 2025 to 2026 school year or later, completing it in Grade 10 Career Studies, with the requirement taking effect in 2026 to 2027.

The 40 hours can begin in the summer before Grade 9, and school boards publish which activities qualify. The page also suggests that students under 18 plan their activities with their parents, and that the guidance counsellor can help.

Online learning credits are worth noting for families who already use online courses: they are a requirement, not just an option, for recent start years.

## How a computer studies course earns its credit

Families often ask what &ldquo;one credit&rdquo; actually means for a course like ICS3U. The 2008 Computer Studies document answers in three short rules.

### One course, one credit

The course table carries a note: &ldquo;Each of the courses listed above is worth one credit.&rdquo; That includes ICS3U, ICS3C, ICS4U and ICS4C.

### Fifty per cent

A credit is granted and recorded for every course in which the student&rsquo;s grade is 50% or higher, so the credit line sits at the bottom of level 1 on the achievement chart.

### Half credits

Most courses in the document may be split into half credits of at least fifty-five scheduled hours each. The Grade 12 university preparation course, ICS4U, may not.

### Who grants the credit

A credit is granted by the school or credit provider that teaches the course and evaluates the student: a day school, a board&rsquo;s night or summer program, or an Ontario credit provider. Those are the only places a credit can come from.

## Counting credits with code: a compulsory credit tally in Python

This program is ours, a teaching example of counting with conditions, for a made-up student. It is not a graduation audit and it simplifies the rules, as its comments say. It shows how a rule such as &ldquo;in addition to&rdquo; becomes a line of code. It was run in Python 3.13.

1. Store the minimums as data. One dictionary maps each compulsory subject to the credits it needs, including the half credits for career studies and civics.
2. Total the transcript. One loop adds up the credits per subject, so two half credits in one subject become one.
3. Compare each subject with its minimum. Anything short goes on the missing list, with how much is there and how much is needed.
4. Look for one spare credit for the STEM-related group. For each subject in the group, subtract its own minimum (zero for business, computer studies and co-op) and see if a full credit is left over. That subtraction is the &ldquo;in addition to&rdquo; rule.
5. Return the missing list. An empty list means every compulsory line is counted.

**Python · compulsory credit tally for a made-up student (ours)** (python)
```python
# Tally compulsory OSSD credits for a made-up student (ours, a teaching
# example of counting with conditions, not a graduation audit).
# Rules: ontario.ca, students who started Grade 9 in 2024 or later.
# Simplified: it ignores which grade each credit was earned in.

COMPULSORY = {"english": 4, "math": 3, "science": 2, "tech_ed": 1,
              "canadian_history": 1, "canadian_geography": 1, "arts": 1,
              "health_pe": 1, "french": 1, "career_studies": 0.5,
              "civics": 0.5}
STEM_GROUP = ["business", "computer_studies", "coop",
              "math", "science", "tech_ed"]   # math, science, tech_ed only beyond their own minimum

def tally(transcript):
    """transcript: list of (subject, credit) pairs. Returns totals."""
    totals = {}
    for subject, credit in transcript:
        totals[subject] = totals.get(subject, 0) + credit
    return totals

def check(transcript):
    totals = tally(transcript)
    missing = []
    for subject, needed in COMPULSORY.items():
        if totals.get(subject, 0) < needed:
            missing.append(subject + ": " + str(totals.get(subject, 0)) + " of " + str(needed))
    stem_ok = False
    for subject in STEM_GROUP:
        spare = totals.get(subject, 0) - COMPULSORY.get(subject, 0)
        if spare >= 1:            # a full credit left over after its own minimum
            stem_ok = True
    if not stem_ok:
        missing.append("STEM-related group: none yet")
    return missing


student = [("english", 1), ("english", 1), ("english", 1), ("english", 1),
           ("math", 1), ("math", 1), ("math", 1), ("science", 1), ("science", 1),
           ("tech_ed", 1), ("canadian_history", 1), ("canadian_geography", 1),
           ("arts", 1), ("health_pe", 1), ("french", 1),
           ("career_studies", 0.5), ("civics", 0.5)]

print(check(student))
student.append(("computer_studies", 1))     # ICS3U, say
print(check(student) or "all compulsory credits counted")
```
Output: first ['STEM-related group: none yet'], because the third math credit only fills the math line; then, with one computer studies credit added, all compulsory credits counted.

**Further test cases for the tally, all run (ours; each adds to the same made-up student before the computer studies credit)**

| Added to the transcript | Result | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| A fourth math credit | all compulsory credits counted | Extra math counts toward the group |
| A third science credit | all compulsory credits counted | Extra science counts too |
| Half a credit of computer studies | STEM-related group: none yet | A half credit is not yet a full credit |
| Two half credits of computer studies | all compulsory credits counted | Two halves make one |
| A co-op credit, but no French credit | french: 0 of 1 | The group cannot cover a missing compulsory line |
| Nothing at all | Every line listed as missing | No crash on an empty transcript |

### Where the program simplifies

It ignores grades: the real rules say which grade some credits must come from, such as English in each grade and technological education in Grade 9 or 10. It also adds credits per subject, so half a credit of business and half of computer studies would not count toward the group here, a case worth asking the school about.

It does not count optional credits or add anything up into a total, and it knows nothing about literacy, online learning or volunteer hours. Those are separate requirements.

### Why it is a good Grade 10 or 11 exercise

It uses a dictionary, a loop that totals, a comparison against a minimum and a condition with subtraction: the core of many school programming tasks, in one program a student can check against their own course calendar.

A good extension is to add the grade of each credit and enforce the grade rules. Another is to print the missing lines in a friendlier way. Both are honest practice in turning written rules into code.

## Questions to bring to a guidance appointment

Our list, for families who want to make the most of a meeting with the school. The school has the record and the course calendar; these questions help get the answers you need.

1. Which set of rules applies to my teen? It depends on the year Grade 9 began, so confirm it, especially for a student who changed schools.
2. Which compulsory lines are already met, and which remain? Ask for the record, not an estimate.
3. How will the STEM-related group or Group 3 credit be met? If computer studies is the plan, ask which course and in which year.
4. Which technological education courses does the school offer in Grade 9 and 10? For the newer group this credit is compulsory.
5. When will the OSSLT be written, and what are the options if it is not passed? The province&rsquo;s page says the principal can explain other ways to meet the requirement.
6. Which courses count as online learning credits here? Two are required for recent start years.
7. How are volunteer hours recorded? Boards have their own forms and lists of eligible activities.
8. For university plans, which Grade 11 and 12 courses does each program list? Then check those programs&rsquo; own pages.

## Ontario high school credits: common questions

### How many credits do you need to graduate high school in Ontario?

For students who started Grade 9 in 2024 or later, ontario.ca lists 17 compulsory credits and 13 optional credits. For students who started in 2023 or earlier, it lists 18 compulsory credits and 12 optional credits. Both groups also have literacy, online learning and community involvement requirements.

### What are the OSSD requirements for students who started Grade 9 in 2024 or later?

The page lists 17 compulsory credits, 13 optional credits, the provincial secondary school literacy requirement, the provincial financial literacy graduation requirement, at least 2 online learning credits and 40 hours of community involvement activities.

### What is the technological education credit?

For students who started Grade 9 in 2024 or later, one compulsory credit must be in technological education, in Grade 9 or Grade 10. For earlier students, technological education is one option in Group 3.

### What is the STEM-related course group?

For students who started Grade 9 in 2024 or later, one compulsory credit comes from a group of business studies, computer studies, cooperative education, or an extra credit in mathematics, science or technological education beyond those subjects' own compulsory credits.

### Does computer studies count toward the OSSD?

Yes. Computer studies is an option in the STEM-related course group for students who started Grade 9 in 2024 or later, and in Group 3 for students who started in 2023 or earlier. Each computer studies course in the 2008 curriculum is worth one credit.

### Does ICD2O count as the technological education credit?

We found no source saying so. ICD2O is a computer studies course on the Ontario curriculum website, and computer studies and technological education are separate lines on the graduation requirements page. Ask the school before planning around it.

### How many volunteer hours do you need in Ontario?

The page says everyone needs 40 hours of community involvement activities to get their diploma, and hours can be collected from the summer before Grade 9.

### Do you need online courses to graduate in Ontario?

Students who started Grade 9 in the 2020 to 2021 school year or later must earn at least 2 online learning credits, according to the page, which also describes an exemption process through the school board.

### When can students write the OSSLT?

EQAO says students can take the test starting in Grade 10. On 22 October 2026 it listed its next window as 3 to 30 November 2026.
