Ans. The short version

Bag of words class 10 AI is the method in CBSE 417 Unit 6 that turns text into numbers: normalise the corpus, list every unique word as the dictionary, then write each document as a row of word counts, called the document vector table. TFIDF then weighs each count. Following the CBSE handbook, IDF(W) = total documents / documents containing W, and TFIDF(W) = TF(W) x log10(IDF(W)), so a word found in every document scores 0. Q1. What is it?

What is Bag of Words in Class 10 AI?

Bag of Words is an NLP model that represents each document by how many times every word of the corpus occurs in it, ignoring grammar and word order. The name fits: the words are tipped into a bag and only counted.

The algorithm in four steps

  1. Normalise the text

    Clean all the documents together: split, tokenise, drop stopwords and symbols, lower the case, reduce words to their base.

  2. Create the dictionary

    List each unique word of the normalised corpus once. This list is also called the vocabulary.

  3. Make a vector for one document

    Under every dictionary word, write how many times it appears in that document.

  4. Repeat for every document

    Stack the rows and you have the document vector table, ready for TFIDF.

What the model gives, and what it loses

  • Gives: a fixed-length row of numbers for every document, which a machine can compare and learn from.
  • Gives: a quick view of which words dominate each document.
  • Loses: order. "Dog bites man" and "man bites dog" get the same vector.
  • Loses: importance. A common word gets a big count without telling documents apart, which is the problem TFIDF fixes.

Bag of Words and TFIDF are part of the theory half of Unit 6 in the 2026-27 curriculum, so a calculation on a small corpus is fair game for a four-mark question. The other Unit 6 topics, the stages, chatbots and applications, are answered on NLP class 10 questions and answers.

Q3. Count the words

The document vector table, and term frequency

A document vector table has one row per document and one column per dictionary word; each cell is the number of times that word occurs in that document. Those cells are the term frequencies, TF, that TFIDF needs.

Check 1: row totals

Each row should add up to the words left after cleaning: 8 for D1, 5 for D2, 4 for D3. If it does not, a word was missed or double counted.

Check 2: no empty column

Every dictionary word came from some document, so every column has at least one non-zero cell.

Presence or count?

Some textbook examples only mark 1 for present and 0 for absent. In a TFIDF question you need the true counts, so D1's dog is 2, not 1.

Q4. Weigh the words

TFIDF class 10: a term frequency inverse document frequency example

TFIDF scores a word high when it is frequent in one document but rare across the corpus. We use the CBSE handbook convention: IDF(W) = N / DF(W), written as a fraction, and TFIDF(W) = TF(W) x log10(IDF(W)), where N is the number of documents and DF(W) is how many documents contain W.

Reading the result

  • dog scores 0 everywhere, even with TF 2 in D1, because it is in all three documents. It behaves like a stopword for this corpus.
  • feed, wag and tail are D1's most telling words at 0.477 each: they appear nowhere else.
  • sleep and sun identify D3; follow identifies D2.
  • meera in D1 beats stray (0.352 against 0.176) only because it occurs twice there.

Q5. Practise the sums

TFIDF class 10 numericals, and the formula traps

Short calculations in the shape objective and short-answer questions take. Use log to base 10, as the handbook does, and round to three places.

  1. A corpus has 8 documents. "monsoon" occurs 3 times in one document and appears in 2 documents in all. Find its TFIDF in that document.
    Answer 3 x log10(8 / 2) = 3 x log10(4) = 3 x 0.602 = 1.806.
  2. In the same corpus, "is" appears in all 8 documents. What is its TFIDF in any document?
    Answer 0, because log10(8 / 8) = log10(1) = 0, whatever its TF.
  3. "glacier" appears once, in one document out of 8. Its TFIDF there is
    Answer 1 x log10(8 / 1) = 0.903, the largest value any single occurrence can reach in this corpus.
  4. "river" appears twice in a document and in 5 of the 8 documents. Find its TFIDF.
    Answer 2 x log10(8 / 5) = 2 x log10(1.6) = 2 x 0.204 = 0.408.
  5. Which has the higher TFIDF: a word with TF 1 found in 1 of 8 documents, or a word with TF 3 found in 2 of 8?
    Answer The second: 1.806 against 0.903. Rarity helps, but frequency in the document multiplies it.
  6. Name two applications of TFIDF.
    Answer Any two of: document classification, topic modelling, information retrieval (search), and stopword filtering.

ConventionWord in 1 of 3 docs, TF 1Use in the exam?

Natural log: TF x ln(N / DF)1.099No
Smoothed, as in scikit-learn: ln((1 + N) / (1 + DF)) + 11.693No

The handbook calls the fraction N / DF the IDF and applies the log in the TFIDF step. Many websites put the log inside IDF instead. With base 10 the final TFIDF is identical, so either layout earns the marks as long as you are consistent. A Python library run with default settings will not match the handbook, so never copy a library's output into a written answer.

Questions about Bag of Words and TFIDF

Bag of Words and TFIDF in Class 10 AI: common questions

What is bag of words in class 10 AI?

It is the NLP model that turns each document into a row of word counts. You normalise the corpus, list every unique word as the dictionary, then count how often each dictionary word appears in each document. Order and grammar are ignored; only counts matter. It is part of Unit 6 of CBSE 417.

What is the TFIDF formula in class 10 AI?

The CBSE handbook uses TFIDF(W) = TF(W) x log(IDF(W)), with IDF(W) = total number of documents divided by the number of documents containing W, and log to base 10. TF is the count of the word in that document, read from the document vector table.

What is a document vector table?

A table with one row per document and one column per dictionary word. Each cell holds how many times that word appears in that document. A single row is that document's vector, and the cells are the term frequencies used in TFIDF.

Why is TFIDF zero for some words?

If a word appears in every document, IDF = N / N = 1 and log10(1) = 0, so its TFIDF is 0 however often it occurs. Such a word cannot tell documents apart, which is why very common words behave like stopwords.

Which log is used for TFIDF in CBSE class 10?

Log to base 10. The handbook says so directly. On a calculator use the log key, not ln. Using the natural log gives larger numbers, for example 1.099 instead of 0.477 for a word in one of three documents. Check your calculator once before the exam: log10 of 100 should show 2.

Can you give a term frequency inverse document frequency example?

In a corpus of 8 documents, a word appearing 3 times in one document and in 2 documents overall has TFIDF = 3 x log10(8 / 2) = 3 x 0.602 = 1.806. A word in all 8 documents scores 0. The full three-document example on this page shows every table.

What are the text normalisation steps before Bag of Words?

Split the corpus into sentences, break them into tokens, remove stopwords and punctuation, put everything in lower case, then stem or lemmatise. State your stopword list first, because it decides what reaches the dictionary. Then show the cleaned text of every document, since the examiner checks your vector table against it, and a slip at this stage carries through every later table.

Do I write TF as a count or as a fraction of the document length?

For CBSE 417, as a count: the handbook reads TF straight from the document vector table. Some websites divide by the document length; that is a different convention and will not match the scheme. In our worked corpus, dog has TF 2 in the first document, not 2 out of 8, and that count is what you multiply by the log.

Is a TFIDF calculation asked in the board exam?

TFIDF is listed under text processing in the theory part of Unit 6 for 2026-27, and a small-corpus calculation suits a four-mark question. The 2026-27 sample paper for 417 is not out yet, so we cannot say exactly how it will be asked this year.
