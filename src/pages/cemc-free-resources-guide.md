# CEMC's free resources, and what each one is for

Many Canadian families know the University of Waterloo's Centre for Education in Mathematics and Computing for its contests. Fewer know that CEMC also publishes a whole shelf of free material: a weekly math problem at five grade levels, a monthly problem for strong senior students, online courses in math and computer science, a Python tutorial, ten years of past contests, and a tool that builds practice sets from old papers. This guide describes each one in CEMC's own words, says what it is good for and what it does not do, and ends with weekly routines that combine them for different grades.

At a glance: Publisher: CEMC, University of Waterloo; Cost: Free, no registration; Grades: 3 to 12; Weekly: Problem of the Week; Monthly: Problem of the Month; Python course: CS Circles; Classes: Weekdays or weekends, flexible; Start: A free first class. Rated 4.9 across 547 Google reviews.

### What "free" covers

CEMC's resources page says the materials are "free to use without the need for registration", and the Courseware page repeats: "No registration is required to access these resources." There is no account to make for most of them and nothing to pay.

The one exception in practice is the CCC Online Grader, which needs a school number to register; it belongs with the contests rather than the resource shelf.

### What free does not include

CEMC is honest about the limits. Its Courseware page says "there is no certificate or other form of credential awarded", "There is no feature that allows you to track your own personal progress", and "We do not provide additional instruction beyond the lessons and activities included."

In other words, the material is excellent, and the structure, encouragement and feedback are up to the family, the school or a teacher.

## Problem of the Week: five levels, every Thursday

CEMC describes the Problem of the Week as problems "e-mailed to educators for use with their students from Grades 3 to 12". Anyone can also open the current problems on CEMC's site or subscribe by email. CEMC adds: "New problems are sent every Thursday, and their solutions are sent one week later."

**Problem of the Week levels, from CEMC's Problem of the Week page (read 22 October 2026)**

| Level | Grades |
|---|---|
| Problem A | Grade 3/4 |
| Problem B | Grade 5/6 |
| Problem C | Grade 7/8 |
| Problem D | Grade 9/10 |
| Problem E | Grade 11/12 |

**The five Problem of the Week themes and their broad topics, as CEMC lists them (read 22 October 2026)**

| Theme | Broad topics |
|---|---|
| Algebra (A) | Patterning, relations, sequences, functions |
| Computational Thinking (C) | Logic, coding |
| Data Management (D) | Probability, statistics, counting |
| Geometry and Measurement (G) | Spatial sense, shape and space, trigonometry |
| Number Sense (N) | Numeration, numbers, number theory, financial literacy |

Themes run across every level, and CEMC notes that "A problem may have more than one theme." The Computational Thinking theme, with logic and coding as its topics, is the one that leads most naturally towards CEMC's computing contests.

### How to use it at home (our advice)

Thursday evening: print or open the problem at the student's level. Give it twenty minutes, then put it away. Come back on the weekend with fresh eyes.

The following Thursday, the solution arrives with the next problem. Compare the student's method with CEMC's, not just the answer. Then start the new one.

### Going one level up

A student who solves their own level comfortably can try the level above the same week. Because all five levels arrive together, this costs nothing and shows how the same theme deepens with age.

CEMC keeps an archive page with booklets of problems and solutions from the last two years, organised by theme, which suits a student who wants to work through one theme at a time.

## Problem of the Month, for strong senior students

The Problem of the Month is CEMC's stretch problem for older students. CEMC says it "is intended to challenge enthusiastic high school mathematics students" and that "Most problems are designed with the assumption that students attempting them have a solid understanding of Grade 11 math".

### The monthly rhythm

CEMC posts the first problem in September, then "a new problem will be posted on the first Tuesday of every month. A hint will follow roughly 10 days later, and a solution another 10 days after that."

That three-stage release is a gift for a student learning to persevere: ten days alone with the problem, then a nudge, then the full answer.

### Who should try it

Students in Grades 11 and 12 who enjoy the Euclid or the CSMC, and younger students CEMC describes as motivated. We suggest attempting it before the hint every month, and writing the solution out in full before reading CEMC's.

It pairs well with CEMC's CSMC and Euclid preparation material, which is also free and aimed at the same grades.

## CS Circles: CEMC's free Python course

Computer Science Circles, tagged on its own site as "[Python Programming Tutorials]", is the resource most relevant to a student heading for the Canadian Computing Competition. CEMC's Courseware page describes it as "a text-based introduction to programming in Python, intended for students with little to no programming experience."

**What CS Circles covers, from CEMC's CS Circles Courseware page (read 22 October 2026), with our note on why each matters for contests**

| Topics CEMC lists | Why it matters later (ours) |
|---|---|
| Variables, Functions, Types, Input | Reading a contest's input correctly |
| If Statements; Else, And, Or, Not | The conditions behind every early CCC Junior problem |
| Debugging | Finding the bug before the grader does |
| Defining a Function | Breaking a long solution into parts |
| Strings, Lists, Methods | The data most Junior problems are made of |
| Recursion | A tool for the last Junior question and many Senior ones |
| Efficiency | The idea that separates Junior and Senior marks |

### How the lessons work

CEMC says students "read simple explanations, run example code, and have an opportunity to practice what they have learned through coding exercises", some fixing existing code and some writing programs from scratch. Code runs in the browser, so nothing needs installing.

CEMC also notes: "All lessons are text-based, so there are no videos to watch." That suits readers well and video-learners less well.

### Where CS Circles ends

The course stops at recursion and efficiency, which is roughly where contest preparation begins. A student who finishes it can start on past CCC Junior problems, using the method in our practice guide.

What CS Circles cannot do is notice that a student has misunderstood something. That is the gap a teacher fills, and it is the one we are asked about most.

Our bridge exercise: Mirror Plates. To show the step from CS Circles to a first contest-style problem, here is a short exercise we wrote; it is ours, not CEMC's. Read N, then N licence plates. Ignoring spaces, count how many read the same backwards as forwards. Input: N, then N lines of capital letters, digits and spaces. Output: one integer.

**Mirror Plates · sample** (text)
```text
Sample input:
4
ABC CBA
HOCKEY
12 21
X

Sample output:
3
```

**Mirror Plates · our solution (Python)** (python)
```python
# Our bridge exercise: Mirror Plates (not a CEMC problem)
# Needs only input, a loop and string slicing: beginner Python.
n = int(input())
mirror = 0
for _ in range(n):
    plate = input().replace(" ", "")   # spaces do not count
    if plate == plate[::-1]:           # reads the same backwards
        mirror += 1
print(mirror)
```
Three of the four sample plates qualify: ABC CBA becomes ABCCBA, 12 21 becomes 1221, and a single letter is always its own mirror. The trap is the space: a plate such as "AB CBA" only reads the same backwards once the space is removed, as ABCBA. Every piece here (input, a loop, string methods, slicing) sits inside a first Python course.

## CEMC Courseware: eleven free courses, Grades 4 to 12

CEMC describes its Courseware as lessons, interactive activities, enrichment challenges and practice with immediate feedback, "Covering topics from Grades 4 to12 mathematics and computer science". We counted eleven courses on its list, six in math and five in computer science.

**The CEMC Courseware list with CEMC's grade tags (read 22 October 2026)**

### Ways CEMC suggests using it

For students, CEMC names self-paced learning and refresher material. For parents, it suggests keeping "current with the material their children are learning at school" and supporting learning at home with lessons and extra practice.

The two Grade 12 courses carry the titles of Ontario's university-preparation math courses, Advanced Functions and Calculus and Vectors, which makes them a natural companion for students taking those courses in any province.

### What to keep in mind

It is a resource, not a course you enrol in. CEMC says plainly that it should not be viewed as "a Massive Open Online Course", that no certificate is given, and that progress is not tracked.

So a student who works through it needs their own record of what is done. A notebook with dates and quiz scores is enough.

## Past contests and the Problem Set Generator

Two resources turn CEMC's contest history into practice. The archive holds whole papers; the generator mixes individual questions from four of the math contests.

**Contest practice tools, from CEMC's past contests and Problem Set Generator pages (read 22 October 2026)**

| Tool | What CEMC says | Use it when (ours) |
|---|---|---|
| Past contests archive | "Below you will find resources from the last 10 years." Papers, solutions and results, filtered by grade, year and contest | Practising a whole paper under time, then checking it |
| Problem Set Generator: Topic Generator | "Randomly chooses up to 30 problems according to contest, level of difficulty (Part A/B/C), and topic." | A student is weak on one topic, such as geometry, in the Gauss or PCF |
| Problem Set Generator: Contest Generator | "Randomly chooses 25 problems (one each at #1, #2, #3, and so on) according to the contest selected." | A fresh mock paper made of real past questions |

## Weekly routines built from CEMC's free resources

A shelf of good material helps only if it is used regularly. These routines are ours: about an hour a week of CEMC material for each grade band, in the order we would use it. Adjust freely.

**About an hour a week of free CEMC material, by grade band (our routines)**

| Grades | Weekly | Every few weeks | Before a contest |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 and 4 | Problem of the Week, Problem A | One Courseware lesson from Grade 4/5/6 math | None needed |
| 5 and 6 | Problem of the Week, Problem B | Courseware lessons on the current school topic | One past Beaver Computing Challenge paper |
| 7 and 8 | Problem of the Week, Problem C | A Topic Generator set of ten Gauss questions | Two past Gauss papers, timed |
| 9 and 10 | Problem of the Week, Problem D, plus one CS Circles lesson | A Contest Generator set for Pascal or Cayley | Past Pascal or Cayley papers, and past CCC Junior problems |
| 11 and 12 | Problem of the Week, Problem E | Problem of the Month, before the hint | CSMC and Euclid preparation material, past Euclid papers |

### Why one problem a week beats ten

The Problem of the Week works because it is a single problem with a week to think. Students who rush through a stack of problems learn the answers; students who live with one learn the method.

Keep a folder of finished problems with the student's own working, not just CEMC's solutions. Looking back through it in June is encouraging.

### Where a teacher adds most

In explaining why a method works, in spotting the misunderstanding behind a wrong answer, and in choosing the next problem at the right level. These are the three things CEMC's self-study resources cannot do, by design.

Our classes use CEMC's past papers in exactly this spirit, alongside problems we write ourselves, and we never charge for access to anything CEMC gives away.

## CEMC free resources: common questions

### Are CEMC's resources really free?

Yes. CEMC says there is no registration or fee required to access its tools and resources, and its Courseware page adds that no registration is needed. That covers the Problem of the Week, the Problem of the Month, Courseware, CS Circles, past contests and the Problem Set Generator.

### What is the CEMC Problem of the Week?

A free weekly math problem at five levels, from Grade 3/4 to Grade 11/12, sent to educators and posted on CEMC's site. New problems go out every Thursday, and the solutions follow one week later.

### Can students get the Problem of the Week without a teacher?

Yes. CEMC posts the current problems on its Problem of the Week page and offers an email subscription, so families can follow it at home.

### What is CS Circles?

Computer Science Circles is CEMC's free, text-based introduction to programming in Python, for students with little or no experience. It runs in the browser and covers topics from variables and if statements to recursion and efficiency.

### Is CEMC Courseware a course with a certificate?

No. CEMC says no certificate or other credential is awarded, progress is not tracked, and it gives no extra instruction beyond the lessons. It is free material to use at your own pace.

### Where are CEMC past contests?

On CEMC's past contests, solutions and results page, which covers the last ten years and can be filtered by grade, year and contest. The Problem Set Generator also builds random sets from past Gauss, Pascal, Cayley and Fermat questions.

### What is the Problem of the Month?

A monthly challenge for enthusiastic high school students, mostly assuming a solid understanding of Grade 11 math. A new problem appears on the first Tuesday of each month, with a hint about ten days later and a solution about ten days after that.

### Which CEMC resource should a beginner programmer start with?

CS Circles, which CEMC writes for students with little to no programming experience. CEMC's Courseware list also includes Python from Scratch and Language Independent Lessons for students who want another approach.

### Do you charge for CEMC material in your classes?

No. CEMC's resources are free and we never charge for access to them. Our fee covers live teaching, and we write our own practice problems for anything we publish.

### When are your classes for Canadian families?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Class times are set in Eastern time and converted for each province.
