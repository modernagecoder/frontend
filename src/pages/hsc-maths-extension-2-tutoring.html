In short

HSC Mathematics Extension 2 is a one-unit Year 12 NSW course, now set by the Mathematics Extension 2 11-12 Syllabus (2024). Its five areas of study are proof, vectors, complex numbers, calculus through further integration, and mechanics. Students must also take Advanced and Extension 1. The HSC paper is worth 100 marks over 3 hours plus 10 minutes reading time, and Extension 2 students sit the Extension 1 paper too.

Inside the course

Five areas of study in one Year 12 course

Unlike Advanced and Extension 1, Extension 2 has no Year 11 course in the 2024 syllabus. The structure below is NESA's, read on the NSW Curriculum site on 7 October 2026.

Mathematics Extension 2 11-12 Syllabus (2024): Year 12 structure
Area of studyFocus areaWhat it adds
ProofThe nature of proofFormal reasoning beyond induction

NESA lists the course as number 15260, worth 1 unit and 60 indicative hours. The enrolment rules show how tightly it is stacked: the prerequisites are Year 11 Advanced (11255) and Year 11 Extension 1 (11250), and the corequisites are Year 12 Advanced (15255) and Year 12 Extension 1 (15250). There is no shortcut into Extension 2.

The "What it adds" column is our own summary, not NESA wording, and the focus area names are the ones to plan revision around. Each of the five areas is substantial on its own, which is why Extension 2 feels heavier than its one-unit size suggests.

The 2024 syllabus "is to be implemented from 2026", according to NESA, with teaching of the new course from Term 4 of 2026 and the first HSC examination on it in 2027. A student starting Year 12 in that term is in the first cohort.

Students in the changeover year sometimes find that older worked examples cover topics in a different order or under different headings. The mathematics is still valuable; the topic list to trust is the one in the 2024 course structure.

The Extension 1 examination page adds the piece families often miss: Extension 1 students must also complete either the Advanced paper or the Extension 2 paper, depending on enrolment. For an Extension 2 student that means two papers, the Extension 1 paper and the Extension 2 paper, and no Advanced paper.

Two demanding papers in one exam period is a planning problem as much as a mathematics one. We build timed practice for both into the final term so that neither feels like a surprise.

The Extension 2 paper has the same shape as Advanced: 100 marks, three hours, 37 to 42 items in Section II. The difference is depth. A three-mark item can combine a vector argument with an integral, and the marks go to students who set out every step with reasons.

NESA's 2026 key dates put the HSC written exams between 13 October and no later than 5 November 2026. First-cohort students on the new syllabus will sit in 2027 and should check the 2027 timetable when it appears.

Introduction to complex numbers

Multiplying by i is a quarter turn

Complex numbers frighten students who meet them only as symbols. Drawn on the Argand diagram, where a + bi is the point (a, b), they become a way to rotate and stretch the plane.

A worked lesson, written by us. Start with z = 3 + 2i, the point (3, 2). Multiply by i again and again, using i squared = minus 1, and plot each result.

Repeated multiplication of z = 3 + 2i by i
ExpressionResultPointDistance from origin
z3 + 2i(3, 2)square root of 13
iz-2 + 3i(-2, 3)square root of 13
i squared times z-3 - 2i(-3, -2)square root of 13
i cubed times z2 - 3i(2, -3)square root of 13
i to the fourth times z3 + 2i(3, 2)square root of 13

The distance from the origin never changes, and after four multiplications the point is back where it started. Each multiplication by i has moved the point a quarter turn anticlockwise about the origin: (3, 2) to (-2, 3) is exactly the rotation (x, y) to (minus y, x). That single picture explains why i to the fourth is 1.

Now multiply by 1 + i instead. (3 + 2i)(1 + i) = 3 + 3i + 2i + 2i squared = 1 + 5i. The length grows from the square root of 13 to the square root of 26, a stretch by the square root of 2, and the angle grows by 45 degrees, from about 33.7 degrees to about 78.7 degrees.

Those two facts, lengths multiply and angles add, are the heart of the complex numbers area of study. They turn a page of algebra into a sketch a student can check by eye, and they reappear when roots of complex numbers are drawn evenly around a circle.

The same idea powers graphics and games: rotating a point on screen is multiplication by a unit complex number. Students who code can test the table above in a few lines of Python, which has complex numbers built in, and see the quarter turns for themselves.

Questions about Extension 2

What Extension 2 students ask us

What is HSC Mathematics Extension 2?

A one-unit Year 12 NSW course set by the Mathematics Extension 2 11-12 Syllabus (2024). It covers the nature of proof, further work with vectors, an introduction to complex numbers, further integration, and applications of calculus to mechanics.

Is there a Year 11 Extension 2 course?

No. Under the 2024 syllabus Extension 2 is a Year 12 course, and it needs Year 11 Advanced and Year 11 Extension 1 as prerequisites.

Which other mathematics courses must I take?

NESA lists Year 12 Mathematics Advanced and Year 12 Mathematics Extension 1 as corequisites for Extension 2.

How long is the Extension 2 exam?

The paper is worth 100 marks and runs for 3 hours plus 10 minutes reading time, with a reference sheet and approved calculators.

How many HSC mathematics papers do Extension 2 students sit?

Two: the Extension 2 paper and the Extension 1 paper. NESA states Extension 1 students also complete either the Advanced or the Extension 2 paper, depending on enrolment.

What is the Argand diagram?

A plane where the complex number a + bi is drawn as the point (a, b). On it, multiplying by i turns a point a quarter turn anticlockwise about the origin, and multiplying two complex numbers multiplies their lengths and adds their angles.

When does the new syllabus start?

NESA says it is implemented from 2026, with teaching from Term 4 of 2026 and the first HSC examination in 2027.

How does Extension 2 affect the ATAR calculation?

UAC explains that when Extension 2 is completed, Extension 1 counts as 2 units, and a completed Advanced result is not included. UAC's published rules are the authority; we do not advise on course choice for the ATAR.

Can you promise a top band?

No. We teach the mathematics and the written method; the outcome belongs to the student.

Will you help with school assessment tasks?

We teach the mathematics with our own problems and never write, edit or check anything that will be marked by a school.
