Online coding and AI classes in the Lima provinces

Huacho, Huaral, Chancay, Barranca, Cañete, Mala and the valleys up towards the Andes belong to Región Lima, not to the capital. Families here are counted separately, governed from Huacho and often far from Lima's academies. This page sets out what makes the region its own place, how quickly its homes came online in 2025, where school stops, and a data lesson built on a Huacho university's enrolment file.

Yes. Learners across Región Lima can join Modern Age Coders for coding and AI lessons that are live, online and in English. Not the capital

Is Región Lima the same as Lima city?

No. INEI counts Lima Metropolitana, the 43 districts of the province of Lima, as one unit and Región Lima as another. Región Lima is made of the nine provinces of Barranca, Cajatambo, Canta, Cañete, Huaral, Huarochirí, Huaura, Oyón and Yauyos, and its regional government sits in Huacho.

Why the difference matters

Search for coding classes "in Lima" and almost everything you find is in the capital: Miraflores, San Isidro, Surco. A family in Huaral or Mala is a long road trip from any of it.

The household survey shows the gap too. Homes in Región Lima are less likely than homes in the capital to have a computer or an internet service, though the gap is narrowing.

Where the regional government is

The Gobierno Regional de Lima lists its head office in Agua Dulce, Huacho, in the province of Huaura. Huacho is therefore the capital of Región Lima, even though it is a much smaller town than some neighbours on the census lists.

Families inside Lima Metropolitana have their own page with us: English-medium coding classes in Lima. This page is for everyone outside it.

The towns

INEI has not yet published 2025 figures for towns, so we use its 2017 directory of populated centres, which counts each centre separately: Huaral 79,455, Huacho 61,100, Barranca 58,749, Chancay 35,466, San Vicente de Cañete 31,219, Huaura 24,668 and Mala 22,325.

A town that runs on into the next centre is undercounted by this method, and we do not try to repair that by adding neighbours together.

Children and teenagers

Of the 970,550 people the 2025 census counted in person in Región Lima, 95,725 were aged 6 to 11 and 98,865 were 12 to 17, the ages of primaria and secundaria.

Many of them live in valleys and coastal towns where a specialised coding teacher is simply not available. Online, the town is irrelevant; only the connection and a keyboard matter.

School

Do schools in Región Lima teach programming?

Not as a timetabled subject. Schools in the nine provinces follow the national curriculum, the CNEB, which expects technology to be woven into every area rather than taught on its own.

The national rules

Law 28044 fixes six years of primaria and five of secundaria, and MINEDU assigns grades by age on 31 March. The CNEB's single technology competency, number 28, is about working in virtual environments and is shared across all areas.

Count the 35 weekly hours of a regular secundaria timetable and you will find Matemática and work education, but no slot that carries the word programming.

What the DRELP reported in November 2026

The regional education office, the DRELP, held its first regional congress on educational innovation in 2026, gathering more than 1,060 teachers from all nine provinces. It reported that Lima Provincias moved from 17th place in 2023 to fourth in the country in promoting educational innovation in 2026.

At the same event 140 teachers graduated from a diploma on emerging technologies applied to education, and 1,745 teachers were certified in a diploma on designing innovation projects. That is training for teachers; it does not add a coding class to the pupils' week.

Technical and vocational centres in the region are active too: in November 2026 the DRELP announced the winners of its third regional CETPRO contest on innovation and enterprise. The ministry's own coding scheme, Fábrica de Programadores, chose its first thousand Python pupils (July 2025) from Lima Norte, which lies inside the capital rather than the provinces. A teenager in Barranca or Huacho who wants serious programming still has to look beyond school.

A jump in one year

Home internet in Región Lima barely moved between 2022 and 2024, then rose from 50.4 to 58.8 per cent of homes in 2025, putting the region slightly above the national figure. Internet use by people aged six and over reached 83.8 per cent.

Computer ownership grew more slowly, to 32.2 per cent of homes. For a coding class the computer is usually the harder part to arrange.

Once a learner is about nine, a real keyboard on a laptop or desktop, because coding means typing and testing. Younger children doing blocks can manage on a tablet.

Language

Are classes taught in English or in Spanish?

In English, every time. Our teachers work from India on live video and adapt to the learner's level of English without switching to Spanish.

What to expect

In practice, many learners in the provinces are still building their English, so mentors keep sentences short and repeat key words.

Answers in Spanish are welcome; the mentor gives back the English phrase and carries on. Confidence usually grows within a few weeks.

A Huacho data project

How many new students start at a Huacho university each semester?

The Universidad Nacional José Faustino Sánchez Carrión (UNJFSC), whose offices listed on gob.pe are in Huacho, publishes six years of enrolment records as open data. Counting new students looks easy. The file's start date makes it a trap.

The dataset

The UNJFSC publishes "Matriculados de los años 2020 al 2025 a la UNJFSC" on datosabiertos.gob.pe under a Creative Commons Attribution licence (CC-BY), which permits reuse, commercial too, with credit. It was created on 23 December 2025. The university's gob.pe page says it has 13 faculties and 38 programmes, and the file has 38 programme names.

Our copy, matriculados_unjfsc_2020_2025.csv, has 136,218 rows: one per student per semester, from 2020-1 to 2025-2. Students appear only as a scrambled code, so nobody can be identified, and the CICLO column says which cycle of the degree each student was in that semester.

The question

How many new students joined the university in each semester? The obvious approach: for every student code, find the first semester it appears in, and call that student new in that semester.

Before running anything, notice that 136,218 rows does not mean 136,218 students. A student enrolled for six years appears up to twelve times. Adding up the semesters would count the same people again and again.

The 25,700 different codes is a sound figure. The 10,589 "new" students in 2020-1 is not: that is every single student enrolled that semester, because the file begins there. Someone in the fifth year of a degree in 2020 also appears for the first time in 2020-1, simply because the window opens then. Statisticians call this left censoring: the data cannot see what happened before its first date.

Version 2 uses the CICLO column as a second witness. A student whose first appearance is in cycle 01 really did start then. A student who first appears in cycle 05 was already studying, and the window just cut into their degree.

Now the picture changes. Only 586 of the 10,589 students first seen in 2020-1 were in their first cycle. The rest were already on course. By 2025-1, almost every first appearance really is a beginner: 1,460 starters against 66 others. The few "already further on" in later years are worth a question of their own, perhaps transfers or returning students, and we do not guess.

  1. Under 8, with a photo album. If the album starts on your sixth birthday, does that mean you were born that day? What is missing from the album?
  2. Block coding, around 8 to 10. Keep a list of names seen each week. Write a loop that marks a name "new" the first time it appears, then talk about the first week.
  3. Typing Python, about 10 to 12. Try Version 1 and explain in two sentences why 10,589 cannot be the number of new students in 2020-1.
  4. Ages 13 and over. 2022-2 has far fewer rows than the semesters around it. Write down three possible reasons, and what extra data would tell them apart.
  5. Going further. For students who started in cycle 01 in 2021-1, count how many still appear in 2025-1. State clearly why students who started in 2020-1 or before cannot be treated the same way.

Olympiads

Can secundaria students in Región Lima take part in the ONEM?

Yes, through their colegio, which runs the first stage. Modern Age Coders has no tie to MINEDU, the DRELP or the ONEM.

The ONEM in 2026

Colegios ran the first ONEM round of 2026 on the same Wednesday, 8 July 2026, splitting pupils into a younger, a middle and a final-year level. El Peruano reported more than 1.7 million pupils involved.

We never reuse the organiser's problems. We build the habits olympiads reward with problems we write.

A practice problem of ours

A club records members only from January. In January it lists 40 members, 5 of them joined that month. In February it lists 44, with 6 joining. How many members left in February, and how many of January's 40 joined before January? (Ours. Answer: 2 left, because 40 plus 6 minus 44 is 2; and 35 had joined before January.)

It is the enrolment project in miniature: what a list starting on a date can and cannot tell you.

What time would classes be in Huacho or Cañete?

Whenever your family decides. The provinces share Peru's single time zone, and your mentor builds the working day around the slot you book.

In the tz database, America/Lima sits five hours behind UTC and has observed no summer time since 1994; INACAL maintains Peru's official hour. India is UTC plus five and a half hours, giving a constant gap of ten and a half hours.

A slot booked in summer stays at the same local time all year. Families in the provinces often like an early-morning class, before school or work, which is late afternoon or evening for the mentor.

Questions from the provinces

Coding and AI classes in Región Lima: questions and answers

Are the classes in Spanish?

No. Every class is live, online and in English only. Mentors speak clearly and patiently, adapt to each learner's pace and accent, and answer a Spanish reply with the English words.

Is this page for Lima city?

No. It is for Región Lima, the nine provinces outside Lima Metropolitana, governed from Huacho. Families in the capital have a separate page about English-medium coding classes in Lima.

Do we have to travel to Lima for classes?

Never. Classes are live over video, so a learner in Huaral, Barranca, Cañete or a highland village in Yauyos joins from home.

When are the classes held?

Any day, any time, flexible: choose a batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at the hour that suits your family. India is ten and a half hours ahead, so 07:30 in Huacho is 18:00 for the mentor.

Do schools in Región Lima teach coding?

Not as a subject. The national curriculum spreads digital skills across all areas, and the regular secundaria timetable has no programming hours. The DRELP's 2026 work focused on training teachers in innovation and emerging technologies.

At what age can my child start?

From six with Scratch. Most children are ready for typed Python at nine or ten, and the free first class helps us judge readiness.

Can university students join?

Yes. Students at universities in the region often take Python, Java, databases or algorithms with us, in English, alongside a degree taught in Spanish.

Do you prepare pupils for the ONEM?

We train olympiad reasoning with problems we write, never official papers. Pupils enter through their colegio. We have no connection with MINEDU, the DRELP or the ONEM.

How are fees charged?

Monthly, by card, in US dollars, with your bank converting to soles. Someone viewing the page from India sees rupees. The first class is free and needs no card.

Do learners get a certificate?

Where a course includes one, learners receive a Modern Age Coders certificate of completion. It is our own record, not a Peruvian qualification, and it is not issued or endorsed by MINEDU, the DRELP or any school or university.
