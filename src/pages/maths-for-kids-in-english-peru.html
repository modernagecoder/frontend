Maths for kids in English, live from Peru

Children learn maths most easily by touching it: counters, strips of paper, rows of buttons on a table. Learning it in English adds one more layer, and young children usually cope with that layer far better than adults expect. This page is for parents of children aged 6 to 12 in Peru. It covers when a child can start, what primaria maths is trying to build, how a live class works for a small child, and one lesson you can try at the kitchen table tonight.

Starting age

At what age can a child start learning maths in English?

From 6, the age at which Peruvian children begin primaria. A child who is starting to read numbers and follow simple instructions can follow a live maths class in English, provided the class is built for six year olds.

What Peruvian law and MINEDU say

Under the Ley General de Educación, primaria is the second level of regular basic education and lasts six years. Its Reglamento, D.S. 011-2012-ED, says primaria serves children from the age of 6.

MINEDU's table of normative ages, measured on 31 March each year, places a child of 6 in 1st grade of primaria and a child of 11 in 6th grade. So "maths for kids" on this page means roughly the six primaria years, plus the first year of secundaria for twelve year olds.

What that means in our classes

We group children by age, not just by level: a six year old never shares a class with an eleven year old. The youngest groups use far more objects, pictures and gestures than words, and lessons are shorter in attention span if not in clock time.

Below 6, we usually suggest waiting, or a very gentle one-to-one start with a parent beside the child. From 9 or 10, many children can add a little typed Python to their maths, as in the lesson further down.

A common worry is that maths in English will confuse a child who is still learning to read in Spanish. In practice the opposite often happens: numbers and shapes look the same in both languages, and a child who already knows that 3 + 4 = 7 only needs the words "three", "plus" and "seven". The language arrives on top of maths the child can already see.

Primaria maths

What should a child learn in primaria maths in Peru?

The national curriculum, the CNEB, organises school maths around four kinds of problem rather than a list of tricks. That suits learning in English well, because each kind of problem can be shown with objects first.

Quantity, and change

Two of the CNEB's four maths competencies are about solving problems of quantity and problems of regularity, equivalence and change. In plain words: counting, operations, fractions, and spotting patterns and rules. Our arrays lesson below lives exactly here.

Shape, and data

The other two cover form, movement and location, and data and uncertainty: shapes, measuring, maps, simple graphs and "how likely is it?". Young children meet these through drawing, folding paper and counting things in a picture.

How much time school gives

The CNEB plan of studies gives maths five 45-minute periods a week in grades 1 and 2 and four in grades 3 to 6, which is 225 and then 180 minutes a week by our arithmetic. It gives English two periods a week in the first two grades and three afterwards.

The CNEB is frank that English in primaria depends on the school having an English teacher: the area is phased in "dependiendo de la contratación de estos docentes". So one child may reach a maths class with us having had several years of English at school, and another almost none. We place children by what they can follow, not by what the timetable says they should have had.

What the national test found

MINEDU's ENLA 2024 was a census test of 532,000 pupils in 4th grade of primaria, in 18,112 public and private schools. MINEDU reported a recovery in reading and maths, and for rural pupils a fall in the share at the lowest maths level, from 36.8% to 28.7%.

Those are national figures and say nothing about any single child. They do show that fourth grade is where Peru measures primary maths, which makes grades 3 and 4 a sensible moment to check that number sense is secure.

What we look for in a first class

Can the child count on from a number rather than from one? Do they know that 4 x 3 and 3 x 4 are the same, and can they show why? Can they say which of two fractions is bigger with a drawing?

Those three checks tell a teacher more than a worksheet of sums. They are also the ideas our arrays lesson is built on.

A lesson to try

How do you teach multiplication to a child in English? Arrays, counters and a little Python

This is one of our favourite lessons for children of 7 to 11. It begins with buttons or coins on a table and ends, for older children, with a few lines of Python. Along the way a child meets multiplication, its order, factor pairs and, without being told, prime numbers.

  1. Make a rectangle. Give your child 12 buttons and ask them to lay them out in neat rows, with every row the same length. Ask in English: "How many rows? How many in each row?"
  2. Say it as multiplication. Three rows of four is twelve. Write it as 3 x 4 = 12. The word "rows" and the word "of" carry the whole idea.
  3. Turn the table. Walk round to the other side, or rotate the paper. Now it is four rows of three. Nothing moved, so 3 x 4 must equal 4 x 3. A child who sees this never needs to memorise it.
  4. Find every rectangle. How many different rectangles can 12 buttons make? Write each one down: 1 by 12, 2 by 6, 3 by 4, and their turned versions.
  5. Try a stubborn number. Now use 7 buttons. However hard you try, the only rectangle is one long line. Ask: "Why does 7 not make a rectangle?"

From the table to the screen

For children of about 9 and up, we then draw the same arrays with Python. Version 1 prints rows of the letter "o" and multiplies. Running it with 3 and 4, then with 4 and 3, puts the turned table on the screen.

Version 2 asks the computer to do what the child did by hand: try every number of rows and keep the ones where the buttons come out even. The % sign gives the remainder, so a remainder of 0 means a neat rectangle.

What the child discovers

Twelve has six rectangles, counting turned ones. Seven has only two, both of them a single line. Sixteen has five, an odd number, because one of its rectangles is a square that looks the same when turned.

The last line of Version 2 lists every number below 30 whose only rectangles are lines. Children recognise the list as "the awkward numbers". The teacher then gives them their proper name: prime numbers.

Notice what the child did not do: memorise a definition. They built rectangles, noticed that some numbers refuse, and only then heard the word "prime". A prime is a number of counters that will only make a line. A child who learns it that way can explain it in English to a grandparent, which is the real test of understanding.

  1. Ages 6 to 7. Make rectangles with 6 buttons. How many can you find? Say each one in English: "two rows of three".
  2. Ages 8 to 9. Which numbers from 10 to 20 make a square as one of their rectangles? Draw them.
  3. Ages 10 to 12, Python. Change Version 2 to count the rectangles of every number up to 50. Which numbers have an odd count? What do they have in common?

What the table tells a parent

About two children in three aged 6 to 11 used the internet in 2026, against more than nine in ten teenagers. For a young child, a live class may be their first time using a computer for something other than videos or games.

That is fine, but it changes the first lessons. Our teachers spend part of the first class on the basics: where the microphone button is, how to raise a hand on screen, how to draw on the shared board.

What a parent does

With a child of six or seven, stay close for the first two or three classes, set up the camera and step back once the child is comfortable. From about 9, most children manage alone after a week or two.

Keep the class on one device in a shared room rather than behind a closed door. That is good practice for any child online, and it lets you hear the English your child is using.

In class

What happens in a live maths class for a young child?

Short activities, lots of talking and very little sitting still. A class for six year olds looks quite different from one for eleven year olds, and both look different from a school lesson.

Objects at home

Before class we may ask for twenty small things on the table: buttons, beans, pasta shapes. The teacher shows an idea on screen and the child copies it with real objects, then says what they did.

One word at a time

Teachers introduce English words slowly and repeat them often: "more", "less", "equal", "half", "rows". Each word is written on the board next to a picture, and the child says it back.

Short, varied tasks

Young children switch activity every few minutes: a counting game, a drawing, a puzzle, a quick story problem. The maths idea stays the same while the activity changes.

Children are never put on the spot for their English. A child who answers "seis" gets a smile and the English word, then a chance to say it. Within a few weeks most children reply in English without thinking, because the words are attached to things they can see and touch.

For older primaria children, the classes become more like problem solving. Story problems are read aloud, drawn as a picture, then solved and checked. A child of 10 or 11 is asked to explain one answer in a full English sentence each class, starting with "I know this because".

English level

My child speaks very little English. Can they still learn maths in English?

Usually yes, if the class is designed for it. Maths gives a child something concrete to hold on to while the language grows around it.

Why maths is a good first subject in English

Numbers, shapes and symbols are shared between Spanish and English. A child can follow a great deal of a maths lesson from what they see on the board, and the English words become labels for ideas they already have.

Peru's adults rate "Moderate" in English overall in the EF English Proficiency Index 2025, and Lima "High". Many children therefore hear some English at home already, even if they rarely speak it.

When we suggest one to one

If a child understands almost no spoken English, or is shy about speaking, a few one-to-one classes help. The teacher can slow down, use more gestures and wait longer for an answer without holding a group back.

Most children move into a group of their own age once they can follow simple instructions. The free first class is the easiest way to find out where your child stands.

Thinking, not copying

Will my child understand maths, or just learn tricks?

Understand. The arrays lesson shows how: the child builds the idea before anyone names it. Tricks come later, if at all, and only once the child can say why they work.

Why before how

A child who knows why 3 x 4 equals 4 x 3 has half the times tables for free. We teach that before asking anyone to recite anything.

No chatbots for children

Children do not use AI chatbots in our classes. Claude is for adults of 18 and over under Anthropic's terms, and ChatGPT needs a parent's permission for under-18s; a primary-age child has no need for either to learn maths.

The same teaching everywhere

A child in Lima, Arequipa, Piura or a small town with a steady connection gets the same teachers and the same lessons. Distance changes the clock, not the quality.

Peru times

What time would my child's maths class be?

At a time you choose. Peru never changes its clocks and sits at UTC minus 5; India sits at UTC plus 5:30. Add ten and a half hours to a Peru time and you have the time in India, on any date. The rows are conversions, not a timetable.

Maths for kids in English: questions and answers

Are the classes in Spanish?

No. Every class is live, online and in English only. Teachers speak slowly and clearly, adapt to each child's pace and accent, and show everything with objects and pictures, so a child who is still learning English can follow.

What age can my child start?

From 6, the age at which children in Peru begin primaria. The youngest groups use counters, drawings and very simple English. For children under 6 we suggest waiting, or a gentle one-to-one start with a parent nearby.

My child speaks almost no English. Is that a problem?

Not usually. Maths uses numbers and shapes that look the same in both languages, and teachers introduce English words slowly with pictures. A child with very little English may start one to one and join a group later.

What maths do the classes cover?

Counting, the four operations, place value, fractions, shapes, measuring, simple data and lots of reasoning puzzles. We teach alongside school and follow the stages children of each age usually reach, without replacing the school's own programme.

When can my child's classes be?

Any day, any time, flexible: choose a batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at the hour that suits your family. A class at 16:30 in Peru is at 03:00 the next morning for the teacher in India, and our teachers are used to that.

How long is a class, and is it too long for a six year old?

Classes for young children are broken into short activities of a few minutes each, with movement, objects and drawing. Teachers watch attention closely and change activity before a child tires.

Does my child need a computer?

A laptop, desktop or tablet works well. A phone screen is too small for drawing and moving things on the shared board. A headset helps a young child hear the teacher clearly.

Do I need to sit with my child?

Yes for the youngest, during the first few classes, to help with the camera and microphone. Most children of 9 and over manage on their own after a week or two.

Are you connected with MINEDU or my child's school?

No. Modern Age Coders is a separate, independent school teaching online from India. We have no link with MINEDU, a UGEL or any Peruvian school, and we give no Peruvian marks or qualifications. A child who finishes one of our courses gets a certificate of completion from us.

How are the fees paid?

With a card, in US dollars, once a month. The bank turns each charge into soles at its own rate, which is why the figure on a statement moves a little. Booking the free first class needs no card.
