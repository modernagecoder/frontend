CLEP College Algebra prep

The CLEP College Algebra exam lets a student show, in one 90-minute sitting, that they already know the algebra of a one-semester college course. Homeschool families use it to turn a strong algebra year into transcript credit; adults use it to skip a requirement they learned at work or years ago. Whether it earns credit is your college's decision, not College Board's. This page sets out what College Board says is on the exam and how it is scored, how to check a college's policy, and how to prepare, with examples and practice we wrote.

In short

CLEP College Algebra is a College Board credit-by-exam test covering a one-semester college algebra course: about 60 questions in 90 minutes, with a built-in TI-30XS scientific calculator. Scores run from 20 to 80, and the American Council on Education recommends 3 semester hours for a 50, though each college sets its own policy. The exam

How long is the CLEP College Algebra exam, and how many questions does it have?

College Board says the exam contains approximately 60 questions to be answered in 90 minutes, and that some of them are pretest questions that do not count.

CLEP College Algebra at a glance (clep.collegeboard.org College Algebra exam page and score pages, read 31 October 2026)
FeatureWhat College Board says
Content levelMaterial usually taught in a one-semester college course in algebra
LengthApproximately 60 questions in 90 minutes, some unscored pretest questions
Problem mixAbout 50% routine problems, about 50% nonroutine problems
CalculatorTI-30XS MultiView scientific (nongraphing), built into the exam software
ArithmeticThe test "places little emphasis on arithmetic calculations"
ScoringOne point per correct answer; nothing deducted for wrong or skipped answers
Score scaleA scaled score from 20 to 80
ACE recommendationCredit-granting score 50; 3 semester hours
RetestNot the same exam title within three months

Sources: CLEP College Algebra exam, Understand Scores and CLEP Exam Policies, read 31 October 2026. CLEP is a College Board trademark; we are not connected with College Board or CLEP.

Routine and nonroutine, half and half

College Board says "Nearly half the test is made up of routine problems requiring basic algebraic skills; the remainder involves solving nonroutine problems in which test takers must demonstrate their understanding of concepts."

Routine problems reward fluency: factoring, solving, simplifying. Nonroutine problems reward understanding: what a function's graph says about its inverse, or why an equation has no real solution.

Ninety minutes for about sixty questions

That is about a minute and a half per question on average, our arithmetic. Routine items should take less, which banks time for the nonroutine ones.

Because wrong answers cost nothing, College Board's advice is to answer every question. A blank can only score zero.

Topics

What is on the CLEP College Algebra exam?

Four topic areas with approximate weights. Functions are the largest single share, at about 30 percent.

CLEP College Algebra topics and approximate share of questions (College Board College Algebra exam page, read 31 October 2026)
Topic areaShareWhat it includes
Algebraic operations25%Operations with exponents; factoring and expanding polynomials; operations with algebraic expressions; absolute value; properties of logarithms
Equations and inequalities25%Linear, quadratic and absolute value equations and inequalities; systems of equations and inequalities; exponential and logarithmic equations
Functions and their properties30%Definition, interpretation and representation; domain and range; evaluation; algebra of functions; graphs (intercepts, symmetry, transformations); inverse functions
Number systems and operations20%Real numbers; complex numbers; factorials and binomial theorem

College Board adds that each test may contain "a variety of functions, including linear, polynomial (degree ≤ 5), rational, absolute value, power, exponential, logarithmic, and piecewise defined." It also says test takers are expected to be familiar with "currently taught algebraic vocabulary, symbols, and notation", so the notation itself, f(x), f inverse, i for the square root of -1, has to be second nature.

The calculator is a tool, not a crutch

The built-in TI-30XS MultiView is available "during the entire testing time", and College Board says students "are expected to know how and when to make appropriate use of the calculator."

It is nongraphing, so it will evaluate a logarithm or a power but will not draw the graph for you. Students used to graphing calculators should practise reading graphs from equations by hand.

Where students usually lose marks

Hold your own Algebra 2 notes up against the number systems list. Complex numbers, factorials and the binomial theorem are not part of every high school course, and inverse functions are easy to skim.

By College Board's weights, functions and number systems together make up about half the exam, which is why our plan gives them the most time.

Scores and credit

What score do you need on CLEP College Algebra to get credit?

The American Council on Education recommends 50, but your college decides. Check its policy before you register.

How the score is built

College Board explains that the raw score, the number of questions answered correctly, "is then converted to a scaled score that ranges from 20 to 80, and this is the score that appears on your score report."

For this exam, the American Council on Education (ACE) recommends a credit-granting score of 50 and 3 semester hours, but College Board notes that "individual institutions can set their own CLEP credit policies."

When you see it

For multiple-choice exams like College Algebra, College Board says "Scores are displayed on your test screen immediately following the CLEP test," and a test-taker copy is available in the My CLEP portal within 24 hours.

Colleges usually need an official score. College Board mentions two free score sends during registration, and says scores for exams taken on or after July 1, 2022 remain available for 10 years.

  1. Confirm your college grants credit for College Algebra. College Board tells students to read the CLEP policy on the college's website.
  2. Check the score it requires and the credit it gives. It may differ from ACE's 50 and 3 semester hours.
  3. Check that the credit counts toward your degree. College Board suggests consulting degree requirements or an academic adviser.
  4. Check for restrictions. College Board warns that "Some colleges won't grant credit for a CLEP exam if you've already attempted a college-level course closely aligned with that exam."
  5. Choose a test center or remote proctoring. College Board says remote exams have "the same timing, content, format, and on-screen experience" as a test center, but your computer and room must meet its requirements first.
  6. Use your score sends. Send the score to the college at registration while the sends are free.

Worked examples

Four college algebra problems, worked the way the exam rewards

Our own examples, one from each topic area. They are not CLEP items and do not reproduce College Board's sample questions.

Functions (ours): find the inverse of f(x) = (2x + 3)/(x - 1)
StepReasoning
1. Swap the rolesWrite y = (2x + 3)/(x - 1), then solve for x in terms of y
2. Clear the fractiony(x - 1) = 2x + 3, so xy - y = 2x + 3
3. Gather x termsxy - 2x = y + 3, so x(y - 2) = y + 3
4. Solve and renamex = (y + 3)/(y - 2), so f inverse (x) = (x + 3)/(x - 2)
5. Read the domainsf excludes x = 1 and never outputs 2; the inverse excludes x = 2 and never outputs 1
Algebraic operations (ours): evaluate log base 2 of 48 minus log base 2 of 3 without a calculator
StepReasoning
1. Use the quotient rulelog_b M - log_b N = log_b (M/N)
2. Simplifylog_2 (48/3) = log_2 16
3. Recognise the power16 = 2^4, so the value is 4
Number systems (ours): the x^3 term of (x + 2)^5, and (3 + 2i)/(1 - i)
ProblemReasoningResult
Binomial termChoose x three times from five factors: C(5, 3) = 10 ways, and the other two factors give 2^2 = 410 x 4 x^3 = 40x^3
Complex division, step 1Multiply top and bottom by the conjugate 1 + i(3 + 2i)(1 + i) / ((1 - i)(1 + i))
Complex division, step 2Top: 3 + 3i + 2i + 2i^2 = 1 + 5i; bottom: 1 - i^2 = 21/2 + 5/2 i

Questions learners ask

CLEP College Algebra: common questions

How many questions are on the CLEP College Algebra exam?

College Board says the exam contains approximately 60 questions to be answered in 90 minutes. Some are pretest questions that are not scored, and you cannot tell which ones they are.

What is a passing score on CLEP College Algebra?

CLEP has no single pass mark. Scores run from 20 to 80, and the American Council on Education recommends credit for a 50, but each college sets its own required score.

How many credits is CLEP College Algebra worth?

ACE recommends 3 semester hours for a score of 50. Your college may grant a different amount, or none, so check its CLEP policy.

Can you use a calculator on CLEP College Algebra?

Yes. A TI-30XS MultiView scientific calculator is built into the exam software for the whole test. It is nongraphing, and College Board expects you to know when to use it.

What topics are on CLEP College Algebra?

College Board lists algebraic operations (25%), equations and inequalities (25%), functions and their properties (30%) and number systems and operations (20%), including complex numbers and the binomial theorem.

Is CLEP College Algebra hard?

It covers a full semester of college algebra, and about half the questions are nonroutine. Students who are fluent through Algebra 2 and review functions and complex numbers usually find it manageable.

Is there a penalty for guessing on CLEP?

No. College Board says points are not deducted for wrong or skipped answers, so answer every question.

Can you retake CLEP College Algebra?

Yes, but not within three months of the earlier attempt. College Board says a retake inside that period is invalid, the score is canceled and fees are forfeited.

When are your CLEP College Algebra classes?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays.

Are you connected with College Board or CLEP?

No. We are an independent online school. We teach the published topics with our own problems and point you to College Board's free sample questions.
