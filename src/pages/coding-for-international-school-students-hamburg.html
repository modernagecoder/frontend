Coding for international school students in Hamburg

Hamburg has one member of the Association of International Schools in Germany, and its directory entry lists IB programmes and Cambridge Lower Secondary. Hamburg's state schools, meanwhile, now teach every pupil Informatik and set a central Abitur paper in it with no computer in the room. A teenager who moves between the two, or who simply wants computer science in English, needs to know how each route works. This page explains them, shows how we teach for both, and ends with a Python project on Hamburg's bike-share stations about the question every computer science exam asks sooner or later: how much work does your algorithm do?

Where Computer Science fits the IB rule

The KMK agreement that recognises the IB Diploma in Germany asks for a particular mix among the six exam subjects: two languages, a natural science from Biology, Chemistry or Physics, mathematics, and a social science. The sixth subject can come from a further list.

Computer Science is on that further list, alongside Design Technology and Digital Society. So a pupil who wants the German recognition takes Computer Science in addition to a natural science, not instead of one. Your IB coordinator is the person to confirm a subject plan.

Cambridge before the IB

AGIS lists Cambridge Lower Secondary for the Hamburg school but not IGCSE, so a Hamburg pupil who wants IGCSE Computer Science would be taking it through another school, privately, or after moving from another city.

For those pupils the 0478 syllabus is the reference, and it is set out in the next section. Everything we say about a particular school's offer comes from AGIS; the school's own pages are the authority on what it teaches in a given year.

Routes compared

How does computing differ between IB, IGCSE and the Hamburg Abitur?

All three teach the same core ideas. They differ in how many hours you get, what language you answer in, and whether a computer is allowed in the exam.

Hamburg is a paper-and-pen Land

The Informatik-Monitor says Hamburg's written Abitur papers in Informatik are developed centrally and are uniform across the city, and that no practical parts are currently allowed. The only aids are a calculator that cannot be programmed or draw graphs, and a spelling dictionary.

That surprises pupils from international schools, where code is often written and tested on a laptop all the way to the final project. A pupil joining a Hamburg Oberstufe has to learn to trace and write programs by hand, which is the skill IGCSE Paper 2 also rewards.

Hours and teachers

Hamburg is one of four Länder that meet the recommendation of four compulsory Informatik hours, and the Informatik-Monitor gives the share of Informatik teachers among all Hamburg teachers in 2025/26 as 5 percent. The GI itself says a share of at least 8 percent would be needed to teach six hours.

In other words, the subject is compulsory but specialist teachers are still scarce. A pupil who arrives from an international school with strong programming may find the class moves slowly; a pupil arriving with none may find the paper tasks hard. Both are reasons families look for help outside school.

A Hamburg data project

How much work does a nearest-station search do? An algorithm project on StadtRAD data

Hamburg's transport authority publishes the locations of its StadtRAD bike-share stations as open data. Finding the nearest station to each station is a classic exercise, and it teaches what IB and IGCSE call efficiency.

The data

The dataset StadtRAD-Stationen Hamburg comes from the Behörde für Verkehr und Mobilitätswende under the Datenlizenz Deutschland Namensnennung 2.0. On 3 November 2026 we downloaded it from the city's open API and saved it as stadtrad.json: 358 stations, each with a name and a longitude and latitude.

To measure the distance between two points on the Earth we use the haversine formula, with the Earth's radius taken as 6,371 km. It is a short function, and every distance in the project comes from it.

The question

For every station, which other station is nearest, and which station is the most isolated? The simplest algorithm compares every station with every other station. With 358 stations, how many distance calculations is that?

Pupils should guess before running anything. Most guess a few thousand. The real number is the point of the lesson.

Two lessons in one run. First the cost: 358 stations times 357 others is 127,806 distance checks. Double the stations and the checks roughly quadruple, which is what quadratic time means. Second, the answer is absurd: a station at Hamburg Dammtor station cannot be 6,013 km from its nearest neighbour. Its record in the file has the coordinates 0, 0, a point in the Atlantic off West Africa that mapping people call Null Island. The algorithm is right; the input is not.

The distance from A to B is the same as from B to A, so the inner loop can start just after the outer one. That halves the work to 63,903 checks, still quadratic but twice as fast, and it finds the two stations closest together, 82 m apart near Osakaallee in the Überseequartier. Leave out the station at 0, 0 and run Version 1 again on the 357 stations with Hamburg coordinates: the most isolated real station is S Rissen / Gudrunstraße, 3,813 m from its nearest neighbour, and the median distance to a nearest neighbour is 463 m.

Distance checks for the nearest-station search (our calculation)
MethodChecks for 358 stationsFormula
Every station against every other127,806n times (n minus 1)
Each pair once63,903n times (n minus 1), halved
Each pair once, for 716 stations255,970Twice the stations, about four times the checks

Data: Behörde für Verkehr und Mobilitätswende, via Transparenzportal Hamburg, StadtRAD-Stationen Hamburg, Datenlizenz Deutschland Namensnennung 2.0; downloaded 3 November 2026 through api.hamburg.de. Every count and distance is our own. The last row of the table is a calculation for a city with twice as many stations, not real data.

  1. IGCSE. Write Version 2 as Cambridge-style pseudocode with two nested FOR loops, and trace it by hand for four stations. How many times does the inner loop body run?
  2. IB. Explain in Big O terms why Version 2 is faster but not in a different class from Version 1. Then describe a grid-based method that would avoid comparing stations kilometres apart.
  3. Abitur. Add a validation step that rejects any station outside a rectangle around Hamburg, and write two test cases: one that must pass, one that must fail.
  4. Everyone. Before you run any program on a new file, plot the points or print the extremes. One look would have found Null Island.

Coding for international school students in Hamburg: questions

Are the classes in German?

No. All classes are live and taught only in English, by mentors who adjust to your pace and accent. IB and Cambridge exams are written in English anyway, and Hamburg Informatik loses nothing, because Python and Java keywords are English words.

Do you help with IB Diploma Computer Science?

Yes. The course is built on the IB guide first examined in 2027 and walks you through the internal assessment step by step. Exam details are not repeated here, since ibo.org was closed to us; ask your IB coordinator for the official version.

Does IB Computer Science count toward German university entry?

The KMK agreement on the IB Diploma lists Computer Science among the subjects that can be the sixth exam subject, alongside a required natural science from Biology, Chemistry or Physics. We do not give admissions advice; check your subject plan with your school.

Is a computer allowed in the Hamburg Abitur in Informatik?

No. The Informatik-Monitor 2026/27 says the written Hamburg Abitur in Informatik is developed centrally, the same across Hamburg, with no practical parts; only a non-programmable calculator and a spelling dictionary are allowed.

Is Informatik compulsory at Hamburg state schools?

Yes. Since 2025/26 four weekly hours of Informatik are compulsory at Stadtteilschulen and Gymnasien, spread over grades 7 to 10 by each school. A pupil moving from an international school may join classmates who already have that background.

Do you teach IGCSE Computer Science?

Yes. We follow Cambridge 0478 as valid from 2026 to 2028. Both papers last 1 hour 45 minutes and carry 75 marks; the second, on algorithms and programming, accepts answers in pseudocode or in Python, Visual Basic or Java.

Which school do your Hamburg students go to?

We never name schools as sources of students. The school on this page appears only because the AGIS directory lists its qualifications. Neither AGIS nor any school in Hamburg has a link with us.

What times are classes from Hamburg?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. A class at 16:35 in Hamburg is 21:05 for the mentor in India in winter.

What does it cost, and in what currency?

A month of eight live classes is one fee. We quote it in euro from the European Central Bank reference rate, take the payment in US dollars, and leave the conversion to your bank, which can shift the final figure a little. Your first class costs nothing.

Can you write my coursework for me?

No. We teach you to plan, build and test it yourself and explain the assessment criteria in plain English. The work you submit stays yours from the first line to the last.
