## How do German rules read the MYP and an IB Diploma with Computer Science?

Two short answers matter most to families who may stay in Germany. AGIS speaks for the MYP; the Kultusministerkonferenz speaks for the Diploma.

### The MYP and the Mittlere Reife

AGIS states that member schools offering the Middle Years Programme, or the IGCSE, make sure pupils receive "einen der Mittleren Reife gleichwertigen Abschluss": a qualification equal to the Mittlere Reife, the German intermediate school-leaving certificate.

For a pupil who might later move to a German school or into vocational training, that is the bridge. It does not depend on which subjects the pupil chose in the MYP, so computing in those years can be taken for its own sake.

### Computer Science in the Diploma

Under the KMK recognition agreement, a Diploma must include "ein naturwissenschaftliches Fach (Biology, Chemistry, Physics)", and Computer Science appears only as an option for the sixth subject. A pupil who wants German university entry needs a science in addition to Computer Science.

In the agreement's Anlage 1 of 26 March 2026, the International School of Dusseldorf is entry 27, one of the schools whose graduates with Mathematics at Standard Level can still receive general university access.

**Where computing stands after Klasse 10: IB Diploma against the NRW Oberstufe**

| Question | IB Diploma (KMK agreement) | Informatik in the NRW Oberstufe (Informatik-Monitor) |
|---|---|---|
| Can it stand in for a science? | No; it may only fill the sixth place | No, Informatik cannot replace the natural sciences |
| Hours | Set by the IB and the school | Einführungsphase three hours; Grundkurs three, Leistungskurs five |
| Written exam | Set by the IB | Central tasks; four per set, the teacher picks two (GK) or three (LK) |
| Computer in the written exam? | As the IB rules state | No; dictionary and scientific calculator or CAS only |

Sources: AGIS homepage (MYP and IGCSE equivalence, IB Diploma equivalence with prescribed subject combinations); Kultusministerkonferenz, Vereinbarung über die Anerkennung des IB Diploma (i. d. F. vom 15.06.2023) with Anlage 1 as of 26 March 2026; Informatik-Monitor 2026/27 of the Gesellschaft für Informatik, NRW page. The NRW written Abitur in 2027 runs from 13 April to 5 May 2027. We describe the rules; we give no admissions advice.

The interesting point is how similar the two systems are here. Neither lets computing replace a laboratory science, and the NRW written Abitur in Informatik is done on paper, without a computer, with the pupil reading and writing code by hand. A Diploma pupil who practises explaining an algorithm in words and tracing code on paper is preparing for the habits both systems reward.

## Rain on the Rhine promenade: which weather column should you trust?

Düsseldorf publishes hourly bike counts from its permanent counters together with the weather for each hour. Before asking whether rain keeps cyclists away, an IB pupil must answer a quieter question: the file describes the weather twice, so which description is right?

### The data

The Landeshauptstadt Düsseldorf publishes the Wetterabhängige Jahresübersicht der Dauerzählstellen Radverkehr 2025 through Open.NRW with a Datenlizenz Deutschland Namensnennung 2.0 licence, one file per counter, recorded hour by hour. We use the counter at Mannesmannufer on the Rhine, which we fetched on 3 November 2026.

Each row has a time, two direction counts, a weather label such as "Leichter Regenschauer", a temperature, and rain in millimetres written with a decimal comma.

### The question behind the question

There are two ways to decide whether an hour was rainy: the label says so, or the millimetre column is above zero. If they agreed, it would not matter which we use. Version 1 counts how often they disagree, and also tests the label against something we know for certain.

Prediction first: how many hours of 2025 should have rain, and should both columns give the same number?

**Version 1: test the two weather columns** (Python)
```python
import csv

rows = []
with open("Mannesmannufer_IN_OUT_2025.csv", encoding="utf-8-sig") as counts:
    reader = csv.reader(counts, delimiter=";")
    next(reader)
    for when, south, north, label, temp, rain in reader:
        rows.append((int(when[11:13]), int(south) + int(north), label, float(rain.replace(",", "."))))

label_rain = [r for r in rows if "regen" in r[2].lower()]
measured = [r for r in rows if r[3] > 0]
print("Hours in the file:", len(rows))
print("Label says rain:", len(label_rain))
print("Rain measured:", len(measured))
print("Label says rain, 0 mm measured:", len([r for r in label_rain if r[3] == 0]))
print("Sonnig between 00:00 and 03:59:", len([r for r in rows if r[2] == "Sonnig" and r[0] < 4]))
```
It prints: Hours in the file: 8759, Label says rain: 2122, Rain measured: 1359, Label says rain, 0 mm measured: 833 and Sonnig between 00:00 and 03:59: 627.

The two columns disagree badly. On 833 hours the label mentions rain while the gauge shows 0 mm, and 627 hours between midnight and four in the morning are labelled "Sonnig", sunny, which cannot be literally true. The label reads like a general forecast symbol for the hour, "sunny" meaning a clear sky; the millimetre column is a measured amount. Going the other way, 70 hours have measured rain under a label without the word, most of them snow labels, which is fair: snow is precipitation too. For a question about rain, the measured column is the one to use, and the label is better left out.

With that settled, the comparison can be fair in a second way too. Cyclists ride far more at 17:00 than at 03:00 whatever the weather, so Version 2 compares wet and dry hours at the same time of day.

**Version 2: wet against dry, hour by hour** (Python)
```python
def average(values):
    return sum(values) / len(values)

for hour in [8, 13, 17, 21]:
    wet = [r[1] for r in rows if r[0] == hour and r[3] > 0]
    dry = [r[1] for r in rows if r[0] == hour and r[3] == 0]
    print(f"{hour:02d}:00 wet {average(wet):.1f} dry {average(dry):.1f} ratio {average(wet) / average(dry):.2f}")
```
Run after Version 1. It prints 08:00 wet 182.1 dry 230.0 ratio 0.79, 13:00 wet 128.1 dry 194.1 ratio 0.66, 17:00 wet 202.0 dry 283.6 ratio 0.71 and 21:00 wet 56.5 dry 89.6 ratio 0.63.

**Average bikes per hour at Mannesmannufer in 2025, hours with and without measured rain (our calculation)**

| Hour | Rain measured | No rain | Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| 08:00 | 182.1 | 230.0 | 0.79 |
| 13:00 | 128.1 | 194.1 | 0.66 |
| 17:00 | 202.0 | 283.6 | 0.71 |
| 21:00 | 56.5 | 89.6 | 0.63 |

Data: Landeshauptstadt Düsseldorf, Wetterabhängige Jahresübersicht der Dauerzählstellen Radverkehr 2025, file Mannesmannufer IN OUT, licence DL-DE BY 2.0, retrieved 3 November 2026. The file has 8,759 hourly rows; the hour that does not exist is 02:00 on 30 March 2025, when the clocks went forward. The comparisons, averages and ratios are ours, with both directions added together.

At every hour we checked, rainy hours carry clearly fewer bikes, roughly two thirds to four fifths of the dry-hour level, and the morning commute at 08:00 holds up best. That is a correlation in one year at one counter, not proof of why; a pupil writing it up should say so, and should report that the weather label was tested and set aside.

1. **MYP.** List three facts you know for certain about any weather data (no sun at midnight, snow is cold) and write one check for each.
2. **Diploma, SL.** Rewrite Version 1 so it reports every label with how many of its hours had 0 mm, sorted by the share. Which labels are most often wrong?
3. **Diploma, HL.** Repeat Version 2 for all 24 hours and plot the ratio. Is the morning commute really different, or is it the noise of a small sample?
4. **Going further.** Download a second counter from the same dataset. Do the two files carry identical weather columns, and what would that tell you about where the weather comes from?

AGIS states that member schools offering the MYP or the IGCSE make sure pupils receive a qualification equivalent to the Mittlere Reife. For an individual case, ask the school; we describe the published position and give no advice on recognition.
