Live online, explained

How does a live online Python class work from Oberhausen?

You need a computer, a headset and a link. Everything else happens in the class.

  1. Before the first class. You receive a video link and a short checklist. No software needs to be bought; Python is free.
  2. In the first class. The mentor shares their screen and installs Python and a code editor with you, step by step, on your own computer. You write and run a first program before the hour is over.
  3. In every class after that. You share your screen while you type. The mentor can see each line as it appears, which is the whole point: mistakes are caught in seconds rather than discovered alone at midnight.
  4. Between classes. A short practice task, sized to finish in twenty or thirty minutes. Your code is looked at together at the start of the next class.
  5. Every few weeks. A small project of your own, explained back to the mentor. If you can explain it, you have learned it.

What you see

Your own editor, the mentor's face in a corner, and whichever screen is being shared. In a group, other learners' screens appear when they share to ask a question, and those moments teach the whole group, because everyone watches a real mistake being fixed.

What you hear

Clear English at a calm pace. The mentor reads code aloud, line by line, and asks you to do the same. Reading code aloud is one of the quickest ways to find a bug, and it builds the vocabulary of programming at the same time.

What you do not do

Watch for an hour. A class where the learner only watches is a video with extra steps. In our classes the learner's hands are on the keyboard for most of the hour, and the mentor talks less than you might expect.

The mentors are in India and the lessons are in English only; we do not teach in German. Oberhausen families sometimes worry about that and then find it matters less than expected, because Python itself is written in English words and everything said is also shown on screen. Mentors slow down, repeat, and adjust to each learner's accent within the first few classes.

A real Oberhausen project

Can one Python function read five Oberhausen visitor files?

The city of Oberhausen publishes how many people visited its high-ropes course, its swimming pool, the LVR industrial museum, the short film festival and the theatre. One function should be able to read them all. Should.

The data

The Stadt Oberhausen publishes these visitor figures through Open.NRW under the Datenlizenz Deutschland Namensnennung 2.0, which allows any reuse, including commercial, with the source named. The files were last updated on 30 September 2026; we downloaded the five CSV files on 3 November 2026 and gave them short names, such as hallenbad.csv and theater.csv.

Each file has one row per year and a column called "Besucher*innen insgesamt", total visitors. The files cover different spans: the high-ropes course since 2006, the swimming pool since 2008, the museum from 2000 to 2021, the festival since 2000 and the theatre since the 2004/2005 season.

The plan

Write one function, visitors(path), that opens any of the files and returns a dictionary from year to total. Then loop over the five files and print each one's peak and its latest figure.

A beginner would copy the reading code five times. That works until something changes, and then it must be fixed five times. A function is written once and tested on every file.

Four files work; the theatre breaks the program. The traceback's last line, KeyError: 'Jahr', says the dictionary for a row has no key called Jahr. Open theater.csv and the reason is in the first line: the theatre counts seasons, not years, and its first column is called "Spielzeit Theater Oberhausen", with values such as 2016/2017.

There are two ways to fix it. The tempting one is to copy the function, call the copy theatre_visitors and change one word. The better one is to notice what all five files share: the period, whatever its name, is always the first column. So the function should ask the file for its first column name instead of assuming it.

Programs and table are ours. The files carry a footnote column with the city's own notes on particular years. We are not connected with the city or the venues.

The table also hides a trap for the careless reader. "Latest" for the museum is 2021, four years older than the others, because that file stops there. A sentence like "all five venues are below their peak" is true, but comparing the museum's latest figure with the swimming pool's would compare different years. A good program prints the period next to every number, which ours does, and a good programmer reads it.

  1. Ages 10 to 12. Without running anything, guess which line of Version 1 fails on theater.csv. Then run it and check.
  2. Teenagers. Add a third column to the output: the percentage of the peak that the latest year reached. Round to one decimal place.
  3. Adults. The festival figures from 2007 to 2019 end in 000 or 500 much more often than the others. Count how many, and decide what that suggests about how they were measured.
  4. Everyone. Write a sixth file of your own with a different first column name and check that Version 2 reads it without any change.

Online Python classes in Oberhausen: answers

Are the Python classes taught in German?

No. We teach in English only. The mentors are based in India and keep a calm pace, rephrase when needed and adapt to how each learner speaks; since Python is made of English keywords, the language of the class and of the code is the same.

Is it really live, or a video course?

Really live. A mentor is on the call for the whole hour and watches the learner's shared screen while they type. There are no recorded lessons standing in for the mentor.

What do I need at home in Oberhausen?

A laptop or desktop computer, a headset or quiet room, and a steady internet connection. Python and the code editor are free and installed with the mentor in the first class.

Do NRW schools teach Informatik?

Yes, in grades 5 and 6 every pupil at a general school has the compulsory subject Informatik. From grade 7 it is an elective that depends on the school type, and in the Oberstufe it can be a three-hour Grundkurs or, in the Qualifikationsphase, a five-hour Leistungskurs.

From what age can someone learn Python with you?

From about 10 in our Maths Through Coding course, from 9 in Python and AI for Kids, and at any age as a teenager or adult. Younger children usually start with Scratch.

When can I take classes in Oberhausen time?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Pupils tend to take late afternoons and adults evenings.

What is the visitor project on this page?

An exercise we wrote on five visitor files published by the city of Oberhausen. One Python function reads four of them and fails on the theatre file, whose first column is a season rather than a year. Fixing the function, not copying it, is the lesson.

Can adults learn Python from scratch?

Yes. Our adult courses assume no experience. Many adults in the Ruhr area want Python to automate office tasks, and they usually write their first useful script within the first month.

How many people are in a batch?

Between fifteen and twenty learners at a similar stage and age. One-to-one is the alternative: two private classes a week with a mentor of your own.

How is the monthly fee paid?

The fee is shown in euro at the European Central Bank reference rate and paid in US dollars, converted by your bank, so the booked amount may differ a little. The trial class costs nothing and needs no card.
