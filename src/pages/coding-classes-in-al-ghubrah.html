Local education evidence

Indian School Al Ghubra is a specific community, not a generic keyword

The Board of Directors' profile supplies the facts below. They describe the district audience and do not imply a partnership or endorsement.

Founded for Indian expatriate families

The Board of Directors for Indian Schools in Oman says Indian School Al Ghubra was founded in July 1990 by Dr P Mohamed Ali, primarily for children of Indian expatriates in Oman. It describes the school as private, independent and co-educational.

The profile records more than 3,250 students, over 200 faculty members, 13 expatriate administrative staff and 31 Omani administrative staff. These counts show a large, mixed operating community. They still cannot tell a teacher which of those learners has written code outside a lesson.

Kindergarten through Senior Secondary

The same profile places the school from Kindergarten to Senior Secondary and states its affiliation to the Central Board of Secondary Education in New Delhi. It names Science and Commerce streams in Classes 11 and 12.

Senior subject combinations include Computer Science, Informatics Practices, and Multimedia and Web Technology alongside sciences, economics, engineering graphics, mathematics, accountancy and business studies. A Modern Age Coders route can deepen a technical strand, but it remains external to CBSE and the school.

Languages and facilities widen the context

The Board profile lists Malayalam, French, Arabic, Sanskrit and Hindi as second and third-language choices from Grades 6 to 10. It also names smart classrooms, an audio-visual room, laboratories and a 3D printer, plus sports, art, music and hobby clubs.

That range is a reason to ask what the learner has actually used. Access to a laboratory does not prove fluency with experimental design; a listed computer subject does not prove independent debugging. The free class turns opportunity into observable evidence without judging the school.

A wider Indian-school admissions network

The Board's capital-area admissions page places Indian School Al Ghubra in a central online-admission system alongside six other named Indian schools in the Muscat area. That makes transfers and school allocation part of the family context, but not part of external course placement.

The official profile also says the school has been a Round Square member since 1996. Dates, memberships and affiliation are verifiable attributes. Phrases such as best, guaranteed or perfect are not evidence and do not belong in a placement decision.

Al Ghubrah mathematics and code project

From 4% to 6%: two points, fifty percent, one honest report

The example is synthetic. It is not an examination score, patient statistic or claim about any Al Ghubrah institution.

Keep the counts beside the rates

Practice period A records 20 successes in 500 attempts. Period B records 30 successes in 500 attempts. The rates are 4% and 6%. Because the denominators match, the absolute increase is also visible as ten additional successes.

old_rate = 20 / 500  # 0.04
new_rate = 30 / 500  # 0.06

point_change = (new_rate - old_rate) * 100  # 2
relative_change = ((new_rate - old_rate) / old_rate) * 100  # 50

The percentage-point change subtracts two rates and expresses the result on the percentage scale: 6% minus 4% equals 2 percentage points. The relative percentage change divides that difference by the old 4% baseline: 2 divided by 4 equals 50%.

Why both answers need labels

"Up two percent" is ambiguous and should be rejected. A reader cannot tell whether it means a two-percent relative increase or a move of two percentage points. "Up 50%" is mathematically correct as relative change, but it sounds dramatic without the underlying 20-of-500 and 30-of-500 counts.

The learner must publish the baseline, new value, denominators, absolute counts and time window. If the denominators change, those differences matter too. If a sample or definition changes, even correctly calculated changes may not be comparable.

Preferred sentence

The synthetic success rate rose from 4% (20 of 500) to 6% (30 of 500), an increase of 2 percentage points or 50% relative to the earlier rate.

Questions

Direct answers for Al Ghubrah learners

Can Indian School Al Ghubra students join these classes?

Yes. The Board of Directors for Indian Schools in Oman says the school is CBSE-affiliated from Kindergarten to Senior Secondary and serves more than 3,250 students. Modern Age Coders is independent of the school. A free live task checks the individual learner's coding or mathematics level instead of treating a class number or examination stream as proof of skill.

Do the classes support CBSE Computer Science or Informatics Practices?

They can develop programming, database and analytical skills that complement those subjects. The school's official profile lists Computer Science, Informatics Practices and Multimedia and Web Technology among senior options. Modern Age Coders does not claim CBSE affiliation or replace school teaching. Placement identifies the exact gap, and examination-specific priorities remain with the learner's school.

What is the Al Ghubrah percentage-points project?

It uses invented data: 20 successes among 500 attempts is 4%, while 30 among 500 is 6%. The change is 2 percentage points, and it is also a 50% relative increase from the 4% baseline. Learners calculate both, preserve the denominators and choose wording that cannot make the improvement sound larger or smaller than it is.

Is Modern Age Coders located in Al Ghubrah?

No. Modern Age Coders is an Indian provider teaching Al Ghubrah families live online. There is no Muscat classroom, Omani branch, street address or local telephone number. The published +91 number reaches the real provider through WhatsApp or telephone. A family can evaluate the teacher and connection in the free class before making a payment.

Why do official sources use both Al Ghubrah and Al Ghubra?

They are English spelling variants for the same Muscat district. This guide uses Al Ghubrah in its title, URL and service schema. Indian School Al Ghubra uses its registered school spelling, so that name is preserved when referring to the institution. Neither spelling implies a different physical class location because the Modern Age Coders lessons are online.

Can classes work around Class 10 or Class 12 preparation?

Possibly, but school examinations take priority. The free class can narrow the goal to one programming, database or mathematics weakness, recommend a lighter pace, or conclude that the learner should wait. Modern Age Coders does not promise a board-examination mark and will not present an unrealistic compressed course as a substitute for school revision.

Are classes available for adults in Al Ghubrah?

Yes. The taught age range is 6 to 67. Adults can begin with web development, Python, databases, automation, data analysis or mathematics after the free placement class. The teacher asks for a concrete outcome such as a report, application or repetitive task. Completion does not grant credit from an Omani, Indian or other external institution.

What do online coding classes in Al Ghubrah cost?

A group of five to eight learners costs USD 100 per month for eight live lessons. Private teaching with one learner and one teacher costs USD 150 for eight lessons. Both formats normally meet twice each week. The first live class is free, and Modern Age Coders adds no enrolment fee, compulsory materials purchase or minimum term.

Does the data project use Indian School Al Ghubra results?

No. The 4% and 6% example is completely synthetic and is not an examination result, health rate or performance claim about any local institution. School facts and the practice dataset are kept separate. That separation lets learners study percent change without attributing invented numbers to students, teachers, patients or residents of Al Ghubrah.

What equipment is needed for an Al Ghubrah learner?

Use a laptop or desktop with a microphone and stable video-capable internet. Bring a recent coding file, school exercise or mathematics solution if available.
