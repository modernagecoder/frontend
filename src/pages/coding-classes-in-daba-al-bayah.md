Daba Al Bayah today A town shared between three administrations Most ambiguous place names are accidents of language. This one is a fact of government, which makes it far harder to clean up and far easier to demonstrate. Three parts, three authorities The Wilayat of Dibba is politically divided into three parts: Dibba Al Fujairah ruled by the Emirate of Fujairah, Dibba Al Hisn ruled by the Emirate of Sharjah, and Dibba Al Baya ruled by Oman. Three governments means three statistical offices and three sets of definitions. The way into Musandam Dibba is described as the gateway to the Musandam peninsula. Being a gateway means traffic passes through in both directions, which is precisely the kind of record most likely to be attributed to the wrong side. An old capital The wilayat was the symbol of an ancient civilisation in the northern part of the Sultanate and its first capital. Long history under changing authority is how one name ends up meaning several things. A name is a label and a key is a promise A label describes something. A key promises that exactly one thing answers to it. Storing a place by name is treating a label as if it were a key, and the moment two real places share that label the promise was never true and nothing in the data ever said so.

Is the three-way division real? Yes. The Wilayat of Dibba is politically divided into Dibba Al Fujairah under the Emirate of Fujairah, Dibba Al Hisn under the Emirate of Sharjah and Dibba Al Baya under Oman. Only the Omani population of 10,416 from the 2020 census is a sourced figure on this page; the two Emirati numbers used in the project are illustrative and labelled as such.
Why can this not be fixed by cleaning the data? Because nothing is dirty. The strings are spelled correctly, the records are accurate and the places are real. Trimming, casing and fuzzy matching all leave it exactly where it was. The only fix is an identifier that is not the name, decided before the data is collected.
Is there a Modern Age Coders centre in Daba Al Bayah? No. No premises are claimed in Daba Al Bayah or anywhere in Musandam. Classes are live online and need a laptop or desktop, working audio, a current browser and a stable connection. The page describes where the service reaches, not a building.
Which of the four answers is correct? Whichever one the question meant, and the question did not say. That is the point. A system holding places by name cannot distinguish them, so the honest response is to return all three candidates and ask, rather than to pick one and look confident.
Is this only for schoolchildren, or can working adults enrol? Both, with 67 as the upper limit. The adult route runs through Python, databases and reporting. Joining two systems on a company name, a branch name or a city name puts anybody in exactly this position sooner or later. If the tables are yours to share, they beat anything we could invent.
What comes out of this project? Three things: places keyed by code instead of by name, an authority and a source pinned to every figure, and a lookup that raises on an ambiguous match rather than quietly choosing one. They travel into whatever system the learner works on next.
