Are English-taught online classes a good match for learners in Apurímac?

Often for teenagers and adults; less often, at first, for young children from Spanish-speaking homes. All our classes are live and online and taught in English, never in Spanish, so English and access to a computer are the first things to weigh.

English: what is published

EF's English Proficiency Index 2025 reports a score of 519 for Peru, moderate on its scale, and lists scores for fifteen regions. Apurímac does not appear on that list, so no regional English figure exists to quote.

Inglés takes three hours a week in each grade of the regular secundaria timetable, so teenagers here have studied it for several years. How much of it they can follow in a live lesson differs from pupil to pupil, which is what the free class finds out.

Learners who usually find their feet

Secundaria pupils who are keen on English or maths. Students at universities and technological institutes in Abancay and Andahuaylas, who already deal with English in software. Adults who read English in their work.

Children of six to nine can learn with us as well, usually in private lessons. The mentor slows right down, shows rather than tells, and lets a young child answer by doing.

The free class is also an honest assessment. The mentor sets a simple task in easy English and watches how the learner manages. If English stands in the way for now, we will tell you and suggest private lessons, a later start or a different course. Spanish-speaking parents can read a guide to coding classes for children written in Spanish, and learners curious about doing both at once can see coding and English together.

An Apurímac data project

How far are Apurímac's secondary schools from Abancay or Andahuaylas?

Measured as a straight line on the globe, about a third of active secundaria entries in the regional school list are more than 50 km from both towns. Getting there needs a formula with sines and cosines, and one small unit mistake turns the answer into nonsense.

The list

The Gobierno Regional de Apurímac publishes "Instituciones Educativas de Apurímac" on datosabiertos.gob.pe and describes it as the list of the region's educational institutions "elaborado a partir del padrón del Ministerio de Educación". It was released on 30 October 2026 under the Open Data Commons Open Database License (ODbL), which allows reuse, commercial included, with credit, and asks that adapted databases are shared on the same terms.

Our copy, ie-apurimac.csv, has 4,113 rows with a cut-off of 26 October 2026: 2,846 active and 1,267 inactive, 3,258 rural and 855 urban. Every row has a latitude and longitude. School names are in the file, and this lesson does not print them.

The method

Latitude and longitude are angles. The usual way to turn two of them into a distance over the Earth's surface is the haversine formula, which uses sines, cosines and the Earth's radius of about 6,371 km.

We need a point for each town. As a simple, stated choice of ours, we take the average position of the list's rows in Abancay district and in Andahuaylas district. It is not the exact town centre, and for straight-line distances of tens of kilometres it is close enough.

Nothing crashed, and the answer is impossible. The rows in the file span only about one and a half degrees of latitude, under 200 km from north to south, so no school can be almost 7,000 km from Abancay. The reason is units: math.sin and math.cos in Python expect radians, and Version 1 gives them degrees. Sin of 13.6 degrees is about 0.24, but math.sin(13.6) treats 13.6 as radians and returns about 0.86. The program is not wrong in a way the computer can see; only a person who asks "is this possible?" will notice.

Version 2 converts all four angles with map(math.radians, ...) before any trigonometry. The farthest row from Abancay is now 145 km away, which fits the size of the region. It then looks only at the 355 active secundaria rows, measures each to both towns and keeps the smaller distance with min(). Half of them are within 34.5 km of the nearer town in a straight line, and 122 are more than 50 km from both. Roads in the Andes wind, so the real journeys are longer than these lines; the numbers are a floor, not a travel time.

  1. For ages 6 to 8. On a map of your town, stretch a string from home to school, then lay it along the streets you really walk. Which is longer, and why?
  2. Ages 8 to 11, with blocks. Make a sprite move from one point to another and count its steps along x and y. Is the straight path shorter than going across then up?
  3. Ages 11 to 13. Print math.sin(30) and math.sin(math.radians(30)). Which one is 0.5, and what does the other one mean?
  4. Teenagers. Add a third reference town, such as Chalhuanca in Aymaraes, using the same averaging method. How many of the 122 rows come within 50 km?
  5. University and adults. Compare the haversine result with a flat-map approximation that multiplies degrees by 111 km. How large is the difference at Apurímac's latitude?

Asking "could this possibly be right?" before trusting a number is one of the most useful habits in programming, and in using AI tools. It is the habit our page on building with AI without copying keeps returning to.

Education in the region

Is programming part of school in Apurímac?

No, not as a subject. Apurímac follows the national curriculum, which spreads technology across every area. The regional education office's recent news is about equipment for institutes and free enrolment.

National framework

The General Education Law sets six years of primaria and five of secundaria. A child's grade depends on their age on 31 March, with six as the normative age for first grade and sixteen for fifth of secundaria.

The CNEB names ICT as competency 28 and makes it transversal, so it belongs to all areas at once. The regular secundaria week totals 35 hours: Matemática four, Inglés three, and programming none.

From the DRE Apurímac

In a press note of 15 December 2025, the Dirección Regional de Educación Apurímac reported handing computer equipment and furniture to 12 public technological institutes, and also to the Escuela Superior de Formación Artística Chabuca Granda in Abancay.

On 15 January 2026 it reminded families that school enrolment for 2026 is free and done in person, following the published schedule.

MINEDU's Python initiative for secundaria, Fábrica de Programadores, started in July 2025 with 1,000 pupils in Lima Norte; we found nothing showing it in Apurímac. Every pupil in Peru can take part in contests: the ONEM maths olympiad's school round was on 8 July 2026 and drew more than 1.7 million secundaria pupils, and the OPI computing olympiads from UTEC are free. We have no link with the DRE, MINEDU, UTEC or any contest, and we teach with our own problems.

One rising line, two flat ones

Internet use among people aged six and over climbed from 55.7 per cent in 2022 to 76.0 per cent in 2025. Home internet stayed at about 36 per cent, and home computers slipped from 25.5 to 22.6.

In other words, more people are online every year, almost all of them through a phone.

What that means for a class

Joining a call works on a phone. Programming does not, because the learner types, runs and repairs code for most of the lesson; from about nine we ask for a laptop or desktop.

Sharing one family laptop on agreed days works well. University and institute students can often book a lab. If a computer is out of reach, we will say in the free class that it is better to wait.

Apurímac questions

Online coding and AI classes in Apurímac: common questions

Are the classes in Spanish?

No. All teaching is live, online and in English only. The mentors talk clearly and unhurriedly, keep pace with each learner, tune in to their accent, and if a pupil answers in Spanish they help find the English for it, yet the class stays in English throughout.

Does EF score English in Apurímac?

No. The 2025 EF index gives Peru 519 and publishes fifteen regional scores, none of them for Apurímac. We judge each learner in the free class instead.

We live outside Abancay and Andahuaylas. Is that a problem?

Not at all. A learner in Chincheros, Grau, Cotabambas or Antabamba joins the same live lesson, as long as the internet holds for the hour and there is a suitable device.

Can my child use a phone?

Only to watch. Writing code needs a keyboard, so from about nine we ask for a laptop or desktop; younger children can start with blocks on a tablet.

When are the classes?

Any day, any time, flexible: choose a batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at the hour that suits your family. Apurímac is 10 hours 30 minutes behind India throughout the year; 07:45 in Abancay is 18:15 for the mentor.

Do Apurímac schools teach coding?

No separate subject exists. Digital skills are a competency every area is meant to develop, and the regular secundaria week has no hours for programming.

Are these classes useful for institute and university students?

Very often. Students in technological institutes and universities in Abancay and Andahuaylas meet English in software every day, so Python, Java and data analysis taught in English fit their studies well.

Do you teach artificial intelligence?

Yes. Teenagers and adults build and test small AI models and learn where they go wrong. Children meet the ideas in simple projects, and no one under 13 uses adult chatbot accounts in our classes.

How do we pay?

Monthly by card, priced in US dollars, with your bank handling the conversion to soles. People viewing from India see rupees. The first lesson is free.

Is there a certificate at the end?

Courses that come with one finish with our own certificate of completion, describing what was built. It is not a Peruvian qualification, and MINEDU, the DRE and schools do not issue or endorse it.
