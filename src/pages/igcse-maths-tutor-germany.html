Two syllabuses

How do Cambridge 0580 and Edexcel 4MA1 IGCSE maths compare?

Most international and British schools in Germany use one of these two. They cover similar maths but are examined differently, and the difference matters for practice.

All read 3 November 2026. A new 0580 syllabus is published for exams from 2028 to 2030.

The single biggest difference for preparation is the calculator. Cambridge now makes one paper at each tier a dedicated non-calculator paper; its own page says this lets candidates show more of what they know without changing the standard. So a Cambridge pupil needs fluent written methods for fractions, percentages, surds and algebra, and answers left exactly, for example "in terms of π", as the syllabus puts it. The Edexcel specification allows a calculator in its examinations and supplies a formulae sheet at each tier, which shifts practice toward longer problems.

The grade scales differ too. Cambridge 0580 reports A* to G; Edexcel reports 9 to 1. Cambridge also runs a separate 9 to 1 syllabus, 0980, and its page warns that a school cannot switch a pupil between the two scales after the entry deadline. Ask the school which code your child is entered for before buying any practice book.

Core or Extended

Should my child take Core or Extended IGCSE maths?

Both boards tier their exams, and the tier sets a ceiling on the grade. The school decides the entry, usually in the final year, so the question for a parent is what the tier allows.

What each tier allows

Cambridge says the Core content is meant for learners aiming at grades C to G and the Extended content for those aiming at A* to C. A pupil entered for Core can be awarded C at most; a pupil entered for Extended can be awarded A* down to E.

At Edexcel, Foundation covers grades 5 to 1 and Higher grades 9 to 4. Higher questions assume the Foundation content, just as Cambridge's Extended content includes all of Core.

What changes in the papers

The longer Extended papers do not only add topics. Cambridge weights its assessment objectives differently: "Analyse, interpret and communicate mathematically" carries 30 to 40 % of Core marks but 50 to 60 % of Extended marks.

In other words, the higher tier rewards reasoning and explanation more than routine technique. A pupil moving up a tier needs practice in writing working that an examiner can follow, not just more formulas.

Cambridge designs the course for about 130 guided learning hours, which it calls guidance only. A pupil in Germany who joins an international school in Year 10 from a German Gymnasium, or who returns from a year abroad, can be months behind on the content and the format at once. That is the situation where a tutor pays off most: the maths is often familiar, and what is missing is the IGCSE way of setting it out.

Aiming at A or A*

IGCSE in Germany

Does an IGCSE count in Germany?

There is no national register of IGCSE pupils in Germany that we can quote. What we can quote is what AGIS, the association of international schools in Germany, says about its own members.

AGIS states on its homepage that member schools offering the IB Middle Years Programme or the IGCSE make sure their pupils receive a qualification equivalent to the Mittlere Reife, the German intermediate school certificate taken at the end of Klasse 10. One member school's AGIS entry describes its IGCSE as "including German Mittlere Reife recognition". AGIS directory pages list the IGCSE, or Cambridge Upper Secondary, for member schools in Berlin, Augsburg, Bremen, Ulm and Neu-Ulm, the Frankfurt area and Westpfalz.

That equivalence is the schools' statement about their own pupils, and the detail of how a Land recognises a particular certificate is a matter for the school and the Land authorities. We teach the maths; we do not advise on recognition, and we do not award grades or certificates of any kind.

We name these places only as context. Modern Age Coders is not connected with AGIS, Cambridge International, Pearson or any school, and no school refers pupils to us. A pupil at any school entering IGCSE maths, in Germany or anywhere else, follows the same syllabus with us.

A lesson

Why do IGCSE pupils lose accuracy marks, and how do you stop it?

The Cambridge syllabus gives the rule plainly: give non-exact answers to 3 significant figures, or 1 decimal place for angles in degrees, and avoid rounding until the final answer. Most pupils know the first half. Our own lesson below shows why the second half costs marks.

Task (ours, calculator paper style). A string 30 cm long fits exactly round the middle of a ball. Find the volume of the ball. Working: the circumference is 2πr = 30, so r = 30 / (2π). Then V = (4/3)πr³. The question is when to round.

A pupil who writes r = 4.77 and carries on gets 455 cm³. A pupil who keeps the full calculator value gets 456 cm³. The two unrounded volumes, about 454.6 and 455.9, differ by roughly 0.3 %, and that is still the difference between the accuracy mark and no accuracy mark, because 455 is not the correct value to 3 significant figures. Rounding the radius cost about 0.1 %; cubing it made that error about three times larger. Any formula with a power, a product of several measurements or a division by a small number does the same.

On a calculator paper

Store the unrounded value in the calculator memory, or write 4.774648... with the dots, and round only the final line.

Where a later part of a question uses an earlier answer, Cambridge asks for the value before it was rounded. Write both: the rounded answer for the mark, the long value for the next step.

Use the π key, or 3.142 if the question says so, never 3.14.

On a non-calculator paper

Task (ours). A sector has radius 6 cm and angle 120°. Find its area and its perimeter, in terms of π.

Working: 120° is one third of the circle. Area = (1/3) · π · 6² = 12π cm². Arc = (1/3) · 2π · 6 = 4π cm, so the perimeter is 12 + 4π cm.

Here the rule becomes "do not round at all". An answer of 37.7 cm² on a paper that asked for π has thrown the exact answer away.

  1. Keep every digit until the last line. Rounding is a presentation step, not a calculation step.
  2. Round once, to what the question asks. 3 significant figures by default, 1 decimal place for angles in degrees.
  3. On a non-calculator paper, stay exact. Fractions, surds and π stay as they are unless the question asks for a decimal.

IGCSE maths tutoring in Germany: common questions

Is there an IGCSE maths tutor in Germany who teaches in English?

Yes. Modern Age Coders teaches Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 and Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Mathematics A live online in English, for pupils at international and British schools across Germany, in a group or one to one.

Are the classes in German?

No. All classes are live and taught in English only, the language of the IGCSE papers. Mentors adjust to each pupil's pace and accent.

What is the difference between Cambridge 0580 and Edexcel 4MA1?

Cambridge 0580 has Core and Extended tiers graded A* to G, with one non-calculator and one calculator paper at each tier. Edexcel 4MA1 has Foundation and Higher tiers graded 9 to 1, with two papers of 2 hours and 100 marks at each tier, and calculators may be used.

How long are the Cambridge IGCSE maths papers?

In the 0580 syllabus for 2025 to 2027, Core Papers 1 and 3 last 1 hour 30 minutes with 80 marks each, and Extended Papers 2 and 4 last 2 hours with 100 marks each.

Is a calculator allowed in IGCSE maths?

In Cambridge 0580, calculators are not allowed in Papers 1 and 2 and a scientific calculator is required for Papers 3 and 4. The Edexcel 4MA1 specification says a calculator may be used in its examinations.

How many decimal places should IGCSE answers have?

Cambridge asks for non-exact answers to 3 significant figures, or 1 decimal place for angles in degrees, unless the question says otherwise, and tells candidates not to round until the final answer.

Is an IGCSE recognised in Germany?

AGIS says its member schools that offer the IGCSE or the IB Middle Years Programme give pupils a qualification equivalent to the Mittlere Reife. Ask your school how this applies to your child; we do not advise on recognition.

What times are possible?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. India runs four and a half hours ahead of German winter time and three and a half ahead of German summer time.

Who are the tutors?

Mentors based in India, teaching IGCSE maths live online in English, with the same mentor for a pupil from month to month.

What does it cost?

The fee shown here is monthly, in euro at the European Central Bank reference rate. The payment itself is in US dollars and your bank converts it. The first class is free, with no card.
