London 11 plus consortium maths for independent school entry

Many London independent schools choose their Year 7 pupils with an entrance test sat in Year 6, and the largest shared one is the London 11+ Consortium assessment: a single 100-minute online test, designed with Quest Assessments, that counts for every member school a child applies to. Maths opens it, with 20 adaptive minutes. Modern Age Coders teaches that maths live online, in a class or one to one, through our 11 plus maths preparation course.

What is the London 11+ Consortium test?

It is a shared entrance assessment for 13 London independent schools, sat once in Year 6 and sent to every member school the child applies to. The consortium describes it as 100 minutes in total, taken online, with six sections in a fixed order: 20 minutes of maths, 10 of non-verbal reasoning, 20 of English and 10 of verbal reasoning, a break, then 15 minutes of problem solving and 25 of creative comprehension. Maths and both reasoning sections are adaptive. The maths uses the Year 5 national curriculum, "with opportunities for additional challenge". For September 2027 entry the test falls on 27 November, 1 December or 3 December 2026. Modern Age Coders teaches the maths, live online, with practice we write ourselves.

The consortium test

How is the London 11+ Consortium test structured?

Six sections in a fixed order, three adaptive and three not, with a break after the fourth. Each section is locked once finished, and the on-screen clock counts only minutes.

The London 11+ Consortium assessment, in the order it is sat (Consortium FAQs and parent information sheet, September 2027 entry)
OrderSectionMinutesAdaptive?
1Maths20Yes
2Non-verbal reasoning10Yes
3English (comprehension of a specially written fiction text, then standard English in context)20No
4Verbal reasoning10Yes
BreakUp to 30
5Problem solving15No
6Creative comprehension (several sources in different formats)25No

The consortium says the exam "is 100 minutes long (1 hour 40 minutes)" with a 30-minute break in the middle. By our own arithmetic, the three adaptive sections take 40 of those 100 minutes and maths alone is a fifth of the time. The non-adaptive sections change for each of the three test days, "to ensure the security of the test", and results are standardised across the three sittings. The adaptive sections are different for every candidate anyway, since each next question depends on the last answer.

No going back

The consortium explains that extra time is given section by section "because once a section has been completed, candidates cannot go back to amend it." So a child must use the whole 20 maths minutes for maths.

A clock in minutes only

Candidates see a clock that counts down "in minutes" for each section, chosen "not to count down in seconds" so it does not distract, and "They will not be given any additional time warnings."

Any device

The test runs on "iPads, PCs, laptops and chrome books", and the consortium says the device "does not make any difference". There is no extended writing, so typing speed does not count.

Where it is sat

Usually in the child's current primary or prep school, which runs it to the consortium's rules: a start inside a set window, one of the three dates, and the break after verbal reasoning for everyone.

If the current school cannot host it, the family names a first and second choice consortium school as a centre, and both must be schools the child has applied to.

What else counts

The test is one of "three complementary assessment approaches". The others are a common reference form from the current head and an interview that each senior school runs in its own way.

Some member schools select for interview from the test results and some do not; the consortium tells families to check each school's own site.

Source: The London 11+ Consortium, home page, Code of Practice and FAQs, read 31 October 2026.

The maths section

What maths is in the London 11+ Consortium test?

Year 5 national curriculum maths, nothing from Year 6, made harder by depth rather than by new topics. Because the section adapts, a strong child keeps meeting harder questions.

The consortium is precise about content: "The questions are based on the National Curriculum for Year 5 and we do not test anything on the Year 6 curriculum." Its reason is fairness, since "different schools will teach the Year 6 curriculum in different orders". Then comes the line that matters most for preparation: "Because we have many very able candidates taking the Consortium exam, we provide stretch and challenge through the style and depth of the questions we ask." In other words, a child will not meet algebra or ratio from Year 6, but will meet Year 5 ideas such as fractions, multiplication, measures and shape asked in ways that need two or three steps of thought.

What adaptive means for practice

An adaptive section has no fixed "hard part at the end". Questions rise in difficulty after correct answers and ease after wrong ones, so a child may meet a demanding question in the first minute.

That rewards steady accuracy over racing. A careless slip on an easy question pulls the next one down; a careful right answer lifts it.

The problem-solving section is maths too

Section five, 15 minutes, "tests the ability of candidates to use words and numbers to solve multi-step problems". It is not adaptive, and the consortium notes that candidates "are likely to complete different numbers of questions in the time available."

The creative comprehension section asks children to use information from several sources at once, which often includes reading tables, timetables or charts.

Worked examples, written by us

How should a child work through a deeper Year 5 problem?

Three original problems in the style of the stretch the consortium describes: Year 5 content, more than one step. None is from the consortium test or its familiarisation material.

1. The leftover pencils

A school buys 7 boxes of 24 pencils and gives 15 pencils to each of its 9 classes. How many are left? Two calculations, kept separate: 7 times 24 is 168 pencils bought; 9 times 15 is 135 given out. 168 minus 135 leaves 33. Writing each step as a sentence ("bought", "given", "left") stops the classic slip of subtracting 15 from 168.

2. The reversed number

A two-digit number has digits that add to 11. Reversing the digits makes it 27 larger. What is it? List the candidates with digit sum 11: 29, 38, 47, 56, 65, 74, 83, 92. Reversal only increases a number whose tens digit is smaller. Test 47: 74 minus 47 is 27. Answer 47. A systematic list beats guessing every time.

3. What a remainder means

A pool opens from 07:00 to 21:00 and lanes are booked in 45-minute slots back to back. How many full slots fit in a day? 14 hours is 840 minutes; 840 divided by 45 is 18 remainder 30. The answer is 18, not 19 and not 18.7: half a slot cannot be booked. Deciding what the remainder means is the step adaptive questions test.

A practice set written by Modern Age Coders, Year 5 content with added depth
Question (ours)AnswerHow to think

The common thread is method before arithmetic: name each quantity, list possibilities in order, check units, and decide what a remainder means before writing an answer. These are the habits the consortium's own phrase, "style and depth", points to, and they are useful far beyond one test.

Key dates

When is the London 11+ Consortium test for 2027 entry?

Registrations close on Friday 6 November 2026, and the test runs on one of three dates at the end of November and start of December 2026. Offers arrive in February 2027.

Key dates for September 2027 entry, as the London 11+ Consortium publishes them
StepDate
Closing date for registrations, and for evidence of access arrangementsFriday 6 November 2026
Assessment, on one of three datesFriday 27 November, Tuesday 1 December or Thursday 3 December 2026
Offers sent to arriveFriday 12 February 2027
Deadline to accept12 noon on Wednesday 3 March 2027
  1. Register with each consortium school your child is applying to, before the closing date. The test itself is sat only once.
  2. If your child has extra time at school, send the specialist and school evidence to every consortium school applied to by the same closing date. The consortium follows the 2025-26 JCQ regulations for this sitting, and 25 per cent extra time needs standardised scores of 84 or less in the areas the rules set out.
  3. Look at the familiarisation materials linked from the consortium's site before the test day. The consortium recommends this for every candidate.
  4. Check with your current school whether it will host the test, and on which date and session.
  5. Check each school's site for its interview process; some select for interview from the test and some do not.

For families planning ahead to September 2028 entry, expect a similar pattern a year later, but confirm on the consortium's site once it publishes, since dates are set each year. Overseas applicants can travel to sit at a member school or ask a consortium school's admissions team about sitting abroad.

What the schools say

Does the London 11+ Consortium recommend tutoring?

No. It says a child "does not need special tuition or intensive practice", and it actively discourages interview coaching. Families should weigh that before booking anyone, including us.

In the consortium's words

The home page sets out the aim: "We are concerned about the pressure the 11+ application system can place on young children and the damage to learning which relentless 'teaching to the test' produces."

On preparation, the FAQ says the test checks the Year 5 maths curriculum and reading of an extended fiction passage, "skills that your daughter will be developing in school anyway", and on non-verbal and verbal reasoning, "any gains quickly become very marginal". On interviews: "We actively discourage preparation for the interview."

What we offer instead

Teaching, not coaching. If a child in Year 5 is unsure of fractions, factors, measures or multi-step problems, that is a gap in ordinary school maths, worth closing for Year 7 whatever happens in December.

We do not prepare children for interviews, we do not claim to know the consortium's questions, and we never present our practice as theirs. We will not predict a result.

London 11 plus questions

What parents ask about the London 11+ Consortium test

What is the London 11+ Consortium?

It is a group of 13 London independent schools that share one entrance test for Year 7. A child sits the test once, and the results go to every member school they have applied to.

How long is the London consortium test?

The consortium says it is 100 minutes in total, with a break of up to 30 minutes in the middle. It is split into six timed sections, starting with 20 minutes of maths.

Is the consortium maths section adaptive?

Yes. The consortium says maths, verbal reasoning and non-verbal reasoning are adaptive, so the questions adjust to each answer. English, problem solving and creative comprehension are not.

What maths is tested?

Year 5 national curriculum maths and nothing from Year 6. The consortium adds stretch through the style and depth of the questions, because many very able children sit it.

Can my child go back to an earlier section?

No. The consortium says that once a section is completed, candidates cannot go back to amend it, which is why extra time is given section by section.

When is the test for 2027 entry?

On one of Friday 27 November, Tuesday 1 December or Thursday 3 December 2026. Registrations close on Friday 6 November 2026 and offers arrive on Friday 12 February 2027.

Who writes the consortium test?

The consortium designed it with its test provider, Quest Assessments, together with curriculum specialists from its own schools. It was first sat in December 2022 for September 2023 entry.

Does my child need tutoring for it?

The consortium says no special tuition or intensive practice is needed and recommends its familiarisation materials. We teach Year 5 maths with depth for children who have real gaps, and we never coach for interviews.

When can lessons happen?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Each slot is confirmed in UK time.

Are you linked to the consortium or its schools?

No. Modern Age Coders is independent, has never seen the consortium's questions and writes all of its own practice. We do not predict results or promise places.
