Coding classes for kids in Ludwigshafen am Rhein

Rhineland-Palatinate has decided to make Informatik compulsory in every school, but not until the 2028/29 school year. A child who is six, eight or ten in Ludwigshafen today will spend most of their school years before that change. This page sets out what a child can learn at each age, what Rhineland-Palatinate schools teach now, what an English-speaking mentor does with a primary child, and a project on real Oggersheim rain where a child discovers that the same numbers can tell two different stories.

For Ludwigshafen am Rhein children from 6 to 12, Modern Age Coders runs coding classes live online in English. One mentor stays with a child week after week and adjusts to their pace and accent, in a class of fifteen to twenty peers or alone. At what age can a child in Ludwigshafen start coding?

At six, if the first steps are blocks and stories rather than typing. What a child does changes a lot between six and twelve, so groups are always formed by age.

The youngest

Six and seven year olds do not need to read long instructions. The mentor shows, the child copies, then changes one thing and sees what happens. Lessons are broken into short pieces with a small win every ten minutes.

In Rhineland-Palatinate, as almost everywhere in Germany, Grundschule runs from grade 1 to grade 4, so these are Grundschule children.

The older ones

From about nine, many children are ready to type. Python is a real language used by professionals, and children are proud to write the same code adults use.

By eleven or twelve, after the move to a secondary school such as a Gymnasium or a Realschule plus, a child can read a data file and answer a question about it, the kind of project in the rain section below.

Rhineland-Palatinate schools

What do Rhineland-Palatinate schools teach about computing?

For most children, not much yet. That is changing, but slowly, and the facts are clear in the Gesellschaft für Informatik's Informatik-Monitor 2026/27, read on 2 November 2026.

Today

Rheinland-Pfalz is named in the monitor as one of six Länder without Informatik that is compulsory for all pupils. The others are Berlin, Brandenburg, Bremen, Hessen and Sachsen-Anhalt.

There is one exception worth knowing: since the 2021/22 school year, 21 Informatik-Profil-Schulen in the Land teach compulsory Informatik in grade 5. Ask your school whether it is one of them.

From 2028/29

The monitor reports that Informatik becomes a compulsory subject at all schools in Rheinland-Pfalz from the 2028/29 school year, with four lessons a week spread over the years in total.

A child who is eight today will be in about grade 5 when that starts; a child who is eleven will already be in grade 8. For both, the habits of step-by-step thinking are easier to build at home now than to catch up on later.

In English

Can a Ludwigshafen child learn coding in English?

Yes. Every class is live and in English only, and young children usually find it easier than their parents expect.

English only, and we say so

Every lesson is in English. Nothing is taught in German, and it is fairer to say so on this page than after a trial. The mentors work from India over a live video link.

Matched to your child

The mentor talks slowly, uses short sentences, shows rather than describes, and gets used to how your child speaks. If a child answers with a German word, the mentor understands and offers the English one.

The screen does the work

A Scratch block that says "move 10 steps" is easy to guess, and most of a lesson is building and trying. Children pick up the handful of words they need within a few weeks.

Because the mentor is the same every week, a shy child gets time to warm up. After a month the mentor knows whether your child likes a challenge straight away, or a few easy wins first.

A real Ludwigshafen project

How rainy is Oggersheim? Sorting 24 years of days into buckets

The Deutscher Wetterdienst measured rain every day at a station in Ludwigshafen-Oggersheim from 1978 to 2004. Our older children use those numbers to learn that a chart can hide the answer or show it, depending on how you group the data.

The data

Station 3077, Ludwigshafen-Oggersheim, stood 97 metres above sea level and recorded daily rain in millimetres. Its record is published by the DWD Climate Data Center under the Creative Commons BY 4.0 licence. We downloaded it on 3 November 2026.

We keep the 24 complete years from 1978 to 2001, which is 8,766 days, in a file called oggersheim_rain.csv. One millimetre of rain is one litre of water on every square metre of ground.

The question

What is a normal day in Oggersheim: dry, a little rain, or a lot? A child sorts every day into a bucket and counts how many fall in each. The surprise is that the first way of sorting gives an answer that is true but useless.

The wettest day in the file is 23 May 1978, with 69.2 millimetres, and an average year brought 562.4 millimetres. Both are our own sums from the file.

Almost every day, 8,461 of 8,766, lands in the first bucket. The chart says "it is nearly always 0 to 10 millimetres", which is true and tells you almost nothing: a dry day and a day of steady rain look the same. The buckets are too wide.

Now the story is visible: more than half of all days were completely dry, and only 127 days in 24 years, about five a year, were soaking days of 15 millimetres or more. Nothing in the data changed between the two versions. Only the edges of the buckets did.

The bucket names and edges, the counts and the averages are ours. The station closed at the end of 2004; 2002 and 2004 are incomplete in the file and left out.

  1. Ages 6 to 8, no screen. Look out of the window every day for two weeks and draw a sun, a cloud or a raindrop. Then decide: is "cloud" one bucket, or two?
  2. Ages 8 to 10, Scratch. Make a list of ten rain numbers and three counters called dry, wet and soaking. Move each number into the right counter.
  3. Ages 10 to 12, Python. Run Version 1 with buckets 5 millimetres wide, then 1 millimetre wide. Which width tells the clearest story?
  4. Going further. Change name_of so that "a soaking day" starts at 10 millimetres. Predict the new counts before running it.

Free contests

Which free maths and computing contests can a Ludwigshafen child try?

Two are run through schools all over Germany this school year, and the maths olympiad has its national final in Rhineland-Palatinate in 2027.

Dates published by the organisers
ContestForWhen
Informatik-BiberGrades 3 to 139 to 20 November 2026
Känguru der MathematikGrades 3 to 138 April 2027
Mathematik-Olympiade, BundesrundeInvited teams from grade 830 May to 2 June 2027, Kaiserslautern

In the Biber, primary pupils solve nine logic puzzles in 30 minutes, and no coding knowledge is needed. The Känguru gives grades 3 to 6 twenty-four problems in 75 minutes; in 2027 it moves to 8 April because the usual March date falls just before the Easter holidays in almost every Land. Both are entered through the school, so ask the class teacher. Real contest tasks stay with their organisers: the practice puzzles our mentors use are invented by us and marked as ours. BWINF, the Känguru team and the Mathematik-Olympiade have no connection with us.

Ludwigshafen parents ask

Coding classes for kids in Ludwigshafen: common questions

Are the classes in German?

No, they are in English, live, every time. A mentor speaks in short, simple sentences, adapts to the way your child talks and shows each step on screen, which lets a seven year old with very little English take part from week one.

Is six too young to start coding?

Not with the right start. Six year olds begin with Scratch, where blocks snap together and there is almost nothing to type or read. Lessons are short and playful, and the free first class shows whether your child is ready.

Do Ludwigshafen schools teach coding?

Mostly not yet. The Gesellschaft für Informatik lists Rheinland-Pfalz among six Länder without Informatik that is compulsory for all, with 21 Informatik-Profil-Schulen teaching it in grade 5. Informatik becomes compulsory at all schools in the Land from 2028/29.

When can my child switch from Scratch to Python?

Usually around 9 or 10, once typing is comfortable and they have used loops and choices in Scratch. The mentor suggests the switch when a child starts to find Scratch too slow.

When are classes in Ludwigshafen time?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Most children here take classes in the late afternoon or on a weekend morning.

Can my child enter the Känguru or the Biber?

Yes, if the school takes part, because both are entered through schools. The Informatik-Biber runs from 9 to 20 November 2026 and the Känguru der Mathematik is on 8 April 2027. Ask the class teacher.

Who are the teachers?

Mentors based in India who teach live online, in English. A child keeps one mentor for the whole course, and the time difference is the reason German afternoons are evenings for them.

Is the rain project real Ludwigshafen data?

Yes. It uses the daily rain record of Deutscher Wetterdienst station 3077, Ludwigshafen-Oggersheim, from 1978 to 2001, published under the CC BY 4.0 licence. The buckets and counts are our own.

How many children are in a group?

Fifteen to twenty children of a similar age. With the one-to-one plan your child has the mentor to themselves for two classes a week.

What currency do I pay in?

You see a euro figure here, based on the European Central Bank reference rate, while the card payment itself is in US dollars. The bank does the conversion, so a few cents of difference on the statement is normal. A first class is free of charge.
