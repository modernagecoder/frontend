The 66th olympiad

How does the Mathematik-Olympiade run in 2026 and 2027?

In rounds that get harder each time, from the school to the Land and, from Klasse 8, to a national final. The Mathematik-Olympiaden e.V. is the organiser.

Rounds by school year

The organisers say pupils in Klasse 5 to 7 compete in three rounds and pupils from Klasse 8 in four. Primary schools have taken part since 2005, in three rounds for Klasse 3 and 4.

About 200,000 pupils take part every year, according to the organisers. The Bundesrunde brings together around 200 of them, and since 2014 it has included five guests from German schools abroad.

Who writes the problems

A problem committee of around 60 mathematicians writes about 150 different problems each school year, pitched to each Klassenstufe, the Mathematik-Olympiaden e.V. says.

For pupils who reach the Bundesrunde, the hosting Land pays board and lodging for the four days, so it costs the participants nothing.

Taking part

How does a pupil in Germany take part in the Mathematik-Olympiade?

Through the school. The organisers send each round's papers to schools through the Landesbeauftragte, and they tell pupils to go straight to their maths teacher.

  1. Ask the maths teacher at the start of the school year whether the school runs the Schulrunde, and how.
  2. Solve the Schulrunde. It is usually done at home, sometimes as a paper in school. In primary schools the school decides the format, alone or in groups.
  3. Sit the Regionalrunde if invited. In the 66th olympiad it is on 11 November 2026, with four problems at a central place.
  4. Go on to the Landesrunde at the end of February if selected: 26 to 28 February 2027 in this round.
  5. From Klasse 8, the Bundesrunde for the Land's team: 30 May to 2 June 2027 in Kaiserslautern.

Source: Bundesweite Mathematik-Wettbewerbe, Mathematik-Olympiade FAQ on mathe-wettbewerbe.de/mo, read on 3 November 2026.

If the Schulrunde has already passed

For 2026/27 the Schulrunde ran from the start of the school year into the autumn, and the Regionalrunde is already set for 11 November 2026. A pupil who did not take part this autumn will usually start with next year's Schulrunde, from August 2027 at the earliest.

That is not wasted time. Nine months of steady problem solving before the next Schulrunde is a strong preparation.

What we do and do not do

We are not connected with the Mathematik-Olympiaden e.V. or with Bildung & Begabung, and we do not enter pupils. Entry is the school's.

The organisers allow only non-commercial websites to republish old problems, and then without solutions. So we never copy them: we link to the organisers' archive and teach with problems we write ourselves.

Written solutions

What does a Mathematik-Olympiade solution need to show?

The reasoning, not only the result. The organisers say the problems ask less for skill at calculation than for precise thinking, applying knowledge and finding creative solutions to unusual problems.

What separates a found answer from a full solution (our marking checklist)
A found answer saysA full solution also shows
"The answer is 18."Why no smaller number works, and why 18 does
"I tried many cases and it always works."An argument that covers every case, not only the ones tried
"It is obvious from the picture."Which property of the figure is used, and why it holds
"The computer found no example."A proof that no example can exist

Our own checklist, used in class. The organisers' description of what the problems test is from mathe-wettbewerbe.de/mo, read on 3 November 2026.

Pupils who are quick at mental arithmetic often lose most marks here, because the answer arrived in their head and never reached the paper. Our classes slow that moment down. A pupil solves the problem, then writes the solution so that a stranger could check every step, then reads it aloud in English to the mentor, who asks the one question a marker would ask. Writing in English and explaining aloud are a good test: if a step cannot be said clearly, it usually is not yet understood.

Practice, ours

What do olympiad problems look like? Three of our own, with full solutions

These three problems were written by Modern Age Coders for this page, one per age band. None is from the Mathematik-Olympiade or any other contest. The program at the end checks small cases; read why that is not enough.

Problem A, about Klasse 5 and 6 (ours)

How many three-digit numbers have digits that add up to 5?

Solution. Sort by the hundreds digit, which cannot be 0. If it is 1, the other two digits add up to 4: 04, 13, 22, 31, 40, so 5 numbers. If it is 2, they add up to 3: 4 numbers. Then 3 numbers for a hundreds digit of 3, 2 for 4, and 1 for 5 (the number 500). The total is 5 + 4 + 3 + 2 + 1 = 15, and the list is complete because every case of the hundreds digit has been counted.

Problem B, about Klasse 7 and 8 (ours)

Call a number balanced if its digit sum equals the digit sum of twice the number. Find the smallest balanced number above 9, and prove that every balanced number is divisible by 9.

Solution. A number and its digit sum leave the same remainder on division by 9. If n is balanced, n and 2n have equal digit sums, so they leave the same remainder; then 2n minus n, which is n, leaves remainder 0. So n is a multiple of 9. The multiples of 9 above 9 start with 18, and 18 has digit sum 9 while 36 also has digit sum 9. So the answer is 18. The converse fails: 333 is a multiple of 9, but 666 has digit sum 18.

Problem C, about Klasse 9 and up (ours)

For which whole numbers n is n² + 3n + 5 divisible by 11? Can it ever be divisible by 121?

Solution. Rewrite: n² + 3n + 5 = (n - 4)² + 11(n - 1), which is easy to check by multiplying out. The second part is always a multiple of 11, so the whole expression is divisible by 11 exactly when (n - 4)² is, and because 11 is prime that happens exactly when n - 4 is. So n must leave remainder 4 on division by 11.

The second part

Now write n = 11k + 4. Then (n - 4)² = 121k², and 11(n - 1) = 11(11k + 3) = 121k + 33. Together that is 121k² + 121k + 33, which leaves remainder 33 on division by 121 for every k. So the expression is never divisible by 121.

Every step holds for all whole numbers, not only for the ones anyone has tried. That is what makes it a proof.

The program agrees with all three solutions, which is reassuring. But look at what it actually did: it tested the numbers 1 to 10,000 for Problem C and found none divisible by 121. That cannot rule out a larger number, and an olympiad marker would give no credit for it. The algebra above covers every whole number at once. We use small programs like this in class for one purpose only: to test a guess before trying to prove it, and to catch an arithmetic slip in a solution. In the olympiad itself, as in any written paper, there is no computer and the proof is the whole answer.

  1. Extend A. How many four-digit numbers have digits adding up to 5? Predict first, then check with one change to the program.
  2. Test B. Find the five smallest balanced numbers above 9 by hand, then compare with the program's list.
  3. Vary C. For which n is n² + 5n + 7 divisible by 3? Write the full solution before running any code.
  4. Explain. In two sentences, say why 909 numbers from 1 to 10,000 leave remainder 4 on division by 11.

Beyond the olympiad

Where can the Mathematik-Olympiade lead?

For a few pupils, further: the organisers describe a path to the international olympiad. For everyone, to clearer thinking in school maths.

The international path

Bildung & Begabung says the six-person German team for the International Mathematical Olympiad is chosen through a selection contest, the AIMO, which invites top performers from the Mathematik-Olympiade Bundesrunde, the second round of the Bundeswettbewerb Mathematik and the maths winners at Land level of Jugend forscht. The AIMO begins each year at the end of November or the start of December.

The Bundeswettbewerb Mathematik is a separate, home-based contest for pupils on the way to the Hochschulreife, run over three rounds.

Where the old problems are

The Mathematik-Olympiaden e.V. keeps an archive of official problem sheets on mathematik-olympiaden.de, and puts the problems of the current round online after it has run. For the 66th Regionalrunde, Klasse 5 to 12, it gives 1 December 2026 as the date.

Working through old papers at home is free and useful. We send families to that archive; we do not copy from it.

Mathematik-Olympiade preparation: common questions

Are the classes in German?

No. Our classes are live and in English only, with a mentor who matches your child's pace and accent. We give the German maths words beside the English, so the olympiad paper itself holds no surprises.

When is the Regionalrunde of the Mathematik-Olympiade 2026?

The Mathematik-Olympiaden e.V. lists 11 November 2026 for the Regionalrunde of the 66th olympiad. The Landesrunde follows from 26 to 28 February 2027 and the Bundesrunde from 30 May to 2 June 2027 in Kaiserslautern.

How does my child enter?

Through school. The organisers tell pupils to ask their maths teacher, and schools receive the papers through the Landesbeauftragte of their Land. We are not connected with the organisers and do not enter anyone.

Can primary school children take part?

Yes. The organisers say primary schools have been part of the Mathematik-Olympiade since 2005, with three rounds for Klasse 3 and 4. Dates for primary schools are often later than for secondary schools.

Do you use real olympiad problems?

No. The organisers allow only non-commercial websites to republish old problems, so we write our own in the same spirit, like the three on this page, and send families to the official archive on mathematik-olympiaden.de.

Is it about speed?

No. The papers ask for written, argued solutions. That is why our classes spend as much time on writing a solution clearly as on finding it.

Who reaches the Bundesrunde?

Teams of up to 15 pupils per Land, from Klasse 8 upwards, according to the organisers. About 200 pupils take part, and for Klasse 3 to 7 the contest ends at the Landesrunde.

When are classes in German time?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Younger pupils usually come after school, older ones often at weekends.

How are fees shown and paid?

Fees appear in euro, converted with the European Central Bank's reference rate, and are paid in US dollars; your bank converts the payment, so the euro figure can differ slightly. The first class is free.

Does the course also cover the Känguru?

Yes. Our Mathematik-Olympiade and Känguru Training course prepares for both: written proofs for the Olympiade and quick, careful reasoning for the multiple-choice Känguru der Mathematik.
