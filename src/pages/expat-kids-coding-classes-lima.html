Continuity

How can my child keep coding while we move to Lima?

Because the classes are online, the move changes only the room the child sits in. The mentor, the group and the project stay the same, and that steadiness is worth a lot to a child whose school, friends and home are all changing at once.

  1. Start before you leave. A few weeks of classes in the old home lets the child get to know the mentor while life is still normal.
  2. Plan the gap honestly. Most families lose a week or two to flights and boxes. Tell us the dates and we will pause, or set a small project the child can finish offline.
  3. Restart with the same mentor. Once the internet is connected in Lima, classes resume at a new hour if needed. Nothing is repeated and nobody is a stranger.
  4. Fit the new school day. Lima schools set their own hours. Once the timetable is known, we move classes around it.
  5. Use the summer if you arrive in it. A family arriving in January or February has weeks before the school year starts. That is a good time for one-to-one classes and a bigger project.

Connectivity in the city is good: INEI found that 84.7% of households in Metropolitan Lima had internet in the second quarter of 2026, and 53.3% had at least one computer. A laptop from home is all a child needs; if the family's computer arrives in a container weeks later, a borrowed one or a tablet for younger children will do in the meantime.

The Peruvian school year

When does the school year start in Peru, and which grade will my child join?

The Peruvian school year runs roughly from March to December, the other way round from schools in Europe, North America and India. For a family arriving from the northern hemisphere, that changes which grade a child joins and when.

The year

MINEDU's norm for 2026, RM 501-2025-MINEDU, set the first block of lessons from 16 March to 15 May 2026 and the last from 19 October to 18 December 2026, thirty-six teaching weeks in all, with short breaks in May, July to August and October. January and February are the long summer holiday.

The norm covers public and private schools alike, but regional education offices may shift the weeks and each school organises its own bimestres or trimestres. Ask the school for its calendar; that is the one that matters.

Grade placement by age

MINEDU uses a "normative age" for each grade, measured on 31 March of each year: a child aged 6 on that date belongs in 1st of primaria, and the ages run up to 16 for 5th of secundaria. Primaria lasts six years and secundaria five.

A child moving mid-year from a September-start system may therefore land half a grade ahead of or behind where the family expected. The school decides placement; we simply match the coding course to the child's real level.

Peruvian schools teach no computing subject in the national plans of study, and English gets two or three periods a week in primaria under the CNEB. Children arriving from systems where computing is taught at school often lose it on arrival, which is one reason families keep an outside class going. Moving school records

How are foreign school years recognised in Peru?

Through a MINEDU procedure, described on the government's gob.pe portal. We are not advisers and this is not legal or immigration advice, but families often ask, so here is what MINEDU's own page says, in plain English.

The examples in the last row are ours: MINEDU names only the countries that do have agreements. Requirements and fees are on that page and can change; check it, and ask the receiving school.

What MINEDU's page says

Only completed grades are recognised, grade by grade, and there is no conversion of marks. Certificates must be legalised by Peru's foreign ministry or carry a Hague apostille where the country of origin is a party to that convention, and documents not in Spanish need a translation.

The procedure is done in person, only in Lima, at MINEDU's offices in San Borja, and the page gives a maximum of 30 working days for the resolution.

What it means for coding

Nothing directly: our classes are outside the school system, so no recognition is needed and no Peruvian grade is awarded. Our certificate of completion is our own, not a school record.

It does mean the first months in Lima can be busy with paperwork. A class that needs no paperwork at all, and carries on regardless, is one less thing to arrange.

A real lesson

What time will the classes be once we are in Lima?

Whatever hour suits the new routine. Peru stays on UTC minus 5 all year with no daylight saving, which makes Lima easy; the countries families come from often are not. Here is a lesson we give older children who have relatives in several time zones.

The problem

A child in Lima has a coding class at 09:00 during the summer holidays and wants to call grandparents in London, cousins in New York and friends in Madrid straight afterwards. What time is it for each of them, in January and in July?

Doing it by adding hours goes wrong twice a year, because London, New York and Madrid change their clocks and Lima does not.

The ideas

Python's zoneinfo module knows the rules of every time zone, from the same IANA database computers worldwide use. A datetime with tzinfo=lima is a real moment, not just a clock reading.

astimezone() moves that moment into another zone. %z prints the offset, so the child can see Lima stays at -0500 in both months.

Read the columns. Kolkata, where our mentors are, is 19:30 in both months, because neither Peru nor India changes its clocks, so the gap is always ten and a half hours. London, New York and Madrid each move an hour between January and July. A family that sets a weekly call by "the usual gap" will be an hour out for half the year; a program that uses the time-zone rules never is.

  1. Add your own city. Put your previous home's zone into places, for example "Australia/Sydney", and see which way its clock moves.
  2. Find the change. Loop over every day of 2027 and print the dates when London's gap to Lima changes.
  3. Check India. Print %z for Kolkata in both months and explain why it never changes.
  4. Write it up. In two sentences, explain to a grandparent why the call time moves even though nobody changed the plan.

Life in a big city

Why choose online coding classes over a centre in Lima?

Mostly because of distance. Metropolitan Lima, the Province of Lima with its 43 districts, counted 10,141,775 people in INEI's 2025 census, and crossing it after school takes a slice of the evening no child gets back.

No commute

A class at home in Miraflores, San Isidro, Surco or La Molina takes exactly as long as the class.

No new paperwork

No enrolment forms in Spanish, no school records, no residency questions: the class works the same wherever you live.

Same quality anywhere

The teaching is identical in Lima, in your old home and anywhere you move next, which matters for families on rotation.

Families from India are a small community in Peru; the Embassy of India in Lima puts it at about 500 people. Many of the expat families we expect on this page come from elsewhere. Questions from families moving to Lima

Coding classes for expat children in Lima: questions and answers

Are the classes in Spanish?

No. Every class is live, online and taught in English only, whether the family lives in Lima or anywhere else. Mentors speak clearly and type key words, so children whose first language is not English can follow too.

Can we start before we move to Lima?

Yes, and we recommend it. The child gets to know the mentor at home, pauses for the travel weeks if needed, and carries on in Lima with the same mentor and group.

What time will classes be in Lima?

Any day, any time, flexible: choose a batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at the hour that suits your family. Lima stays at UTC minus 5 all year and India is 10 hours 30 minutes ahead, so a 16:00 class in Lima is 02:30 the next day for the mentor, which can be arranged.

When does the school year start in Peru?

In March. Under MINEDU's norm for 2026, lessons ran from 16 March to 18 December 2026, with January and February as the summer holiday. Regional offices and schools can adjust the weeks, so check with the school.

Which grade will my child join?

Schools decide. MINEDU places children by their age on 31 March: 6 for 1st of primaria up to 16 for 5th of secundaria. A child coming from a September-start system may join a different grade than expected.

Do we need to convalidate school records for coding classes?

No. Our classes are outside the school system, so no recognition is needed. For school, MINEDU describes convalidación and revalidación on its gob.pe page; ask the receiving school. We do not give legal or immigration advice.

Do you award a certificate?

Yes, our own Modern Age Coders certificate of completion after a course's final exam. It is not a Peruvian school record or qualification and is not recognised by MINEDU.

Are the classes suitable for children whose first language is not English?

Usually. Code is mostly English keywords and symbols, the mentor keeps language simple, and the screen carries much of the meaning. The free class shows quickly whether a child is comfortable.

Do children use ChatGPT in class?

No adult AI accounts are used by under-18s with us. Claude's terms require users to be 18, and ChatGPT needs a parent's permission for minors. Children learn to reason through code themselves.

How do we pay?

Monthly, by card, in US dollars. A card from your home country is fine; your bank converts the charge. The first class is free and needs no card.
