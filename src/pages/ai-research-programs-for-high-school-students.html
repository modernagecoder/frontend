AI research programs for high school students

Plenty of teenagers now train a model. Far fewer turn one into research, which means asking a question nobody has answered, testing it fairly, and writing up what happened, including what failed. American high schoolers have a handful of serious places where that kind of work is read and judged. This guide takes the ones where machine learning fits naturally, quotes their current rules, and is plain about the new part: what each organizer lets a student do with generative AI, and what it does not.

Competitions and programs are different things

STS and ISEF judge finished work. They do not supply a mentor or a lab; the student arrives with a project. RSI, RISE and PRIMES are the opposite: they place a student with a mentor and the research happens inside the program.

A realistic plan for an AI project aimed at STS spreads the work over a junior year and a summer, sometimes inside one of the mentored programs. The two kinds fit together rather than compete.

What fits an AI project, and what does not

PRIMES-USA currently describes its research projects as being in mathematics and bioinformatics, so it suits a student whose AI interest is mathematical rather than applied. Read its own page for the current list before applying.

At RISE, computer science is one of the internship areas the program lists. At RSI, each student works on an individual project with a mentor who is an experienced scientist or researcher. Both are summer programs, so their deadlines fall in the school year before.

What this means for an AI project

The rules draw a clear line: "Use of AI tools for student research projects is permitted, and should be disclosed and appropriately cited." A model you train is a research tool. A chatbot that writes your discussion section is not allowed.

Keep a prompt log from the first week. It costs a minute a day and it is the evidence the rules ask for if any code or analysis advice came from an AI.

The numbers, read honestly

Society for Science says around 2,600 students enter each year and 40 become finalists. That is 40 / 2,600, about 1.5 in 100, our arithmetic. Entering is still worth it: the rule book itself is a short course in research ethics.

Top 300 scholars are announced on January 7, 2027 and the top 40 finalists on January 21, 2027. Finals Week runs March 11-17, 2027 in Washington, D.C.

Choosing between ROBO and Software Design

If the question is about the learning itself (does this model generalize, which features matter, how does accuracy change with data size), the Machine Learning subcategory is the natural home.

If the project is an app that happens to use a trained model, and the contribution is the software, Software Design may fit better. Society for Science asks students to choose the primary subcategory rather than Other.

Getting there

The 2026 finalists were selected from 365 affiliate fairs. Your school or district fair is the first step; ask your science teacher which fair it feeds.

Local and state fairs may use their own categories, and Society for Science says so. Check your affiliated fair's list before you print a board.

Sources: ISEF categories and subcategories and the full category definitions, read 17 October 2026. Competition is a good reason to finish a project; for event-style alternatives see hackathons for high school students and the Congressional App Challenge guide.

A worked example

The first experiment every AI research project needs: beat a baseline

Judges ask one question of every machine learning project before any other: better than what? This script is ours. It compares three versions of one model against a baseline that always guesses the most common answer, using five-fold cross-validation on the handwritten digits dataset that ships with scikit-learn. Results below are from our run of 17 October 2026 (scikit-learn 1.7.2, Python 3.13).

Reading the result like a researcher

The digits dataset has 10 classes, so a model that always picks the most common digit should be right about 1 / 10 = 0.1 of the time. The baseline's 0.101 confirms the test is set up correctly before anything else is believed.

k=1 and k=5 differ by 0.001 in their means while their spreads overlap almost entirely. A careful write-up says they performed about the same on this data, not that k=1 won.

Turning it into a question

The script is a method, not a finding; any student can run it. Research starts when the student changes something nobody has tested in their setting: their own dataset, a deliberate imbalance, or noise added to see where the model breaks.

scikit-learn describes DummyClassifier as a simple baseline to compare against other classifiers. Using one is the cheapest way to look rigorous, because it is rigorous.

  1. Write the question in one sentence. If it needs two, it is two projects.
  2. Name the baseline before training anything. Most frequent class, a simple rule, or last year's method.
  3. Fix the split. Cross-validation or a held-out test set, chosen before you see any result.
  4. Report the spread, not just the mean. Overlapping spreads mean no clear winner.
  5. Change one thing at a time. Two changes at once make the result impossible to explain.
  6. Write down what failed. A dead end that is explained is a result, and a judge reading the report will want to see it.
  7. Log every AI assist. Prompts, dates and which code came from where, as the STS rules require.

Getting help the right way

What a teacher can and cannot do for a student researcher

Every program here expects the work to be the student's. The STS rules require entrants to disclose support of any kind, naming paid support and AI use among the examples. That shapes how we teach.

What we do

Teach the skills: Python, scikit-learn, experimental design, statistics, and how to read a paper. A student who understands cross-validation can design their own experiment.

Review the method, not write it. We ask the questions a judge would ask and the student answers them, in their own code and their own words.

What we never do

Choose the question, run the experiments, or draft any part of a report or application. That would break the rules and it would not be the student's research.

Promise a result. No teacher can promise that a project places, and we do not. A family should disclose our classes if a program asks about outside support.

If your teenager is still at the project stage, our AI projects for high school students page sets out a ladder from a first model to research-grade work. For extracurriculars beyond research, see computer science extracurriculars.

For parents

Stage 3 is the real turning point. Ask your teenager what their model was compared with; a clear answer means they are thinking like a researcher.

A project that does not place can still be excellent research. The write-up is what lasts.

For students

Pick a question small enough to finish. A tight question with an honest answer beats a grand one that stops halfway.

Questions from families

AI research programs for teenagers: common questions

What are AI research programs for high school students?

They are programs where a student's own research is mentored or judged. In the US, Regeneron STS and Regeneron ISEF judge finished projects, while RSI and BU RISE place juniors in summer research. Machine learning work fits all of them.

When is the Regeneron STS 2027 deadline?

The 2027 rules say applications and recommendations are due on Thursday, November 5, 2026 at 8:00 pm Eastern Time.

Can I use ChatGPT or other generative AI in an STS project?

Only in limited ways. The 2027 rules forbid generative AI for writing application answers, drafting the Research Report or generating citations. AI tools may be used in the research itself if disclosed and cited, and AI-written code needs a citation and a prompt log.

Which ISEF category is a machine learning project?

Usually Robotics and Intelligent Machines, subcategory Machine Learning, which Society for Science defines as the construction and/or study of algorithms that can learn from data. An app built around a model may fit Software Design instead.

Can a team enter Regeneron STS?

No. The rules say applicants must complete individual research projects, and students may not split a team project and enter as individuals.

Who can apply to RSI 2027?

CEE says high school juniors are eligible and seniors are not. Applications for RSI 2027 are open through 11:59pm on December 11, 2026, and in 2027 CEE is adding a site at Stanford for 50 scholars.

Does my teenager need a university lab to do AI research?

No. Many machine learning questions can be tested on a laptop with public datasets. STS also permits research done alongside adult researchers in an institution, as long as the student's own role is clear.

Will your classes help my child win?

We cannot promise any result, and we never write a student's research. We teach the methods, coding and statistics so the student can design and defend their own work.

Are you connected with Society for Science or CEE?

No. We are an independent online school. Every rule on this page comes from the organizers' own published pages, which are linked so you can check them.

When are classes for US students?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays.

How much do your classes cost?

The first class is free. After that, teaching is paid monthly, and the fees section of this page gives the figure in US dollars. Programs and competitions set any costs of their own.
