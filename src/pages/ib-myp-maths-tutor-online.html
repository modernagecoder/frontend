What is IB MYP maths?

IB MYP mathematics is the mathematics course of the International Baccalaureate Middle Years Programme, for students aged 11 to 16. The IB's subject brief says its framework "outlines four branches of mathematical study": number, algebra, geometry and trigonometry, and statistics and probability, and that schools can offer it at two levels of challenge, standard and extended. Students are assessed against four equally weighted criteria, A to D, each with eight achievement levels, and in MYP year 5 may sit an on-screen examination of three 40-mark tasks. Modern Age Coders teaches MYP maths live online, in a group or 1-on-1, through our lower secondary maths mastery course.

The framework

What is in the IB MYP maths syllabus?

There is no single fixed syllabus. The IB sets a framework of four branches, key and related concepts and global contexts, and each school chooses its content within it. The brief says the framework "offers schools flexibility to determine engaging, relevant, challenging and significant content that meets local and national curriculum requirements."

What this means for a family

Two MYP schools can teach different topics in the same year. A student moving school, or a family comparing notes, should ask for the unit plan rather than assume. The four branches are fixed; the order and examples are not.

That flexibility is why a tutor who only knows one country's exam syllabus can be unhelpful. We start from the school's unit and the criterion being assessed, then teach the maths underneath.

The concept-led style

The brief says the MYP "promotes sustained inquiry in mathematics by developing conceptual understanding within global contexts". In practice a unit on linear functions might sit inside a context such as fairness and development, and the summative task will ask students to apply the maths there.

Students who are fluent with procedures but have never had to explain why a method works can find this uncomfortable at first. That is the gap we work on.

Two levels

What is the difference between MYP standard and extended maths?

The brief answers in two sentences: "Standard mathematics aims to provide a sound knowledge of basic mathematical principles. Extended mathematics supplements the standard curriculum with additional topics and skills, providing greater breadth and depth of study."

What the brief does and does not say

It says schools "can develop courses at two level of challenge", so extended is an addition to standard, not a separate subject. On-screen examinations, it says, "are available in mathematics and extended mathematics".

It does not give a list of extended-only topics in the copy we read, and we will not invent one. Your school's scope and sequence shows which topics it treats as extended.

How we decide which to teach

We ask which level the school has placed the student in, and look at the last unit's task. A student in extended maths who is struggling usually has a gap in algebraic fluency from an earlier year; we repair that first.

A standard student who is secure and curious can be taught extended topics alongside, without pressure to change sets. The decision about sets belongs to the school.

How work is judged

What are the MYP maths criteria A, B, C and D?

Four equally weighted criteria. The brief says each "has eight possible achievement levels (1 to 8), divided into four bands with unique descriptors that teachers use to make judgments about students' work."

The four MYP mathematics criteria, as the IB subject brief describes them
CriterionNameWhat the brief says students do
AKnowing and understandingSelect and apply mathematics to solve problems in familiar and unfamiliar situations, across the four branches
BInvestigating patternsWork through investigations to become risk-takers, inquirers and critical thinkers
CCommunicatingUse appropriate mathematical language and different forms of representation, orally and in writing
DApplying mathematics in real-life contextsTransfer theoretical knowledge to real situations, apply problem-solving strategies, draw valid conclusions and reflect on results

Not a percentage

A test can score 6 out of 8 on Criterion A and 3 out of 8 on C. The second number is often the more useful one for a student to hear about.

Bands, not ticks

Each criterion's eight levels sit in four bands, each with its own descriptor. Moving up a band usually needs a different kind of work, not more of the same.

Teacher judgement

Teachers match work to descriptors. We never grade a school task or predict a level; we teach the skills the descriptors name.

Criterion C is where marks hide

Students who get right answers but write only the answers often sit low on communicating. The brief asks for appropriate mathematical language and different forms of representation. In our lessons that means: say what you are finding, show the method in order, and state the result with units.

It is a habit, so we practise it on ordinary exercises, not only on assessed tasks.

Criterion D needs a judgement

Applying maths to a real situation ends with a question the maths cannot answer alone: is this result reasonable here? The brief asks students to "draw valid conclusions and reflect upon their results".

We teach students to finish every applied problem with one sentence on whether the answer makes sense in context, and one on what the model leaves out.

MYP year 5

How does the MYP maths on-screen exam work?

It is optional for a school and its students. The brief says students "seeking IB MYP course results or the IB MYP Certificate" complete "an end-of-course on-screen examination", which in mathematics comprises three tasks.

MYP mathematics on-screen examination, as the IB subject brief describes it
TaskCriteria assessedMarks
Knowing and understandingA and C40
Investigating patternsB and C40
Applying mathematics in real-life contextsC and D40

What else the brief says

On-screen examinations "address all four branches of mathematical study and may include any topics or skills in the MYP mathematics framework." They are available at both levels, mathematics and extended mathematics.

For the MYP certificate the brief lists two-hour on-screen examinations in language and literature, individuals and societies, sciences, mathematics and interdisciplinary learning, an ePortfolio, a moderated personal project, and service as action.

What we could not check

Session dates, grade boundaries and any changes since this brief was published sit on IB pages that refused our requests. Your school's MYP coordinator has them.

We do not reproduce IB examination tasks. Our lessons use problems we write, at the level and in the style of the criteria, and we point students to the practice materials their school provides.

Notice that Criterion C appears in all three tasks. Clear communication is assessed every time the student writes maths in the exam, which is one more reason we build it into ordinary lessons from MYP 1.

Our lesson family

How do you investigate a pattern for MYP Criterion B?

Move from a few cases, to a table, to a general rule, to a check, to a reason why. The investigation below is ours, written for this page; it is not an IB task.

  1. Ask a question. Our example: how many diagonals does a polygon with n sides have?
  2. Try small cases carefully. A triangle has 0 diagonals, a quadrilateral 2, a pentagon 5, a hexagon 9. Draw them; count twice.
  3. Tabulate. Sides 3, 4, 5, 6 give diagonals 0, 2, 5, 9. The differences are 2, 3, 4, so the pattern is not linear.
  4. Conjecture a rule. From each vertex you can draw a diagonal to every other vertex except itself and its two neighbours: n minus 3 diagonals. With n vertices that is n(n minus 3), but each diagonal has been counted from both ends, so the conjecture is n(n minus 3) divided by 2.
  5. Test it on a new case. For an octagon the rule gives 8 x 5 / 2 = 20. Draw an octagon and count: 20.
  6. Justify. Explain in words why the rule must hold for every n, which the vertex argument above already does. A rule that fits the table is a conjecture; a reason makes it a result.
Our checklist for an investigation, mapped to the criteria the brief names
StepWhat to show (ours)Criterion it serves
Cases and tableOrganised data, not scattered sumsB
RuleA general statement in words and in algebraB and C
TestA new case not used to find the ruleB
ReasonWhy the rule works, not only that it doesB and C
PresentationLabelled diagrams, correct notation, a clear conclusionC

Our practice tasks. Investigate each one through all six steps. (1) Add the first n odd numbers: 1, 1 + 3, 1 + 3 + 5 and so on. What do you notice, and why? (2) How many handshakes happen when n people each shake hands once with everyone else? Compare with the diagonals rule. (3) How many squares of any size are on an n by n grid? Our hints: (1) the totals are square numbers, and an L-shaped layer of dots shows why; (2) n(n minus 1) divided by 2, because each person shakes n minus 1 hands and each handshake is counted twice; (3) the totals 1, 5, 14, 30 are sums of square numbers.

Why this lesson family

Investigating patterns is one of the four criteria and one of the three exam tasks, and it rewards a skill that school timetables rarely leave time to practise slowly: going from examples to a reason.

It also builds the algebra extended maths needs. Writing n(n minus 3) divided by 2 from a counting argument is algebra used for something.

Where we stop

If a student brings a school investigation that will be assessed, we do not work on that task with them. We teach the method on a different problem, like the ones here, and the student applies it alone.

That keeps the assessed work the student's own, which is the point of assessing it.

MYP maths questions

What families ask about IB MYP maths

What topics are in the IB MYP maths syllabus?

The IB framework has four branches: number, algebra, geometry and trigonometry, and statistics and probability. Each school chooses the content and order within that framework, so ask your school for its unit plans.

What is the difference between MYP standard and extended maths?

The IB brief says standard maths gives a sound knowledge of basic principles, while extended maths adds topics and skills for greater breadth and depth. Extended builds on standard rather than replacing it.

How is MYP maths graded?

Against four equally weighted criteria: knowing and understanding, investigating patterns, communicating, and applying mathematics in real-life contexts. Each criterion has eight achievement levels in four bands.

Is there an MYP maths exam?

Schools may enter MYP year 5 students for an on-screen examination, available in mathematics and extended mathematics. The IB brief describes three tasks of 40 marks each.

Are there MYP maths past papers?

We do not reproduce IB examination material. Your school's MYP coordinator can tell you which IB practice materials the school uses; our lessons use problems we write ourselves.

How many hours of maths does MYP require?

The IB brief says at least 50 hours of teaching time for each subject in each year, and recommends 70 hours of guided learning a year in MYP years 4 and 5 for students in eAssessment.

Can a tutor help with an MYP maths investigation?

We teach the method of investigating on different problems and never work on an assessed school task. The student then applies the method alone, so the assessed work stays their own.

My child is moving from GCSE or a national curriculum into MYP. Where do we start?

Usually with written reasoning and investigation, which national courses practise less. A free diagnostic lesson shows whether the maths content itself also needs work.

Does MYP maths prepare students for the IB Diploma?

The IB brief says MYP mathematics on-screen examinations are aligned with understanding and skills that prepare students for IB Diploma Programme maths courses. Most MYP students go on to one of the two Diploma maths courses.

When are your lessons?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. UK families book in UK time; families elsewhere book in their own time zone.
