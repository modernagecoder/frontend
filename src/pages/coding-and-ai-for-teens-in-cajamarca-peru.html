Devices

Internet use has risen more than fourfold in ten years, to 67.8% of people aged six and over. Computers have not kept pace: 17.5% of homes had one in 2025, about half the national share.

A teenager who codes needs a keyboard for at least two hours a week. A shared family laptop, a relative's computer or a quiet hour at a cabina can work. A phone alone does not, and we will not pretend otherwise.

English

EF's English Proficiency Index 2025 scores the Cajamarca region 383, in its very low band and the lowest of the Peruvian regions it lists. Peru scores 519 and Lima city 555. EF publishes no city figure for Cajamarca.

On the regular school day, Inglés gets three of the 35 weekly hours in secundaria. A teenager who follows simple spoken English will cope and improve quickly with code on the screen; one who follows almost none should start one to one, or wait.

Secundaria

Is programming taught in Cajamarca's secundaria schools?

No. Cajamarca follows the national curriculum, which has no programming area at any grade.

What the week holds

On the regular school day, every secundaria grade has a 35-hour week: four hours each of Matemática and Comunicación, three of Inglés and two of Educación para el Trabajo. None of these is a programming class.

Digital skill is a transversal competency of the CNEB, number 28, used across areas rather than taught in one. A teenager can finish 5° de secundaria without ever writing code.

Where that leaves a teen

University courses in engineering, economics, sciences and health increasingly assume some programming or data skill. A teenager who arrives having written real programs starts ahead.

Admission itself is by public competition with a compulsory knowledge exam, under the Ley Universitaria, and those exams are in Spanish. Our classes run beside school, the university alone decides who gets in, and we make no promise about it.

How Peru's main admission exams work, from the universities' own pages, is explained in Peru university admission exams explained.

A real Cajamarca project

How does a university in Cajamarca turn class hours into credits?

The Universidad Nacional de Cajamarca publishes the list of every undergraduate course section it ran in the 2026-I semester. Our teenagers use it to do what scientists and AI models both do: guess a rule, test it on every row, and keep the exceptions in sight.

The data

The university released "Dataset Cursos pregrado matrícula semestre académico 2026 I" as open data on datosabiertos.gob.pe, under ODC-By, which lets anyone reuse it with credit. Our copy, unc-cursos-pregrado-2026-I.csv, has 2,882 rows, one per course section, cut on 6 October 2026. Each row gives the course, its credits, its weekly hours of theory and of practice, and how many students enrolled in that section. No student can be identified.

It is a useful look at university for a teenager: 1,653 different courses in 30 professional schools, with a typical section of 16 students. We have no connection with the university.

The guess

Looking at a few rows, a teen spots a pattern: two hours of theory plus two of practice gives three credits, the commonest row of all with 1,334 sections, and three of theory plus two of practice gives four. Our guess, before testing it, is that one hour of theory is worth one credit and one hour of practice half a credit.

The first program turns that guess into a function and counts how many of the 2,882 sections it fits exactly.

Two thirds right is not bad for a guess, but 961 misses is too many to ignore. Printing a few of them shows the cause: most have an odd number of practice hours, so the guess gives a half, such as 3 theory and 3 practice giving 4.5. The university's credits are whole numbers. So it must round, but which way?

Rather than argue, Version 2 tests three rival rules side by side: the exact guess, the guess rounded up and the guess rounded down. It counts the misses for each, and then lists the rows that even the strongest of the three cannot explain.

Three guessed credit rules tested on 2,882 course sections (our calculation)
RuleSections it missesSections it fits
Theory + practice / 2, exactly9611,921
The same, rounded up8372,045
The same, rounded down1992,683

Course list for 2026-I from the Universidad Nacional de Cajamarca, reused under its Open Data Commons Attribution licence. The rules are our guesses and every count is ours. Hours are weekly hours as the university's data dictionary defines them. Four sections carry 0 credits, among them Deporte and Danzas Típicas Peruanas.

Rounding down wins clearly: it fits 2,683 of 2,882 sections, about 93%. But 199 sections still break it, and they are not random. The largest group, 122 sections with 2 hours of theory and 3 of practice, gets 4 credits where the rule says 3. Perhaps those courses follow an older study plan, or a school sets its own credits; the file does not say, and an honest analyst says "we do not know" rather than inventing a reason.

This is how an AI model should be judged too. A model that is right 93% of the time is useful, and the 7% it gets wrong is where the interesting questions are. Teens who learn to list the exceptions, instead of hiding them, become the people who can be trusted with real data.

  1. Ages 13 to 14, on paper. For five rows of the table, work out the guess by hand, then round it both ways. Which rule would you have bet on before seeing the counts?
  2. First Python. Run Version 1, then print the first ten rows it misses, with their hours. Write down the pattern you see before reading on.
  3. Ages 15 and over. Group the 199 remaining misses by professional school. Are they spread out, or concentrated in a few schools?
  4. Going further. The file also gives the number of students in each section. Add them all up: you get 52,488. Explain why that is a number of seats, not of students, and why the two are different.

AI for teens

Which AI tools can a Cajamarca teenager use, and how?

AI is worth learning properly, from the inside. Using a chatbot to do the homework is not the same thing, and the tools set their own age limits.

Age limits

Under 18, ChatGPT may only be used with a parent's permission, and Claude is restricted to adults. Our mentors keep every under-18 learner to tools their age permits.

Our AI tools age guide lists the rules tool by tool, for parents who want to check.

How we use AI in class

Teens learn how a model is built: examples, a rule, a measure of error, and adjustment. Then any AI answer is treated like the credit rule above, as a guess to be tested on cases where the right answer is known.

Contests set their own rules, and UTEC's OPI forbids generative AI as a way to solve its tasks. Teens who can reason unaided are ready either way.

At what times could a teenager in Cajamarca have class?

At any hour the family books. Cajamarca uses Peru time throughout the year, and the mentor follows it.

Peru is fixed at UTC minus 5 hours and has used no summer time since 1994 (see the America/Lima zone of the tz database; legal time comes from INACAL). India is fixed at UTC plus 5 hours 30 minutes, which keeps it 10 hours 30 minutes ahead of Cajamarca.

Because both clocks stay put all year, a booked slot never drifts. The family books in Cajamarca time and the mentor sees India time. The long summer break from January is a natural moment to begin, and the same hour can carry on into the school year.

Cajamarca families ask

Coding and AI for teens in Cajamarca: questions and answers

Are the classes in Spanish?

No. Every class is live and online, and the only teaching language is English. Mentors slow down, write key words in the chat, adapt to your teenager's pace and accent, and answer Spanish questions with the English words. We check at the free class that your teen can follow.

Can a teenager in Cajamarca find coding classes?

Yes, through live online classes. A teen in the city, in Los Baños del Inca or in a provincial town logs in from a computer at whatever hour has been booked.

EF scores Cajamarca's English very low. Is this realistic?

For many teens, yes, because code is visual and the vocabulary is small; for some, not yet. EF's 2025 regional score for Cajamarca is 383. Start one to one, or book the free class and let the mentor tell you honestly.

We do not have a computer. What can we do?

A phone is not enough for coding. A shared or borrowed laptop for two hours a week works well. INEI counted a computer in 17.5% of Cajamarca homes in 2025, so many families plan this way.

Does secundaria in Cajamarca teach programming?

No. The national secundaria plan has no programming area, and digital skills are a transversal competency rather than a subject.

Will this help my teenager get into university?

It helps them arrive prepared for courses that use programming and data. Admission is by each university's own exam, in Spanish, and the university decides. We teach alongside school and promise no admission result.

What time are classes for Cajamarca?

Any day, any time, flexible: choose a batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at the hour that suits your family. With India ten and a half hours ahead all year round, 09:00 in Cajamarca means 19:30 for the mentor.

Can my teenager use ChatGPT in class?

Only with a parent's permission, as ChatGPT requires under 18, and never as a way to skip thinking. Tools for adults only, such as Claude, are not used with under-18 learners.

How are fees paid?

By card each month, in US dollars; the soles amount is your bank's conversion. From an Indian connection the page shows rupees. No card is taken for the trial, which is free.

Is there a certificate?

Courses that include one give our own certificate of completion. It is not a Peruvian school or university qualification and is issued by no ministry or school; it shows the work your teen actually did.
