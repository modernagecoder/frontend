## Is Informatik a compulsory subject in Hessen?

No. The rules below are summarised from the Gesellschaft für Informatik's Informatik-Monitor 2026/27, which compiles each Land's regulations. Modern Age Coders is independent of the GI and of the Hessian ministry.

### Seven school types, no requirement

After Grundschule, Hessen has seven secondary school types: Hauptschule, Mittelstufenschule, verbundene Haupt- und Realschule, Realschule, integrated and cooperative Gesamtschule, and Gymnasium. The monitor states that across all of them there is no compulsory Informatik.

At the Gymnasium, Informatik can be offered as an elective of one hour a week from grade 7. In the Haupt- and Realschule tracks it can be set up as a Wahlpflicht subject, but the monitor notes there is no curriculum of its own for those tracks.

### A trial that left the timetable

From 2022/23 Hessen trialled a subject called "Digitale Welt" at pilot schools. The monitor reports that from 2026/27 it may only be offered within the all-day programme, so it is no longer part of those schools' timetables.

Nationally, the GI names Hessen among six Länder with no compulsory Informatik for all, and among three where no binding plan to introduce it is known.

**Where Informatik can appear in a Hessen school career**

| Stage | Informatik |
|---|---|
| Grundschule, grades 1 to 4 | No subject |
| Gymnasium, from grade 7 | Optional Wahlunterricht, one hour a week, if offered |
| Haupt- and Realschule tracks | Wahlpflicht possible, without its own curriculum |
| Einführungsphase | Two to three hours if offered; no prior Informatik needed |

Source: Gesellschaft für Informatik, Informatik-Monitor 2026/27, Hessen page and national comparison, read 2 November 2026. The Gymnasium middle years run for five years (grades 5 to 9) or six (grades 5 to 10), or both in parallel.

The monitor's remark about the Einführungsphase is telling: no prior knowledge from the Sekundarstufe I is required. It has to be that way, because for many pupils there was none. A pupil who arrives in grade 10 or 11 already able to program, read data and reason about algorithms starts the Oberstufe course with a real advantage, and that is what our Companion course builds.

The Hessen page gives about 10 percent of Sekundarstufe II pupils taking Informatik in 2024/25 and about 5 percent of Abitur candidates with it as an exam subject. In 2025/26 the Land had 485 qualified Informatik teachers, about 2 percent of its teaching staff.

## When is the Informatik Abitur in Hessen in 2027?

Hessen's Landesabitur sets Informatik centrally, the same paper across the Land, and the ministry has published the days for 2027.

### How it is examined

A Leistungsfach in Informatik is examined in writing. A Grundkurs taken without a break through the Einführungs- and Qualifikationsphase can be the third, written exam subject, the fourth, oral one, or the fifth, as a presentation, special learning achievement or oral exam.

The monitor adds that Informatik counts towards the overall Abitur grade like any other exam subject, and can partly replace a natural science in the course requirements.

### What is on the desk

The written tasks are set centrally and are the same throughout Hessen. Practical work on a computer is not permitted.

The only aids are a list of operators, the instruction verbs that tell a candidate what kind of answer is wanted, and a dictionary. Everything else must be in the candidate's head and pen.

A central paper written by hand, with an operator list and nothing else, rewards a particular kind of practice: reading an operator and answering exactly that, writing code that is correct without a compiler, and explaining a design in words. ## How much of Hessen is forest? A table-reading project

The Hessisches Statistisches Landesamt publishes its Kreiszahlen, a workbook of figures for every Kreis, twice a year. Table 38 shows how the Land's surface is used. Before any program can answer a question about it, the pupil has to read two small German words in the header.

### The data

We downloaded the October 2026 edition of the Hessische Kreiszahlen on 3 November 2026 and exported Table 38, "Bodenfläche in Hessen 2025 nach Nutzungsart", to bodenflaeche_2025.tsv. The series is listed under CC BY on Open Data Hessen, and the workbook allows reproduction with the source named. The figures are in hectares, as at 31 December 2025.

For each Kreis and for the whole Land there is a total, then columns for settlement, transport, vegetation, farmland, forest and water.

### The trap in the header

Above the columns, the table says "davon", meaning "of which, making up the whole". Above farmland and forest it says "darunter", meaning "among which, a selection". Farmland and forest are part of vegetation, not separate from it.

Version 1 ignores the header and adds every column for the Land. The result is about 1.8 times the size of Hessen.

**Version 1: add every column** (Python)
```python
import csv

areas = {}
with open("bodenflaeche_2025.tsv", encoding="utf-8") as src:
    for row in csv.reader(src, delimiter="\t"):
        if len(row) > 8 and row[0].isdigit() and row[2].isdigit():
            areas[(row[0], row[1])] = [int(x) for x in row[2:9]]

total, settled, transport, vegetation, farmland, forest, water = areas[("000000", "Land Hessen")]
print("Total area:", total, "ha")
print("Sum of all six columns:", settled + transport + vegetation + farmland + forest + water, "ha")
```
It prints Total area: 2111563 ha and Sum of all six columns: 3826423 ha.

The program has counted most of the land twice: once as vegetation, and again as farmland or forest. Version 2 follows the header. It adds only the "davon" columns, checks that farmland and forest fit inside vegetation, and then tests every row of the table the same way.

**Version 2 (continues Version 1): respect davon and darunter** (Python)
```python
print("Sum of the davon columns:", settled + transport + vegetation + water, "ha")
print("Farmland + forest inside vegetation:", farmland + forest, "of", vegetation, "ha")

gaps = {key[1]: v[1] + v[2] + v[3] + v[6] - v[0] for key, v in areas.items()}
print("Rows off by rounding:", sum(1 for g in gaps.values() if g), "largest gap:", max(abs(g) for g in gaps.values()), "ha")
```
It prints Sum of the davon columns: 2111563 ha, Farmland + forest inside vegetation: 1714860 of 1740399 ha and Rows off by rounding: 13 largest gap: 1 ha.

For the whole Land the "davon" columns add up exactly. In 13 rows they are out by a single hectare, which is what you expect when every column is rounded to whole hectares on its own. That is the right moment to tell pupils a rule statisticians live by: published parts are rounded separately, so they do not always add up to the published total, and nobody should "fix" the total to match.

**Version 3 (continues Version 2): forest share by Kreis** (Python)
```python
kreise = {key[1]: v for key, v in areas.items() if key[0][0] in "456" and key[0][1:3] != "00"}
share = {name: v[5] / v[0] * 100 for name, v in kreise.items()}
print("Kreise and kreisfreie Städte:", len(kreise))
for name in sorted(share, key=share.get, reverse=True)[:3]:
    print(name, round(share[name], 1), "% forest")
```
It prints Kreise and kreisfreie Städte: 26, Odenwaldkreis 56.3 % forest, Rheingau-Taunus-Kreis 53.9 % forest and Hochtaunuskreis 49.1 % forest.

1. **Grades 5 and 6, on paper.** A class of 28 has 15 girls and 13 boys; "darunter" 6 play football. Which numbers add up to 28, and why can you not add the 6?
2. **Grades 7 and 8, Python.** Run Version 1, then find the line in the table header that explains the wrong answer. Write Version 2 before you read ours.
3. **Grades 9 and 10.** Version 3 ranks by forest share. Rank the same rows by forest area in hectares. Which Kreis moves the most, and why?
4. **Oberstufe.** Compute the forest share for Frankfurt am Main, Stadt, and for the Land as a whole. Explain in two sentences why the Land figure is not the average of the 26 rows.
