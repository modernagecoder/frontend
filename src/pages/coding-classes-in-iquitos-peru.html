Iquitos in figures

How big is Iquitos?

The 2017 census counted 377,609 people in the city of Iquitos, the eighth largest in Peru. The 2025 census has so far been published by department, province and district, so those figures carry their own year below.

Districts that share one city

The district of Iquitos holds the old centre, but the city also covers Punchana, Belén and San Juan Bautista. A class at home is the same distance from all of them.

Outside the city, Maynas runs along rivers where families may be hours from the nearest academy of any kind. Wherever a usable connection reaches, so does a live class.

A region that is catching up

MINEDU's report on ENLA 2024 singled out Loreto: "las regiones Loreto y Ucayali son las que más han elevado su promedio en lectura y matemática". The two Amazon regions raised their average scores in reading and maths more than any others.

That progress is a good base for coding. A child who is gaining confidence in maths is ready to see numbers move on a screen and to make them obey a rule.

School and computers

Do schools in Iquitos teach coding?

Programming is not a school subject anywhere in Peru, Loreto included. Families who want it look for it outside the timetable.

What the curriculum asks for

The national curriculum (CNEB) treats digital skill as one of two transversal competencies, number 28, shared by every area rather than taught as its own. Its capacities cover using digital spaces, handling information and making digital objects. No programming language appears in its standards.

In secundaria the regular week runs to 35 hours, with Matemática and Comunicación at 4 hours each, Inglés at 3 and Educación para el Trabajo at 2. Informatics is absent.

Connecting schools first

MINEDU announced in September 2026 that it "pondrá en marcha un plan para conectar 55 000 escuelas públicas a internet satelital". In much of Loreto the first task is a reliable connection, before any question of what to teach on it.

At home the national picture is uneven too. INEI found that in the second quarter of 2026 internet reached 84.7% of homes in Lima Metropolitana, 66.2% of homes in other urban areas and 27.1% of rural homes, and that 36.4% of homes in Peru had at least one computer.

Connection and device

What does a child in Iquitos need for an online coding class?

Less than many families fear. A computer matters more than a fast plan, and a steady connection matters more than a fast one.

  1. A laptop or desktop. Scratch runs in a browser, and Python can be installed or used online. A phone is fine for joining the call but too small for writing code; a tablet with a keyboard is a workable stopgap for under nines.
  2. Headphones with a microphone. In a busy house, or under a loud rain on a tin roof, they make the mentor's English much easier to follow.
  3. A connection that holds for an hour. Video can drop to audio when the signal weakens; the shared screen and the code stay visible. If power or signal cuts out, a one-to-one class can be moved.
  4. A quiet corner at a regular time. The same hour each week builds the habit faster than any app reminder.
  5. A parent nearby for the youngest. Six and seven year olds do better with an adult in the room for the first few classes.

The free first class doubles as a technical check. If audio, screen sharing or the code editor misbehave on your equipment, the mentor sorts it out with you before anything is paid for.

Language of the classes

Are the classes in Spanish?

No. We teach live, online and only in English, and we are clear about it from the first message.

How mentors make English easier

Mentors are based in India. They use short sentences, show as well as tell on a shared screen, and pause so the learner can answer. A child who mixes Spanish into a reply is understood, and gets the English phrase back to try.

The vocabulary of code is small. Words like print, if, else and repeat come up hundreds of times, and children learn them by using them.

Two skills in one hour

For many Iquitos families the English is part of the appeal. The child practises listening and speaking with a patient adult while building something they care about, which is often easier than a grammar lesson.

EF's English Proficiency Index 2025 puts Peru in its moderate band, with a score of 519 and 52nd place of 123 countries; Iquitos is not among the eight cities it scores. We plan for learners whose English is still growing.

An Amazon data project

How many kinds of animal can you hear in the Amazon sound library?

The Instituto de Investigaciones de la Amazonía Peruana publishes a catalogue of its animal sound recordings as open data. It is a perfect file for learning that counting is harder than it looks.

The data

The IIAP describes the dataset, on Peru's national open-data platform, as a "Catálogo de 193 registros de sonidos de fauna amazónica", with birds, frogs, insects and bats among others, recorded at the Estación Biológica Allpahuayo in Loreto. It carries the Open Data Commons Attribution Licence (ODC-By). We downloaded catalogo_fonoteca_iiap_0.csv on 9 November 2026.

Every row has a recording code, a scientific name in the column titulo and, usually, a Spanish common name: Tucán de pecho blanco, Rana Gladiadora, Mielero verde. The catalogue marks every identification as still awaiting taxonomic verification, so our counts describe the file, not the forest.

The question

A ten year old asked: how many different animals are in there? The first answer, 193, counts recordings. Some animals were recorded twice, so Version 1 also counts distinct names with a set.

Then a harder problem appears. Some names are not species at all but labels for sounds not yet identified, such as "Sonoespecie sp3" or "Saguinus sp.", a tamarin whose species is unknown. Those should not count as named species.

In scientific naming, "sp." after a genus means "some species of this genus, not yet identified", and labels like sp1 and sp2 number several such unknowns. So a student writes a quick rule: if the second word starts with "sp", it is a placeholder.

Look at the first two names in the list. Attila spadiceus is a real bird with a full species name, and so is Chlorophanes spiza, the Mielero verde. Their second words just happen to begin with "sp". The rule was tested only on the names it was meant to catch, never on the names it had to leave alone.

Data: IIAP, Registros de la Fonoteca del IIAP: biblioteca acústica de fauna amazónica, Plataforma Nacional de Datos Abiertos, ODC-By. All counts are ours, run with python3 -I. Of the 193 codes, 166 use the newer IIAP-FONO-2026 format and 27 older formats; among the 40 doubled names, the other 22 pair a new code with an old one.

The last line is the detective part. Eighteen names, such as the Tucán de pecho blanco at codes 0116 and 0204, appear twice with new-format codes exactly 88 apart. A gap that never varies suggests a block of entries entered twice, rather than eighteen birds that each happened to be recorded again. It could also be a deliberate second recording; the file cannot tell us which. A careful analyst reports the pattern and asks the IIAP before deleting anything.

  1. Under 8, no computer. Write ten animal names on cards, two of them twice and one as "frog, unknown". How many animals do you have? Argue about the unknown one.
  2. Scratch, 8 to 10. Build a list of names and a button that adds a name only if it is not already in the list.
  3. First Python, 10 to 12. Run Version 2 in your head on "Attila spadiceus" and "Myotis sp.". Which answer is wrong, and why?
  4. Teenagers. Write three names your rule must reject and three it must accept, before you write the rule. Then write the rule.
  5. Going further. Group the 134 named species by genus, the first word of the name. Which genus has most recordings? (In this file, the tree frogs of Boana lead.)

Every age

Which coding course suits my child, my teenager or me?

The main question is age; the second is what the learner wants to make. Our course ages are flexible by a year either way.

Children from 6

Scratch first: pictures, sounds and simple games built by snapping blocks. From about 9, a child types Python and makes quizzes and turtle drawings. Groups are by age, so younger children are never lost in a class of older ones.

Teenagers

Python as the base, then a direction: data, Java, apps or websites. Secundaria students also learn to test their own programs and to explain their reasoning, which is exactly what Version 3 above asks of them.

Adults

Anyone from 18, whether studying at a university in Iquitos or working, can begin with Python and move on to data analysis or AI tools. People start in their thirties, fifties and sixties; age is not the hurdle people expect.

Peru is on UTC minus 5 hours throughout the year without daylight saving (tz database, America/Lima). India is on UTC plus 5 hours 30 minutes. The difference is always 10 hours 30 minutes.

A time chosen once stays put, because neither country changes its clocks. You book in your own Iquitos time; the mentor sees the India equivalent. Coding classes in Iquitos: questions and answers

Are the classes in Spanish?

No. Classes are live and online and the teaching language is English only. Mentors keep to short, clear sentences, adjust to how fast and how the learner speaks, and gently turn Spanish answers into English ones.

Can a child in Iquitos learn coding online?

Yes. Children from 6 join live from home in Iquitos, Punchana, Belén, San Juan Bautista or anywhere in Loreto with a usable connection, starting in Scratch and moving to Python at about 9.

Is a phone enough?

For joining the call, yes; for writing code, not really. A laptop or desktop works much better. A tablet with a keyboard is a reasonable start for children under 9.

What if the internet or power cuts out?

Video can fall back to audio while the shared screen stays up. If a class is lost, one-to-one lessons can be rescheduled with your mentor, and group learners can catch up on what was covered.

Do schools in Iquitos teach programming?

No school in Peru has programming as a subject. The national curriculum treats digital skills as a competency across all areas, and the regular secundaria week has no informatics hours.

What time are classes?

Any day, any time, flexible: choose a batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at the hour that suits your family. Iquitos is on Peru time; India runs 10 hours 30 minutes ahead, so 09:30 in Iquitos is 20:00 there.

From what age?

Six, with Scratch. Typed Python from about 9, teenage courses from 13, and adult courses at any age from 18.

Can adults in Iquitos join?

Yes, in adult groups or one to one, usually starting with Python and moving to data analysis or AI tools for work.

How are fees paid?

Monthly, by card, in US dollars as shown on this page; your bank changes the amount into soles. Visitors from India see rupees. The first lesson is free.

Is there a certificate?

Courses that include one end with our own certificate of completion. It is not a Peruvian school qualification and is not issued or recognised by MINEDU or any university.
