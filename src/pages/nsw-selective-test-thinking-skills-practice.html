Puzzles we wrote

Spot the flaw in the argument

Each short argument below sounds reasonable but contains one weakness. The skill is naming the weakness precisely, not just feeling that something is off.

Find the conclusion

Ask what the speaker wants you to believe. It is often the sentence starting with "so" or "therefore".

Find the reasons

List what is offered as support. Then ask: if the reasons were true, would the conclusion have to be true?

Name the gap

The flaw is the step between reasons and conclusion that does not hold. Naming it exactly is what earns the mark.

Original arguments and the flaw in each
ArgumentThe flawWhy it fails
Every time our netball team wears the green bibs, we win. So the green bibs make us win.Treats two things that happen together as cause and effectThe team may wear green bibs for home games, which they win for other reasons
I read two books by this author and both were boring. Her books are all boring.Generalises from too few examplesTwo books cannot represent everything an author has written
If it rains, the oval is closed. The oval is closed today. So it must have rained.Reverses the ruleThe oval could be closed for many other reasons, such as maintenance
Only students with a signed note can go on the excursion. Lena has a signed note, so she is definitely going.Treats a requirement as a guaranteeA note is needed, but Lena could still be unwell or miss the bus
Most students in our class prefer cricket, so most children in Australia prefer cricket.Relies on an unrepresentative sampleOne class is not a fair sample of a whole country
Either we finish the project tonight or we will fail it.Offers a false choiceThere may be other options, such as finishing tomorrow before school

The third and fourth arguments are the trickiest and the most useful to understand. A rule of the form "if A, then B" does not mean "if B, then A". And a requirement, such as needing a note, is not the same as a promise. Once students can name these two patterns, a surprising number of reasoning questions become straightforward.

A good classroom routine is to make the argument stronger after naming its flaw. For the netball argument, the speaker would need to show the team also wins more with green bibs in away games. Fixing an argument proves you understood what was wrong with it.

A second puzzle type we wrote

What must be true, and what only might be

Many reasoning puzzles give a few facts and ask which statement must follow. The trap is choosing something that could be true rather than something that has to be.

Here is a short set of facts. All the children in the chess club are in Year 6. Some of the children in the chess club also play in the band. Priya is in the band. Which statement must be true? Option one says Priya is in Year 6. Option two says some band members are in Year 6. Option three says every Year 6 child plays chess. Option four says Priya plays chess.

Only option two has to be true. Some chess club members play in the band, and every chess club member is in Year 6, so those band members must be in Year 6. Options one and four might be true, but nothing says Priya is in the chess club at all. Option three reverses the first fact: the rule says chess players are in Year 6, not that Year 6 children play chess.

Drawing the facts helps. A circle for the chess club sits inside a bigger circle for Year 6, and the band circle overlaps the chess circle. Priya can be placed anywhere in the band circle, which shows at once why the statements about her are only possibilities. Students who sketch the circles before choosing make far fewer mistakes than those who reason in their heads.

Logic and code

Why programming sharpens the same thinking

Rules, conditions and careful checking are the daily work of a programmer.

In a program

An if statement is a rule: if the oval is wet, close it. Programmers learn quickly that the reverse rule is a different statement, because code that confuses the two produces bugs.

Testing code means hunting for the case that breaks an assumption, which is exactly what spotting a flawed argument asks a student to do.

In our classes

Upper-primary students write short programs with conditions and loops, then try to break each other's programs with unusual inputs.

The same students discuss arguments and puzzles aloud. Explaining why an answer is right, not only choosing it, is the habit we care about most.

Balance matters

The department warns against too much coaching. Reading widely, playing strategy games and talking through everyday decisions build the same thinking.

If practice becomes stressful, stop and play a logic game together instead.

Questions about Thinking Skills

What families ask about this section

How many questions are in the selective test Thinking Skills section?

40 multiple-choice questions in 40 minutes, each with four possible answers, according to the NSW Department of Education.

What does Thinking Skills test?

The department describes it as testing general critical thinking and problem-solving abilities.

Does my child need to learn anything new for it?

No. The department says no previous knowledge is required for this test.

Is the OC Thinking Skills section the same?

It is shorter. The Opportunity Class Placement Test has 30 Thinking Skills questions in 30 minutes, also with four options each.

Is coaching needed?

No, in the department's view: it calls coaching unnecessary and does not back it. Working through the official practice tests at home is enough for most children.

What does "spot the flaw" mean?

Finding the step in an argument where the reasons do not actually support the conclusion, such as treating two things that happen together as cause and effect.

Is the test on paper?

No. Every section is answered on screen, using a department computer at the test centre.

Can you help us choose a selective school?

No. We give no school-choice, placement or appeals advice.

Will your classes raise my child's chance of a place?

Nobody can honestly promise a place, and we do not try. We teach reasoning that is worth having anyway.

When would classes run?

We fix one weekly time after the free trial. In Sydney during daylight saving, 4 pm is 10.30 am India time.
