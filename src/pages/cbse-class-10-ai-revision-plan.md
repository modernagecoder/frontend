> A class 10 AI revision plan for CBSE 417 in 2026-27 should follow the theory marks: Advanced Concepts of Modeling (11) and Evaluating Models (10) first, then NLP (8), the AI project cycle and ethics (7), Part A employability skills (10, quick to recall) and Computer Vision (4). Over 30 days that means about 80 minutes a day, a first round of every unit by day 17, three timed full papers and a second round on the weak units. ## How should a class 10 AI revision plan follow the unit marks?

Give each unit time in proportion to its theory marks, then adjust for how hard it is to score. The 50-mark theory paper splits as below, and the day counts are the ones used in the 30-day table further down.

- Unit | Theory marks | Share of the paper | First-round days (30-day plan) | Why that many

- Unit 2: Advanced Concepts of Modeling in AI | 11 | 22% | 4 | Largest unit; the only one with two long answers in the latest blueprint

- Unit 3: Evaluating Models | 10 | 20% | 4 | Numericals need practice, not reading, so it gets the most days per mark

- Unit 6: Natural Language Processing | 8 | 16% | 3 | Normalisation steps plus Bag of Words and TFIDF tables

- Unit 1: AI Project Cycle and Ethical Frameworks | 7 | 14% | 2 | Mostly ordered lists and one case to apply them to

- Part A: five employability skills units | 10 | 20% | 2 | Short recall; kept warm in the 20-minute blocks afterwards

- Unit 5: Computer Vision | 4 | 8% | 1 | Small in theory: tasks, pixels, grayscale and RGB

- Units 4 and 7: Statistical Data, Advance Python | practical only | none of the theory | short blocks | Only if your school practical is still ahead

### Read the table as a rule, not a ratio

Units 2 and 3 together are 21 of the 40 Part B marks, more than half, so every plan below opens with them and returns to them. Part A gets fewer days per mark than its share suggests because its answers are lists you either recall or do not; a few minutes a day keeps them, where a whole day would not add much.

### What each day produces

A plan row is done when something is on paper: a set of answers, a worked numerical, a list written from memory, a marked paper. Reading alone does not count as a finished row. Anything you get wrong goes on one running page, the error list, which you will read again in the final week.

Days 1 to 17 are the first round, days 18 to 26 are papers and the second round, and the last four days are for consolidation. ## How to revise AI in 1 week: the 7-day plan

With a week left, revise in mark order and write two full papers. The table assumes about four to five hours a day for AI, the kind of gap a date sheet sometimes leaves before a paper. In 2027 it does not: counting back from 24 February, 7 days left is Wednesday 17 February, the Mathematics paper, and Science falls on 22 February. So keep the morning blocks and the two papers on the free days, and shorten the rest.

- Day | Morning, 2 hours | Afternoon, 1.5 to 2 hours | Evening, 1 hour

- 7 days left | Unit 2: learning types, neural networks, perceptron sums | Unit 3: confusion matrix and the four metrics, eight numericals | Start the error list from today's misses

- 6 days left | Unit 6: normalisation, Bag of Words, TFIDF | Unit 1: cycle, 4Ws, frameworks, bioethics case | Unit 5: tasks and pixel counts

- 5 days left | Full paper 1, timed, two hours | Correct it; rewrite each lost answer | Part A lists, all five units

- 4 days left | Weakest unit from paper 1, from the beginning | Units 2 and 3 numericals with new figures | Assertion and reason set

- 3 days left | Full paper 2, at the morning hour of the real paper | Correct it, same method | Error list read aloud

- 2 days left | Four long answers to the word limit | Thirty mixed objective items, timed | Formula sheet from memory

- 1 day left | Follow the 1-day plan below

### Why papers on days 5 and 3
Paper 1 shows the weak unit early enough to fix it on day 4. Paper 2 then checks the fix, with two days still left for anything it turns up.

### Why numericals twice
Confusion matrix, perceptron, pixel and TFIDF questions are the quickest marks once the method is automatic, and the easiest to lose by swapping two cells under time pressure.

### Why Computer Vision gets an hour
It carries 4 theory marks. An hour on its tasks and pixel counts secures them; more time would come straight out of Units 2 and 3.

Fresh numericals for day 4 are on [confusion matrix numericals](/confusion-matrix-class-10-ai-numericals), and the assertion and reason sets on [CBSE Class 10 AI assertion reason questions](/cbse-class-10-ai-assertion-reason-questions).

## The 1-day revision plan for the day before the AI paper

The day before is for recall and confidence, not new topics. The CBSE date sheet released 6 November 2026 puts the AI paper on Wednesday 24 February 2027 at 10:30 AM IST, so the day before is Tuesday 23 February; set your sleep to that hour.

- Time | Do this

- 7:30 to 9:00 AM | The one shot read-through, checkpoints answered on paper

- 9:15 to 10:15 AM | Three Unit 3 numericals and two perceptron sums, new figures

- 10:30 to 11:30 AM | Normalise one sentence, make one vector, find two TFIDF values

- 11:30 AM to 12:00 PM | Part A lists, said aloud

- 2:00 to 3:00 PM | Unit 1 stage order and frameworks; Unit 5 task table

- 3:00 to 4:00 PM | Thirty mixed objective items, timed

- 4:30 to 5:30 PM | Two long answers to 80 words, one from Unit 2

- 6:30 to 7:30 PM | Formula sheet from memory, then the error list

- 8:00 PM | Admit card, pens, school ID packed. Stop studying.

### Leave these out the day before

- A new full paper. A bad score the evening before helps nobody; papers belong to the days before.

- New sources. A different book's wording now only creates doubt about the version you know.

- The practical units. Statistical Data and Advance Python are not in the theory paper.

- A late night. Recall the next morning depends on sleep more than on one extra hour.

Every formula and rule for the 6:30 PM block is on [the Class 10 AI formula sheet](/cbse-class-10-ai-formula-sheet).

## Making the revision timetable for 417 survive a real month

Every plan slips: a school test, a fever, a hard day. What matters is deciding in advance what gives way, so the high-mark units are never the ones that lose out.

-
**Lose a day, drop from the bottom** Cut the second round of Computer Vision first, then extra Part A sessions. Never cut the numericals of Units 2 and 3 or a full paper.

-
**Correct every paper the same day or the next** An uncorrected paper is half the value. Mark it against the value points, rewrite each answer that lost marks, and add the reason to the error list.

-
**Write answers to the word limits** Two-mark answers in 20 to 30 words and four-mark answers in 50 to 80. Extra length earns nothing and steals time from Section B.

-
**Keep the practical separate** The practical exam, file and viva are set by your school. If they fall inside your 30 days, give the lab programs the short blocks and do not let them push out theory papers. The questions examiners ask are on [CBSE Class 10 AI practical exam questions](/cbse-class-10-ai-practical-exam-questions).

-
**Re-plan on day 10 and day 20** Look at the error list. If one unit fills half of it, give that unit the next free main block and drop a lower-weight unit's revisit.

### Papers marked in class
Full papers are written at home and then marked against the scheme on screen, with the lost marks rewritten while the reasons are fresh.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I make a revision plan for class 10 AI?** Start from the theory marks: Advanced Concepts of Modeling 11, Evaluating Models 10, NLP 8, the project cycle unit 7, Computer Vision 4 and Part A 10. Give the heavy units the first and most days, plan timed full papers with a correction day after each, and keep a running list of every mistake to read in the final week.

**How to prepare for AI class 10 in the last 30 days?** Revise every unit once in the first 17 days, heaviest units first, with about 80 minutes a day. Then write three full timed papers, correct each one the next day, and spend the gaps on a second round of the units the papers expose. Keep the final day for light recall.

**How to revise AI class 10 in 1 week?** Spend the first two days on a fast round of all the units in mark order, write a full paper on day three, fix its weakest unit on day four, write a second paper on day five, and use the last two days for numericals, long answers and the formula sheet. The 7-day table on this page lays it out by morning, afternoon and evening.

**How many hours a day should I study AI for class 10 boards?** In the last month, about 80 minutes a day alongside your other subjects is enough for most students who studied the units during the year. In a free week just before the paper, four to five hours a day lets you write two full papers and still revise every unit.

**Which unit should I revise first in class 10 AI?** Advanced Concepts of Modeling, then Evaluating Models. Together they carry 21 of the 40 Part B theory marks, and Evaluating Models needs practised numericals. Computer Vision, at 4 marks, can safely come later and take less time. Part A is worth 10 marks but is quick recall, so short daily blocks keep it fresh.

**Should I solve sample papers in my AI revision timetable?** Yes, but only with a correction step. A paper you write and never mark shows a score, not the reason for it. Correct it against the marking points the same or next day, rewrite every answer that lost marks, and log the cause. The 30-day plan here has three papers and a correction day after each.

**What should I do the day before the class 10 AI exam?** Read the whole syllabus once in the morning, rework a few numericals with new figures, say the Part A lists aloud, do a timed objective set, write the formula sheet from memory and read your error list. Do not start a new paper or new material, pack your admit card and sleep early.

**Is 1 week enough to prepare for class 10 AI?** One week is enough to revise if the units were taught during the year and you put four to five hours a day into it. It is not enough to learn the subject from scratch; in that case start with Units 2 and 3, which carry the most marks, and ask for help early.

**Do I need to revise Python for the AI theory paper?** No. Advance Python and Statistical Data are assessed only in the practical exam, file and viva. If your school practical is still ahead during your revision month, use the short daily blocks for the eight suggested lab programs, and keep the main blocks for theory.
