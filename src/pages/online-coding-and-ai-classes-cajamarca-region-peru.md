## Do most Cajamarca children study in rural schools?

It sounds like one question, but a program can answer two: what share of the schools are rural, and what share of the pupils are. The regional register of schools gives very different answers, and the difference is the lesson.

### The register

The Gobierno Regional de Cajamarca publishes its "Padrón Regional de Instituciones Educativas de la Región Cajamarca" on datosabiertos.gob.pe, licensed under Open Data Commons Attribution (ODC-By). The publisher describes it as covering "las 9346 Instituciones Educativas de las 13 Unidades de Gestión Educativa Local".

Our copy, padron-cajamarca.csv, has 12,896 rows, one for each school level and form, all with a cut-off date of 11 September 2019. So this project describes the register as it stood in 2019, not today. The file also holds directors' names and contact details, which the lesson never touches.

### The trap

Each row has a level (Primaria, Secundaria and others), a status (Activa or Inactiva), an area (Rural or Urbana) and a column for enrolled pupils.

Count the active primary rows marked Rural and divide by all active primary rows, and you get the share of schools. That is a true number. It is not the share of children, because a one-teacher school with twelve pupils and a city school with eight hundred each count as one row.

**Version 1: count the rows** (Python)
```python
import csv

schools = rural = 0
with open("padron-cajamarca.csv", newline="", encoding="latin-1") as padron:
    for row in csv.DictReader(padron, delimiter=";"):
        if row["NIVEL"] == "Primaria" and row["ESTADO"] == "Activa":
            schools += 1
            if row["AREA"] == "Rural":
                rural += 1

print("Active primary schools:", schools)
print("Rural:", rural)
print("Rural share:", round(100 * rural / schools, 1), "%")
```
It prints: Active primary schools: 3780, Rural: 3139 and Rural share: 83.0 %.

Eighty-three per cent sounds like an answer to "do most children study in rural schools?", and many people would stop here. To answer that question we need to add up pupils, not schools. Trying it straight away fails, because the enrolment column sometimes says "No Cuenta" instead of a number, and int("No Cuenta") stops the program.

**Version 2: add up the pupils, and say what was skipped** (Python)
```python
import csv

pupils = {"Rural": 0, "Urbana": 0}
schools = {"Rural": 0, "Urbana": 0}
no_count = 0
with open("padron-cajamarca.csv", newline="", encoding="latin-1") as padron:
    for row in csv.DictReader(padron, delimiter=";"):
        if row["NIVEL"] != "Primaria" or row["ESTADO"] != "Activa":
            continue
        enrolled = row["TOTAL_ESTUDIANTES_MATRICULADOS"].strip()
        if not enrolled.isdigit():
            no_count += 1
            continue
        schools[row["AREA"]] += 1
        pupils[row["AREA"]] += int(enrolled)

print("Skipped, no number:", no_count)
share_schools = 100 * schools["Rural"] / sum(schools.values())
share_pupils = 100 * pupils["Rural"] / sum(pupils.values())
print("Rural share of schools:", round(share_schools, 1), "%")
print("Rural share of pupils:", round(share_pupils, 1), "%")
for area in ("Rural", "Urbana"):
    print(area, "pupils per school:", round(pupils[area] / schools[area], 1))
```
It prints: Skipped, no number: 79, Rural share of schools: 83.4 %, Rural share of pupils: 43.9 %, Rural pupils per school: 25.8 and Urbana pupils per school: 165.2.

Version 2 makes three changes. It skips the 79 active primary rows with no number, and says how many it skipped, so nobody is misled. It adds the enrolment column for each area. And it reports both shares side by side. Among the schools with a number, 83.4 per cent are rural, but they teach 43.9 per cent of the primary pupils, because the average rural primary school had 25.8 pupils and the average urban one 165.2. Both answers are correct; they answer different questions.

## Do Cajamarca's schools teach any programming?

No, not as a subject. Schools in Cajamarca follow Peru's national curriculum, in which digital skills are expected inside every area and no area is dedicated to programming.

### What the curriculum says

Primary school lasts six years and secondary five, under the General Education Law. MINEDU places children by their age on 31 March, so a child of six starts first grade and a sixteen year old is normally in fifth of secundaria.

The curriculum's only technology competency, number 28, is transversal: it is meant to be developed in every area, which in a small multigrade school with one teacher for several grades is a lot to ask.

### What the timetable shows

The regular secundaria week has 35 hours. Four go to Matemática, four to Comunicación, three to Inglés and two to Educación para el Trabajo. None is called programming or computing.

MINEDU's Python programme for secundaria, Fábrica de Programadores, began in July 2025 with 1,000 pupils in Lima Norte. Nothing comparable runs in Cajamarca's schools on the sources we checked.

Contests are open to Cajamarca pupils as to every pupil in Peru. The ONEM maths olympiad starts in each school; in 2026 its first round was on 8 July and more than 1.7 million pupils took part nationwide. UTEC's OPI programming olympiad is free for secundaria students. We are not connected with MINEDU, UTEC or either contest, and teach with problems we write ourselves.

## When can a learner in Cajamarca take a class?

At any hour that suits. Cajamarca keeps Peru time, and the mentor in India arranges the day around the learner.

**Example conversions from Cajamarca time to India time (not a timetable)**

| Cajamarca | India | In India it is |
|---|---|---|
| 05:30 | 16:00 | Afternoon |
| 07:00 | 17:30 | Early evening |
| 09:00 | 19:30 | Evening |
| 12:30 | 23:00 | Late evening |
| 17:00 | 03:30 | Next day, night |
| 21:00 | 07:30 | Next day, morning |

Peru: UTC minus 5 hours throughout the year, no daylight saving since 1994 (tz database, America/Lima). India: UTC plus 5 hours 30 minutes. Cajamarca is always 10 hours 30 minutes behind India.

Neither country changes its clocks, so a time agreed once holds all year. You choose the hour in your own clock when booking. Peru's 2026 school year started on 16 March 2026; families with school children often begin in the January to March holidays, while adults simply start when they are ready.

The free class answers that. Many Cajamarca children are still early in English, so the mentor tests with a small puzzle in simple English. If it is too hard today, we will say so and suggest one-to-one lessons or waiting a while.

A phone works for watching but not for real programming, which needs typing. Young children can begin block coding on a tablet. From about nine, a laptop or desktop is needed, even a shared one.

Yes. Classes are online, so every province of the region works the same way, as long as the connection is steady and there is a suitable device.

Any day, any time, flexible: choose a batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at the hour that suits your family. Cajamarca is 10 hours 30 minutes behind India all year, so 07:00 in Cajamarca is 17:30 for the mentor.

Yes, and they are often the strongest fit in this region. Python, Java and data analysis in English help with engineering, systems and science degrees, where much of the technical material is in English anyway.
