Reading the numbers

Access grew quickly from 45% to 60% and then held at 60% for two years. Put the other way round, about two in five US high schools in the data did not offer a computer science course, which is our reading of the published 60%.

Participation is the starker figure. Even where courses exist, only about six in every hundred high school students were enrolled in computer science in a given year.

Advanced Placement

AP Computer Science Principles and AP Computer Science A in 2027

The two AP computer science courses are the most recognisable high school options in the US. College Board describes both as introductory college-level courses, but they test quite different things.

Which to take first

For a student with little coding behind them, AP CSP is the gentler start: it is broad, the Create task lets them build a program of their own choice, and much of the multiple choice is about data, the internet and the impact of computing.

AP CSA is narrower and deeper. Every free-response question asks students to write Java, including one on ArrayList data and one on a 2D array, so it rewards a year of steady programming practice. Our longer comparison is in AP CSP vs AP CSA.

Dates and how they work

College Board says the 2027 AP Exams run over two weeks in May, May 3 to 7 and May 10 to 14. AP CSP has two pieces with two dates: the Create task deadline at the end of April and the exam in the second week of May.

Credit and placement for AP scores are set by each college, not by College Board or by us. Students can look up a college’s policy with College Board’s AP Credit Policy Search.

By the end of grade 12

What a high school graduate should understand, according to the K-12 CS Framework

The K-12 Computer Science Framework sets its last endpoint at grade 12. Its programming statements describe a student who makes engineering decisions, not one who simply gets code to run.

Grade 12 endpoints as the framework words them (k12cs.org, read on 17 October 2026)
StrandGrade 12 wordingWhat it means in class
Algorithms"People evaluate and select algorithms based on performance, reusability, and ease of implementation."Comparing a linear search with a binary search on the same data
Control"Programmers consider tradeoffs related to implementation, readability, and program performance when selecting and combining control structures."Choosing a for loop or a while loop, and saying why
Modularity"Complex programs are designed as systems of interacting modules"Classes in Java, modules in Python
Program development"Systematic analysis is critical for identifying the effects of lingering bugs."Tracing a loop by hand, as in the Java example below
Networks"Each device is assigned an address that uniquely identifies it on the network."AP CSP Big Idea 4: Computer Systems and Networks

Where AP fits

The framework’s own FAQ says AP Computer Science Principles "influenced the development of the" framework, and many of its writers worked on AP CSP. That is why the grade 12 statements and the AP CSP big ideas read so similarly.

AP CSA goes further on the programming side, especially classes and data collections, which map onto the modularity statement.

Beyond the exam

None of the grade 12 statements is about a particular language. They are about judgement: which algorithm, which structure, which trade-off. A student who can explain those choices out loud has understood far more than one who memorised syntax.

This is the standard we hold our high school students to: every program comes with a sentence on why it is written the way it is.

A bug worth knowing

Why removing from an ArrayList while counting up skips an element

Every year, high school Java students meet the same bug. It is short enough to show in full, and understanding it is a real step towards the "systematic analysis" the framework asks for. This is our teaching example, not an exam question.

Tracing the buggy loop by hand (ours)
iList before the checkWord checkedAction
0[ant, bee, tiger, cat, zebra]antRemoved, so bee moves to index 0
1[bee, tiger, cat, zebra]tigerKept; bee was never checked
2[bee, tiger, cat, zebra]catRemoved, so zebra moves to index 2
3[bee, tiger, zebra]noneLoop ends, since 3 is not less than size 3

Why the fix works

Counting down means every removal shifts only words to the right of i, and those have already been checked. Nothing slips past.

Another fix is to decrease i by one after each removal. Both work; students should be able to explain why, which is exactly the trade-off thinking in the grade 12 control statement.

How we use examples like this

We ask students to predict the output before running anything, then trace it in a table like the one above. The habit of tracing by hand is what lets a student debug code they cannot run, for example on paper.

We teach concepts like this so students can solve their own assignments. We do not write graded schoolwork for anyone.

If the school offers AP

Take the school course and use outside classes for practice and projects. The extra hours matter most in AP CSA, where fluent Java writing decides the free-response section.

If AP CSP and AP CSA are both on offer, many students take CSP first. Strong programmers sometimes go straight to CSA.

If it does not

Students can study for either AP exam outside school; ask your school counselor early about how exam registration works there, because schools handle it differently.

A self-built portfolio also carries weight on its own: an app, a data project or a contest record shows what a student can do, whatever their school offered.

Showing your work

A high school coding portfolio: what to keep and how to describe it

Whatever a student plans after high school, a small, honest record of real projects is useful. This is the checklist we give eleventh and twelfth graders; it asks for evidence of thinking, not polish.

A portfolio checklist for high school programmers (ours)
ItemWhat it showsHow to present it
One finished project people usedYou can build for someone elseA short write-up: who it was for, what they asked, what you changed after testing
One project you found hardYou can push through problemsThe bug that took longest and how you found it
Code you can explain line by lineThe work is yoursBe ready to walk through any function on request
A record of versionsYou work like a developerA Git history with clear commit messages
Any contest or challenge entryYou can work to a deadlineWhat you entered and what you learned, whatever the result
A note on AI useYou use tools honestlyWhich parts used AI help and how you checked them

Why explanation matters most

Anyone reading a teenager’s project now knows that AI can produce code. What it cannot produce is the student’s own account of why a design was chosen and what went wrong on the way.

That is why the checklist keeps returning to explanation. A modest project explained well says more than an impressive one the student cannot discuss.

What we do not promise

A portfolio is a record, not a ticket. We make no claims about admissions, scholarships or jobs; those decisions belong to the people who make them.

What we can say is that building and explaining real projects is the most direct way we know to become a better programmer, and that is the part a student controls.

From beginner to builder

Four stages of a high school programmer

Most students who join us in ninth or tenth grade are at stage 1 or 2.

Stages of a high school programmer (ours)
StageHow it shows
1. Syntax learnerWrites short programs, needs help reading errors
2. Problem solverBreaks a task into functions, traces loops by hand, fixes own bugs
3. DesignerWrites classes, chooses data structures, explains trade-offs
4. SpecialistBuilds a substantial project in AI, web or algorithms, documents it

For parents

The move from stage 2 to stage 3 usually happens during AP CSA or an equivalent year of Java.

Ask your teenager to explain one design decision in their latest project. Their answer tells you which stage they are at.

For students

Trace before you run. Predicting output is the fastest way to find where your mental model is wrong.

Finish small projects. A finished small app beats an abandoned big one in any portfolio.

Coding in high school: common questions

What percentage of US high schools offer computer science?

Code.org (2025), in its State of AI + CS Education data, reports 60% of US high schools offering computer science in 2024 to 2025, up from 45% in 2018 to 2019, when data came from 39 states.

How many high school students take computer science?

The same Code.org data reports 871,490 of 14,364,449 high school students enrolled in computer science, or 6.1%, in its most recent national figures.

Should I take AP CSP or AP CSA first?

Most beginners take AP Computer Science Principles first because it is broader and the Create task lets them build a program of their own choice. Students who already program confidently often go straight to AP Computer Science A, which is all Java.

When are the AP computer science exams in 2027?

College Board lists the AP CSA exam on Wednesday, May 12, 2027 and the AP CSP end-of-course exam on Friday, May 14, 2027. The AP CSP Create task must be submitted as final by April 30, 2027, 11:59 PM ET.

What language is AP Computer Science A?

Java. All four free-response questions ask students to write Java code, including methods, a class, ArrayList data and a 2D array.

What if my high school does not offer computer science?

Students can learn the same material outside school, including AP preparation. Ask your school counselor how AP exam registration works at your school, since schools handle it differently.

What coding language should a high schooler learn first?

Usually Python: it reads almost like English and gets students building real projects within weeks. Students heading for AP Computer Science A then add Java, usually in tenth or eleventh grade.

Do you help with homework or graded assignments?

We teach the concepts so students can solve their own assignments. We never write graded schoolwork or answer keys for anyone.

What times are classes for US families?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible. Choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays.

How much do high school coding classes cost?

First lesson free, then a fee each month. The fees section shows the figure in US dollars.
