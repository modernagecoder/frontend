Coding and AI classes in Ottawa

Who teaches coding and AI to Ottawa students live, with a real teacher?

Modern Age Coders teaches coding, Python and AI live online to Ottawa learners aged 6 to adult, either one to one or in a group of fifteen to twenty. Children usually start with Python and AI for Kids; teens move to AI and Machine Learning for Teens or our ICS3U and ICS4U companion. The first class is free.

Ottawa is a capital with a provincial border running through its middle. A student in Kanata or Orleans follows the Ontario curriculum, where code turns up in Grade 6 science, in MTH1W, the Grade 9 math course every student now takes, and in a newer Grade 10 technology course that names artificial intelligence. A student a bridge away in Gatineau follows Quebec's program instead, on the same clock. The city itself publishes its open data in English and French, side by side. This page uses all three facts: what Ontario schools ask an Ottawa student to code, what changes across the river, and a data project we built on the City of Ottawa parks file, where the most useful lesson is the moment the English and French columns disagree.

That is a good design and a thin one: the ideas are present, but a science unit or a math strand gives each child only a few hours at the keyboard. Our classes add the missing hours, and we spend them on the part school has least time for, which is building something big enough to break.

The Grade 9 math assessment

EQAO describes its Grade 9 math assessment as online, in two sessions of 54 questions in all, using a “multi-stage computer adaptive” model. The 2026 to 2027 window runs from 12 January to 5 February 2027, and EQAO says results count in the final course mark.

Computer studies and the diploma

For students who began Grade 9 in 2024 or later, ontario.ca lists one technological education credit among the compulsory ones and names computer studies as one option in a group of STEM choices. We stop there: the page does not say ICD2O fills the technology credit, so neither do we.

Phones and social media

PPM 128, in force since 1 September 2024, requires school boards to restrict access to social media platforms on school networks and devices. It is a fair reason to keep a coding class at home, on a laptop, away from the school day.

Beside school, never instead of it

We teach alongside a student's school courses and we do not grant Ontario credits; ICS3U, ICS4U, MTH1W and every other credit are earned at school. We are not connected with the Ontario Ministry of Education, EQAO, any Ottawa school board or the City of Ottawa.

Families who want the full provincial picture, including how the course codes chain together, can read our Ontario coding classes page and the Ontario computer science pathway. Grade 9 families will find more on MTH1W, and Grade 10 students on ICD2O.

Ottawa and Gatineau

One capital region, two school systems, one clock

Statistics Canada treats Ottawa and Gatineau as one census metropolitan area, which it names Ottawa - Gatineau and publishes in an Ontario part and a Quebec part. For a family choosing a class, three practical differences follow.

The capital itself

Section 16 of the Constitution Act, 1867 settles where Parliament sits: the Seat of Government of Canada shall be Ottawa. The city that holds that role still runs its schools under a provincial curriculum, like every other Canadian city.

Across the bridge

Quebec's secondary program runs from Secondary I to Secondary V, and students then usually go to CEGEP, where a pre-university program takes two years of full-time study. A Gatineau teenager of sixteen is placed by what they can do, not by the Ontario grade they would be in.

The same hour

Ontario and Quebec keep the same Eastern clock, so a class booked from Gatineau and one booked from Barrhaven start at the same moment. Both provinces move to UTC-5 on 1 November 2026 and back to UTC-4 on 14 March 2027.

Gatineau families with a student in Secondary IV or V can read our pages on Quebec Secondary 4 math options and Secondary 5 math, and students heading to college on preparing for CEGEP computer science. One honest line for every family on either bank: our teaching, our materials and our written feedback are in English.

Ottawa data project

Two languages, one parks file: an Ottawa data audit

The City of Ottawa publishes its Parks and Greenspace inventory as open data, with an English and a French version of most columns. Our students decode it, count it, and then check whether the two languages tell the same story. Every figure below is ours, from the file as downloaded on 22 October 2026.

The first surprise is that the CSV holds only digits in that column. The meaning of 0 to 4 lives in the city's map service, as a list the service calls a coded-value domain, and a student has to fetch that list before a single count makes sense. The script below carries the list as a Python dictionary, counts the parks, and then runs the audit. It needs nothing beyond Python 3 and the downloaded file in the same folder:import csv LABELS = { "0": "Dogs Allowed (off-leash)", "1": "Dogs on Leash", "2": "Mixed", "3": "No Dogs", "4": "Not Designated (Dogs on Leash)", } SWAPPED = {("Active Recreation", "Loisirs passifs"), ("Passive Recreation", "Loisirs dynamiques")} counts = {} type_clash = 0 dog_clash = 0 with open("parks-and-greenspace.csv", encoding="utf-8-sig") as parks: for row in csv.DictReader(parks): label = LABELS.get(row["DOG_DESIGNATION"], "(blank code)") counts[label] = counts.get(label, 0) + 1 if (row["PARK_TYPE"], row["PARK_TYPE_FR"]) in SWAPPED: type_clash += 1 if row["DOG_DESIGNATION"] != row["DOG_DESIGNATION_FR"]: dog_clash += 1 for label in sorted(counts, key=counts.get, reverse=True): print(label, counts[label]) print("English and French park type disagree:", type_clash) print("English and French dog code disagree:", dog_clash)

1. Decode before counting

A dictionary turns "4" into Not Designated (Dogs on Leash). Rows with no code at all fall through to the label we chose, (blank code), so 28 parks are reported as missing rather than silently dropped.

2. Compare the two languages

PARK_TYPE says Active Recreation or Passive Recreation; PARK_TYPE_FR should say Loisirs dynamiques or Loisirs passifs to match. On 36 rows the pair is crossed, 18 in each direction, so the English and French park type disagree.

3. Find the one that matters

The dog code is stored twice, once per language, and on exactly one park the English column says 1 (on leash) while the French says 3 (no dogs). A dog owner reading the French map and one reading the English map would get different instructions.

What the file does not tell you

A park coded on-leash can still keep dogs away from its play area: the city's detail text mentions play areas on 981 of the 987 parks coded 1 or 4, which the code alone cannot show. Nor does the file say which of two disagreeing columns is right. The honest finding is a list of 37 rows to report to the city, not a correction we invent.

Coding and AI

If an AI tool writes the script, what is left for an Ottawa student to learn?

The part where someone notices that the French column and the English column disagree.

Give an AI assistant the Ottawa parks file and ask for a count of off-leash parks, and you will get working code and a number. You will probably not be told that one park says on-leash in English and no dogs in French, or that 36 rows swap their park type between languages. The tool answered the question asked. Knowing which question to ask next is the skill.

That is why we teach students to reason first and type second: plan the steps, write the loop, then interrogate the output. As they get older they use AI tools within age rules we follow, and they use them to check and extend their own thinking, never to hand in someone else's.

Ontario put coding into science and math so that students learn to break a problem into steps; an AI tool can follow those steps, but only a student who understands them can tell when it has followed the wrong ones. The longer argument is in our essay on why writing code still pays off for a capital-region student.

FAQ

Ottawa coding and AI questions

What Ottawa and Gatineau families ask us before a first class.

Is there a coding class for kids in Ottawa that is taught live?

Yes. Our classes are live online with a real teacher, so a child in any Ottawa neighbourhood joins from home. We have no Ottawa building; we teach groups of fifteen to twenty or one to one.

Can a student from Gatineau join an Ottawa class?

Yes. Gatineau and Ottawa share the Eastern clock, so the class starts at the same moment on both banks. We place a Quebec student by secondary level and skill rather than by Ontario grade. Teaching is in English.

What coding do Ottawa schools already teach?

Under the Ontario curriculum, Grade 6 science asks students to use coding in investigations, the Grade 9 math course MTH1W uses code for algebra, ICD2O in Grade 10 covers programming and artificial intelligence, and ICS3U and ICS4U are optional computer science courses.

Does a class with you earn an Ontario credit?

It does not: credits such as ICS3U or ICS4U are earned at school, and we do not grant Ontario credits. Our companion course adds practice and projects alongside the school course.

Are there robotics classes for Ottawa students?

We teach the programming half of robotics, not robot hardware: Python, logic, sensors as data and debugging. A student who wants to build a physical robot should pair our classes with a school or community robotics team.

Which AI classes suit an Ottawa teenager?

AI and Machine Learning for Teens, after a solid start in Python. Under-18 students do not use tools rated 18 and over, such as Claude, and a teen using ChatGPT needs a parent's permission, so we plan lessons around that.

Do you run Python classes in Ottawa for adults?

Yes. Adults learn in their own groups or one to one, from a first program to working with real files and data. The Ottawa parks file on this page is a typical first data project.

Can you coach an Ottawa student for the CCC in French?

The contest itself is bilingual: CEMC offers the 2027 CCC, on Wednesday 17 February 2027, in English and French. Our coaching is in English, using problems we write ourselves in Python, C++ or Java, and the student writes the contest through their school.

When do Ottawa classes run?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. We confirm every time in Eastern time and again when the clocks change.

Can I enter a 613 or 819 number in the form?

You can. Ottawa numbers with 343, 613 or 753 and Gatineau numbers with 819, 873 or 468 are all accepted. Enter the ten digits after the +1, which the form supplies.

How are fees shown to an Ottawa family?

Scroll to the fees section: it lists the free lesson first, then the monthly fee for a group place and for one-to-one teaching, each beside its currency code. Nothing is billed before the free lesson.
