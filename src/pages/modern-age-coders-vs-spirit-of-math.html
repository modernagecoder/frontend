Modern Age Coders vs Spirit of Math

Spirit of Math and Modern Age Coders both want children to think hard about mathematics rather than memorise steps. Where they part company is the door. Spirit of Math describes itself as a selective school for high-performing students, with a B+ average as the entry bar and an assessment before a place is offered. Modern Age Coders has no grade requirement: any student can join, and the first class is used to place them. This page compares the two on entry, format, the shape of the year, contests and coding, using Spirit of Math's own pages as read in October 2026, and it says where Spirit of Math is the more natural home for a child.

The one difference that drives the rest

Spirit of Math chooses its students. Its home page puts it plainly: "We selectively admit high-performing students". That choice shapes everything else: the pace can be fast because everyone in the room has already shown they can keep up, and the peer group is part of what families are buying.

We do not choose. A student who is struggling with fractions and a student who is bored by Grade 8 algebra can both join; what changes is the course and the group they are placed in. The pace is set by the group, and a one to one plan exists for the student who fits no group.

About fees

None of the Spirit of Math pages we read lists tuition. If cost matters to the decision, ask your nearest campus for the full figure for the 39-week program.

Our fee is a monthly figure for eight live classes, shown lower on this page in the currency of your region. Families pay one month at a time, so there is no year to commit to up front.

  1. With us, step one is a free first class. The teacher gives the student a few problems of rising difficulty and listens to how they reason, not only to the answers.
  2. Step two is a placement. The teacher suggests a course and a group: for example, elementary math for a Grade 4 student who needs number sense, or contest problem solving for a Grade 7 student who already finds school math easy.

Why a selective door suits some children

A child who is always the first to finish in class can find it a relief to be among peers who also want the hard question. Selection guarantees that kind of room.

The contest team takes this further, with an application, an entrance competition and a small number of places per level. For a student who wants that challenge and wins a place, it is a clear, demanding path.

Why an open door suits others

Plenty of students who would enjoy thinking-first mathematics do not have a B+ average yet. Some are bright but bored, some had a weak year, some are new to the Canadian curriculum. An open door lets them start now.

Open enrolment also suits a student who is past Grade 11, or an adult returning to math, since our courses carry on to university-level topics.

The shape of the year

A Spirit of Math year, and a year of live classes with us

The two programs organise time differently. Spirit of Math runs a structured school-style year. We run month by month around a course.

At Spirit of Math, by its own account

The program runs for 39 weeks. Students attend once a week; the FAQ gives class lengths from 45 minutes in Kindergarten up to 120 minutes for high school, and the program page describes a 90-minute class. Each class combines four elements: "drills, core curriculum, problem solving, and collaborative group work."

Drills are deliberate. Spirit of Math writes that "Repetition builds fluency." There is weekly homework, and the FAQ says "Students complete tests, assignments, and a final exam each year". Teachers, the program page says, "receive more than 100 hours of initial training".

With Modern Age Coders

Students meet their teacher live twice a week, eight classes a month, all year round with no fixed term. A class usually opens with a problem from last time, introduces one new idea, and spends most of the hour on students explaining their thinking.

Between classes there is a short set of practice problems, and progress shows in the problems a student can now explain from start to finish. A family can stop after any month, or move from a group to one to one.

Fluency

Spirit of Math builds speed with drills. We build it inside problems, so the arithmetic always serves a question.

Assessment

Spirit of Math sets tests and a final exam. Our teachers judge progress by asking the student to explain a solution aloud.

Commitment

Spirit of Math is a 39-week program. Ours runs a month at a time.

Spirit of Math also runs online: its Fibonacci Virtual Campus, which it calls an online after-school program "Designed in Canada specifically for students who are already excelling in math and ready for more." Our classes are online by design, so the comparison is really between two online options for families without a nearby campus.

A worked example

Our teaching in one problem: count carefully, then test the shortcut

This is our own problem, written for our classes. It is not from Spirit of Math, CEMC or any contest. We picked it because it rewards the habit we teach most: reason step by step first, look for a shortcut second, and always check the shortcut against the rules of the problem.

Warm-up. How many three-digit numbers have digits that add up to 5? The real question. How many three-digit numbers have digits that add up to 10?

  1. List by the first digit. For a sum of 5, the hundreds digit can be 1, 2, 3, 4 or 5. If it is 1, the other two digits add to 4, which gives 5 pairs (0 and 4, 1 and 3, 2 and 2, 3 and 1, 4 and 0). If it is 2, they add to 3: 4 pairs. Then 3, 2 and 1 pairs. The total is 5 + 4 + 3 + 2 + 1 = 15.
  2. Spot the pattern. Two digits that add to k can be chosen in k + 1 ways, as long as neither digit has to exceed 9. The student should say that condition out loud, because it is where the real question bites.
  3. Apply it to 10. The hundreds digit runs from 1 to 9, so the other two digits add to 9, 8, 7 and so on down to 1. Each sum is 9 or less, so neither digit can exceed 9, and the counts are 10, 9, 8, 7, 6, 5, 4, 3 and 2. The total is 10 + 9 + 8 + 7 + 6 + 5 + 4 + 3 + 2 = 54.
  4. Test a shortcut. A student who knows a counting formula may turn the problem into sharing 9 among three places and get 55. Our question back: does every one of those 55 make a real three-digit number? One does not. It is the one that puts all 9 into the hundreds place, which would make the hundreds digit 10. So 55 - 1 = 54, and the two methods agree.
  5. Check with a program. Only now does the computer come in, to confirm the reasoning rather than replace it.

What the teacher is listening for

Not the number 54. The teacher wants to hear the student say why the warm-up pattern still holds for 10, and to hear them find the one impossible case in the shortcut without being told it exists.

A student who reaches 55 and stops has learned a formula. A student who asks whether all 55 are allowed has learned to check their own work, which matters far more in a contest and in a math class.

Where it goes next

Next class: digits that add up to 20. Now some pairs would need a digit above 9, so the simple pattern breaks and the student has to adapt it.

For a contest student, the same idea leads into counting with restrictions, a theme that runs through many CEMC problems. We teach it with problems we write, and send students to CEMC's free archive for past papers.

Who each suits

When Spirit of Math fits, and when open enrolment fits

Both are serious about mathematical thinking. The right choice depends on the child, the family's week, and how the child responds to selection.

Choose Spirit of Math when

Your child already holds a B+ or higher average, enjoys being stretched, and would thrive in a room chosen for high performers.

There is a campus near you and you want an in-person class with a structured 39-week year, drills, homework and exams, much like an extra school subject.

Your child wants the Competitive Contest Team experience, with a selective group and a demanding application.

Choose open enrolment with us when

Your child is curious and capable but not yet at B+, or you would rather they started without an entrance step.

You want two shorter live sessions a week rather than one long one, with no campus to travel to and no full year to commit to.

Your child is past Grade 11, wants coding and math from one teacher team, or needs one to one help aligned with a Canadian school course such as MHF4U or Math 30-1.

Strong and selected

A B+ student who wants a selective peer group: Spirit of Math is built for exactly this.

Strong, not yet selected

A capable student below the bar, or new to Canada: open enrolment lets them begin now.

Beyond Grade 11

Spirit of Math's weekly program stops at Grade 11. Our courses carry on to Grade 12 and university math.

Deciding

Five questions to settle the choice

Work through these with your child. The answers usually point one way.

  1. Does your child meet the bar today? Spirit of Math asks for a B+ or higher average. If not, an open-entry class lets them start without waiting for a better report card.
  2. How does your child react to selection? Some children are lifted by earning a place; others are put off by a test. You know which.
  3. What does your week allow? One longer weekly class at a campus, or two live sessions from home. Count the drive.
  4. Is coding on the list? Spirit of Technology runs at select campuses. Our coding courses run alongside math for every student.
  5. Try before deciding. Spirit of Math begins with a placement interview and an assessment class; our first class is free. Doing both is the clearest test.

Where a student starts

How we place a student who has never sat an entrance test

Our own four placement stages, judged in the first class.

Placement stages for problem solving in math (ours)
StageWhat we see
1. CalculatorGets answers to routine questions but cannot yet say why a method works
2. ExplainerExplains a familiar method in their own words and catches some of their own slips
3. ExplorerTries an unfamiliar problem by listing cases or drawing, without being shown how
4. CheckerFinds a shortcut, then tests it against the rules of the problem before trusting it

For parents

A child with a B+ average can sit at any of these stages. Marks show how well a student handles school questions; the stages show how they handle a question they have never seen.

Most growth happens between stages 2 and 3, when a student first accepts that being stuck is part of the work.

For students

When you are stuck, list small cases. Patterns hide in lists.

When you find a shortcut, ask what it assumes. Then check that assumption.

Questions from Canadian parents

What parents ask about Spirit of Math and about us

Is Modern Age Coders connected to Spirit of Math?

No. We are a separate live online school with no tie to Spirit of Math Schools. What this page says about Spirit of Math comes from its own website, read on 22 October 2026.

What grades does a student need for Spirit of Math?

Spirit of Math says its selective 39-week program is designed for students who consistently perform at a B+ level or higher, and its campus locator says a child should have a B+ or higher average grade. Families apply and schedule an assessment class.

Is there a Spirit of Math alternative without an entrance requirement?

Modern Age Coders has no grade requirement and no entrance test. Every student starts with a free first class, which the teacher uses to place them in a suitable course and group, or in one to one lessons.

Does Spirit of Math teach online?

Yes. Spirit of Math runs the Fibonacci Virtual Campus, which it describes as an online after-school program for students who are already excelling in mathematics. It also has in-person campuses in over 30 locations across Canada, by its own count.

How long are Spirit of Math classes?

Its FAQ says classes meet once a week, from 45 minutes in Kindergarten to 120 minutes for high school students, and its program page describes a 90-minute class. Our live classes run twice a week.

How much does Spirit of Math cost?

Spirit of Math does not list tuition on the pages we read in October 2026, so ask your nearest campus for the yearly figure. Our own fee appears on our pricing page, in the currency of your region.

What grades does Spirit of Math cover?

Its weekly program runs from Kindergarten to Grade 11, and its contest team takes students from Grade 3 to Grade 12. Our math courses run from Grade 1 to university level, for ages 6 to 67.

Does Spirit of Math teach coding?

Spirit of Math says Spirit of Technology is offered in select campuses, with Python, JavaScript and Arduino. Coding is a core part of what we teach, from Scratch for young children to C++ for contest students.

Can my child prepare for the Waterloo math contests with you?

Yes. Our Waterloo Math Contests course prepares Grades 7 to 12 for Gauss, Pascal, Cayley, Fermat and Euclid with original problems. We are not connected with CEMC; students write the contests through their schools.

When are your live math classes for Canadian families?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. We set the time in your province's own time zone.
