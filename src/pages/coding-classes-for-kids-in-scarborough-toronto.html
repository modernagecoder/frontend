Coding classes for kids in Scarborough

Where can a Scarborough child aged 6 to 12 learn to code with a real teacher?

Modern Age Coders runs live online coding classes for Scarborough children aged 6 to 12, in a batch of fifteen to twenty or one to one. Younger children begin with Coding for Kids or Scratch; from about 9, Python and AI for Kids. A teacher watches every screen, and the first class is free.

In Scarborough, as everywhere in Ontario, a child is asked to write code in math class from Grade 1. By Grade 2 the ministry wants two things happening at once on the screen. What a classroom of thirty rarely has is the time for each child to get it wrong, look closely and fix it while someone asks why. That is the hour we give. Below we set out the first two years of Ontario coding in the ministry’s words, a small data project on the City of Toronto’s own recreation file filtered to Scarborough, the one CEMC contest built for children of this age, and how our live classes fit around a Scarborough school week.

Coding at school in Scarborough

Grade 1 and Grade 2: the coding a Scarborough child meets first

Scarborough public schools teach the Ontario curriculum. We read the coding expectations from the ministry’s curriculum service on 22 October 2026. Quoted words are the ministry’s; the right-hand column is what we do with them.

Grade 1, C3.2Children “read and alter existing code, including code that involves sequential events, and describe how changes to the code affect the outcomes”.
We hand a six-year-old a short program that almost works, ask what it will do before it runs, and let the child change one block at a time. Saying the prediction aloud is the habit we are after.
Grade 2, C3.1Writing and running code, “including code that involves sequential and concurrent events”.
Concurrent means two things at once: a cat and a dog sprite crossing the stage together. In our Scratch classes the child times them so they meet in the middle, and works out why they did not.
Grade 2, teacher notesThe ministry’s own example: “Students will develop code to move multiple objects from one location to another on a grid at the same time”.
We use a squared grid on paper first, then the screen. Counting squares turns the code into arithmetic a Grade 2 child already knows.
Grade 2, teacher notes“Changing the sequence of instructions in code can sometimes produce the same outcome and can sometimes produce a different outcome.”
A favourite puzzle in our classes: swap two lines, then decide whether anything changed. Children learn that order sometimes matters and sometimes does not, and to test rather than guess.

Grades 3 to 7 keep building on this ladder, with repeating events, nesting, conditions, efficient code and subprograms, and our guide to Ontario coding from Grade 1 to Grade 8 sets out each year. The point for a Scarborough parent is that coding at school is part of math, assessed alongside everything else, and squeezed into a strand that shares the year with number, geometry and data. Children who enjoy it usually want more of it than the timetable allows.

EQAO in Grade 3

EQAO lists the 2026 to 2027 Grade 3 Reading, Writing and Mathematics assessment for 4 May to 8 June 2027. We do not coach to it; the counting and pattern work in early coding sits comfortably beside it.

Phones in the younger grades

Under Ontario’s PPM 128, from Grade 6 down, personal devices stay out of view and silent through the whole school day. Our classes happen after school, at home, on a laptop or desktop.

Blocks, then typing

The ministry allows block-based and text-based coding, so Scarborough classrooms vary. We start most children under 9 in blocks and move them to Python when reading and typing are no longer the hard part.

Who we are, and are not

Modern Age Coders teaches alongside school; it is not a school and gives no report-card marks. We are not connected with the Ontario Ministry of Education, EQAO, the Toronto school boards or the City of Toronto.

Scarborough data project

What can a 9-year-old drop in to this week in Scarborough? Counting the City’s own file

The City of Toronto publishes its recreation schedule as open data. Our older kids, around ages 10 to 12, join two of its tables, keep only Scarborough, and count the sessions a child of a given age can join. Every number below is our count, not the City’s.

The file is Registered Programs and Drop In Courses Offering, published by Parks, Forestry & Recreation and refreshed weekly. Its notes say a drop-in “is a program offered within PFR facilities where registration is not required”. Two of its tabs matter here: Drop-in, one row per session, and Locations, one row per place, with a District column. Our download held 7,039 drop-in rows at Scarborough locations, spread over 39 of them. Here is the script a 10-year-old can type, using nothing but the Python standard library:import csv district = {} with open("locations.csv", encoding="utf-8") as f: for row in csv.DictReader(f): district[row["Location ID"]] = row["District"] age = 9 days = {} with open("dropin.csv", encoding="utf-8") as f: for row in csv.DictReader(f): if district.get(row["Location ID"]) != "Scarborough": continue low = int(row["Age Min"]) high = 120 if row["Age Max"] == "None" else int(row["Age Max"]) if age in range(low, high + 1): day = row["DayOftheWeek"] days[day] = days.get(day, 0) + 1 print(sum(days.values()), days)

1. Join the tables

A session row only gives a Location ID. The child builds a small dictionary from Locations, ID to District, then looks each session up in it. That is a join, the same idea a database uses, written in four lines.

2. Filter by age

Each row has Age Min and Age Max. A blank maximum means no upper limit, so the script treats it as 120. A session fits a 9-year-old when 9 sits inside the range, which Python checks with range.

3. Count by day

Every fitting row adds one to its weekday. For age 9 we got Saturday 191, Thursday 174, Friday 154, Sunday 150, Wednesday 144, Monday 137 and Tuesday 120: 1,070 in all, swim and skate making up most of them.

The trap in the data dictionary

The City’s field note for Age Min says “minimum allowable age in months”. The values tell a different story: ranges such as 0 to 5, 6 to 12 and 13 to 18, and a youth council open from 13 to 24. Those only make sense as years, so we read them as years and say so. Spotting that a label and its data disagree is a real skill, and children enjoy catching a grown-up file being wrong.

What the counts do not say matters as much. 273 of the sessions open to a 9-year-old name a caregiver or family in the title, so an adult has to come too. A count of sessions is not a count of places on the ice, and the file is a snapshot: change the download date and the numbers move. A good next step for the class is the after-school question: of the 1,070, how many start at 3 pm or later on a weekday? Our answer was 315. Data used under the Open Government Licence - Toronto. Puzzles, not programs

BCC questions read like logic stories: a beaver sorting logs, a path through a maze, a rule to discover. A Scarborough Grade 5 child who has spent a term predicting what code will do is well prepared without ever opening a contest paper.

Ask the school early

CEMC says it is not possible for students to register individually. If your child’s school has not ordered the BCC, ask the teacher; schools that already run CEMC contests often add it.

Practise on our puzzles

CEMC keeps past contests from the last ten years free online. We point families there and teach with puzzles of our own, written for our classes and labelled as ours, rather than copying CEMC material.

Coding and AI

Should a Scarborough child learn to code when AI can write code?

Yes, and for the same reason children still learn to add when calculators exist.

The drop-in project above shows why. A tool can produce the script, but only a child who understands it notices that the age field says months and the numbers say years, or that a session needing a caregiver is not one a child can attend alone. Knowing what to doubt comes from having built the loop by hand.

Children under 13 in our classes do not use adult chatbots. They learn what AI is, how a model guesses from examples, and where it goes wrong, through age-appropriate tools and activities a teacher runs. Above all they practise thinking a problem through, step by step, and checking the answer rather than copying one.

Ontario put coding into Grade 1 math to teach step-by-step reasoning, and that reasoning is the part no tool can do on a child’s behalf. The longer argument is in is coding still worth learning in 2026.

FAQ

Scarborough parents ask

Questions we hear most often from families in Scarborough.

Are there coding classes for kids in Scarborough?

Yes, live and online. We have no Scarborough premises; your child joins from home with a real teacher, in a batch of fifteen to twenty children or one to one.

What age can my child start?

Six is a good age to start, which is about Grade 1, when Ontario math first asks children to read and change simple code. Our kids classes run to age 12.

Do Scarborough schools already teach coding?

Yes. Coding is part of the Ontario math curriculum from Grade 1, and by Grade 2 children write code with two things happening at the same time. It is one strand among many, so time for each child is limited.

Does my child need to know how to type?

No. Children under about 9 start with blocks they drag and drop. Typing comes later, when the child moves on to Python.

Is Scratch or Python better for a 7-year-old?

Scratch, for most children. It lets a 7-year-old build moving stories and games without spelling errors getting in the way. We move children to Python when they are ready, often around 9 or 10.

What time are classes for Scarborough families?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Times are always given in Eastern time.

Can my child enter a coding contest at this age?

Children in Grade 6 or below can write the Grade 5/6 Beaver Computing Challenge, which CEMC runs from 9 to 20 November 2026. It needs no coding, and entry goes through the school.

Will my child use ChatGPT in class?

No. Children under 13 in our classes do not use adult chatbots. ChatGPT needs a parent's permission for anyone under 18, and we teach young children about AI with age-appropriate activities instead.

Do you teach at a centre in Scarborough?

No. Every class is online, so there is nothing to drive to. Children need a computer with a keyboard and sound, and a steady internet connection.

How do I see the fees?

The fees section on this page lists the free first class, the monthly batch fee and the monthly one-to-one fee, each with its currency shown. Nothing is billed before the free class.
