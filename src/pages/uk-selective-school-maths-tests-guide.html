11 plus exams explained by area and provider

Ask ten parents in ten counties what the 11 plus is and you will hear ten different tests. This guide puts every area we cover into one table, from the councils' and consortia's own pages, then explains each provider and each entry point from age 6 to 16. Modern Age Coders teaches the maths behind these tests live online, as a class or 1-on-1, and says so plainly when a test has none.

Which 11 plus test does my area use?

It depends on where you live and which schools you want. In 2026 Buckinghamshire, Birmingham, Redbridge, Medway, Trafford and Lincolnshire used GL Assessment papers; Bexley, Plymouth and the Wirral used Quest Assessments; Calderdale and two Lancashire schools used FSCE; Bromley's and Barnet's schools set their own. Lincolnshire's test has no maths paper. Most children sit in September of Year 6. Tests by provider and entry point

Who writes the 7 plus, 11 plus, 13 plus and 16 plus, and how do they differ?

Four organisations write most of the 11 plus papers in England, and two more set the independent school pre-tests. Each sells to councils or schools, not to parents, and each builds a different paper for each customer. The entry points either side of 11, at 7, 13 and 16, have their own setters again.

11 plus by area

How does the 11 plus change from one county or borough to the next?

Each area page below takes one row of the table and goes deep: the papers, the dates, the scoring and one maths topic that the local format makes worth teaching. Here is what sets each one apart, grouped by region.

London boroughs. In north London, Barnet has three selective state schools whose maths arrives at three different moments, and Enfield is about one school whose test is changing shape for today's Year 5. East of the City, Redbridge carries a warning from its own council that free practice material might not match the real test. In the south-east, Bexley registers children in March of Year 5, earlier than almost anywhere, and Bromley has two grammar schools that give opposite answers on maths. South-west of the centre, Sutton asks the same maths twice, once with options and once without, while Kingston teaches answers written into digit columns rather than chosen from a list.

South East and the Home Counties. Kent has its own page, built from the council's leaflet because the council's website would not answer our requests. Its neighbour Medway runs a separate test and gives maths an unbroken 50-minute paper. Buckinghamshire enters state primary pupils automatically and paces the room with an audio file. Hertfordshire has a consortium of schools in the south-west of the county and, separately, Dame Alice Owen's School, each running its own test. Slough turns the clock on the exam room wall into its maths topic, and Reading runs two tests on two calendars, one of them in July of Year 5. In Essex, the Essex page covers the CSSE schools around Colchester and Chelmsford, and the Southend page the Southend schools on the same test, where maths gets a full hour.

South West. Gloucestershire is moving its test from September of Year 6 to Saturday 3 July 2027 and adding a paper answered in short written form. Bournemouth, Christchurch and Poole shortens its maths paper and caps it at Year 5 when Quest takes over. Wiltshire focuses on the Salisbury boys' grammar school, whose 2028 leaflet is already published. Further west, Plymouth sets maths on a Saturday of its own, and Torbay tests English and maths only, with one set of results shared by every selective school in the bay.

Midlands. Three pages share one regional test from the West Midlands Grammar Schools group, each from a different angle. The Birmingham page builds the angle facts that need no measuring instrument; the Warwickshire page takes the council at its word that the difficulty may go beyond end-of-key-stage expectations; and Wolverhampton and Walsall explains why one school calls the same section numerical reasoning and sets its pass mark only after results. To the east, Lincolnshire is the honest exception: two reasoning tests, no maths paper, and a page that says so first.

North of England. Trafford names four maths areas in its consortium test, and statistics is the one families tend to skip. Across the Mersey, the Wirral puts maths in Paper two with non-verbal reasoning, in a Quest assessment sat on a Tuesday. Lancashire had three schools testing on one Saturday with two styles of paper. Calderdale, for the Halifax schools, marks English and maths first and reads creative writing only for children who clear that bar. North Yorkshire added a maths section to the Ripon test for the first time in 2026, while Skipton's schools set something different.

Northern Ireland transfer test

How does the Northern Ireland transfer test work, and how much of it is maths?

Northern Ireland has one common test, run by the Schools' Entrance Assessment Group (SEAG) for post-primary schools that select. It is sat in P7 over two Saturdays in November, and maths is exactly half of the result.

Two pages cover it. Transfer test maths practice for Northern Ireland is the format page: the shared hour, the order a child chooses, the multiple-choice and free-response questions and the content list. Transfer test maths tuition in Belfast is the local page, for a city with the densest cluster of SEAG schools, and it focuses on what happens after the test, because each school decides for itself how to use the score.

One difference from England is worth spelling out. An English area test is usually sat in September and results arrive in October, before the national application deadline. The SEAG outcome comes in late January, and the application process for Northern Ireland, run by the Education Authority rather than SEAG, follows it. So a Northern Ireland family meets the test later, in P7, and decides on schools with the score already in hand.

Find your test

How do I find out exactly which test my child will sit?

Work from the schools back to the test, not from the test brand forward. These steps take an evening, and they save a year of preparing for the wrong paper.

  1. List every selective school you might name on the application, including any just over a county or borough line. Selection follows the school, not your postcode.
  2. For each school, open its own admissions page and find the test for your child's entry year. Check the year printed on the page: several areas are changing provider for 2028 entry.
  3. Write down who runs the test (council, consortium or school), who writes it (GL, Quest, FSCE, Cambridge Insight or the school), and whether one registration covers several schools.
  4. Find the paper list. Note whether maths is a paper of its own, a section inside a mixed paper, or absent; and whether answers are multiple choice, written into digit boxes, or worked in full.
  5. Note the registration window. In 2026 Bexley's ran in March of Year 5, Buckinghamshire's in May and June, Tiffin School's closed on 1 September; missing it usually means no test.
  6. Download only the familiarisation material the test's own provider or council offers. Several of them say plainly that commercial packs may not match the real paper.
  7. Then match the maths preparation to the paper: depth across the Year 5 curriculum for most English tests, key stage 1 for a 7 plus, GCSE algebra for 16 plus.

Worked example (ours): pace

Plymouth's 2026 maths paper was 60 questions in 40 minutes. Our arithmetic: 40 times 60 is 2,400 seconds, divided by 60 questions is 40 seconds a question. Ripon's new section is about 25 questions in 25 minutes, so about 60 seconds each. Same age, half as much time again per question in one place as the other.

Worked example (ours): share

In Buckinghamshire maths carries 25% of the weighting, and in Bexley numerical reasoning also carries 25%. In the London 11+ Consortium sitting, maths takes 20 of 100 minutes, which our sum makes one fifth of the time. In Lincolnshire, by contrast, the maths share is nil, so a maths tutor cannot raise the score.

Worked example (ours): format

A child at Sutton sees a multiple-choice maths paper in the SET, then a second-stage maths paper that is "not multiple choice". The same skill, choosing the method, is tested two ways within a few weeks. We teach checking by estimation for the first and full working for the second.

Selective test questions

What parents ask about UK selective school tests

Is the 11 plus the same test everywhere?

No. Each council, consortium or school chooses its own test and provider, so the papers, subjects, timing and dates differ by area and sometimes by school within an area. Our master table shows 29 areas side by side.

Which 11 plus test does my area use?

Find your area in our table, then confirm on the admissions page of every school you are considering. In 2026, GL Assessment, Quest Assessments and FSCE each supplied several areas, and some schools set their own papers.

Does every 11 plus include maths?

No. Lincolnshire's consortium tests only verbal and non-verbal reasoning, and Newstead Wood School in Bromley says it tests no subject other than reasoning. Most other areas include maths, either as its own paper or as a section of a mixed paper.

Is CEM still used for the 11 plus?

CEM is now Cambridge Insight; its own timeline says the change came in 2025. Its entrance test, Cambridge Select Insight, is sold only to schools and local authorities, and in the grammar areas we checked the paper tests have moved to other providers.

When is the 11 plus taken?

In most English areas, in September of Year 6, with registration in the summer term of Year 5. The exceptions include Reading School, which tested on 16 July 2026, and Gloucestershire, whose schools plan to test on Saturday 3 July 2027.

What is the difference between GL, Quest and FSCE?

They are separate companies that each write bespoke papers for the schools or councils that buy them. GL and Quest papers are usually multiple choice on an answer sheet; FSCE papers mix multiple choice with short written answers.

How is the Northern Ireland transfer test different?

It is one SEAG test for the whole of Northern Ireland, sat in P7 on two Saturdays in November, with 56 maths questions across the two papers. Outcomes arrive in late January, and each school decides how to use the score.

What about independent schools and the 13 plus?

Many independent schools use the ISEB Common Pre-Tests in Year 6 or 7, UKiset for pupils from abroad, a consortium sitting such as the London 11+ Consortium, or their own test. At 13 plus, ISEB's Common Entrance and CASE scholarship papers apply.

When are your lessons?
