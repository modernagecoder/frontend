IB Computer Science in Germany

Does IB Computer Science count for a German university?

It can be one of the six subjects, but not in place of a science. The Kultusministerkonferenz sets the rules for when an IB Diploma counts as a German university entrance qualification.

What the KMK agreement says

The KMK agreement on recognising the IB Diploma, as amended on 15 June 2023, requires two languages, mathematics, a social science and "ein naturwissenschaftliches Fach (Biology, Chemistry, Physics)" among the six exam subjects. Computer Science appears only in the list of subjects allowed for the sixth slot, so it cannot stand in for the science.

Of the three Higher Level subjects, at least one must be a language, mathematics or a science from that list, and all six subjects need at least an IB grade 4, with one 3 allowed if it is balanced by a 5 at the same level or higher and 24 points in total.

The maths footnote, and the two Frankfurt names

A separate footnote restricts graduates who take Mathematics at Standard Level to subject-bound university access outside maths, science and technology. Schools listed in the agreement's Anlage 1 are the exception.

In Anlage 1, dated 26 March 2026, Frankfurt International School is number 25 and Metropolitan School Frankfurt number 31. For their graduates, Mathematics SL does not by itself limit access. Families should still check every subject choice with the school's own counsellors before the Diploma begins.

Live, in English

How do our lessons fit an international school week?

International school pupils already study in English, so the language is the easy part. The harder part is time, and matching the exact syllabus of each programme.

English, as at school

We teach only in English, never in German. That suits an international school pupil, but we still adapt: mentors adjust their pace and accent to each learner, including pupils for whom English is a third language after German and a home language.

The right syllabus

An IB pupil, an IGCSE pupil and an AP pupil need different things in the same week: a case study, pseudocode rules, or Java free-response practice. Each course follows its own programme, and one to one we work from the pupil's school tasks.

Mentors are based in India and teach live over video. A Frankfurt pupil who finishes school mid-afternoon can take a lesson before dinner, and a weekend slot works well before internal assessment deadlines.

A Frankfurt data project

Joining Frankfurt's car and bike counts: where did 32 rows go?

Frankfurt publishes average traffic volumes for road segments: one table for motor vehicles, another for bicycles. Putting them side by side is a join, the most common operation in database work, and the place where data most often disappears without an error message.

The data

Stadt Frankfurt am Main publishes WFS Verkehrsmengen under the Datenlizenz Deutschland Namensnennung 2.0. The city describes the values as averages of the years 2019 to 2023 and notes that the bicycle counts were taken between the kerbs. We downloaded two layers on 3 November 2026: 530 segments for motor vehicles and 529 for bicycles.

Each segment is identified by two node numbers, the junction it starts from and the one it ends at. We saved both layers as kfz.csv and rad.csv with four columns: from_node, to_node, street and average.

The task

For each bicycle segment, find the motor-vehicle count on the same segment, so we can compare them. The natural key is the pair (from_node, to_node). Version 1 builds a dictionary for each table and keeps the bike rows whose key is also in the car table.

Before running it, predict: with 529 bike rows and 530 car rows, how many should match?

Looking at the 32 lost rows shows the cause at once. Every one of them is in the car table, but with its two node numbers the other way round: the bike layer drew the segment from junction A to B, the car layer from B to A. A road segment has no direction here, so the two keys describe the same thing. The fix is to normalise the key, always putting the smaller node number first, before either table is built.

Now every bike row finds its partner, and only one car segment, on Wilhelmshöher Straße, has no bike row at all. The comparison can start: on 34 segments the bike average is higher than the motor-vehicle average. The head of that list is Oeder Weg, with 3,631.67 bikes against 2,634.67 motor vehicles, followed by Scheffelstraße with 2,304 against 1,323.

The city notes that cycle volumes were measured between the kerbs, so cycle tracks off the carriageway are not in these numbers; 26 bike rows are 0, among them motorway sections of the A 5, A 66, A 648 and A 661.

  1. IGCSE. Write the join in pseudocode with two arrays and a nested loop, then explain why a dictionary is faster for 529 rows.
  2. IB. State the precondition Version 1 silently assumed about the keys, and write a test that would have caught the 32 lost rows.
  3. AP. Rewrite segment() in Java as a method that returns a String key such as "62506009-62506013", and check it with both orders.
  4. Going further. The street names are in both tables. Count how many matched pairs have different street names, and decide whether that is a data problem or not.

Beyond the syllabus

Can international school pupils in Frankfurt enter German computing contests?

Yes. The national informatics contest is open by age, not by school type, and it is a serious challenge for pupils who enjoy hard problems.

Bundeswettbewerb Informatik

The 45th Bundeswettbewerb Informatik opened on 1 September 2026, and its first round closes on 23 November 2026. It is open to young people up to 21 who are not yet at university or in work, and the first round allows group work.

Round two follows in early 2027, round three in September 2027. The organisers choose the German team for the International Olympiad in Informatics from the top of this contest.

How we help

Our informatics competitions course trains with original problems in the style of these rounds, in English. We never solve a pupil's contest submission for them, and we are not connected with the organisers.

Because round one allows group work, a pupil can team up with classmates; round two must be done alone, so it is worth starting early.

Computer science help for Frankfurt international school pupils: questions

Are the classes in German?

No. We teach in English only, and every lesson is live. That matches the language of international schools, and mentors still adjust their pace and accent to each pupil, including those for whom English is a second or third language.

Which programmes do you cover?

IB Diploma Computer Science at SL and HL, Cambridge IGCSE Computer Science 0478, and AP Computer Science A and Principles. We also teach the German Informatik Oberstufe course for pupils who move to a Hessian Gymnasium.

Can IB Computer Science replace a science for German university entry?

No. Under the KMK agreement on the IB Diploma, one of the six subjects must be Biology, Chemistry or Physics; Computer Science is allowed only as the sixth subject. Check every subject choice with the school before the Diploma starts.

Do you write or check internal assessments?

We never write any part of an internal assessment or coursework. We teach the skills behind it, such as planning, testing and evaluating, and we can explain the published criteria. The work and the decisions stay the pupil's.

Which language do you teach, Python or Java?

Whichever the school uses. Many IB pupils work in Python or Java, IGCSE pupils need Cambridge pseudocode alongside a language, and AP Computer Science A is examined in Java.

When are lessons in Frankfurt time?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. An early evening slot after school, or Sunday afternoon, suits many pupils.

Is the Bundeswettbewerb Informatik open to international school pupils?

Yes, it is open to young people up to 21 who are not yet at university or working. The 45th contest's first round runs from 1 September to 23 November 2026, and group work is allowed in that round.

Where are the mentors?

In India, teaching live over video. 17:20 in Frankfurt is 21:50 for the mentor in winter and 20:50 in summer. The pupil keeps the same mentor through the school year.

What do lessons cost, and in which currency?

Lessons are billed monthly. Prices here appear in euro at the European Central Bank reference rate, but the charge is in US dollars and your bank converts it, so the statement can differ a little. The first lesson is free, and no card is needed.

Do you know the schools in Frankfurt?

We follow the published IB, Cambridge and College Board syllabuses that the schools teach. We are not connected with any school, and we describe them on this page only from the AGIS directory.
