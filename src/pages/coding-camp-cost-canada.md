### Why franchise fees are hard to find

A franchise such as Code Ninjas runs one national website, but each centre is locally owned and sets its own camp calendar. The national page sells the idea ("Code Ninjas Camps empower kids to gain valuable tech skills and make new friends") and then hands you to a location finder.

That is normal for franchises, not a warning sign. It does mean the only honest way to learn a Code Ninjas camp price is to ask the centre near you, in writing, with the hours of the day and the number of children per leader.

### What a university camp is really selling

University outreach camps are mostly broad STEM weeks rather than coding weeks. That is a good week for a curious child. It is a different purchase from a coding course: coding is one strand of the day, and the leaders are university students. Both points are worth knowing before you compare a camp fee with a class fee.

## What a week of camp costs per hour, worked out

A weekly fee only becomes comparable once you divide it by what it buys. Here is our worked example on the published Waterloo figures, then a short Python program that does the same for four camps. The method is the point: the answer changes with the hours you divide by.

**Our worked example: one ESQ week at the University of Waterloo, summer 2026, divided three ways (fee and times from the camp page; arithmetic ours)**

| Divide the fee by | Hours in the week | Result (ours) |
|---|---|---|
| Programmed hours, 9:00 am to 4:00 pm, five days | 35 | $10.34 CAD per programmed hour |
| Door to door, drop-off from 8:30 to pick-up by 4:30, five days | 40 | $9.05 CAD per supervised hour |
| Days in the week | 5 days | $72.40 CAD per day |
| Days in the short week of 4 to 7 August, at its own fee | 4 days | $72.50 CAD per day |

Inputs from the [ESQ Summer Camps](https://uwaterloo.ca/engineering-outreach/programs/engineering-science-quest/summer-camps) page: a week at $362 CAD, the short week at $290 CAD, "Programming: 9:00 am - 4:00 pm", drop off from 8:30 and pick up by 4:30. The divisions are ours, rounded to the cent.

Door to door, the same week works out at a lower hourly figure, simply because you divide by more hours. Neither figure is wrong. The first tells you what the planned activities cost; the second tells you what the supervision costs. If what you are buying is childcare for working hours, the second is the fairer number. If what you are buying is learning, even the first is generous, because not every programmed hour is a coding hour.

**Our program · weekly fee to hourly rate** (python)
```python
# Our program: what a week of day camp costs per hour (written by Modern Age Coders)
# Each fee and each start and end time is copied from the camp's own page.
camps = [
    ("Waterloo ESQ, 5-day week",     362, "9:00", "16:00", 5),
    ("Western, Discovery Western",   325, "9:00", "16:00", 5),
    ("uOttawa, EngTech (Gr. 4-6)",   349, "9:00", "16:00", 5),
    ("UVic, Science Venture",        350, "9:00", "16:00", 5),
]

def hours(start, end):
    h1, m1 = map(int, start.split(":"))
    h2, m2 = map(int, end.split(":"))
    return (h2 * 60 + m2 - h1 * 60 - m1) / 60

for name, fee, start, end, days in camps:
    week = hours(start, end) * days
    print(f"{name:30} {week:4.0f} h a week  {fee / week:6.2f} an hour")
```

**What it prints** (text)
```text
Waterloo ESQ, 5-day week         35 h a week   10.34 an hour
Western, Discovery Western       35 h a week    9.29 an hour
uOttawa, EngTech (Gr. 4-6)       35 h a week    9.97 an hour
UVic, Science Venture            35 h a week   10.00 an hour
```
Run under Python on 31 October 2026. The fees are the published 2026 five-day fees; every camp here lists a 9-to-4 day, so each week is 35 hours. Change the start and end times to your own drop-off and pick-up and the hourly rate drops.

### Divide by the hours, then ask which hours are coding

The program can only divide by the hours a camp prints. The question it cannot answer is how many of those 35 hours your child spends writing and fixing code. In a broad STEM week that might be one session a day; in a dedicated coding week it might be most of the afternoon.

Ask the camp for its daily schedule. Waterloo, for one, sends registered families "a weekly schedule of the activities/tours", so the number exists. Once you have it, divide the fee by the coding hours alone, and you have the figure that is actually comparable with a coding class.

Our holiday camp is not a day camp, so we do not pretend its hours match. On the kids track it is eight one-to-one classes of 60 to 75 minutes, which is 8 to 10 hours of a teacher working with one child (our arithmetic from our camp pages). On the teen track each class is 90 minutes, so 12 hours.

## How to compare coding camps fairly, in seven steps

Use these steps on any camp, including ours. They turn a list of weekly fees into a decision you can defend.

1. Write down the fee exactly as the camp publishes it, with its season. A 2026 fee is a record, not a promise for 2027. Note whether it is for a five-day or a four-day week, and whether tax is included.
2. List every extra the week needs. Morning care, afternoon care, lunches, transport, any optional add-ons. Some are free (uOttawa's 8-to-9 and 4-to-5 program on campus); some are not. Keep each as its own line rather than folding them into one guess.
3. Count the hours of instruction, not the hours of the day. Ask for the daily schedule and mark the hours spent coding or building. A 9-to-4 day with a long lunch, outdoor games and a tour has far fewer teaching hours than seven.
4. Ask the ratio, and who the leaders are. How many children share one leader during the coding blocks? Are the leaders trained teachers, university students or volunteers? Ontario Tech, for example, advertises "certified teacher camp counsellors"; ask every camp the same question.
5. Ask what your child will build, and keep. A finished project the child can open at home and explain is worth more than five half-finished ones left on a camp laptop. Ask whether projects can be taken home or shared online.
6. Check what happens afterwards. Does anything continue after Friday? A camp that ends with a clear next step (a club, a course, the same teacher) turns a week into a start. One that simply ends is a good week, and that is all.
7. Only then compare prices. Divide the fee by the coding hours from step 3. Put each camp, and any online option, on that same footing, with its extras listed beside it. The cheapest week per coding hour is rarely the cheapest week on the fee line.

Our promise sits inside steps 3 and 5. We show students how to reason a problem out and write the code themselves, never to lift an answer from someone else or hand the thinking to an AI tool. The same quality of teaching reaches a child in Halifax, Saskatoon or Surrey as one in India or anywhere else we teach, because the class is live and the teacher is the same.

### For parents

Ask your child after the first day what they built, not whether it was fun. A child who can describe a loop or a bug they fixed has had a coding day.

Pick one project and finish it. A small game that works beats three big ideas that never ran.
Save your work somewhere you can open at home. A project left on a camp computer is gone on Friday.

## Coding camp costs in Canada: questions and answers

### How much does a coding camp cost in Canada?

It depends on the kind of camp. University and non-profit STEM day camps print their weekly fees on their own sites, and the 2026 fees we found were close to each other for a 9-to-4 week. The table on this page lists each camp's fee as published, with its season and hours.

### How much does a Code Ninjas camp cost?

The Code Ninjas national camps page prints no fee. Each centre is locally run and sets its own camp prices, so ask the centre near you for the weekly fee, the daily hours and how many children share each leader.

### What does a March Break coding camp cost?

The University of Ottawa ran a STEM camp with micro:bit coding for Grades 2 to 6 from 16 to 20 March 2026, and its fee is in the table above. Most camps had not published March Break 2027 fees when we checked on 31 October 2026.

### Why is a four-day camp week cheaper?

Weeks with a statutory or civic holiday, such as Canada Day or the August civic holiday in Ontario, run four days and are priced lower. Per day, the Waterloo short week works out almost the same as a full week, by our arithmetic.

### Is before and after care included in the camp fee?

Not always. uOttawa offers free care from 8 to 9 and 4 to 5 on campus, while Waterloo, Western and Science Venture charge for extended hours. Ask each camp and list care as its own line when you compare.

### Are there bursaries for coding camps?

Several university camps offer them. Waterloo takes bursary applications in December and January for the following summer, Western reviews a limited number in batches, and SCI-FI in Saskatoon said its 2026 fund was full. Apply before you register.

### What does your online holiday camp cost?

It is one payment for the whole camp, not a monthly fee, and the fees section of this page shows the amount in your own currency. It covers eight live one-to-one classes and the five camp projects.

### How long are the classes in your camp?

Our camp pages give 60 to 75 minutes a class on the kids track, 90 minutes for teens and 90 to 120 minutes for adults. The summer camp runs over two to three weeks and the winter camp over three.

### What times are your online camp classes in Canada?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Each time is set in your own province's clock.

### Is a cheaper hourly rate always better value?

No. A day camp hour includes games, breaks and supervision, while a one-to-one class hour is all teaching. Divide each fee by the hours actually spent coding before deciding which is better value for your goal.

### Can my child do a day camp and an online camp in the same summer?

Yes, and many families do. A campus week gives friends and outdoor time; online classes give steady coding progress at home, in the weeks around it, with one teacher throughout.
