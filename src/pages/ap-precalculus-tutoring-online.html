The exam

What is on the AP Precalculus exam, and when is it?

The 2027 exam is on Tuesday, May 11, 2027, in the morning session. It has 42 multiple-choice questions in 1 hour 45 minutes and four free-response questions in 1 hour 10 minutes, and it covers Units 1, 2 and 3 only.

The date and the day

College Board's 2027 schedule runs AP Exams over two weeks, May 3 to 7 and May 10 to 14, 2027. AP Precalculus is in Session 1 on Tuesday, May 11, and for most schools in the lower 48 states, Hawaii and Washington, D.C., Session 1 starts at 8 a.m. local time.

The same Tuesday has AP Statistics in Session 2, and AP Calculus AB and BC fall on Monday, May 10. A student taking two of these should plan the week, not just the morning.

What kind of exam it is

College Board describes AP Precalculus as a hybrid digital exam. It publishes test previews in the Bluebook app and a sample free-response booklet so students can see in advance how they will answer the free-response questions.

The free-response questions are each worth 6 points, and two of them, according to the AP Students page, "incorporate a real-world function modeling context". Students are expected to explain, not only to compute.

The course

What are the AP Precalculus units and how much is each worth?

Four units, three of them on the exam. On the multiple-choice section, College Board weights Unit 1 at 30 to 40 percent, Unit 2 at 25 to 40 percent and Unit 3 at 30 to 35 percent. Unit 4 is taught where states or districts require it and is not assessed.

The two ideas under every unit

The Course and Exam Description opens Unit 1 with two concepts: covariation, "how output values change in tandem with changing input values", and rates of change, "including average rate of change, rate of change at a point, and changing rates of change". Topic 1.1 is literally called Change in Tandem.

Those ideas return for every function type. An exponential model is a function whose output changes by a constant ratio; a sinusoidal one has rates of change that repeat. Students who learn each function as a separate list of rules miss the thread the exam is built on.

Precise language is assessed

Up to 40 percent of the exam weighting goes to Communication and Reasoning, and the CED shows the standard it means with a model sentence: "On the closed interval 0 to 1, as the value of x increases, the value of y increases then decreases."

Its pool example goes further, naming inputs, outputs and units: the average rate of change of volume from 2 to 5 minutes "is 0.4 cubic meters per minute". We teach students to write like that from the first week, because the free-response scoring expects it.

Calculator rules, our arithmetic

Can you use a calculator on the AP Precalculus exam?

On some parts only. A graphing calculator is required for multiple-choice Part B and free-response Part A, and not permitted on multiple-choice Part A or free-response Part B. Adding College Board's published shares, 62.5 percent of the score is earned without a calculator.

What the calculator is for

The CED is specific: "students must learn to identify zeros, points of intersection, and extrema using graphing calculator technology", and they also fit linear, quadratic, cubic and quartic regressions to data. AP Students adds that the calculator parts are answered in radian mode.

So the calculator sections are not easier versions of the others. They test whether a student can set up a model and read a graph correctly, which is its own skill and needs its own practice.

What it means for study time

Most of the marks need algebra by hand: exact values, log rules, factoring, rewriting trigonometric expressions. A student who practices everything with a calculator at their elbow is practicing for the smaller share of the exam.

We split practice the same way the exam does: hand-only sessions for symbolic work, and separate calculator sessions for modeling, regression and graph reading.

Free response, ours

What are the four AP Precalculus free-response questions?

College Board names them: Function Concepts and Modeling a Non-Periodic Context, both with a graphing calculator, then Modeling a Periodic Context and Symbolic Manipulations, both without. Each is worth 6 points. The examples below are our own, written in that style, not College Board questions.

Our periodic model, in the style of FRQ 3. A Ferris wheel seat is 5 meters above the ground at its lowest point and 45 meters at its highest, and the wheel turns once every 8 minutes. A rider boards at the lowest point at t = 0. A model is h(t) = 25 − 20 cos(πt / 4), with h in meters and t in minutes. (i) State the midline, amplitude and period. (ii) When is the rider first 35 meters up? (iii) Describe how the rate of change of h behaves on 0 ≤ t ≤ 4.

(i) Reading the model

The midline is h = 25, halfway between 5 and 45. The amplitude is 20, half of the 40-meter difference. The period is 8 minutes, since πt / 4 reaches 2π when t = 8.

(ii) Solving without a calculator

25 − 20 cos(πt / 4) = 35 gives cos(πt / 4) = −1/2. The first positive solution is πt / 4 = 2π/3, so t = 8/3 minutes, about 2 minutes 40 seconds after boarding.

(iii) The rate of change

On 0 < t < 4, h is increasing. Its rate of change increases on 0 < t < 2 and decreases on 2 < t < 4, so h is concave up and then concave down, with the fastest climb at t = 2, at the midline.

How the answer should be written

Part (iii) is where most of the points sit, and the language matters. Our model answer: "On the interval 0 < t < 4, h is increasing. The average rates of change over successive one-minute intervals increase from 5.86 to 14.14 meters per minute and then decrease to 5.86, so the rate of change of h increases and then decreases." That is the CED's precise-language standard applied.

A student who writes "it goes up faster then slower" has the right idea and may not earn the reasoning point. The difference is naming the function, the interval and the units.

A symbolic example, FRQ 4 style

Our problem: solve log2(x) + log2(x − 2) = 3. Combine the logs: log2(x(x − 2)) = 3, so x(x − 2) = 8, x2 − 2x − 8 = 0 and (x − 4)(x + 2) = 0. Only x = 4 is in the domain of both logarithms; x = −2 must be rejected, with a reason.

The rejection is the point. Symbolic Manipulations questions reward a correct answer with a justified domain check, not just the algebra.

Why problem 1 is a trap

An average rate of change of 0 does not mean f is constant. On [0, 2] the cubic dips below the axis and comes back; the start and end heights happen to match. The CED separates average rate of change from rate of change at a point for exactly this reason.

A good free-response answer would say so: "the average rate of change over [0, 2] is 0, although f is not constant on that interval".

A semi-log check, Unit 2

Topic 2.15 is Semi-log Plots. Our example: for y = 3 × 2x, log y = log 3 + x log 2, so plotting log y against x gives a straight line with slope log 2, about 0.301 in base 10. A straight semi-log plot is how data reveals an exponential model.

Students meeting this for the first time often expect the curve to stay curved. Seeing the line appear is one of the more satisfying moments in the course.

All seven problems, the explanations and the semi-log example are ours. For official practice, College Board publishes past free-response questions with scoring guidelines on AP Central and test previews in Bluebook; AP Classroom progress checks are available through a student's AP class section.

Questions from parents

AP Precalculus: what students and parents ask

When is the AP Precalculus exam in 2027?

College Board schedules it for Tuesday, May 11, 2027, in Session 1. For most US schools, Session 1 starts at 8 a.m. local time.

How long is the AP Precalculus exam?

2 hours and 55 minutes: 42 multiple-choice questions in 1 hour 45 minutes, then four free-response questions in 1 hour 10 minutes.

What units are on the AP Precalculus exam?

Units 1, 2 and 3: polynomial and rational functions, exponential and logarithmic functions, and trigonometric and polar functions. Unit 4, on parameters, vectors and matrices, is not assessed.

Do I need a graphing calculator for AP Precalculus?

Yes. A graphing calculator is required for multiple-choice Part B and free-response Part A, in radian mode, and not permitted on the other two parts. College Board lists approved models on its calculator policy page.

How much of the exam is without a calculator?

Adding College Board's published shares, multiple-choice Part A (43.75 percent) and free-response Part B (18.75 percent) make 62.5 percent of the score with no calculator allowed.

What are the free-response questions on AP Precalculus?

Four, each worth 6 points: Function Concepts, Modeling a Non-Periodic Context, Modeling a Periodic Context and Symbolic Manipulations. The first two allow a graphing calculator; the last two do not.

Is AP Precalculus the same as regular precalculus?

College Board designs it as the equivalent of a first-semester college precalculus course, with a set framework and an end-of-course exam. Regular precalculus varies by school and has no AP Exam.

Can AP Precalculus earn college credit?

It can, depending on the college. College Board says qualifying scores may earn credit, placement or both, and its AP Credit Policy Search shows each college's rule.

What should my child know before AP Precalculus?

College Board lists the Algebra 1, Geometry and Algebra 2 sequence, including quadratics, factoring, right-triangle trigonometry, systems of equations, exponent rules, radicals and complex numbers.

Which days and times can my child take classes?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays.

Are you affiliated with College Board?

No. Modern Age Coders is an independent tutoring school. AP and Advanced Placement are College Board trademarks, and College Board has no connection with our classes.
