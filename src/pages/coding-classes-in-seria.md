## What is Seria, in figures?

Seria lies sixty-five kilometres west of Bandar Seri Begawan and covers a municipal area of 1.56 square kilometres. The town recorded **2,659** residents at the 2021 census. The mukim surrounding it recorded **18,313**, so the two figures describe different things and are worth keeping apart: the town is roughly a seventh of the mukim named after it.

The field beneath it was located in **1929** by the British Malayan Petroleum Company. Production began in **1931** and the first export followed in 1932. By 1935 royalties accounted for **47 per cent of state revenue**, and between 1932 and 1959 the government received $340 million in royalties. The field has been continuously in production since 1931.

What sits around the town is disproportionate to its population: the Seria refinery, the only refinery in Brunei, the Seria Crude Oil Terminal, a gas compression plant, and at **Panaga** the headquarters of Brunei Shell Petroleum.

## The Seria student data project: a cumulative total can only go up

Every page in this cluster works through one analytical mistake. Seria has a monument to this one.

The Billionth Barrel Monument was built in 1991 to mark the billionth barrel of crude produced from the Seria field. Given that, and that production started in 1931, was the field doing well in 1991?

The first instinct is yes. But **a cumulative total is the sum of everything up to a date, so it can rise, it can stay flat, and it can never go down.** Its direction is fixed by arithmetic before any data arrives, and a quantity whose direction is guaranteed carries no information in that direction.

What the figure does support: 1931 to 1991 is sixty years, so a billion barrels is an average of about **16.7 million barrels a year**, or roughly **45,600 barrels a day**, sustained across six decades. That average is real and it is the only rate the cumulative number gives you. A field producing at ten times that rate in 1955 and a tenth of it in 1990 reaches exactly the same total on exactly the same date.

Plot the two side by side and the point is visible. The cumulative curve climbs and keeps climbing. Its **slope** is the annual production rate, and slope is where the information lives. A cumulative curve that is flattening describes a field in decline, and it is still a line going up and to the right.

The general rule is worth more than the example. **Ask whether the quantity you are looking at is capable of moving in both directions.** Total users ever registered, total cases recorded, total miles driven, total revenue since founding: none can fall, so none can tell you anything has got worse. The correction is one line of code: difference the series and look at the change rather than the level.

## Does coding still matter when AI can write it?

Ask any assistant whether the Seria field was performing well in 1991 and you will usually get a fluent paragraph built around the billionth barrel, because that is what the internet has about Seria in 1991. It will not normally point out that a cumulative milestone says nothing about that year. Ask directly whether a cumulative total can decline and it will answer correctly. The gap is that nobody asked, and it does not volunteer the objection to its own answer.
