The region in figures

Who lives in the Puno region, and is Juliaca bigger than Puno?

The 2025 census counted 1,232,993 people in the department of Puno, over 66,963.7 square kilometres of altiplano and Amazon slope. The capital is the city of Puno, but the province of San Román, home to Juliaca, is the more populous one.

Two cities, 2017 figures

INEI has not yet released city figures from the 2025 census. In 2017, it counted 128,637 people in the city of Puno. For Juliaca the published figure is for one centro poblado, the town itself: 217,743 people at 3,877 metres above sea level. A neighbouring centro poblado in the district of San Miguel is listed separately, and we do not add the two together.

INEI's 2017 directory puts the centro poblado of Puno at 3,848 metres, beside Lake Titicaca. Both sit well above Cusco, whose centro poblado the same directory places at 3,439 metres.

Children and teenagers today

The 2025 census counted 100,965 people aged 6 to 11 in the department of Puno, and 112,234 aged 12 to 17. Those bands match the ages of primaria and secundaria.

They are counts of people actually interviewed, so they sit slightly below what a share of the 1,232,993 headline total, which includes INEI's estimate of people missed, would give.

Other towns families mention, such as Ilave, Juli, Azángaro, Ayaviri or Yunguyo, are smaller, and many learners in the region live in rural centros poblados. That matters for this page, because a live online class depends on a connection, and in Puno that is the real question. Universities are a different story: INEI's compendium counts 20,419 undergraduates at the Universidad Nacional del Altiplano and 3,992 at the Universidad Nacional de Juliaca in 2025, a large pool of young adults within reach of an online class.

Maths results

How well do Puno pupils do in maths?

Very well in primaria. On the 2025 national assessment, 42.1% of Puno's 4° de primaria pupils reached the satisfactory level in maths, against 30.0% nationally. In 2° de secundaria the region also finished above the national figure.

What that means for a family

A child who already reasons well with numbers is ready for more than the timetable offers. Peru's national curriculum, the CNEB, has no computing area at all: digital skills are a transversal competency spread across subjects, and the regular secundaria week holds 4 hours of Matemática and 3 of Inglés but nothing on programming.

The drop between primaria and secundaria is national, not just Puno's: 30.0% of 4° de primaria pupils across Peru were satisfactory in maths in 2025, against 12.7% in 2° de secundaria. Keeping a strong primaria pupil stretched through the first years of secundaria is where outside practice helps most.

The ONEM route

Secundaria students enter the national school maths olympiad, the ONEM, through their colegio. In 2026 the school stage took place on Wednesday 8 July 2026, with level 1 for 1° and 2°, level 2 for 3° and 4°, and level 3 for 5° de secundaria, and separate Alfa and Beta categories for public and private schools. The paper had ten questions and two hours.

Winners move up through the UGEL and regional stages; the 2026 national stage was held on 11 October 2026 at Huampaní with 237 finalists. We prepare students with our own problems in the same spirit and have no connection with MINEDU or the ONEM.

MINEDU places a child by age on 31 March, under Law 28044's six years of primaria and five of secundaria, so a strong 10 year old in 5° de primaria can join an olympiad-style group with 11 and 12 year olds if the mentor agrees. Puno's public schools taught 93.9 thousand primaria pupils and 84.7 thousand secundaria students in 2025, with 27,116 teachers across all levels; the region's results come from their classrooms, and our role is only to add practice alongside them. For maths taught as its own subject in English, see our maths teachers in English for Peru.

Getting connected

How many Puno homes can take a live online class?

Fewer than in most of Peru. Only 24.8% of Puno households had an internet connection in 2025, according to INEI's ENAHO survey, compared with 56.8% nationally. Personal use is much higher, at 69.1% of people aged 6 and over, because many people go online by phone or away from home.

Who we can serve well

Families in Juliaca and Puno city with a home connection and a laptop can take any of our courses. University students who already follow online lectures usually have what they need. These are the learners we expect to teach most often from the region.

A household that goes online only by phone can still try the free class. Scratch and maths reasoning can work on a tablet with a steady signal; typed Python or C++ really needs a keyboard and a bigger screen.

What we will not pretend

We will not promise that a weak mobile signal in a rural centro poblado will carry a one-hour video lesson. The project below shows how much mobile coverage depends on which numbers you read, and families know their own signal better than any table.

If the connection is the problem, one to one at a quiet hour often helps more than a group, because the mentor can pause and repeat without holding others up.

A real Puno project

Does Puno city have 4G? It depends which column you read

OSIPTEL, Peru's telecoms regulator, publishes the share of every centro poblado covered by each operator's mobile network. Our students use the Puno rows to learn two habits: when one place has several columns, combine them before you count, and read the data dictionary before you decide which column answers your question.

The data

The dataset is "Porcentaje de cobertura móvil por centro poblado, empresa operadora y tecnología", published by OSIPTEL on the Plataforma Nacional de Datos Abiertos under the Open Data Commons Attribution Licence (ODC-By). Its dictionary says the figures run to the fourth quarter of 2024, were updated in May 2025, and come from the operators themselves.

Each of the 108,115 rows is one centro poblado in Peru; 10,544 are in Puno, 164 urban and 10,380 rural. For each of four operators and each technology there are two percentages: "CG", the share of the place inside the operator's guaranteed coverage footprint, and "CG+CAR", the share inside that footprint plus additional network capacity.

The question

How many places in Puno have at least half their area under 4G? A first attempt picks the first 4G column in the file and counts the places where it reads 50% or more.

That sounds reasonable until you look at a single row. For the centro poblado of Puno itself, the four operators' guaranteed 4G figures are 27%, 25%, 2.52% and 100%.

Version 1 reads one operator and ignores three. By its count, Puno city is not covered, because 27% is under half. But a family in Puno only needs one network that works, so the right question for each place is: what is the highest figure among the four operators? That is a job for max() across the columns of the same row.

The count rises from 299 to 457, and Puno city and Juliaca both count as covered. Yet only 65 of 164 urban places reach the line, which is surprising until you reread the dictionary: "CG" is the guaranteed footprint, a cautious figure. The file also gives "CG+CAR", which adds the operators' additional network capacity. Which one to use depends on the question. "Where can I rely on 4G?" suggests CG; "where is there 4G at all?" suggests CG+CAR.

With the wider footprint the count jumps more than elevenfold, to 5,304 of Puno's 10,544 places. None of the three numbers is wrong. They answer three different questions, and a careful report says which one it is answering. One more detail rewards a close reader: the dictionary lists the fourth operator's columns under a different company name from the one in the file header, a small reminder that the dictionary and the data can drift apart.

  1. Under 9, with a map and coloured pencils. Four friends each shade the part of a school yard their torch can light. Is the yard lit if one torch covers it all and the others cover a little?
  2. Scratch, around 9 to 11. Store four percentages in a list and show both the first one and the largest one. Which answers "can I get a signal?"
  3. First Python, around 11 to 13. Try both of the first two programs on the Puno rows and explain why Puno city changes from "not covered" to "covered".
  4. Ages 13 and over. Repeat Version 3 for 3G instead of 4G, then for your own province. Write one sentence stating exactly what each count measures.
  5. Going further. Find the places where every column is 0%. Before writing "no signal", decide what the file can and cannot tell you about them.

Taught in English

Are the classes in Spanish?

No. Every class is taught in English, live and online, by mentors based in India. We say so plainly because in Puno it matters more than anywhere on our list.

The honest starting point

EF's English Proficiency Index 2025 gives the Puno region a score of 417, in its very low band. Peru's national score is 519 and Lima city's is 555. EF publishes no separate score for Juliaca or Puno city.

That does not rule a Puno learner out. It means the mentor starts slowly, uses very short sentences and the chat box, and checks understanding often. For younger children Scratch helps, because the blocks carry pictures and colours as well as words.

Who tends to do well

Learners who already study some English at school or at an institute, university students who read English documentation, and children whose parents can sit nearby for the first weeks all settle in faster.

For others, one to one is usually the better start: a single learner can ask for every instruction twice without feeling watched. The free class is where we find out, and we will tell you honestly if we think the English is too big a step for now. At a time you choose. The whole region uses Peru time, UTC minus five all year, and mentors plan around it.

Time zones: America/Lima is UTC minus 5 with no daylight saving since 1994; Asia/Kolkata is UTC plus 5:30 with none either (tz database). The gap is 10 hours 30 minutes on every day of the year.

Early-morning slots in Puno meet the Indian evening, and they are also hours when a shared home connection is often least busy. Online coding and AI classes in Puno: questions and answers

Are the classes in Spanish?

No. Classes are taught only in English, live and online. Mentors keep sentences short, repeat steps, write key words in the chat and adapt to each learner's pace and accent; Spanish answers are understood and met with the English phrase.

Is English a problem for a child in Puno?

It can be at first. EF scores the Puno region 417, in its very low band, so mentors start slowly and many families begin one to one. Scratch and maths puzzles need little vocabulary, and the free class shows whether the step is manageable.

Do you have classes in Juliaca or Puno city?

Not in person. Every class is online, so a learner in Juliaca, Puno, Ilave, Juli or Azángaro joins from home in the same way.

What do we need at home?

Enough signal to keep a sixty-minute video lesson running, plus a laptop or desktop if possible. In 2025, INEI found an internet connection in 24.8% of Puno households, so please test yours in the free class before choosing a course.

From what age?

Our youngest learners are six and work with Scratch blocks. Most children are ready to type Python by nine or ten. Secundaria students and adults are welcome with no experience at all.

Can you help my child with the ONEM?

We train the reasoning the ONEM rewards with problems we write ourselves, never the organiser's papers. Students enter through their colegio; the 2026 school stage was on 8 July. We are not connected with MINEDU or the ONEM.

What time are classes?

Any day, any time, flexible: choose a batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at the hour that suits your family. India is 10 hours 30 minutes ahead of Puno all year, so 08:30 in Juliaca is 19:00 for the mentor.

Can university students join?

Yes. Students at the Universidad Nacional del Altiplano, the Universidad Nacional de Juliaca or anywhere else join adult groups, usually for Python, algorithms or university maths, at hours around their lectures.

How is payment made?

A card payment each month in US dollars; the soles amount is set by your bank's conversion. The page shows rupees only to people viewing it from India. There is no charge for the first lesson.

Do students receive a certificate?

Certain courses close with a Modern Age Coders completion certificate describing the work done. Peruvian authorities play no part in it: MINEDU, the UGEL, universities and schools neither issue nor back it.

What is the 4G project on this page?

An original Python lesson on OSIPTEL's open file of mobile coverage by centro poblado. Reading one operator's column counts 299 Puno places with half their area under 4G; taking the strongest of four counts 457; using the wider footprint counts 5,304.
