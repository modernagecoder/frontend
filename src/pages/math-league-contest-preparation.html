Math League contest preparation

Before preparing for "Math League", find out which one. The Math League at mathleague.com has mailed contests to schools since 1977: six high school contests a year and single grade-level contests from grade 4 up, taken in class on fixed days. mathleague.org is a separate organization that runs local qualifying contests, then state, national and international championships, for grades 3 to 12. The two differ in almost everything a student feels on the day: length, rounds, calculators and, in one high school round, whether a wrong answer costs points. This page sets out both from their own websites as of 31 October 2026, works out the time per question, shows when guessing pays, and ends with practice problems our teachers wrote.

Two leagues, one name

Is Math League the same as mathleague.org?

No. The Math League (mathleague.com) and mathleague.org are separate organizations with different contests, calendars and rules. Ask the teacher or coach which website the school registered with; the answer decides everything below.

How to tell which one

If a teacher hands out a short paper in class on a Tuesday in February, or once a month in high school math, it is almost certainly The Math League from mathleague.com. Its contests are posted to an advisor at the school, and the school reports scores back.

If the family travels to a host site for a contest day with Sprint, Target and Team rounds, it is likely mathleague.org. Its qualifying results lead on to state and national championships.

Why it matters for preparation

The papers are built differently. A mathleague.com grade 7 sample paper has 35 multiple-choice questions in 30 minutes. A mathleague.org middle school contest has a 40-minute sprint of thirty questions, a target round of four pairs and a team round, and a high schooler there may face a penalty for wrong answers.

Some students do both, and they fit together: one runs on school days through the winter, the other on contest days that lead to spring championships.

Sources: The Math League statement of purpose and FAQ; mathleague.org about page, home page and eligibility page; all checked October 31, 2026. The "how to tell" guidance is ours. Modern Age Coders is not affiliated with either organization.

For mathleague.com, high school contests fall on October 20, November 17 and December 15, 2026, then January 12, February 9 and March 16, 2027. Grades 7 and 8 sit on February 16 or 23, 2027. mathleague.org dates vary by region and are on its calendar.

Reading the calendar

Late registrations are accepted, the schedule says, and alternate high school dates may be set one week after each official date if a school has a conflict. The 6th grade row on the schedule prints a deadline of December 31, 2027, which looks like a typo for 2026; ask the school.

For grades 6 to 8, every student whose score is reported must take the paper at the same sitting. The FAQ says a student absent that day may take it later for a school certificate, but the score cannot go to the League.

Calculators this year

The two mathleague.com notices disagree. The registration page announces "No Calculators Allowed Beginning 2026-2027 School Year". A later notice, dated July 4, 2026, explains that printed papers and the procedures sheet contradicted each other, and that the League has "decided to allow students to use a calculator on the contests for this school year", without a QWERTY keyboard, moving to no calculators the following year.

Our advice is to practice without one anyway. The rule changes next year, and contest questions at this level are built to be done by reasoning.

mathleague.org rounds

What rounds are in a mathleague.org contest?

Middle school contests have Sprint, Target, Team and Countdown rounds with no penalty for wrong answers. High school contests have Sprint, Target, Team and Relay rounds, and the high school Sprint deducts a point for each wrong answer.

What the relay teaches

In a mathleague.org high school relay, each student gets all three questions. The answer to the first is plugged in where the second says "TNYWR" (the number you will receive), and the second answer feeds the third. Correct answers score 2, 3 and 5 points, and the sheet is handed in after three minutes.

The weighting rewards accuracy early in the chain, because a wrong first answer makes the next two wrong. Teams that check question 1 twice before moving on usually do better than teams that rush.

The middle school link to MATHCOUNTS

mathleague.org says its middle school "problems and contest formats" are designed to also prepare students for MATHCOUNTS, and the round names and timings line up. It also states it "is not affiliated in any way with MATHCOUNTS".

Our separate MATHCOUNTS guide covers that series round by round, so a student doing both has one set of habits to learn.

Scoring and strategy, ours

Should you guess on a Math League contest?

On mathleague.com papers and mathleague.org middle school rounds, yes: a blank scores nothing and a wrong answer costs nothing. On the mathleague.org high school Sprint, guess only after ruling out at least one choice.

The high school Sprint gives +4 for a correct answer, −1 for a wrong one and 0 for a skip. Suppose a student has k answer choices left and picks one at random. Our calculation of the average result is (1/k) × 4 + ((k − 1)/k) × (−1) = (5 − k)/k points. The table shows what that means.

Where no penalty applies

mathleague.com sample papers state that a correct answer is worth 1 point and unanswered questions get no credit. mathleague.org middle school rounds give one point per correct answer "with no penalty for questions that are skipped or answered incorrectly". In both, every blank is a lost chance.

So the plan for those papers is simple: watch the clock, and in the last minute fill every remaining answer.

The bigger lesson

mathleague.com sample papers remind students that a contest has no passing or failing score, and the grade 4 sample says few students reach 80 percent correct. A student who expects to finish everything will panic at question 20 of 35.

We teach students to read the scoring rule before the first question, set a target for the number they will attempt, and treat the rest as a bonus. Every later contest has its own rule, and the habit of reading it first carries over.

How we would teach problem 5

Write the number as digits a, b, c with a at least 1. Set a′ = a − 1, so a′ + b + c = 4 with every digit at least 0. Counting the ways to split 4 into three ordered parts gives 15 (the "stars and bars" count of 6 choose 2).

A grade 8 student who lists them by hand will also get 15, more slowly. The lesson is to notice the shift that turns a restricted count into a free one, a trick that reappears all through high school contests.

How we would teach problem 6

Square both sides: (x + 1/x)2 = x2 + 2 + 1/x2 = 9, so x2 + 1/x2 = 7. No need to find x, which is not a whole number.

Students who try to solve for x first lose two minutes on a sixty-second question. Spotting that the question asks for a combination, not the value, is the habit Math League high school papers reward.

All eight problems, the relay chain and the explanations are ours. mathleague.com publishes free sample contests with solutions from 2004-05, 2012-13 and 2018-19; mathleague.org sends a free contest problem set to anyone on its mailing list.

Questions from parents

Math League contests: what parents ask

What is the Math League contest?

There are two. The Math League (mathleague.com), formed in 1977, sends contests for grades 4 to 12 to schools to take in class. mathleague.org is a separate organization that runs local contests and championships for grades 3 to 12.

When are the Math League high school contests in 2026-27?

The Math League lists six: October 20, November 17 and December 15, 2026, then January 12, February 9 and March 16, 2027. Alternate dates one week later are allowed for schools with conflicts.

When is the 7th and 8th grade Math League contest in 2027?

The Math League schedule gives the third Tuesday in February, February 16, 2027, or the fourth Tuesday, February 23, 2027. The registration deadline is December 31, 2026.

Can you use a calculator on Math League contests?

For mathleague.com in 2026-27, a July 4, 2026 notice allows calculators without a QWERTY keyboard this school year, with a no-calculator rule from next year. On mathleague.org, sprint rounds forbid calculators and target and team rounds allow them.

How is the mathleague.org high school sprint scored?

Four points for each correct answer, one point off for each wrong answer and nothing for a skip, across 30 multiple-choice questions in sixty minutes. By our calculation a guess pays on average only once at least one choice is ruled out.

How long is a Math League contest?

The mathleague.com 2018-19 sample papers allow 30 minutes, for 30 questions at grade 4 and Algebra Course 1 and 35 at grade 7. A mathleague.org contest is longer, with several rounds such as a 40-minute middle school sprint.

Can a homeschooled child take Math League contests?

Yes. The Math League mails an unofficial homeschool package in May, or a family can arrange official entry through an accredited local school. mathleague.org lets parents or outside coaches act as the contact.

Does my child need a team?

No. The Math League says there is no minimum number of students. mathleague.org scores each student individually, though a school needs at least two students on each middle school test to be eligible for sweepstakes awards.

Where are free Math League practice tests?

mathleague.com posts sample contests with solutions from 2004-05, 2012-13 and 2018-19, and mathleague.org emails a free contest problem set to anyone who joins its mailing list.

Which days and times can my child take classes?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays.

Are you part of Math League?

No. Modern Age Coders has no connection with The Math League (mathleague.com) or with mathleague.org, and we do not register students for either. Families and schools enter directly.
