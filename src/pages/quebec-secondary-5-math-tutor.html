The final year

What makes Secondary V math different from Secondary IV

Québec.ca describes the secondary Québec Education Program as programs of study "from Secondary I to Secondary V", and Secondary V closes it. Three things change in math that year.

New concepts in every option

The program lists "Concepts introduced in the third year of Cycle Two" for each option. In TS and Science these include logarithmic, sinusoidal and rational functions; in CST, linear programming, probability and graph theory.

A longer independent assignment

Each option builds in a self-directed project for the third year: an optional integrating activity in CST, an exploration activity of 10 to 15 class hours in TS, and a detailed investigation of about 15 class hours in Science.

No ministerial math paper on the 2027 schedule

Québec.ca's 2026 to 2027 schedule lists ministerial math papers for Secondary IV only. With none for Secondary V, the math evaluations a Secondary V student meets in 2026 to 2027 are the school's own.

A note on comparisons: because Quebec secondary school ends at Secondary V, Secondary V is often compared to Grade 11 elsewhere in Canada. That comparison is ours and approximate; the ministry pages we read set no grade equivalence. To pass the subject and earn its credits, Québec.ca sets a final result of 60 percent or more. We help with the learning behind that result; the credits themselves come only from the school system.

Sources: Québec Education Program, Mathematics, Secondary Cycle Two; Québec.ca QEP secondary page and 2026 to 2027 exam schedule; all read 22 October 2026. We have no link to the Ministère de l'Éducation or to any school board.

CST in Secondary V: decisions with data

CST's third year is built around choosing well: optimizing a situation by linear programming, comparing odds and expected values, and modelling networks with graphs. It also brings in money: the program says that when assessing financial situations in Secondary V, students use compounding and discounting rules of the form Cn = C0(1 + i)^n.

For a CST student, the hardest part is usually not the arithmetic but the setting up: turning a paragraph of constraints into inequalities.

TS and Science in Secondary V: the function families

Both options spend much of the year on families of functions and their parameters, then trigonometry on the unit circle and vectors. Science adds conics in standard positions and systems that mix a conic with another equation.

This is the material college calculus starts from. A student who leaves Secondary V fluent with logarithmic and sinusoidal functions arrives at CEGEP ready for it.

How we help with it

We help a student choose a topic they can finish, plan the stages week by week, and check the mathematics in their drafts. We do not write the report. The program asks that the student's own autonomy and initiative be valued, and a report written by someone else defeats the purpose.

A good topic is narrow. "Mathematics in music" is a theme; "how the frequencies of a piano's keys form a geometric sequence" is a project.

Coding as a tool for the project

Several of the program's suggested topics involve technology, from instruments to computer-aided drawing. A short program that tests a conjecture on a thousand cases, or plots a family of functions, can make a Secondary V investigation far more convincing.

This is where our two subjects meet. Students who can write a few lines of Python have a laboratory for their math, and we teach both.

Changing option

Switching options between Secondary IV and V: the program's bridges

Appendix F of the program is written for students who move to a different option between the two final years. It says plainly that "Although all option changes are possible, some are more significant than others."

What Appendix F asks a switching student to focus on (Québec Education Program, read 22 October 2026)
ChangeWhat the program says to emphasize

The program adds two expectations of any switching student: they "should be able to work independently and feel comfortable with the approach and requirements of the new option", and, under the Secondary V teacher's supervision, "must also be willing to devote all the time they need" to catch up. Our reading, labelled as ours: a summer of focused work on the bridge list above is the honest minimum for CST to TS, and the school's own plan is the starting point for CST to Science.

Math and CEGEP

Which Secondary V math do CEGEP programs ask for?

The program says every option allows entry to a pre-university program. Individual college programs then set special conditions for admission. Here are three, quoted from the ministry's own program documents on Québec.ca.

Math in the special conditions for admission of three college programs (ministry program documents, read 22 October 2026)
College programTypeSpecial conditions for admission that involve math
Science (200.B0)Pre-university"Secondary V Mathematics: Technical and Scientific option or Science option", with Secondary V Chemistry and Secondary V Physics
Computer Science and Mathematics (200.C0)Pre-universityThe same: Secondary V math in the TS or Science option, with Secondary V Chemistry and Physics
Computer Science Technology (420.B0)Technical"Secondary IV, Technical and Scientific option OR Secondary IV, Science option OR Secondary V, Cultural, Social and Technical option"

Reading the table

For the science pre-university programs, a CST student meets a wall: both documents ask for Secondary V math in TS or Science. For the technical computer science program, Secondary IV in TS or Science is enough, and so is Secondary V CST.

The 200.C0 document also records its own history: in June 2010 the old conditions "Mathematics 536," "Physics 534" and "Chemistry 534" were replaced by the Secondary V option names. Families who hear the old course numbers are hearing that earlier system.

Six credits short

Québec.ca allows some flexibility at the edge: "Students who are six credits or less short of obtaining their SSD could enter college on the condition that they obtain the missing credits during their first term." The college sets the terms.

That is a safety net, not a plan. A student who needs Secondary V TS or Science math for a program should aim to pass it in Secondary V.

Sources: ministry program documents for Science (200.B0), Computer Science and Mathematics (200.C0) and Computer Science Technology (420.B0); Québec.ca final results. All read 22 October 2026. Colleges may add their own requirements; check each one.

A worked example

A Secondary V CST problem: linear programming at a winter carnival

Linear programming, "Analyzing and optimizing a situation and making decisions", is a Secondary V CST concept. This problem and its solution are ours, written for this page.

The problem (ours). A winter carnival committee carves two kinds of ice sculpture. A small one takes 2 hours of carving and 1 block of ice; a large one takes 3 hours and 3 blocks. The committee has 24 hours of volunteer carving and 18 blocks. Judges award 2 visitor points for a small sculpture and 5 for a large one. How many of each should the committee carve to earn the most points?

  1. Name the variables. Let s be the number of small sculptures and L the number of large ones, with s at least 0 and L at least 0.
  2. Write the constraints. Carving time: 2s + 3L is at most 24. Ice: s + 3L is at most 18. These two inequalities, with the two non-negativity ones, are the system of first-degree inequalities.
  3. Find the polygon of constraints. Its corners are (0, 0), (12, 0) where the time line meets the s-axis, (0, 6) where the ice line meets the L-axis, and the crossing of the two lines: subtract s + 3L = 18 from 2s + 3L = 24 to get s = 6, then L = 4.
  4. Test the function to be optimized at each corner. Points P = 2s + 5L give 0, 24, 30 and, at (6, 4), 12 + 20 = 32.
  5. Decide, and say why. Carve 6 small and 4 large sculptures, for 32 points. It uses all 24 hours (12 + 12) and all 18 blocks (6 + 12), which is why the answer sits on the corner where both constraints are tight.

Where students lose marks on problems like this, in our experience: reversing an inequality when writing a constraint, testing only some of the corners, and stating "(6, 4)" without saying in words what it means for the committee. The last one costs most, because the decision is the point of the exercise.

Questions from Quebec families

Quebec Secondary 5 math: common questions

What is Secondary 5 math in Quebec?

It is the final math course of Quebec secondary school. Students stay in the option they took in Secondary IV, unless they switch, and each option brings in its own third-year concepts: linear programming and probability in CST, function families and trigonometry in TS and Science.

Is Secondary 5 the same as Grade 11?

Secondary V is the final year of secondary school in Quebec, so it is often compared to Grade 11 elsewhere in Canada. That is a common comparison, not an official equivalence; schools and universities place students on content, not on the grade number.

Which Secondary 5 math do I need for CEGEP Science?

The ministry's Science program document (200.B0) lists Secondary V Mathematics in the Technical and Scientific option or the Science option as a special condition for admission, along with Secondary V Chemistry and Physics.

Can I get into a CEGEP computer science program with CST math?

For the technical program Computer Science Technology (420.B0), yes: its document accepts Secondary V CST, or Secondary IV TS or Science. The pre-university Computer Science and Mathematics program (200.C0) asks for Secondary V math in TS or Science.

Is there a ministerial exam in Secondary 5 math?

The 2026 to 2027 schedule on Québec.ca lists ministerial mathematics papers for Secondary IV only, and none for Secondary V. Secondary V math evaluations that year are the school's own. Check the schedule each year, as it can change.

Can I switch from CST to TS or Science for Secondary 5?

The program allows option changes at the end of Secondary IV and lists what to catch up on for most of them. For CST to Science, it leaves the content and arrangements to the school. Expect extra work over the summer.

What is the Secondary 5 math project?

Each option includes an independent assignment in the third year of Cycle Two. The program gives between 10 and 15 class hours to the TS exploration activity and roughly 15 to the Science detailed investigation; in CST it is an optional integrating activity.

Do you grant Quebec credits?

No. We tutor alongside the school course and do not grant Quebec credits. Credits and the Secondary School Diploma come from the school system.

When are Secondary 5 math classes?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. We book every slot in Quebec's own Eastern clock.

Will you do my Secondary 5 homework or project?

No. We explain, check and question, so the work stays yours and you can do it alone in a test. That applies to AI tools too: ChatGPT needs a parent's permission for anyone under 18, and an answer it writes teaches nothing.
