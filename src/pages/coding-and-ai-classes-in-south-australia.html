Coding and AI classes in South Australia

Which coding and AI classes work best for a South Australian learner?

Ones planned around SACE subjects and South Australia's half-hour time zone, that turn local streets into real programming problems. South Australia counted 1,781,516 people at the 2021 Census. Our teachers work with ages 6 to 67 live online, from a first Scratch game to Python, algorithms and AI, privately or in a small group at one level, and the trial lesson is free.

Adelaide's city centre is laid out as a grid of streets, and that grid decides how far you actually walk. Two cafes might be 500 metres apart as the crow flies, but on foot you have to go along one street and then down another. Mathematicians call the walking version taxicab distance. Our project takes every cafe mapped inside the square mile, measures both distances for all 7,800 pairs and finds that the grid adds, on average, 27.33 percent: astonishingly close to a figure that pure geometry predicts, 4 divided by pi. Learners discover why, and why the grid being turned three and a half degrees off north hardly changes the answer.

Signature project

How much farther is it when you have to follow Adelaide's grid?

Every cafe OpenStreetMap shows inside a rectangle around the city square mile, every pair measured two ways. Our calculation, 7 October 2026.

1. Measure the grid's angle

Learners take 331 street segments from King William Street, Grenfell Street, Pirie Street and others, find each one's direction and average them, weighted by length. The grid is turned 3.39 degrees from true east-west.

2. Rotate, then add

Turning every point by that angle lines the coordinates up with the streets. Taxicab distance is then just the east-west gap plus the north-south gap, with no square roots.

3. Compare with geometry

For a direction chosen at random, the average of the walking distance divided by the straight one is 4 divided by pi. Adelaide's cafes give 1.2733 against 1.2732: real data matching a formula to four places.

The rotation that barely mattered

Skip the rotation and treat the grid as if it ran exactly north-south, and the average ratio moves only from 1.2733 to 1.2744. Learners are often surprised: a turn of a few degrees changes individual pairs noticeably but averages out across thousands of them. It is a good lesson in when a correction is worth its complexity, and how to measure that rather than guess.

All figures here are our calculations from OpenStreetMap, which volunteers maintain and which will not show every cafe. Taxicab distance follows the main grid only; real walks use laneways, parks and diagonal crossings, so they can be shorter.

Distance in code

Five habits for any program that measures how far

Practised on a city grid, then used on warehouses, game boards, robots and any problem where you cannot cut corners.

How to pick the right distance
HabitIn practiceWhat it prevents
Ask how things moveGrid streets, open ground or a network?Using a straight line where nobody can travel
Align with the grid firstRotate coordinates to match the streetsErrors that grow with the grid's angle
Know the expected answerCompare a large sample with 4 divided by piBugs that hide inside plausible numbers
Drop tiny pairsExclude points closer than a set gapRatios dominated by mapping noise
Measure whether a fix mattersRun with and without a correctionComplexity that buys nothing

The third habit is the most powerful one we teach. When theory predicts 1.2732 and your program prints 1.2733, you can trust it far more than if you had nothing to compare with. When it prints 1.6, you know at once that something is broken.

Coding and AI

An AI tool will compute a distance for you. Why should a South Australian student know which one?

Because the formula that is easiest to write is often the one that describes a journey nobody can make.

Ask an assistant how far apart two Adelaide addresses are and it will most likely give the straight-line figure, calculated correctly and presented neatly. But nobody walks through buildings, and on a grid the true walking distance is usually a quarter longer. Ask it to write code for delivery times and the same assumption slips into the program without a comment to warn the reader.

A student who has measured the Adelaide grid knows what to ask of any generated answer involving distance: how does the thing actually move, what does theory predict, and does the output match? Those questions carry over to logistics, robotics, games and any model that turns positions into decisions.

That is the case for a South Australian student learning to code in 2026: not to race an AI tool, but to know when its distances describe the real world. The longer argument is in why teenagers should still learn to code in 2026.

FAQ

South Australian coding class questions

The things South Australian families usually want to know first.

What was South Australia's population at the 2021 Census?

The 2021 Census counted 1,781,516 people in South Australia, with a median age of 41 against 38 for the whole country.

What is taxicab distance?

Taxicab distance, also called Manhattan distance, is the distance between two points when you can only travel along a grid: the horizontal gap plus the vertical gap. It is always at least as long as the straight-line distance and at most about 1.41 times it.

What is the Adelaide grid project?

Learners measure every pair of cafes mapped in Adelaide's square mile both as the crow flies and along the street grid. Across 7,800 pairs the grid distance averages 1.2733 times the straight one, almost exactly 4 divided by pi.

What is the SACE?

The SACE is the South Australian Certificate of Education. The SACE Board explains that students achieve it by building 200 credits across Stage 1 and Stage 2 through subjects, VET and other recognised learning.

Do you help with SACE Digital Technologies and maths?

Yes, by teaching technique and understanding; we have separate pages for Digital Technologies, Mathematical Methods and Specialist Mathematics. Assessed work stays the student's own, and we never write it.

What time are lessons for South Australian learners?

Afternoons, evenings or weekends, settled during the trial and always shown in SA time. India is four hours behind South Australia in winter and five hours behind during daylight saving.

Why don't you list SA school term dates?

The Department for Education's term date pages refused our automated check, and we only publish dates we have read at the source. Tell the teacher your school's dates and lessons are planned around them.

Do you have a centre in South Australia?

How big are the groups?

Between five and ten learners, placed by level, pace and goals rather than age or suburb. If no group fits at a workable time, one-to-one lessons are offered instead.
