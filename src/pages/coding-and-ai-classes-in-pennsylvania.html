Pennsylvania, United States

Coding and AI classes in Pennsylvania

Do Pennsylvania high schools teach computer science, and is it required?

Most offer it, but no statewide course is required. Code.org (2025) reports 530 of 684 Pennsylvania high schools offering computer science in 2023 to 2024, while the state’s graduation rules run through Keystone Exams and five Pathways to Graduation. Modern Age Coders teaches Pennsylvania students live online, ages 6 to 18, from Scratch to AP Computer Science A.

Pennsylvania sits in an unusual position. Its high schools offer computer science more widely than the national figure, yet in the latest year Code.org reports, only about one high school student in twenty-eight was enrolled in a CS course. There is no state rule that every teenager must take one, and the new technology and engineering standards that took effect on July 1, 2025 are written for grades 6 to 12 as a whole rather than as a programming course. So whether a Pennsylvania student learns to code properly depends a great deal on the school, the timetable and the student’s own push. This page lays out the published numbers, what the state actually requires, a data project on Pittsburgh’s famous public stairways, and how our live classes fit beside school.

Access climbed every year Code.org measured, from 59% to roughly 78% of high schools, so a Pennsylvania teenager is more likely than not to find a CS course in the catalog. Enrollment did not follow. The 2023 to 2024 participation value, 3.53%, is lower than the 5.9% recorded the year before; the sheet gives no reason for the drop and we do not offer one. Those counts cover every AP subject, not only computer science, so they describe the reach of AP in the state rather than the reach of AP CS. What the state requires

Keystone Exams, five pathways and no compulsory CS course

From the Pennsylvania Department of Education (PDE) page on statewide high school graduation requirements, read on 17 October 2026.

The Keystone Exams

PDE describes the Keystones as end-of-course assessments in Algebra I, Literature, and Biology. Students in Commonwealth public schools take part in them for federal accountability.

Five pathways

Effective with the class of 2023, students can meet statewide requirements through one of five Pathways to Graduation, combining Keystone results, local requirements and evidence from other courses or programs.

Where CS fits

Computer science is not one of the statewide requirements on that page. Districts set their own local course requirements, so what counts toward a diploma beyond the state floor is a question for the school.

What this means for a family

Because the state does not require CS, a student can graduate from many Pennsylvania high schools without writing a line of code. The course may well be on offer; the student has to choose it, or learn outside school. That choice is the gap our classes are built for.

The legal basis PDE links to is Title 22, Chapter 4 of the Pennsylvania Code and the Public School Code sections on Keystone Exams and graduation. We quote the department’s summary and do not interpret the statutes ourselves; for a specific student, the district’s course guide is the authority.

Four core standards

The regulation says four core disciplinary standards describe practices in technology and engineering, with specific benchmarks for grade bands 6 to 8 and 9 to 12.

Computational thinking

PDE’s own definition of STEM education lists computational thinking beside design thinking and problem solving, which is the habit of mind every coding lesson trains.

Not a programming syllabus

None of these sets is a stand-alone computer science course. They make room for coding; they do not guarantee a student will learn a language to any depth.

Signature project

Counting Pittsburgh’s public stairways in Python

Pittsburgh is built on hills, and the city publishes its inventory of public steps through the Western Pennsylvania Regional Data Center (WPRDC) under a Creative Commons Attribution licence. It is an ideal first real dataset.

City of Pittsburgh Steps: our counts from the WPRDC download of 17 October 2026 (1,134 records)
Question a student asksOur answer from the data
How many stairways are listed?1,134 records across 80 neighborhoods
Which neighborhood has the most?South Side Slopes with 109, then Mount Washington with 54 and Perry North with 48
What are most of them made of?Concrete 858, wood 121, stone 85, with mixed and other materials making up the rest
How many have 100 steps or more?113, and 11 of those have 200 or more
Which has the most steps?Ray Ave from Fair Ave to Woodward Ave in Brookline, at 378 steps
How complete is the step count?Filled for 1,022 records, blank for 112, and zero for 16

1. Filter

Students load the CSV, keep only rows where the step count is filled in, and print how many survive. That single filter teaches more about real data than a week of textbook lists.

2. Count by category

A Counter over the material column, then over neighborhood, gives the two tallies above. A bar chart of the top ten neighborhoods makes the hills of the South Side visible at a glance.

3. Check the documentation

The published data dictionary lists 17 fields, but the download carries more columns, including the step count. Students learn to compare a file with its documentation before trusting either.

Our numbers, not the city’s

Every count in the table is our own, from one download on one day. The city updates the data, records can be blank or zero for reasons the file does not give, and a zero may mean unmeasured rather than flat. Saying exactly what a number is, and what it is not, is the point of the exercise.

A middle schooler can finish the filter and the material count in a lesson. A high schooler goes further: binning stairways by step count, plotting them on a map from the latitude and longitude columns, and asking whether neighborhoods with many stairways cluster along the steepest slopes. The licence asks only that the city be credited, which is a good first lesson in data ethics too.

The Keystone STEM Competition, as PDE describes it

A free, one-day statewide challenge for grades 5 to 12, with teams of up to five students, grade bands of 5 to 6, 7 to 8 and 9 to 12, regional events across Pennsylvania’s 29 Intermediate Units, and no preparation required. We are not connected with the competition.

Coding and AI

Pennsylvania’s own advice on AI points back to coding

PDE publishes guidance on AI in K-12 education. Two of its lines explain why students still need to understand the code underneath.

Among the department’s cautions: “Do not use AI as a final source of facts.” and “Do not use AI as the only grader. Teachers must review and make final decisions.” Both assume a person who can check what a machine produced. In the stairway project, a careless summary would say all 1,134 stairways have a known step count. That is wrong: 112 records are blank, and nobody notices unless they look.

A student who wrote the filter knows why that answer fails. That is the skill we teach alongside every AI tool: read the output, test it against the data, and decide whether to trust it. Students under 18 use only tools whose terms allow their age.

AI makes code cheap to produce and judgment more valuable, and judgment comes from having built things yourself. The longer argument is in why coding still matters when AI writes code.

FAQ

Pennsylvania coding and AI questions

Short answers sourced to PDE, Code.org and the College Board; anything that is our opinion says so.

Is computer science required to graduate in Pennsylvania?

Not as a statewide course. PDE says students meet statewide requirements through the Keystone Exams or one of five Pathways to Graduation, effective with the class of 2023. Districts may add local requirements.

How many Pennsylvania high schools offer computer science?

Code.org (2025) reports 530 of 684 Pennsylvania high schools offering foundational computer science in 2023 to 2024, about 77.5%.

How many Pennsylvania students take computer science?

For 2023 to 2024, Code.org reports 19,506 of 551,996 high school students, or 3.53%, enrolled in a computer science course.

Which subjects are the Keystone Exams?

PDE lists Algebra I, Literature, and Biology as the three end-of-course Keystone Exams.

What changed in the Pennsylvania standards in 2025?

From July 1, 2025, 22 Pa. Code 4.12 introduced integrated science, environment, ecology, technology and engineering standards for grades K to 5, and separate technology and engineering standards for grades 6 to 12.

Who can enter the Keystone STEM Competition?

PDE describes it as a free, one-day challenge for grades 5 to 12, with teams of up to five students and regional events across the 29 Intermediate Units. We are not connected with it.

Will your classes count toward a Pennsylvania diploma?

No. Credit comes from the student's school. Our classes add depth, projects and AP preparation, and graded school work stays the student's own.

When are classes held for Pennsylvania students?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Times are confirmed in Eastern Time.

Do you have a Pennsylvania location?

No. There is no Modern Age Coders center in Pennsylvania. Every class is live and online, taught from India.

Where can I see the fees?

The fees section above lists the free first lesson, the monthly batch and the monthly one-to-one option. Billing begins only after the free lesson.
