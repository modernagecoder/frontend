COMC guide: the Canadian Open Mathematics Challenge, 2026

The Canadian Open Mathematics Challenge is the Canadian Mathematical Society's national open contest: one paper of twelve questions, two and a half hours, no calculators, written in the fall by students of any grade. It is also the first gate on the road the CMS describes from the COMC to the Canadian Mathematical Olympiad and, for a very few, to Math Team Canada. In 2026 the CMS changed how the COMC is run: two new divisions, a fixed writing window for the one that leads to the olympiad, questions delivered separately from the answer booklets, and no more Repêchage. This guide is built from the CMS's own 2026 COMC pages. It sets out the dates, every change, the Competitive window converted for each province, how the 80 points are marked, six practice problems of ours and what to do now that 2026 registration has closed.

The essentials

The COMC (Canadian Open Mathematics Challenge) is the Canadian Mathematical Society's open national math contest: 12 questions in 150 minutes, scored out of 80, on paper and without calculators, written on Thursday, October 29, 2026 in Canada. Top Competitive Division writers can be invited to the Canadian Mathematical Olympiad. If the school is not taking part

Registration is normally done by teachers through the CMS portal. For students whose school is not participating, the CMS runs a limited number of places at Open Writing Centres at partner universities, mostly in person.

The CMS also suggests contacting other schools in the area to ask whether they will accept a student from elsewhere for the day.

Why a tutoring company cannot proctor

The CMS requires a Qualified Proctor for official results: someone with a current provincial or government teaching certificate who is not related to any of the students.

Its eligibility page says tutoring and enrichment centres may be run as unofficial unless they arrange such a proctor. We are a teaching company, so our students write at their schools or at an Open Writing Centre, never with us.

The CMS published a Changes page addressed to teachers, parents and students. These are the changes it lists, in its own terms, with what each means for a student in Canada.

The CMS gives its reasons on a separate Repêchage page: from 2027 it will send more olympiad invitations straight from COMC results instead, and this lets it move the olympiads earlier, away from March spring breaks. In plain terms, the COMC paper in October now carries the whole weight that used to be shared with a take-home second round.

One inconsistency worth knowing about: the CMS student FAQ, read on the same day, still refers to the old "Canadian" and "International" divisions. The Changes page and the Prizes and Awards page use the new Competitive and Global names, and we follow those.

What this means on the day

In the east the paper is an afternoon sitting that may run past the end of the school day; in British Columbia it can begin mid-morning. Either way, the start time is the school's decision, and families should ask what it will be.

A 150-minute paper started at the last permitted moment in Ontario ends at 6:00 p.m. Students should eat beforehand and plan the trip home.

Writers outside the window

A student who starts outside the window is placed in the Global Division, which has its own awards but, the CMS says, no prize money and no CMO invitation.

The CMS adds that an overseas student who qualifies may write in the Thursday window by registering as an independent student directly with the CMS.

The 80 points

Three sections, three ways of earning points

The CMS FAQ sets out the structure in a few lines. The COMC Problem of the Week page describes the same three parts in words. Together they say exactly how a student should write each section.

COMC structure and marking, from the CMS FAQ and Problem of the Week page (read 22 October 2026)
SectionQuestions and pointsHow it is markedThe CMS description
Introductory (Part A)"Four introductory questions, worth 4 points each"Full marks for a correct answer, with or without workSolutions "do not require work be shown and may be possible to do in your head"
Intermediate (Part B)"Four intermediate questions, worth 6 points each"As Part A; partial marks possible for correct work when the answer is wrongProblems that "draw on more knowledge" and "need a pencil and paper"
Advanced (Part C)"Four advanced questions, worth 10 points each""Advanced questions require fully developed solutions"Problems that "require that work be shown and involve arguments to support the answer"

One line in the FAQ deserves to be read twice: "You are not obliged to show your work, but if you fail to do so and make a small error in your answer, you will not be awarded any points." Our arithmetic is that Part A and Part B together are worth 16 + 24 = 40 points, half the paper, and every one of those points is exposed to that rule. With no calculator allowed, small arithmetic slips are common. Working is not decoration on the COMC; it is insurance, and it costs a few seconds per question.

Six practice problems

COMC-style problems of ours, from Part A to Part C

These six are our practice problems, not CMS problems. We wrote them for our students; none is taken from a past COMC. Two are at introductory level, two intermediate and two advanced, and every answer was checked in Python. Do them without a calculator.

Problem 1. Our practice problem, not a CMS problem. Introductory level. What is the value of 1 - 2 + 3 - 4 + 5 - 6 + ... + 99 - 100?

Solution. Group the terms in pairs: (1 - 2) + (3 - 4) + ... + (99 - 100). There are 50 pairs, each equal to -1, so the sum is -50.

Problem 2. Our practice problem, not a CMS problem. Introductory level. What is the coefficient of x3 when (2x + 1)5 is expanded?

Solution. By the binomial theorem the x3 term is C(5, 3)(2x)3(1)2 = 10 x 8x3 = 80x3. The coefficient is 80. Forgetting to cube the 2 gives 10, the most common wrong answer.

Problem 3. Our practice problem, not a CMS problem. Intermediate level. How many positive integers less than 100 have exactly three positive divisors?

Solution. If n = p1a p2b..., the number of divisors is (a + 1)(b + 1)... For this to equal 3, which is prime, n must be a single prime squared, n = p2. The primes with p2 less than 100 are 2, 3, 5 and 7, giving 4, 9, 25 and 49. The answer is 4.

Problem 4. Our practice problem, not a CMS problem. Intermediate level. In triangle ABC, AB = 7, AC = 8 and angle BAC = 60 degrees. Determine the exact length of BC.

Solution. By the cosine law, BC2 = 72 + 82 - 2(7)(8)cos 60 = 49 + 64 - 56 = 57, so BC = √57. This is the question from the two answer sheets above: the line 2(7)(8)(1/2) = 56 is the one to check.

Problem 5. Our practice problem, not a CMS problem. Advanced level, full solution. Determine all pairs of positive integers (m, n) with m2 - n2 = 60.

Problem 6. Our practice problem, not a CMS problem. Advanced level, full solution. Three different numbers are chosen at random from 1, 2, 3, ..., 10. Determine the probability that their sum is divisible by 3.

Timing the set

Problems 1 and 2: three minutes each. Problems 3 and 4: six minutes each. Problems 5 and 6: fifteen minutes each, written in full. That is roughly the pace a full COMC paper asks for.

Mark problems 3 and 4 as if no working had been shown, then again with your working. The difference is the insurance.

What the six share

Each one becomes short once the right structure is chosen: pairs, a binomial term, a divisor formula, the cosine law, a factorization, remainders.

Choosing the structure is the skill the COMC tests. Our classes spend more time on that choice than on any formula.

150 minutes

Spending two and a half hours on twelve questions

There is no official advice on pacing. This budget is ours, refined by timing our own students on past COMC papers from the CMS archive.

The Part C budget is deliberately large. Part C is worth 40 of the 80 points, and a fully developed solution takes time to write well. A student who reaches Part C with ninety minutes left can afford to think before writing, which is how the strongest Part C answers are produced.

For parents

Many strong students begin at stage 1 simply because school lets them use a calculator. The move to stage 2 takes a few weeks of daily hand arithmetic, and it pays off on every contest.

Stages 3 and 4 are about proof. They take months, and they are what the CMO, if it ever comes, will ask for.

For students

Show a line of working on every Part A and Part B answer. It costs seconds and can save points.

Read all four Part C questions before writing any of them.

COMC: answers to common questions

What is the COMC?

The Canadian Open Mathematics Challenge is the Canadian Mathematical Society national open math contest. It has 12 questions in 150 minutes, scored out of 80, written on paper without calculators, and it is the main route to an invitation to the Canadian Mathematical Olympiad.

When is the COMC in 2026?

The CMS lists Thursday, October 29, 2026 in Canada and the Americas and Friday, October 30, 2026 elsewhere. The last day to register was Thursday, October 22, 2026.

What changed in the COMC in 2026?

The divisions are now called Competitive and Global. Only Competitive Division writers are eligible for prize money and possible CMO invitations, and they must start between 1:00 p.m. and 3:30 p.m. EDT. Questions now come separately from the response booklets, and the Repechage has been discontinued.

Who can write the COMC?

The CMS says it is open to students in all locations and at all grade levels, and recommends it for Grades 8 and up. Official participants must be under 19 as of June 30 and attending school full-time.

Are calculators allowed on the COMC?

No. The CMS says no mobile phones, calculators or other electronic devices are permitted. Compasses and protractors are allowed, so all arithmetic is done by hand.

How is the COMC marked?

Four introductory questions are worth 4 points, four intermediate questions 6 points and four advanced questions 10 points. The first two sections give full marks for a correct answer, but a small error with no work shown earns nothing. Advanced questions need fully developed solutions.

Can my child register without the school?

Only if the school is not participating. The CMS then offers a limited number of places at Open Writing Centres at partner universities, mostly in person, and suggests asking other local schools too. For 2026, registration has closed.

Does the COMC lead to the Canadian Math Olympiad?

Yes, for top Competitive Division writers. The CMS COMC page says about 100 to 120 top-ranking COMC students are invited to the CMO or the Junior CMO, and its Prizes page gives about 100 for the CMO. Invitations are decided by the CMS only.

Can you guarantee a CMO invitation?

No. We teach the mathematics and the written solutions, and we make no promise of a score, an award or an invitation.

When are your COMC classes?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. We plan in Eastern time and confirm each class in your own time zone.
