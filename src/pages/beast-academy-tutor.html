Beast Academy tutor: live help for Levels 1 to 5

Families choose Beast Academy because it asks more of a child than a typical elementary book: comic-style Guides, long Practice pages and starred problems that can take a whole afternoon. That is the point of it, and also why some children stop enjoying it. A good tutor for Beast Academy does not make it easier. They keep a child inside the hard problem long enough to crack it, with the right hint at the right moment. This page sets out how the program is organised, from its own site, and how live help fits beside it.

What it is

What is Beast Academy, and who is it for?

Beast Academy is the elementary math program of Art of Problem Solving. Its FAQ calls it a full math program for ages 6 to 13 that covers grade-level standards in more depth than most elementary curricula.

Guide and Practice

The books page explains the pairing. Guide books teach in an illustrated, comic-style format. Practice books "contain exercises, problems, and puzzles that reinforce the lessons taught in the Guides", with step-by-step solutions, and "Many solutions feature more than one approach."

Books and BA Online "cover the same concepts in the same order of units and chapters", but the Practice books contain completely different problems from the online ones, so a child can use both without repeating work.

Not only for advanced children

The FAQ says the program was created with accelerated learners in mind but is flexible, and that children with a wide range of abilities have used it.

It also says honestly that some children use it independently while others "might need greater support as they encounter new topics and work through challenging problems." That second group is who this page is for.

Levels and units

Each of the five levels is split into four units, A to D. Once a child finishes unit D of a level, the books page says they are ready for unit A of the next.

Starting a level behind

The FAQ recommends that students start "about a level ‘behind’ their current grade level", because the problems are demanding. Its example: a child who is 8 or 9 should likely start with Level 2.

Beast Academy also offers a placement test, which we suggest before any tutoring starts, so the teacher and the book agree on where the child is.

Level 1 is different

Level 1 does not have separate Guide and Practice books; all of its material is combined into a single book per unit. Levels 2 to 5 each have four Guide books and four Practice books.

The FAQ adds that Levels 4 and 5 "also cover many middle-grades standards", which is why some older children use them in grades 6 and 7.

Pacing

How fast should a child move through Beast Academy?

Slower than parents expect. The FAQ suggests 12 to 15 days per chapter, so that one level fills a 180-day school year, and it says children are not meant to do every problem.

Beast Academy’s own pacing advice (beastacademy.com/faq, checked 31 October 2026) and what it means in practice (our reading)
What the FAQ saysWhat it means at home
"Aim to spend 12-15 days teaching a chapter"Two to three school weeks per chapter is normal, not slow

Assign Practice "by time and not by pages"Set 30 or 40 minutes, not "do pages 12 to 18"
"some pages in the Practice books may take more than an hour"A long page is not a sign of failure

In our experience the families who struggle most with Beast Academy are not the ones whose children find it hard. They are the ones trying to finish a set number of pages a day. Switching to a timed session, with the hardest starred problems saved for a class with the teacher, usually changes the mood within a couple of weeks.

When stuck

What should a child do when stuck on a Beast Academy problem?

Beast Academy publishes its own advice, and it is good. A tutor adds a second layer: a hint ladder that gives the least help that still moves the child forward.

Beast Academy’s advice

The FAQ says children should know that hard problems take time, and that taking a long time "does not mean that they’re bad at math." It suggests re-reading the lesson in the Guide and the examples at the top of the Practice pages, trying something new, and using "the guiding questions at the back of the Practice book" for starred problems.

And: "it’s always okay to take a break and come back later with fresh eyes."

What a tutor adds

A book’s hint is the same for every child. A teacher watching the child work can see whether they misread the question, chose a poor first step or simply ran out of patience, and hint at exactly that.

The aim is the same as the book’s: the child solves it. The tutor just makes the gap between stuck and solved smaller.

  1. Rung 1, restate. "Tell me what the problem is asking, in your own words." Many stalls end here.
  2. Rung 2, smaller case. "Try it with smaller numbers first." A pattern in small cases often reveals the method.
  3. Rung 3, a picture. "Can you draw it?" Dots, a number line or a grid.
  4. Rung 4, a first step. The teacher suggests one move, not the whole route.
  5. Rung 5, a parked problem. If it is still stuck, it goes in a notebook and comes back next class. Nobody gives the answer.

This ladder is ours; it is how our teachers work with any challenging curriculum. It sits comfortably beside Beast Academy’s own suggestions.

Factor rectangles (a Level 4 topic)

How many different rectangles with whole-number sides have an area of 36 square units? Our worked answer: list factor pairs in order, 1 x 36, 2 x 18, 3 x 12, 4 x 9, 6 x 6, and stop when the pairs start repeating. Five rectangles, if a 4 by 9 and a 9 by 4 count as the same shape.

The thinking move is the stopping rule: once the first factor passes the square root, every pair has already appeared.

A growing sequence (a Level 5 topic)

What is the 50th term of 4, 7, 10, 13, and so on? Our worked answer: each step adds 3, and the 50th term is 49 steps after the first, so 4 + 49 x 3 = 151.

The common slip is 50 x 3 + 4 = 154: counting terms instead of steps. Asking "how many jumps from the 1st to the 3rd term?" fixes it.

For parents

Stage 2 is where most families ask for a tutor. The goal is time on the problem, not speed.

Praise the attempt and the drawing, not only the answer.

Try the problem with smaller numbers. It works surprisingly often.

Beast Academy tutoring: common questions

Do you offer a Beast Academy tutor online?

Yes. Our teachers work live by video with your child's Beast Academy Guide and Practice books or BA Online, using hints rather than answers. Classes run as a batch of 15 to 20 or one to one.

Who makes Beast Academy?

Beast Academy is the elementary math program created by Art of Problem Solving (AoPS). Its FAQ describes it as a full math program for ages 6 to 13. We are independent and not affiliated with AoPS.

What level of Beast Academy should my child start?

The Beast Academy FAQ suggests starting about a level behind the child's current grade, so a child of 8 or 9 would usually begin at Level 2. It also offers a placement test, which we recommend.

How long should a Beast Academy chapter take?

The FAQ suggests 12 to 15 days per chapter, so that one level fills a 180-day school year. It recommends assigning Practice by time rather than by pages.

Does my child need to do every Beast Academy problem?

No. The FAQ says the curriculum has a lot of material and children are not expected to complete every problem. Some Practice pages can take more than an hour.

My child gets upset on hard problems. What helps?

Beast Academy advises re-reading the Guide, using the guiding questions at the back of the Practice book and taking breaks. A tutor adds hints tailored to the exact point where your child is stuck.

Is Beast Academy aligned to Common Core?

Its FAQ says Beast Academy is loosely based on the Common Core standards and covers the key topics for grades 1 to 5 in greater depth, with Levels 4 and 5 also covering many middle-grade standards.

What comes after Beast Academy Level 5?

Beast Academy says a child who completes Level 5 of BA Online has the background for AoPS prealgebra. Our middle school and competitions courses are also a natural next step.

When are classes held?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays.

Will the tutor give my child the answers?

No. Our teachers use a hint ladder that gives the least help needed. A problem that is still unsolved is parked for next time rather than answered.
