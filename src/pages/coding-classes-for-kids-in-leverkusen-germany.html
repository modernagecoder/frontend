Readiness

Is my child ready to start coding? Five signs to look for

Age matters less than you might think. These are the signs our mentors look for in the free first class, and you can look for them at home.

  1. They follow a short sequence. A child who can follow three steps of a recipe or a Lego plan in order can follow the steps of a Scratch program.
  2. They ask "what if". What if the cat jumps twice? What if the game is faster? That question is the start of every program.
  3. They can sit with a puzzle for ten minutes. Not an hour; ten minutes of trying before asking for help is plenty at six or seven.
  4. They use a mouse or trackpad. Scratch is dragging and dropping. Typing comes later, usually from about nine.
  5. They enjoy making, not only playing. A child who builds worlds, draws comics or invents rules for games usually loves coding.

NRW schools

What will my child meet in Informatik at a Leverkusen school?

Nothing compulsory in Grundschule, then two compulsory years. The facts are from the Gesellschaft für Informatik and the KMK, read on 2 November 2026.

Grades 1 to 4

A Leverkusen child spends the first four school years in Grundschule, as children do in almost every Land, and none of those years has Informatik as a required subject.

Children of six to nine therefore depend on home, clubs or classes like ours if they want to make things with code.

Grades 5 and 6

According to the Informatik-Monitor 2026/27, Informatik is compulsory in grades 5 and 6 at all general school types in NRW. Every Leverkusen pupil, whatever school they move to, has it.

A child who has spent a year or two on Scratch and simple Python arrives in grade 5 knowing loops and conditions, and can spend those lessons going deeper instead of catching up.

In English

How can a young child learn coding in English?

Lessons are given live, in English and nothing else. For a young child that matters less than parents fear, because so much is shown rather than said.

No German, said early

Mentors teach in English and there is no German-language option. We would rather you know that now. The mentors are based in India and teach by live video.

Short, clear sentences

Mentors use few words, repeat them, point at the screen and wait. They get used to your child's way of speaking, and a German word in the middle of an answer is understood and gently swapped for the English one.

Words a child picks up fast

Move, turn, repeat, if, score, start. Most of a young child's coding vocabulary is a few dozen words, and children use them comfortably within a few weeks.

Because the mentor stays the same, your child is not meeting a stranger every week. Quiet children often speak more by the third or fourth class, once they know who is on the other side of the screen.

A real Leverkusen project

How often does it rain in Leverkusen? A lesson in fair samples

The Deutscher Wetterdienst has measured rain at Leverkusen-Pattscheid for decades. Our older children use the 365 days of 2025 to discover that looking at a few days can give almost any answer you like.

The data

Station 2970, Leverkusen-Pattscheid, sits 140 metres above sea level inside the city boundary. Its daily rain totals are free to reuse with credit (CC BY 4.0) from the DWD Climate Data Center. Our copy was fetched on 3 November 2026, and the 2025 part became pattscheid_2025.csv.

We call a day rainy when at least 1 millimetre fell. That is our choice for the project, written down before counting so it cannot be changed to suit the answer.

The question

Out of every hundred days in 2025, how many were rainy? A child guesses first. Then the program looks at only two weeks, then at a different two weeks, then at the whole year.

The total for the year was 830.8 millimetres, and the wettest single day was 8 September 2025 with 35.6 millimetres. Both are our sums from the file.

Two weeks in January say nearly two days in three are rainy. Two weeks in March say it never rains at all. The whole year says about one day in three. A sample of days all bunched together only tells you about those days.

Spreading the sample across the year helps: one day a week gives 40 percent, much closer than either fortnight. Random days help too, but a small random sample still jumps about. With 100 days most tries land within a few points of the true 34. The lesson children remember: look at enough days, from all over the year.

Rain figures: DWD station 2970 Leverkusen-Pattscheid, daily totals from the Climate Data Center, reused under CC BY 4.0 (file fetched 3 November 2026). The 1 millimetre threshold, the samples and all shares are ours. The random tries use a fixed seed, so anyone who runs the code gets the same numbers.

  1. Ages 6 to 8, no screen. Put 10 red and 20 blue counters in a bag. Pull out three. Does your handful say "one in three are red"? Try again.
  2. Scratch, 8 to 10. Build a list of 30 days, ten of them rainy. Pick five at random and show the share. Press the green flag ten times and watch it change.
  3. Python, 10 to 12. Change Version 2 to take 200 random days. Do the five tries get closer to 34?
  4. Going further. Change the rule to "at least 0.1 millimetre". How many rainy days now, and why is the choice of rule part of the answer?

Free contests

Which free contests can a Leverkusen primary child enter this year?

Two national ones that start in grade 3, both entered through the school. We have no connection with either organiser.

Informatik-Biber

A free online puzzle quiz for grades 3 to 13 that needs no prior knowledge. More than 580,000 pupils took part in 2025. The Biberwochen 2026 run from 9 to 20 November 2026, inside a school lesson.

Schools register their pupils, so the class teacher is the person to ask.

Mathematik-Olympiade

About 200,000 pupils take part every year, and it has been open to primary schools since 2005. In the 66th Olympiade the Regionalrunde is on 11 November 2026 and the Landesrunde from 26 to 28 February 2027.

The first round is set at school. For a child who enjoys puzzles, it is a good reason to practise careful written reasoning.

We never copy either contest's tasks. In class, mentors invent their own puzzles in the same spirit, marked as ours, and send families to the organisers' own pages for real past rounds.

Leverkusen parents ask

Coding classes for kids in Leverkusen: answers for parents

Are the classes in German?

No. Classes run live, and the teaching language is English alone. The mentors, based in India, speak simply, show each step on the screen and adjust to your child's pace and accent, so a young child with little English can take part.

How do I know if my child is ready to code?

Look for five signs: following a short sequence, asking what if, staying with a puzzle for ten minutes, using a mouse, and enjoying making things. The free first class confirms it either way.

Do Leverkusen schools teach coding?

Not in Grundschule. From grade 5, Informatik is compulsory in grades 5 and 6 at every general school type in North Rhine-Westphalia, according to the Gesellschaft für Informatik.

Is six too young?

Usually not, as long as the start is Scratch, where blocks snap together and almost nothing is typed. Six year olds have short lessons with small wins and are grouped only with children their age.

When are classes in Leverkusen time?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Leverkusen children mostly pick a weekday afternoon or Saturday and Sunday mornings.

Can my child take part in the Biber or the Mathematik-Olympiade?

Yes, both start from grade 3 and run through schools. This school year the Biber falls between 9 and 20 November 2026, and the Regionalrunde of the 66th Mathematik-Olympiade is on 11 November 2026. Ask the class teacher.

Is the rain project real Leverkusen data?

Yes. It uses the 2025 daily rain at Deutscher Wetterdienst station 2970, Leverkusen-Pattscheid, free to reuse under CC BY 4.0; our copy dates from 3 November 2026. Samples and percentages are our work.

What will my child build in the first months?

Scratch stories and games for the youngest, a scoring quiz around eight, and from about nine or ten, short Python programs such as the rainy-day counter on this page.

How large is a class?

Between fifteen and twenty children close in age. If you prefer, the one-to-one plan books the mentor for your child alone, two lessons each week.

Which currency is the fee in?

The page shows the monthly fee in euro at the European Central Bank reference rate. The payment itself is taken in US dollars and converted by your bank, so the euro amount on your statement may differ a little. The first class is free.
