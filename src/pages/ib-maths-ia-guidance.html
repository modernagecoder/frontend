What is the IB Maths IA?

In IB Diploma mathematics, the internal assessment is a piece of the student's own mathematical writing that the IB's Mathematics: analysis and approaches guide says "is called the exploration". The same guide says the course expects 30 hours for developing inquiry, modelling and investigation skills, including "up to 15 hours for work on the internal assessment", at both SL and HL. Coursework must be "authentic, based on the student's individual and original ideas", with every source acknowledged. The IB's academic integrity policy asks students to refuse non-permitted help "in the completion or editing of work", naming "private tutors" among others. The IB's own site was closed to us, so for the current criteria and marks, use the subject guide your teacher holds. Modern Age Coders teaches the mathematics explorations tend to draw on, live online, and never works on the exploration itself.

The exploration

What is the IB Maths exploration?

It is the internal assessment in each IB Diploma mathematics course: a piece of mathematics the student chooses, investigates and writes up. Here is what we could read in the IB's own documents, and what we could not.

The exploration, from the IB's Mathematics: analysis and approaches guide (published February 2019)
PointWhat the guide says
Its nameThe internal assessment "is called the exploration"
Class time30 hours for inquiry, modelling and investigation skills, including up to 15 hours for the internal assessment
Courses and levelsTwo subjects, analysis and approaches and applications and interpretation, each offered at SL and HL
Teaching hours240 hours at HL and 150 at SL, of which 210 and 120 are for the syllabus
AuthenticityCoursework must be "authentic, based on the student's individual and original ideas with the ideas and work of others fully acknowledged"
Sources"candidates must acknowledge all sources used in work submitted for assessment"

What we could not read

The IB's website returns an error (HTTP 403) to our automated reader on every IB address we tried, and the public copy of the guide we found stops before the assessment chapters. So this page does not state the exploration's criteria, their marks, its share of the grade or any length guidance.

Your teacher has the current subject guide and the criteria for your session. Use those words, not a tutor's summary, including ours.

Why that matters less than it seems

The single question families ask us most about the IA is not "what are the criteria?" but "what help is allowed?". That answer is in a document we could read in full: the IB's own academic integrity policy.

The IB's rules

Can a tutor help with the IB Maths IA?

Not with the work itself. The IB's academic integrity policy names private tutors among those whose help in completing or editing assessed work a student should refuse.

The IB on help with assessed coursework, from Academic integrity (October 2019)
WhoWhat the policy says
StudentsShould "abstain from receiving non-permitted assistance in the completion or editing of work, such as from friends, relatives, other students, private tutors, essay writing or copy-editing services, pre-written essay banks or file sharing websites"
Teachers"students are expected to produce work autonomously and should not receive additional help, such as multiple edits of a piece of work"
TeachersMust ensure "students are not provided with multiple rounds of editing, which would be contrary to instructions described in the relevant subject guides"
ParentsWho support the school "will be less inclined to give unfair assistance to their children, for example, by writing or over-editing their work"
SchoolsInfringements include "over-editing or templating or manipulating finished pieces of work for improvement" and allowing work "produced by third parties such as teachers, tutors, parents, and peers"

The reasoning is set out plainly. When a student receives help "beyond what is recommended in the subject guidelines", the policy says, "the work is not entirely produced by the student in question", and the assessment "can only be fair if it truly and effectively reflects the genuine and authentic effort of the student". It even lists "the easy access to support services, such as writing or tutoring services" among the reasons students fall into misconduct. A tutoring business should read that sentence and draw a line, which is what the next section does.

Plagiarism is the common case

The policy says "Plagiarism is the most common form of student academic misconduct." In maths that includes formulas, derivations, graphs and code taken from elsewhere without acknowledgement, not only sentences.

The guide's rule is simple: acknowledge every source, in a consistent referencing style.

A newer version exists

The copy we read is the October 2019 edition. The IB updated the policy in March 2023, and that edition sits on ibo.org, which was closed to us. Schools use the current version, so ask your coordinator for it, particularly on the use of AI tools.

Nothing in a newer edition is likely to make tutor editing more acceptable, but read it rather than trust us.

Source: International Baccalaureate Organization, Academic integrity, published October 2019, an unaltered copy of the IB's PDF hosted by an IB school, read 31 October 2026. The 2023 update on ibo.org returned HTTP 403 and has not been read.

Our line

What do we do, and what do we never do?

We teach mathematics and the general craft of investigating. We keep away from the exploration itself, entirely.

If a student asks during a lesson for help with their exploration, the teacher will say no and explain why, and will suggest they take the question to their IB teacher. That is not unhelpfulness; it protects the student, whose diploma depends on the work being theirs. It also protects their school, because the policy treats allowing third-party work as a school infringement.

Why teaching the maths still helps

A student who genuinely understands, say, least-squares regression or integration by parts can choose a richer question and pursue it on their own. Understanding is theirs to use; a tutor's paragraph is not.

The guide's time budget, up to 15 class hours for the exploration, is small. Mathematics learned well beforehand is time not spent learning it during those hours.

Where other help belongs

Guidance on the exploration itself comes from the IB teacher, within the limits the subject guide sets. That is the one adult whose help with the work the IB system expects.

Finding a topic yourself

How do I find my own IB maths IA topic?

Not from a list of "IA ideas". The guide asks for the student's "individual and original ideas", so the method below helps you find one; it gives you none. The method is ours.

  1. Write down five things you already spend time on: a sport, a game, music, a subject, a job, a place. Interest sustains work.
  2. For each, write one question that has a number in its answer: how fast, how many, how likely, how much, when.
  3. Cross out any question you could answer with one search or one calculation. You want a question that needs mathematics to unfold.
  4. For the questions left, list the mathematics from your course that might touch them. If you cannot list any, drop the question.
  5. Pick one, and take it to your IB teacher. They know the criteria and the scope, and they are the right person to say whether it will work.

A classroom example (ours)

Not an exploration, just practice in the habit. Question: if every person in a room shakes hands once with everyone else, how many handshakes are there? Count small cases: 2 people give 1, 3 give 3, 4 give 6, 5 give 10.

Make a conjecture

The differences grow by one each time, which suggests n(n minus 1) over 2. For 5 people that is 5 times 4 over 2, which is 10, matching the count. A pattern in four cases is a guess, not a proof.

Then prove it

Each of n people shakes n minus 1 hands, but that counts every handshake twice, so divide by 2. An HL student could also prove it by induction: adding one person adds n new handshakes. Explaining why is the point.

That loop, small cases, a conjecture, a check, a reason, is the general shape of mathematical investigation, and practising it on problems that are nobody's coursework is where we spend lesson time. The guide describes analysis and approaches as being for students who want to "construct, communicate and justify correct mathematical arguments"; the habit above is that, in miniature.

The two courses

Does the IA differ between AA and AI?

Both courses have an exploration. The guide describes the two subjects differently, and that difference often shapes the kind of mathematics a student reaches for.

Analysis and approaches

For students who "enjoy developing their mathematics to become fluent in the construction of mathematical arguments", and who "enjoy the thrill of mathematical problem solving and generalization."

At HL the guide expects "strong algebraic skills and the ability to understand simple proof", and includes proof by induction.

Applications and interpretation

For students "interested in developing their mathematics for describing our world and solving practical problems", harnessing technology alongside mathematical models.

The guide says AI students "enjoy mathematics best when seen in a practical context."

These descriptions are the IB's, from the analysis and approaches guide, which describes both subjects in its introduction. They say nothing about which subject produces better explorations, and neither do we. What they suggest is that a student should pick a question whose mathematics fits the course they actually take, which their teacher will confirm.

Source: IB, Mathematics: analysis and approaches guide, Nature of mathematics section, published February 2019.

IB Maths IA questions

What IB students and parents ask about the IA

What is the IB Maths IA?

It is the internal assessment in IB Diploma mathematics, which the IB's analysis and approaches guide says is called the exploration. It is the student's own piece of mathematical work, with all sources acknowledged.

Can a tutor help with my IB Maths IA?

Not with the work. The IB's academic integrity policy asks students to refuse non-permitted help in completing or editing assessed work, and names private tutors in that list. A tutor can teach the mathematics; guidance on the exploration comes from your IB teacher.

Will you check my IA draft?

No. We do not read, comment on, mark, edit or proofread any part of an exploration, at any stage. The policy says students should not receive multiple edits of a piece of work, and we will not be a source of them.

Can you give me IB Maths IA ideas?

No. The guide asks for the student's individual and original ideas. We share a general method for finding your own question and practise investigation on problems that are no one's coursework.

How much class time is there for the IA?

The analysis and approaches guide expects 30 hours for inquiry, modelling and investigation skills, including up to 15 hours for work on the internal assessment, at both SL and HL.

What are the IB Maths IA criteria?

Your teacher has them in the current subject guide. The IB website was closed to our reader, so we do not print criteria or marks we could not check. Use the IB's own wording.

Is the IA different for AA and AI?

Both courses have an exploration. The guide describes analysis and approaches as built around constructing mathematical arguments and applications and interpretation around describing the world and practical problems, which often shapes the mathematics students use.

What happens if work is not the student's own?

The IB treats it as academic misconduct, and its policy says plagiarism is the most common form. It also lists allowing work produced by tutors or parents as a school infringement. Ask your IB coordinator about the current policy.

What do your IB maths lessons cover, then?

The syllabus mathematics at SL or HL, and practice in investigating on our own problems. If a question about the exploration comes up, we send it to the student's IB teacher.

When are lessons?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. All times are in UK time.
