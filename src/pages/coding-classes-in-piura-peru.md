# Coding classes in Piura, Peru

The question Piura parents ask most is not about Python or Scratch. It is whether a child whose English is still basic can follow a coding class taught in English. This page answers that honestly first, then shows which course suits each age, what school leaves out, and a project built on the river readings taken every morning at the Sánchez Cerro bridge.

At a glance: Teaching language: Live classes in English only, at the learner&rsquo;s own pace; mentors speak clearly and match their speed and accent to the learner; Ages: From 6; adults welcome; English level: Basic is enough to begin; First tools: Scratch, then Python; Class size: Fifteen to twenty, or private; Piura time: Any hour you pick; Trial: Free, no card. ## Can my child learn coding in English if their English is still basic?

Yes, in most cases, and the first class will show you. Coding is one of the easier subjects to learn in a second language, because the screen shows what every word means.

### What the figures say about Piura

EF's English Proficiency Index 2025 scores Piura city 484 and the Piura region 482, both in its "low" band. Peru as a whole scores 519 and Lima city 555. EF's scores come from adults who took its test, so they say nothing exact about any one child, but they match what Piura parents tell us: English is a skill still being built.

We would rather say this plainly than promise that every child will understand everything from the first day. Most do not, and they do not need to.

### Why coding is a good place to practise

In a coding class, the mentor says "move ten steps" and the character on the screen moves ten steps. The meaning is visible. After a few weeks, words like repeat, if, variable and list are simply part of the child's vocabulary.

Python itself uses a few dozen short English words. A learner who knows print, input, if, else, for and while can already write real programs.

**How our mentors adapt a class for a learner with basic English (our practice)**

| Moment in class | What the mentor does |
|---|---|
| Giving an instruction | Says it in a short sentence, then shows it on the shared screen |

The free first class doubles as a check. If a child is lost, the mentor will say so and suggest a slower start, such as a private class for the first month. ## How much English and computing does school give a Piura child?

A few hours of English a week and no computing area. That is the national plan of study, which every school in Piura follows.

**English hours a week in the national plan of study, and what they mean in minutes (our arithmetic)**

| Stage | English, teaching hours a week | Minutes a week |
|---|---|---|
| 1° and 2° primaria | 2 | 90 |
| 3° to 6° primaria | 3 | 135 |
| Secundaria, regular day (JER) | 3 | 135 |
| Secundaria, full day (JEC) | 5 | 225 |

Source: MINEDU, Currículo Nacional de la Educación Básica, plans of study for primaria and secundaria. One teaching hour is 45 minutes; the minutes column is our multiplication. A regular secundaria week has 35 teaching hours and a full-day week 45, neither with a computing area.

### Two hours and a quarter a week

On the regular school day, a secundaria student gets three teaching hours of English, which is 135 minutes a week. A weekly coding class in English adds another hour or more of listening and speaking, with a clear purpose and an adult who waits for the answer.

It does not replace English lessons. It gives the English a job to do.

### No computing on the timetable

The national curriculum treats digital skills as transversal, spread across every area, and has no subject for programming. A Piura teenager can finish secundaria without writing a program.

On 16 March 2026, MINEDU reported, 53,631 schools across Peru reopened for 6,225,000 pupils. Our classes do not follow that calendar, so a Piura family can start in the hot summer months and simply keep the same hour once school resumes.

## How many days does the river under the Sánchez Cerro bridge carry water?

The Gobierno Regional de Piura publishes daily readings from the river, canal and reservoir stations of the Chira Piura project as open data. One station sits at the Sánchez Cerro bridge, between the district of Piura and Castilla. Our older students use it to learn that a missing day is not a dry day.

### The data

The dataset is "Datos Hidrometereológicos [Gobierno Regional Piura]", prepared by the Proyecto Especial Chira Piura and released by the Gobierno Regional de Piura as open data with an ODC-By licence (Open Data Commons Attribution), last updated on 9 September 2026. Our copy, saved on 9 November 2026, has 45,192 rows from dozens of stations.

For each station and day there is a flow measured at seven in the morning, in cubic metres per second, and some daily summaries. The bridge station is spelt two ways in the file, with and without a stray space in "SANCHEZ", so the code removes spaces before comparing names.

### The question

On how many days was there water in the river at seven in the morning? A first program reads the bridge's rows, keeps one reading per date, and counts the dates whose flow is above zero.

Version 1 also prints the first and last date, which looks like enough to say "out of all the days from January 2022 to August 2026".

**Version 1: count the days in the file** (Python)
```python
import csv

days = {}
with open("tb_medida_estaciones_act.csv", encoding="utf-8") as readings:
    for row in csv.DictReader(readings):
        if row["ESTACION"].replace(" ", "") == "PTE.SANCHEZCERRO":
            days[row["FECHA_REGISTRO"]] = row["CAUDAL07H"].strip()

wet = [d for d, flow in days.items() if flow != "" and float(flow) > 0]
print("Days in the file:", len(days), "from", min(days), "to", max(days))
print("Days with water at 07:00:", len(wet))
```
It prints Days in the file: 1672 from 20220101 to 20260831 and Days with water at 07:00: 852.

So the river flowed on 852 of 1,672 days, about half. But is 1,672 the number of days between 1 January 2022 and 31 August 2026? A student who counts on a calendar gets a different number, and that difference is where the real lesson starts. Version 2 turns each text date into a date, counts the calendar days, then walks through the dates in pairs and prints every place where the next date is more than one day after the previous one.

**Version 2: find the days that are not there** (Python)
```python
import csv
from datetime import date, timedelta

days = {}
with open("tb_medida_estaciones_act.csv", encoding="utf-8") as readings:
    for row in csv.DictReader(readings):
        if row["ESTACION"].replace(" ", "") == "PTE.SANCHEZCERRO":
            d = row["FECHA_REGISTRO"]
            days[date(int(d[:4]), int(d[4:6]), int(d[6:]))] = row["CAUDAL07H"].strip()

first, last = min(days), max(days)
calendar = (last - first).days + 1
print("Calendar days:", calendar, "Days in the file:", len(days))

dates = sorted(days)
for before, after in zip(dates, dates[1:]):
    if after - before > timedelta(days=1):
        print("Gap:", before + timedelta(days=1), "to", after - timedelta(days=1))

blank = sum(1 for flow in days.values() if flow == "")
print("Never written down:", calendar - len(days), "Written but blank:", blank)
```
It prints Calendar days: 1704 Days in the file: 1672, then Gap: 2023-12-02 to 2023-12-24, Gap: 2024-02-10 to 2024-02-11, Gap: 2024-07-01 to 2024-07-06, Gap: 2026-01-30 to 2026-01-30 and Never written down: 32 Written but blank: 42.

Thirty-two days were never recorded at all, most of them in one block in December 2023, and another 42 days have a row with an empty flow. That leaves three kinds of day that a careless program would mix up: a day with water, a day recorded as zero, and a day nobody wrote down. Only the first two tell us anything about the river.

**Days recorded and days with water at 07:00, by calendar month, January 2022 to August 2026 (our calculation)**

| Month | Days recorded | Days with water |
|---|---|---|
| January | 154 | 65 |
| February | 139 | 93 |
| March | 155 | 151 |
| April | 150 | 125 |
| May | 155 | 120 |
| June | 150 | 104 |
| July | 149 | 83 |
| August | 155 | 42 |
| September | 120 | 8 |
| October | 124 | 8 |
| November | 120 | 39 |
| December | 101 | 14 |

Data: Gobierno Regional de Piura, Datos Hidrometereológicos (Proyecto Especial Chira Piura), datosabiertos.gob.pe, ODC-By. Station at the Sánchez Cerro bridge, flow at 07:00. Counts are ours. "Days recorded" includes 42 days with an empty value, all in July and August; September to December have only four years of data in the file, January to August five.

The pattern is clear enough for a ten year old to read: water almost every March morning, almost never in September or October. December looks dry partly because one December is mostly missing. Before comparing months, a careful student divides by the days actually recorded, not by the days on the calendar.

1. **Ages 8 to 10, with a calendar.** Count the days from 1 January to 31 March of a year that is not a leap year. Then count them again for 2024. Why is one answer bigger?
2. **Ages 10 to 12, in Python.** Write the dates 1, 2, 3, 5, 6 and 9 in a list and print every number that is missing.
3. **Ages 13 to 15.** Change Version 1 so it also counts days recorded as zero and days left blank, and check that the three counts add up to 1,672.
4. **Ages 15 and over.** For each month, divide days with water by days recorded, not by days in the month. Which month changes most?
5. **Adults.** Pick another station in the file and run Version 2 on it. Are the gaps in the same weeks?

## What time are the classes for Piura families?

Any time. Piura keeps Peru time all year and never changes its clocks, and the mentor plans around school, work and the heat of the day.

**Piura hours and the matching time for a mentor in India (examples, not a schedule)**

| Piura | India | Day in India |
|---|---|---|
| 06:00 | 16:30 | Same day |
| 09:00 | 19:30 | Same day |
| 11:30 | 22:00 | Same day |
| 15:00 | 01:30 | The next day |
| 17:30 | 04:00 | The next day |
| 20:30 | 07:00 | The next day |

Peru is five hours behind UTC all year and has used no summer time since 1994 (IANA tz database, America/Lima; INACAL publishes the legal time). India is five and a half hours ahead of UTC, so India is always ten and a half hours ahead of Piura.

Piura families often like an early class, before the afternoon heat, or an evening one once it cools. You choose the slot in Piura time when booking, and it stays the same through every month of the year.

## Coding classes in Piura: questions and answers

### Are the classes in Spanish?

No. Classes are taught only in English, live and online. Mentors speak slowly and clearly, write new words on screen and adapt to each learner's accent and pace. If a learner answers in Spanish, the mentor understands and replies with the English word.

### My child's English is basic. Is that enough?

Usually, yes. Coding is visual, so meaning comes from the screen as much as from words. The free first class shows how your child copes; if they need it, the mentor suggests a gentler start, such as a month of private classes.

### At what age can a child in Piura begin?

At six, with Scratch blocks and pictures and no typing. Most children start typing Python at about nine. Teenagers and adults with no experience begin with Python in their own age group.

### Is there a coding centre in Piura?

No. We teach only live online, so a child in Castilla, Veintiséis de Octubre, Catacaos or the centre joins the same way, from home, with no trip across the river.

### What time are classes in Piura?

Any day, any time, flexible: choose a batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at the hour that suits your family. Piura never changes its clocks, and 09:00 in Piura is 19:30 for a mentor in India.

### Does school in Piura teach programming?

No school area is called programming or computing in Peru's national plan of study. Digital skills are spread across all subjects, so children who want to code learn it outside school.

### Can university students and adults join?

Yes. Students at Piura's universities take Java, Python and data structures with us alongside their degree, and adults learn Python, data analysis or AI tools. We are not connected with any university.

### How do I pay, and in which currency?

Monthly, by card, in US dollars, which is the currency this page shows; your bank turns it into soles. If you visit from India you will see rupees. The trial class is free and needs no card.

### Does my child get a certificate?

Courses that include one end with a Modern Age Coders certificate of completion. It is our own document, not issued by MINEDU, a school or a university, and not a Peruvian qualification.
