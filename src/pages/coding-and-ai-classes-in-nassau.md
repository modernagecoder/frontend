# The Bahamas built a free national tech academy. Your twelve year old cannot use it.

> Modern Age Coders runs a live online twelve-month Python and AI track for learners in Nassau and across New Providence, aged six to sixty-seven, in batches of five to eight or one to one. Students write real Python, handle real data, train and measure models, build agents and publish on GitHub, with individual progress tracking and a written update to the parent every month. Sessions are ninety minutes, weekly, and the main slots are Saturday and Sunday mornings in Nassau because of the time difference, which this page sets out in full. Group teaching is USD 100 a month, private teaching USD 150, and the first class is free.

## The initiative everyone is talking about starts at sixteen

Upskill Bahamas launched in November 2025 as a national digital-skills and online-learning programme, and it is a genuinely good thing. It is also, by its own eligibility, for people aged sixteen and over.

Which means the most heavily promoted digital-skills effort in recent Bahamian history reaches no child of eight, ten, twelve or fifteen. A parent in Nassau reading about it and concluding that their eleven year old is covered has read it correctly and concluded wrongly, and nobody is going to correct them, because the initiative is not aimed at them and never claimed to be.

That is the gap this programme sits in. Not because the national platform is inadequate, but because a sixteen year old arriving at it having never typed a line of code is starting eight years later than they needed to.

## What the labour market is already saying

The Bahamas Department of Labour's employment portal identifies AI programmers, coding specialists, automation engineers, cybersecurity specialists and data analysts among the emerging occupations associated with automation. That is the government's own list, and every item on it requires the same foundation: Python written by hand, real data handled honestly, and models whose error you can state.

None of those five occupations is reachable from a week of blocks. All of them are reachable from twelve months of weekly building, started early enough.

## Hours, including the ones we refuse to teach

Nassau is nine hours behind India in summer. That arithmetic is not negotiable and it produces a schedule with genuine holes in it, so here it is:

| Nassau time | India time | Can we teach it? |
|---|---|---|
| Saturday 08:00 | 17:30 | Yes, and this is the best slot we have |
| Saturday 10:00 | 19:30 | Yes |
| Weekday 07:00 | 16:30 | Yes, one to one only, before school |
| Weekday 17:00 | 02:30 next day | No. We will not pretend otherwise |
| Weekday afternoon | Small hours in India | No. We will not pretend otherwise |

Most providers would leave the last two rows off the page. We put them on because a family who books an evening slot that quietly turns into a tired teacher at half past two in the morning has been sold something, not taught something.

## What we do not say about anybody else

Volunteer and grant-funded programmes in New Providence have put robotics kits into public-school classrooms and built coding clubs, and a robotics kit in a classroom is a genuine good. Children who have been through one of those arrive here with better instincts than children who have not.

What a volunteer effort cannot easily promise is the same named teacher every week for twelve months, a written progress record per student, and a portfolio with a year of dated commits in it. That is not a criticism of volunteers. It is a description of what continuous paid teaching has to deliver in order to be worth paying for.
