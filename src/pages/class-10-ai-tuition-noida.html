Q2. What does a home need for online AI classes?

What a Noida home needs for an online Class 10 AI class

A laptop or desktop, a headset and a steady connection. The theory half would work on any screen, but the practical half needs a computer where Python, Jupyter and Orange can be installed, because that is where the file programs are written.

The computer

A Windows laptop, a MacBook or a desktop. A tablet or phone can follow a theory class, but Orange has no tablet version and Jupyter is awkward on a small touch screen, so the Wednesday class needs a proper computer. Nothing powerful is needed: these are small programs.

The connection

Ordinary home broadband is enough. Society Wi-Fi can drop in the evening, so keep a phone hotspot as a backup; a dropped student rejoins the same class, and the teacher repeats the step they missed.

The corner

A desk away from the television, with a headset so a younger sibling's cartoons do not reach the microphone. Students unmute often, so a quiet spot matters more than a camera.

  1. Week one: install together

    Python with Jupyter Notebook, NumPy and Orange are installed during the first Wednesday class, screen shared, with the teacher watching for errors. Parents do not need to do it.

  2. Match the school lab

    Ask the computer teacher which Python editor the school lab uses for the practical. If it differs from Jupyter, students also practise there, so the practical day holds no surprises.

  3. Save the file twice

    Every file program is saved in a folder on the laptop and in cloud storage. A lost notebook three weeks before the practical is a problem we would rather prevent.

Q3. What does a Monday theory class look like?

A worked Bag of Words question from Unit 6, solved the way the class solves it

Natural Language Processing is Unit 6 and carries 8 theory marks, and Bag of Words is its most reliable numerical. The three sentences below are our own, about a Noida commute, and the steps are the ones a student writes in the answer.

Question

Build the Bag of Words for this corpus. D1: "The metro was late today." D2: "Today the bus was late and crowded." D3: "The metro was crowded." Lowercase the text, remove full stops and treat the, was and and as stopwords. (a) Write the dictionary. (b) Write the document vector table. (c) Which word has the highest inverse document frequency, and why?

  1. Step 1. Normalise each document

    D1 becomes metro, late, today. D2 becomes today, bus, late, crowded. D3 becomes metro, crowded. Before cleaning the documents had 5, 7 and 4 words; the stopwords carried no meaning for the task.

  2. Step 2. The dictionary

    Every unique word, in order of first appearance: metro, late, today, bus, crowded. Five words.

  3. Step 3. Document vectors

    Count each dictionary word in each document and write one row per document.

Documentmetrolatetodaybuscrowded
D111100
D201111
D310001
Documents containing it22212

(c) bus has the highest IDF. IDF is log(N / document frequency) with N = 3 documents: for bus that is log10(3 / 1) = 0.477, and for each of the other four words log10(3 / 2) = 0.176. A word found in fewer documents tells you more about the document it is in, so it gets a higher weight. We checked these values with Python before printing them.

In class the teacher marks the most common slip on screen: students forget to strike out a stopword in one document and their dictionary grows to six words. The full TF, IDF and TFIDF method with a larger corpus is on the Bag of Words and TFIDF page, and the rest of the unit on the NLP questions page.

Q5. How do online classes fit the school's practical?

The countdown to your school's 417 practical

The school, not CBSE's regional office and not a tutor, conducts the 50 practical marks: file 15, practical exam 15, viva 5, project 10 and project viva 5. Because the school picks the date, we count backwards from it as soon as it is announced.

  1. Eight weeks out: the file is the priority

    At least 15 programs are needed. By now most should be written, run and explained; missing ones are scheduled for specific Wednesdays.

  2. Six weeks out: the project is finished

    The project is complete with its write-up, following the stages of the AI project cycle, so the remaining weeks are for explaining it rather than building it.

  3. Four weeks out: a timed practical rehearsal

    An unseen task, on the student's own laptop, under time, with the teacher watching the screen the way an examiner watches in the lab.

  4. Two weeks out: mock vivas

    Questions on the file and the project, each followed by a second "why". Students who stumble get another round the next week. The question bank is on the viva questions page.

  5. The week itself: light revision only

    Re-run every file program once, read the project summary aloud, and sleep. New topics in practical week help nobody.
