Maths tutor in Box Hill

Print a map of Box Hill North at a scale of 1:10,000 and the suburb covers about 396 square centimetres of paper, roughly a sheet the size of a large paperback. Reprint it at 1:25,000 and the suburb shrinks to about 63 square centimetres. The scale only changed by a factor of 2.5, so why did the area shrink by more than six? Because area depends on two lengths, and both shrink: 2.5 times 2.5 is 6.25. Scale factors for area trip up students from Year 8 to VCE, and they are one of many ideas we teach Box Hill learners with real data, from Foundation counting to VCE Specialist Mathematics.

A Box Hill worked example

Why a map at a smaller scale shrinks areas so fast

A scale of 1:10,000 means 1 cm on paper is 10,000 cm, or 100 m, on the ground. Lengths scale by the scale factor; areas scale by its square.

The ABS gives the area of Box Hill North as 3.9668 square kilometres and Box Hill South as 3.4903 square kilometres. To find each suburb's area on a map, divide the real area in square centimetres by the square of the scale's second number. At 1:10,000 that divisor is 100,000,000.

The quick rule wins

Going from 1:10,000 to 1:25,000 multiplies lengths by 1/2.5, so areas by 1/6.25. Dividing 396.68 by 6.25 gives 63.47 straight away, with no need to start again from square kilometres.

The classic mistake

Halving a map's scale does not halve an area; it quarters it. From 1:5,000 to 1:10,000, Box Hill North drops from 1,586.72 to 396.68 square centimetres, exactly a quarter.

A length check

A square with Box Hill North's area would have sides of about 1.99 km. At 1:25,000 that side is 7.97 cm, and 7.97 squared is 63.5, matching the table.

What students practise

Reading map scales, converting between kilometres, metres and centimetres, and applying the rule that areas scale by k squared and volumes by k cubed.

Years 8 and 9 students work with the table; VCE students later meet the same idea in transformations of functions.

Where it matters

Similar figures, scale drawings, enlargements and models all use the same rule. Students who own it stop guessing which power to use.

A question for Year 9

A model of a building is made at one tenth of full size. The model needs 2 litres of paint for its walls. How much would the real building need? The walls are areas, so the answer is 100 times as much, 200 litres, not 20.

If the model is solid and weighs 3 kilograms, the full-size version, made of the same material, would weigh 1,000 times as much.

Why students get it wrong

Most errors come from applying the length factor to everything. Writing k, k squared or k cubed beside each quantity before calculating removes nearly all of them.

VCE students meet the same idea again with dilations of graphs, where stretching one axis scales areas under curves.

VCE in Victoria

VCE maths and the technology-free exam

Victorian senior students take one or more of four VCE mathematics studies. One feature of the exams shapes how we teach Methods and Specialist.

Two exams without a calculator

The VCAA study design states: "Examination 1 for Mathematical Methods and Examination 1 for Specialist Mathematics are technology-free examinations."

So mental arithmetic, exact values and clean algebra still matter at the very top of school maths. Our scale problems are done by hand for exactly that reason.

Units come in a sequence

The design adds that "Students must undertake Unit 3 and Unit 4 as a sequence", while "There are no prerequisites for entry to Units 1, 2 and 3".

In practice, Methods and Specialist assume strong Year 10 algebra, which is where many students who come to us need help first.

Box Hill maths questions

Maths tutoring in Box Hill: questions families ask

Do you provide maths tutoring for Box Hill students?

Yes, live online in Melbourne time, from Foundation to Year 12 and for adults, in level-matched classes or one to one.

Which VCE maths do you teach?

Foundation Mathematics, General Mathematics, Mathematical Methods and Specialist Mathematics.

Are VCE maths exams done without a calculator?

Examination 1 for Mathematical Methods and Examination 1 for Specialist Mathematics are technology-free, according to the VCAA study design. The other VCE maths exams assume approved technology.

Why does an area shrink by more than the map scale changes?

Because area has two dimensions. Changing the scale by a factor k changes areas by k squared, so a change of 2.5 shrinks areas by 6.25.

How big is Box Hill North on a 1:25,000 map?

About 63.47 square centimetres, from the ABS area of 3.9668 square kilometres.

Can tutoring guarantee a study score?

No. We teach thoroughly and report honestly, but no tutor can promise a study score or an ATAR.

Do lesson times change with daylight saving?

Not for the family. Lessons stay at the same Melbourne time; only the teacher's side moves.

Do you teach adults in Box Hill?

Yes, in adult classes or one to one.

How do volumes change when a model is scaled?

By the cube of the scale factor. A model at one tenth of full size has one hundredth of the surface area and one thousandth of the volume.

Is mental arithmetic still worth practising for VCE?

Yes. Two VCE exams are technology-free, and quick, accurate arithmetic also helps learners notice when a calculator answer cannot be right.

What are the fees?

The first lesson is free. Monthly fees for classes and private lessons are in the fees panel above.
