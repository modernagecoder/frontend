Further Maths when your school does not offer it

Plenty of sixth forms teach A level Maths but not Further Maths, usually because there are too few students to run a class. That does not close the door. Further Maths can be taught by a programme, a tutor, an online course or the student working alone. What cannot be improvised is the exam: someone has to enter the student and host the papers. This page sets out the routes, what the government-funded AMSP offers, the boards' private candidate rules, and how we teach Further Maths online.

The short answer

If your school does not offer A level Further Maths, you can still take it. First secure an exam entry, either through your own school or college or at another exam centre as a private candidate. Then arrange teaching: through the Department for Education's Advanced Maths Support Programme (AMSP) if you are at a state-funded school in England, through a tutor or online course, or a mix. Five routes

How can you take A level Further Maths if your school does not offer it?

There are five realistic routes. They differ in who teaches and who enters you for the exam, and several can be combined.

The Department for Education describes Further Maths as "designed for students with an enthusiasm for mathematics, many of whom will go on to degrees in mathematics, engineering, the sciences and economics." For those students, a school timetable without it is a real obstacle, but a solvable one. The table below is our summary of the routes, built from the sources named under it.

Route 1 usually asks least of everyone: the school already enters the student for A level Maths, so adding a Further Maths entry with the same board is an administrative step, not a new relationship. Everything else on this page assumes you have asked your school about it first.

The AMSP

What is the AMSP, and can it help with Further Maths?

The Advanced Maths Support Programme is a Department for Education programme for state-funded schools and colleges in England. One of its aims is helping more of them offer Further Maths, including tuition support for students.

The GOV.UK guidance page states that the AMSP "provides resources and support to improve the teaching and student uptake of post-16 maths" and that it "provides support for teachers and students in state-funded schools and colleges in England." Among its aims it lists to "improve access to level 3 maths by supporting more schools and colleges to offer AS/A level further maths and core maths", and to "support level 3 students to progress to higher education by providing direct tuition, support and enrichment opportunities for 16- to 19-year-olds".

Two limits are worth stating plainly. The guidance says "Applies to England" and covers state-funded schools and colleges, so students at independent schools, or in Wales or Northern Ireland, need another route. And the AMSP works through schools: GOV.UK tells institutions to "Register through the AMSP website". So the first conversation is still with the school's maths department. We could not read the AMSP's own website when writing this page, so for current details of its tuition, go to the programme directly or ask your school.

Exam entry

Can you sit A level Further Maths as a private candidate?

Yes, with the major boards, provided an exam centre agrees to host you. AQA requires 7367 private candidates to be UK-based; Cambridge International states that private candidates can enter for 9231.

AQA's 7367 specification says: "This specification is available to private candidates." It continues: "A private candidate may be self-taught, home schooled or have private tuition, either with a tutor or through a distance learning organisation. They must be based in the UK." Cambridge International's 9231 syllabus for 2026 and 2027 states: "Private candidates can enter for this syllabus." Pearson's 9FM0 specification sets no formal entry requirement either, though it expects most students to hold or be taking A level Maths.

  1. In Year 11 or early Year 12, ask your school whether it can enter you for Further Maths with the same board as your A level Maths, even without a class.
  2. If not, ask which board and specification your A level Maths uses, so your Further Maths matches it where possible.
  3. Contact exam centres near you that accept private candidates. Ask early; centres may charge and set their own deadlines.
  4. Confirm the specification code and, for Pearson, the option route, before teaching begins.
  5. Only then fix the teaching plan: which content, by when, and with whom.

The timing rules shape the plan. Pearson requires every 9FM0 paper to be sat in one May and June, and AQA describes 7367 as linear, with every exam sat at the end of the course. Cambridge International runs June and November series. Whatever the board, all papers fall together, so the teaching has to cover everything before one exam season.

We do not run an exam centre and cannot enter anyone for an exam. We teach; the entry stays between the family, the school or centre, and the board.

A smaller step

Is AS Further Maths a good option if your school does not offer the A level?

It can be. The Department for Education calls AS Further Maths a very useful qualification in its own right, and every specification must allow it to be taught alongside AS Maths.

The DfE subject content says AS further mathematics "can be taught alongside AS or A level mathematics" and "is a very useful qualification in its own right." It also requires that "In any AS further mathematics specification, at least one route must be available to allow the qualification to be taught alongside AS mathematics". In practice that means a Year 12 student can start AS Further Maths without having finished A level Maths first.

When AS makes sense

When teaching time is limited, for example a student studying alone with weekly lessons.

When the student wants Further Maths content for a university application but is carrying three other A levels.

When the full A level is worth the push

When the student's target degree is maths, physics or engineering and the full A level is listed as preferred or required. Check each university's own entry page.

When the student is moving fast through A level Maths and has the time.

By DfE rules, A level Further Maths has "a prescribed core which must comprise approximately 50% of its content", sections A to I: proof, complex numbers, matrices, further algebra and functions, further calculus, further vectors, polar coordinates, hyperbolic functions and differential equations. AS covers a subset of that core plus options. Either way, those nine headings are the backbone of any self-study plan.

Study plan

How do you study Further Maths without a teacher at school?

Backwards from the exam date, in blocks that follow the core. Here is a 30-week outline we use as a starting point, and three starter problems.

A 30-week Further Maths outline for one academic year of the core, ours (adjust to the board's specification)
WeeksCore blockCheckpoint
1 to 4Proof by induction; roots of polynomialsInduction proofs written with base case, step and conclusion
5 to 9Complex numbers: arithmetic, Argand diagrams, modulus and argumentLoci sketched and equations solved
10 to 14Matrices: arithmetic, determinants, inverses, transformationsTransformations described from a matrix and back
15 to 18Further algebra and functions; seriesMethod of differences used confidently
19 to 23Further calculus and further vectorsVolumes of revolution; lines and planes in three dimensions
24 to 27Polar coordinates and hyperbolic functionsPolar areas and hyperbolic identities
28 to 30Differential equations, then a mixed reviewA full timed core paper from the board

Our starter problem 1, proof by induction. Prove that 5n + 3 is divisible by 4 for all positive integers n. For n = 1, 5 + 3 = 8. Assume 5k + 3 = 4m. Then 5k+1 + 3 = 5(5k + 3) − 12 = 20m − 12 = 4(5m − 3), a multiple of 4. So the result holds for every positive integer.

Our starter problem 2, roots of polynomials. The roots of x3 − 6x2 + 11x − 6 = 0 are α, β and γ. Without solving, find α2 + β2 + γ2. The sum of the roots is 6 and the sum of products in pairs is 11, so the answer is 62 − 2 × 11 = 14. Checking: the roots are 1, 2 and 3, and 1 + 4 + 9 = 14.

Our starter problem 3, matrices. The matrix with rows (0, 1) and (1, 0) reflects the plane in the line y = x. Squaring it gives the identity matrix, which matches the geometry: reflecting twice returns every point to where it started. Ideas like this, a picture behind each calculation, are what make self-study stick.

The outline and problems were written by us on 31 October 2026. Each board publishes its own specification and past papers; follow the content list of the specification you are entered for.

Questions from families

What families ask when the school does not offer Further Maths

Can I do A level Further Maths if my school does not offer it?

Yes. You need an exam entry, through your school or as a private candidate at another centre, and a source of teaching. Many students combine school entry with outside teaching.

What is the AMSP?

The Advanced Maths Support Programme is a Department for Education programme supporting post-16 maths in state-funded schools and colleges in England. Its listed support includes helping schools offer Further Maths and tuition support for Further Maths students.

Is AMSP Further Maths tuition free?

GOV.UK says most AMSP support is free but that there are some costs for student tuition of Further Maths. Ask your school how it handles them, as the programme works through institutions.

Can I take Further Maths as a private candidate?

Yes, with boards that accept private candidates, if an exam centre agrees to host you. AQA requires private candidates for 7367 to be based in the UK, and Cambridge International accepts private candidates for 9231.

Should I do AS Further Maths instead of the full A level?

It depends on your time and your degree plans. The DfE calls AS Further Maths a very useful qualification in its own right, and every specification allows it alongside AS Maths. Check what your target universities prefer.

Can I teach myself A level Further Maths?

Some students do, especially strong ones with a structured plan and marked practice. AQA explicitly lists self-taught students among possible private candidates. Regular feedback on written proofs is the part that is hardest to replace.

Do I need A level Maths for Further Maths?

Further Maths builds on A level Maths throughout, so in practice you study both. Pearson and AQA set no formal prior learning requirement, and Cambridge strongly recommends studying its 9709 Mathematics alongside or before 9231.

Which board should I choose for Further Maths?

Usually the same board as your A level Maths, because the content and notation then match. If you are a private candidate, the centre's available boards may decide it.

When are your Further Maths classes?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. All times are given in UK time.

Are you part of the AMSP?

No. Modern Age Coders is an independent online school, not part of the AMSP, the Department for Education or any exam board, and we do not enter students for exams.
