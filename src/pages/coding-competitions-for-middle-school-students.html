Coding competitions for middle school students

Middle school is when many students enter their first real competition, and the choice is wider than most parents expect. Some contests need no coding at all, some accept Scratch, and a few are the same contests high schoolers take. The catch is that grade limits, team rules and sign-up routes differ a lot, and several fall deadlines arrive before families notice them. This guide lists what a sixth, seventh or eighth grader can enter in 2026-27, checked on each organiser’s own site on 17 October 2026, and adds a practice set and the year plan we use in class.

The no-code doors

Two of these need no programming at all. Bebras tasks are logic puzzles about how computers think, and the ACSL Elementary Division, open to grades 3 to 6, sets a short non-programming test on one topic per contest.

They suit a sixth grader who likes puzzles but has not typed much code yet, and they give honest feedback on reasoning skills before any coding course begins.

Where Scratch counts

The Congressional App Challenge rulebook names block-based coding languages as allowed, so a finished Scratch project with a clear purpose is a genuine entry.

Most other contests on this page expect typed code. A student moving from Scratch to Python in grade 6 or 7 can be ready for ACSL Junior or USACO bronze by grade 8.

Sources: Bebras USA, ACSL Divisions, CyberPatriot, Congressional App Challenge, Presidential SI Challenge eligibility, FIRST and USACO, read 17 October 2026. Modern Age Coders has no tie to any of them.

Bebras

Bebras: a free 45-minute computational thinking challenge

Bebras is the gentlest first competition on this list, and the 2026 US dates are only weeks away.

What the organisers say

The site states: "Participating in the challenge is free, and the tasks can all be completed without any preparation or studying." Students get 45 minutes and are not expected to finish every task.

For 2026 the US challenge is scheduled for November 9 to 20, 2026. Teachers supervise, so a family whose school is not taking part can ask a teacher to register as coordinator.

Which group a middle schooler sits in

Most sixth graders fall in Benjamins (10 to 12) and most seventh and eighth graders in Cadets (12 to 14). Both groups get fifteen tasks, five at each level.

With 45 minutes for fifteen tasks, that is three minutes a task on average by our arithmetic, so the skill on test day is moving on from a stuck puzzle quickly.

Source: USA Bebras Computing Challenge, read 17 October 2026.

The 2026-27 season

The first contest window runs from October 19, 2026 to January 10, 2027. Three more contests follow, the last closing on May 23, 2027, and the online Finals are on Saturday, May 29, 2027.

Students take all four contests. ACSL awards certificates to top teams and top individuals in each state after the fourth contest, using cumulative scores.

Getting a team started

Registration is done by an adult advisor before December 31, 2026, and ACSL says any teacher, parent or adult with some computer science experience can be that advisor. There is no minimum team size.

Short answer topics run, in ACSL’s words, "from Number Systems to Boolean Algebra to Digital Electronics". The practice set below covers the first of those.

CyberPatriot for beginners

CyberPatriot says it is designed for any student, "regardless of prior cybersecurity knowledge". For middle school teams the first scored round of the season is the Introductory Round, which tests the basics.

This season’s team sign-up ended on October 1, 2026. A student with no team yet can learn the basics this year and ask a teacher about coaching one next fall.

Two notes on the other two

The Presidential SI Challenge has a Standard Track and a Trailblazer Track for middle schoolers; Trailblazer is for projects that are novel and could matter nationwide. The program was called the Presidential AI Challenge until a September 29, 2026 executive order changed the federal term.

FIRST says it is developing the next generation of K-8 programs, launching in 2027, so check firstinspires.org before planning a 2027-28 team. Its FIRST LEGO League page says the 2026-2027 BIOGLOW season will be the final season of the program.

Going further alone

USACO, the app challenge and capture the flag for a strong eighth grader

Some middle schoolers are ready for contests that high schoolers take. Three of them welcome younger students outright.

USACO bronze

The USACO rules say all are welcome in the online contests, every newcomer starts in bronze, and bronze problems are designed so that Python can solve them fully. Entry is free. The 2026-27 dates were not posted on 17 October 2026.

Congressional App Challenge

For 2026 a student enrolled in middle school on October 26, 2026 can enter, alone or in a team of up to four. Entries close at noon Eastern (EDT) on Monday, October 26, 2026, so this suits a student whose app is nearly finished.

CyLab Security Academy

The free platform behind picoCTF describes itself as a learning ecosystem for middle schoolers through adults, with an annual flagship event for US middle and high school students. Its practice challenges stay open between events.

Our Congressional App Challenge guide covers that rulebook in detail, and the USACO coaching page explains how bronze problems are scored. For security puzzles, start with picoCTF for beginners; for team defense, read CyberPatriot preparation; and for an SI project, the Presidential AI Challenge guide covers the renamed program.

Practice set

Six number systems questions to try before an ACSL contest

Number systems is the first short answer topic most ACSL students meet. These six questions are ours, written for this page, at roughly Junior Division difficulty.

  1. Convert 45 (base 10) to binary.
  2. Convert 2F (base 16) to base 10.
  3. Convert 75 (base 8) to binary. Hint: each octal digit is exactly three binary digits.
  4. Add the binary numbers 1101 and 1011. Give the answer in binary.
  5. Which is largest: 3A (base 16), 71 (base 8) or 111011 (base 2)?
  6. Convert 1F (base 16) to base 8.
Answers and the reasoning (ours)
QuestionAnswerHow
110110145 = 32 + 8 + 4 + 1
2472 x 16 + 15 = 47
31111017 is 111 and 5 is 101; side by side gives 111101, which is 61 in base 10
4110001101 is 13 and 1011 is 11; 13 + 11 = 24, and 24 = 16 + 8
5111011 (base 2)3A is 58, 71 is 57, and 111011 is 59
6371F is 31; 31 = 3 x 8 + 7

Why place value is the whole trick

Every question above reduces to one idea: each position is worth the base times the position to its right. Once a student can say why 2F means two sixteens and fifteen ones, the rest is careful arithmetic.

The shortcut in question 3 works because 8 is 2 to the power 3. The same shortcut links base 16 and base 2 in groups of four.

Turning it into a habit

We give students five fresh questions like these each week for a month before Contest #1. Speed comes from repetition, not from memorising tables.

A good follow-up is writing a short Python function that converts any base 10 number to any base from 2 to 16 without using bin, oct or hex.

For parents

A first competition is about learning what a timed test feels like. Treat the score as a starting line.

Check the sign-up route early: Bebras and ACSL need a coordinator or advisor, and that adult may be you.

For students

Pick one contest that sounds fun and one that sounds hard, and enter both.

After each contest, write down one question you got wrong and why. That list is your study plan.

Questions from parents

Questions parents ask about middle school contests

What coding competitions can middle school students enter?

In 2026-27 a student in grades 6 to 8 can enter Bebras, ACSL (Junior, Classroom or Elementary for grade 6), CyberPatriot's Middle School Division, the Congressional App Challenge, the Presidential SI Challenge, FIRST LEGO League and the USACO online contests.

Is there a coding competition that needs no coding experience?

Yes. Bebras USA says its tasks can be completed without any preparation or studying, and the ACSL Elementary Division for grades 3 to 6 uses a non-programming test.

When is Bebras in 2026?

The Bebras Challenge USA 2026 is scheduled for November 9 to 20, 2026. Students have 45 minutes, and a teacher registers as the coordinator.

Can a seventh grader do ACSL?

Yes. The ACSL Junior Division is meant for junior high and middle school students, and no student beyond grade 9 may compete in it. An adult advisor registers the team before December 31, 2026.

Can my child enter a coding competition with Scratch?

For the 2026 Congressional App Challenge, yes: the rules allow block-based coding languages. Most other contests here expect typed code such as Python.

Is CyberPatriot open to middle school?

Yes, through the Middle School Division. Its first scored round in 2026-27 is the Introductory Round on November 12 to 15, 2026, but the deadline to create a team this season was October 1, 2026.

Can middle schoolers take USACO?

Yes. USACO says all are welcome in its online contests, newcomers start in bronze, and bronze problems are meant to be solvable in Python.

What is the Presidential SI Challenge for middle school?

It is the program formerly called the Presidential AI Challenge. Its Middle School Student Category covers grades 6 to 8, and a team is one adult Team Leader plus between one and four students.

When are your classes?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays.

Do you run any of these competitions?

No. We are an independent online school with no connection to any organiser. We teach the skills, and families register with each competition directly.
