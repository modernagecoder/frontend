## How old is the play equipment in Halifax parks? A project on the municipality&rsquo;s own file

Halifax Regional Municipality publishes an Outdoor Recreation Equipment file as open data: swings, slides, hoops and play structures, one row each. Our teen students download it and ask a plain question that turns out to need careful code. Every figure below is our count, not the municipality&rsquo;s.

**Our count of the Halifax Regional Municipality Outdoor Recreation Equipment file, downloaded 22 October 2026 (2,218 rows). In-service rows only, with an install year rated confidence 1 or 2 by the file**

| Equipment (decoded from ASSETCODE) | Rows used | Median install year | Oldest year |
|---|---|---|---|
| Climber | 60 | 2007.5 | 2000 |
| Spring Toy | 41 | 2012 | 1998 |
| Play Structure | 274 | 2013 | 1999 |
| Slide | 19 | 2014 | 1997 |
| Swing | 149 | 2015 | 1995 |
| Basketball Hoop | 101 | 2018 | 1994 |
| Other (our group: triple hoops, gym equipment, teeter totters, ramps and items coded OTHER) | 68 | 2013 | 1998 |

Here is the script that prints the table. It needs only Python 3 and the downloaded CSV in the same folder; the equipment names come from the coded values the municipality publishes with the layer:

```
import csv
import statistics

NAMES = {"BBH": "Basketball Hoop", "PYST": "Play Structure", "SWNG": "Swing",
         "CLIM": "Climber", "SPTY": "Spring Toy", "SLID": "Slide"}

years = {}
blank = 0
with open("outdoor-recreation-equipment.csv", encoding="utf-8-sig") as f:
    for row in csv.DictReader(f):
        if row["ASSETSTAT"] != "INS":
            continue
        if row["INSTYR"] == "":
            blank += 1
            continue
        if row["INSTYRCONF"] not in ("1", "2"):
            continue
        name = NAMES.get(row["ASSETCODE"], "Other")
        years.setdefault(name, []).append(int(row["INSTYR"]))

print("in service, no install year:", blank)
for name, ys in sorted(years.items(), key=lambda p: statistics.median(p[1])):
    print(name, len(ys), "median", statistics.median(ys), "oldest", min(ys))
```

### 1. Decode the codes

The ASSETCODE column holds short codes such as PYST and SPTY. The layer&rsquo;s own metadata maps them to Play Structure and Spring Toy, so the first job is a small dictionary that turns codes into words a reader understands.

### 2. Keep what is in service

Of 2,218 rows, 2,195 carry the status In Service. The rest are out of service, pending, proposed or blank, and a question about equipment in parks today should leave them out.

### 3. Ask how sure the year is

Each install year has a partner column, INSTYRCONF, rating it from 1 (a measured value or known fact) to 5 (inaccurate). We keep ratings 1 and 2 only, which leaves 712 of the 839 in-service rows that have a year at all.

### The number the script prints first

Before any median, the script reports that 1,356 in-service rows have no install year. That is most of the file. A student who publishes &ldquo;Halifax climbers date from about 2007&rdquo; without saying the median rests on 60 rows out of 205 climbers has told a smaller truth than it sounds. Saying how much of the data an answer stands on is the habit this project exists to build.

Good next steps for a strong student: compare the ACCESSIBLE column (228 rows marked yes, 378 no, and 1,612 left blank across the whole file) by equipment type, then plot the equipment on a map with the X and Y columns. We use the file under the municipality&rsquo;s open licence, and its required wording is: Contains information licenced under the Open Government Licence - Halifax ([licence](https://data-hrm.hub.arcgis.com/pages/open-data-licence), [dataset page](https://data-hrm.hub.arcgis.com/datasets/5dbc7a7c78c54dfbae7b53ac428fda90_0)). Nothing here is endorsed by Halifax Regional Municipality.

## Two Waterloo computing contests a Halifax school can enter

The University of Waterloo&rsquo;s Centre for Education in Mathematics and Computing (CEMC) runs contests that schools across Canada order for their students, Nova Scotia schools included. Dates are CEMC&rsquo;s, read on 22 October 2026.

### Beaver Computing Challenge

For students in Grade 10 or below, in three levels. Its 2026 window runs from 9 to 20 November 2026, and CEMC says the questions only require familiarity with concepts in the mathematics curriculum, so no programming is needed. It lasts 45 minutes.

### Canadian Computing Competition

CEMC lists the 2027 CCC for Wednesday 17 February 2027. Writers pick the Junior or Senior paper to suit their programming level, whatever grade they are in, and the accepted languages include Python and Java, which suits a Halifax student coming from either Python or the Java of CP12.

### Entry runs through a school

A student cannot sign up alone: CEMC&rsquo;s rule is that students take part through a school. If a Halifax school does not order a contest, CEMC suggests looking for another local school that will let the student join as a participant.

The Beaver challenge is the gentler door, and it rewards exactly what the equipment project trains: reading a rule carefully and applying it without guessing. For the CCC, the step from Junior to Senior is mostly about efficiency, and we work on it with practice problems we write ourselves. Ten years of past CEMC papers sit online at no cost and need no account; we send students to [CEMC&rsquo;s archive](https://cemc.uwaterloo.ca/resources/past-contests) for real papers and never copy its questions into our lessons. Our [Beaver Computing Challenge guide](/beaver-computing-challenge-guide) and [CCC preparation page](/canadian-computing-competition-ccc-prep) go further. Modern Age Coders has no link to CEMC or to the University of Waterloo.

## If AI can write code, why should a Halifax teen learn it?

Because a script can run perfectly and still answer the wrong question.

Request the median install year of Halifax play structures from an AI assistant and you will get code that runs. It will probably not stop to ask whether out-of-service equipment belongs in the answer, or whether a year rated as a guess should count. Those choices change the result, and only someone who understands the data can make them well.

So we teach thinking before typing: what is the question, which rows answer it, how sure is each value. As students get older they meet AI assistants too, within the age limits each tool sets, to check and explain their work, never to hand in output they cannot defend line by line.

The aim is a student who can tell a correct answer from a confident one, which is a skill no tool supplies on the student&rsquo;s behalf. The longer argument is in [our essay on judging code you did not write yourself](/blog/is-coding-worth-learning-2026).
