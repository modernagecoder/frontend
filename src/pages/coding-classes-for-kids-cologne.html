A Cologne child will meet Informatik sooner than children in many other Länder: North Rhine-Westphalia makes it compulsory in grades 5 and 6 at every school type, and its Grundschule maths and Sachunterricht already touch on informatics in grades 3 and 4. Below: what that means for a child of 6 to 12, how our live classes in English work, the dates of the NRW school year 2026/27, and the city's own tree register, where the question "which tree is most common?" has two honest answers.

Schools in Cologne

Do children in Cologne learn Informatik at school?

Yes, and earlier than in many Länder. The Gesellschaft für Informatik's 2026/27 monitor sets out what North Rhine-Westphalia requires.

Grades 3 and 4: a first taste

Grundschule in NRW covers grades 1 to 4. The monitor reports that the Land's revised Grundschule curricula name informatics-related expectations inside two existing subjects, Mathematik and Sachunterricht, especially for grades 3 and 4.

It also mentions a project called Informatik an Grundschulen, IaG, run with children in grades 3 and 4 to teach basic informatics concepts, with a handbook for teachers. So a Cologne eight or nine year old may meet algorithms in class, but as part of maths or Sachunterricht, not as a subject of its own.

Grades 5 and 6: compulsory for everyone

From grade 5 the picture changes. At every general school type in NRW, the monitor says, Informatik is a compulsory subject in grades 5 and 6: Hauptschule, Realschule, Sekundarschule, Gesamtschule and Gymnasium alike.

After grade 6 it becomes a choice. Depending on the school type, pupils can pick Informatik as a Wahlpflicht subject in grades 7 to 10, or at the nine-year Gymnasium in grades 9 and 10.

For a parent, the useful fact is the timing. A child who is seven now will sit a compulsory Informatik lesson within a few years, whichever school they move to after grade 4. Children who arrive at grade 5 able to plan a sequence of steps and fix a small bug tend to enjoy the subject instead of fearing it. That is what our classes for six to ten year olds build, through games and stories rather than worksheets.

Taught in English

Are the classes taught in English, and is that hard for a young child?

All teaching is in English. Most children cope far better than parents expect, because so much of a coding lesson is shown on screen.

English, and only English

We do not run German-language classes, and we say so before the first lesson. For Cologne families who speak English at home or whose children attend an international or bilingual school, that is the point; for others it becomes a weekly hour of real, useful English.

Matched to your child

The mentor speaks at your child's speed, repeats with simpler words when needed and gets used to their accent within a few lessons. A child who mixes in a German word is understood, then gently given the English one.

Show, try, change

A typical lesson is mostly doing: the mentor shows a step, the child tries it and then changes it to make it their own. Coding words like loop, if and repeat appear again and again, so the vocabulary grows by itself.

Mentors teach from India over live video, which is why Cologne afternoons work well: they fall in the Indian evening. Your child keeps the same mentor every week, someone who soon knows whether they like a puzzle to wrestle with or a nudge in the right direction.

A real Cologne project

Which tree is most common on Cologne's register? Two programs, two answers

Stadt Köln publishes its register of individual trees as open data that anyone may reuse. Our older children count the trees by name and then by genus, and discover that the grouping you choose decides the winner.

The data

The Baumkataster Köln 2017 lists 134,189 trees, each with a location, a height, an estimated age, a genus in Latin (Gattung) and a German name (DeutscherN). The city notes that it includes species, genus and age of the recorded trees.

Not every row is complete: 52,660 trees have no German name and 51,380 no genus. We give the child the file as koeln_trees.csv and tell them to skip empty names rather than count them as a kind of tree.

The question

Which tree does Cologne have most of? The first program tallies the German names with a dictionary: every time a name appears, its count goes up by one. Then it sorts the names by count and prints the top three.

Before running it, children guess. Chestnut is a popular answer, and so is oak. Both are wrong, but the real surprise comes with the second program.

Linde, the lime tree, comes first with 11,833. But look at third place: Winterlinde, the small-leaved lime, with 6,249. That is also a lime. The register uses many names for trees of the same kind, so counting names splits one family into several smaller ones.

The second program counts by genus instead, the Latin family name in the Gattung column, so every lime lands in Tilia and every maple in Acer.

Data: Stadt Köln, Baumkataster Köln 2017, file Bestand_Einzelbaeume_Koeln_0.csv via Open.NRW, licence Datenlizenz Deutschland Zero 2.0, downloaded November 2026. All counts are our own; 82,809 rows have a genus. The register's state is 2017, so newer plantings are not in it.

Maples show the effect even more strongly. By name, Ahorn manages 6,218 and never reaches the top three; by genus, Acer is second with 16,498. Neither program is wrong. They answer different questions, and a good coder says which question they asked.

  1. Ages 6 to 8, no screen. Collect ten leaves on a walk. Sort them once by colour and once by shape. Does the biggest pile stay the biggest?
  2. Ages 8 to 10, Scratch. Make a list of tree names and a counter for each. Add a "lime family" counter that goes up for Linde and Winterlinde alike.
  3. From 10, typed Python. Run Version 1 and Version 2, then print the top five for each and explain in one sentence why the lists differ.
  4. Going further. The file has a height column. Find the average height of the limes and the maples, but first decide what to do with heights recorded as 0.

A free contest this month

The Informatik-Biber, run by Bundesweite Informatikwettbewerbe, takes place in the Biberwochen from 9 to 20 November 2026. It is a free online round of logic puzzles that any pupil from grade 3 to grade 13 can try without having coded before; well over half a million pupils took part in 2025.

Schools enter their classes, so ask your child's teacher whether their school takes part. Results arrive in December 2026, certificates in January 2027.

Using the long breaks

The six summer weeks from 19 July 2027 are a good time for a short, focused project, for example finishing a Scratch game or extending the tree tally to heights and ages.

In class we never use actual Biber tasks. We write puzzles of our own about order, hidden rules and shortest paths, which many children enjoy as practice.

Cologne parents ask

Questions Cologne parents ask about coding classes

Are the classes in German?

No, never: all classes are live and taught only in English. The mentor slows down, rephrases and adjusts to your child's pace and accent, so a child with basic English can take part. Code keywords such as if and repeat are English anyway.

Is Informatik compulsory in NRW schools?

Yes, in grades 5 and 6 at every general school type, according to the Gesellschaft für Informatik's Informatik-Monitor 2026/27. After that, Informatik is an elective in most school types, and at the nine-year Gymnasium in grades 9 and 10.

Does my child learn any informatics in Grundschule?

Some, inside other subjects. The monitor reports that the revised NRW Grundschule curricula include informatics-related content in Mathematik and Sachunterricht, especially in grades 3 and 4, while the compulsory subject itself starts in grade 5.

What age is right to start?

Six is fine. The youngest begin in Scratch with blocks that click together, so almost nothing needs typing. Around 9, most children are ready for typed Python, which suits the year or two before grade 5.

What time are classes in Cologne?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Common Cologne slots are mid-afternoon, around 15:50, or late Saturday morning.

Who are the mentors and where are they?

They work from India and teach live over video in English, and each child stays with one of them. Until 28 March 2027, a 15:50 class in Cologne begins at 20:20 in India.

Do classes stop in the NRW holidays?

Only if you want them to. Lessons run on through autumn, Christmas and Easter breaks unless you ask for a pause; the KMK lists the summer holidays 2027 for NRW as 19 July to 31 August 2027.

How many children are in a batch?

Fifteen to twenty, all of a similar age. For a child who wants the mentor to themselves, one to one means two private classes a week at agreed times.

How much is it, and what currency is charged?

There is one monthly fee. We display it in euro, converted at the reference rate of the European Central Bank; the actual charge is in US dollars, which your bank changes into euro, so the booked sum can differ slightly. Your first class costs nothing and needs no card.

What will my child need at home?

A laptop or desktop with a real keyboard, a camera and decent internet. Scratch needs only a browser; for Python we help you install what is needed during the first typed lessons.
