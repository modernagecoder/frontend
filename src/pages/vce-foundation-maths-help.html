In short

VCE Foundation Mathematics Units 3 and 4 apply mathematics to real contexts across four areas of study: algebra, number and structure; data analysis, probability and statistics; discrete mathematics; and space and measurement. Three mathematical investigations make up 60 per cent of the study score (40 in Unit 3, 20 in Unit 4), and a 2-hour examination with a scientific calculator makes up 40 per cent.

Inside the study design

Four practical areas of study

Taken from the VCE Mathematics Study Design on 7 October 2026. All four areas are completed across Units 3 and 4, with content equivalent to two areas in each unit, developed through contexts from students' other studies.

VCE Foundation Mathematics Units 3 and 4: areas of study
Area of studyWhat it covers, in the study design's terms
Algebra, number and structureEstimation, different forms of numbers and calculations, and algorithmic and computational thinking
Data analysis, probability and statisticsCollecting, presenting and analysing gathered and provided data from community, work, recreation and media contexts
Discrete mathematicsNumbers, relationships and formulae applied to analysing and critiquing practical situations
Space and measurementThe metric system and related measurement in domestic, societal, industrial and commercial contexts

The study design is clear about the purpose: providing students with the knowledge and skills to solve problems in real contexts "for a range of workplace, personal, further learning, community and global settings". It suits students heading into trades, apprenticeships, TAFE, or work, and students who need maths that makes sense rather than maths that impresses.

The estimation key knowledge is a good example of its practical spirit. It lists interval estimates, rounding, significant figures, leading-digit approximations, floor and ceiling values and percentage error, all of which decide real answers about how many, how much and how sure.

Technology is part of the study, not an extra. Outcome 3 asks students to apply computational thinking and use the numerical, graphical, symbolic and statistical functions of technology in practical situations. Spreadsheets are the natural tool, and a little coding helps students see what a formula is doing.

The study design even suggests investigation contexts, including pandemic data, climate change modelling, financial life stage planning and the operations of a community organisation. Schools choose, but the range shows how wide "practical" is meant to be.

How the study score is built

Sixty per cent in three investigations

Foundation's weights, task lengths and exam rules come straight from the study design, and its exam date from the VCAA timetable we checked on 7 October 2026.

How Foundation Mathematics is assessed
WhatShareDetail
Unit 3 coursework40 per centMathematical Investigations 1 and 2
Unit 4 coursework20 per centMathematical Investigation 3
Every investigationWithin those sharesFour to six hours across one or two weeks, drawing on at least two areas of study; each area features in one investigation or more
Final examination40 per centTwo hours of multiple-choice and written questions, with a scientific calculator and one bound reference text

Each investigation has three parts in the study design: identifying the context and the questions worth asking, investigating and analysing with mathematics and technology, and summarising and interpreting what was found. Marks follow all three, so a page of correct calculations with no explanation earns much less than it could.

Because the investigations carry so much weight, steady work through the year matters more in Foundation than in any other VCE maths study. A student who learns to explain their reasoning plainly in Term 1 benefits in every investigation after.

The 2026 examination is on Tuesday 17 November, in the session the VCAA timetable prints as 3:00 pm to 5.15pm (that page writes the end time with a full stop and no space). It is the last VCE maths exam of the year, which gives Foundation students extra revision weeks.

The exam assumes a scientific calculator, not a CAS, and allows one bound reference text that may be annotated. Building the reference as the year goes, with a worked example for each kind of problem, is one of the most useful habits a Foundation student can form.

A worked lesson

Round up, round down, or give a range?

The study design lists floor and ceiling values and interval estimates beside ordinary rounding. They matter because the context, not the calculator, decides which way an answer should go. Every number below is from an invented school excursion.

Ceiling: round up

130 students travel on 45-seat buses. 130 divided by 45 is 2.89, but 2.89 buses cannot leave the car park. The ceiling is 3 buses, with 5 seats spare.

Floor: round down

The hall has 130 chairs set in full rows of 8. 130 divided by 8 is 16.25, and a quarter of a row is not a row. The floor is 16 full rows, with 2 chairs left over.

Interval: give a range

A room measures 12.4 m by 7.8 m to the nearest 0.1 m. Each length could be 0.05 m either way, so the true area lies between 95.71 and 97.73 square metres.

The same division, three different answers (our worked examples)
QuestionCalculationRuleAnswerCheck
How many buses?130 / 45 = 2.89Ceiling3 buses3 x 45 = 135 seats, 5 spare
How many packs of 12 name tags?130 / 12 = 10.83Ceiling11 packs11 x 12 = 132 tags, 2 spare
How many full rows of 8 chairs?130 / 8 = 16.25Floor16 rows16 x 8 = 128, 2 chairs left
What is the room's area?12.4 x 7.8 = 96.72Interval95.7125 to 97.7325 square metresAbout 1 per cent either side

Ordinary rounding would give "3 buses" by luck and "11 packs" by luck, but it would also round 16.25 rows to 16 and 2.4 buses to 2, leaving 22 students on the footpath. The question to ask every time is what the leftover part means in the real situation, and then choose the floor, the ceiling or a range.

The interval answer is the most honest. Stating 96.72 square metres suggests precision the measurements do not have; the percentage error of the stated area is about 1.05 per cent above and 1.04 per cent below. That is exactly the kind of reasoning an investigation rewards when it asks how reliable a result is.

Spreadsheets and code both have ceiling and floor functions, and they are worth meeting early. Python's math.ceil(130 / 45) gives 3. One trap is worth knowing: Python's round() sends 2.5 to 2 and 3.5 to 4 (it rounds halves to the even number), which is why careful students choose the function deliberately.

In the exam, written-response questions often hinge on this choice. A correct calculation followed by the wrong kind of rounding loses the final mark, so we practise stating the rule in words: "rounded up because a part bus is still a bus".

The investigations

Your investigations stay yours

Investigations are 60 per cent of Foundation Mathematics, and the VCAA accepts them only as the student's own work. The points below come from the study design.

Coursework, as the VCAA frames it
AspectRequirement
AuthorshipTeachers sign off that work not acknowledged was produced by the student
TimingMostly in class, inside a limited window
LengthFour to six hours over one or two weeks per investigation, covering two or more areas of study
StructureSet the context and questions, investigate and analyse, then summarise and interpret

We teach the mathematics, the use of spreadsheets and the habit of explaining findings, using practical problems we invent, like the excursion above. We never plan, calculate, write, check or edit any part of a student's investigation. A student who has practised the whole cycle on our problems can complete the school's investigations with confidence and full ownership.

Questions about Foundation

Foundation questions we often hear

What is VCE Foundation Mathematics?

It is the VCE mathematics study focused on practical problems in workplace, personal, further learning, community and global settings. Units 3 and 4 cover four areas of study and are assessed mainly through three mathematical investigations.

How much do the investigations count?

School-assessed Coursework contributes 60 per cent (40 per cent from Investigations 1 and 2 in Unit 3, 20 per cent from Investigation 3 in Unit 4), and the end-of-year examination contributes 40 per cent.

When is the 2026 Foundation Maths exam?

The VCAA timetable lists Foundation Mathematics on Tuesday 17 November 2026, in the 3:00 pm session ending at 5:15 pm. The examination itself is 2 hours.

Which calculator is allowed?

The study design says student access to a scientific calculator is assumed in the examination, and one bound reference text, which may be annotated, may be brought in.

What are floor and ceiling values?

The floor of a number is the largest whole number not above it; the ceiling is the smallest whole number not below it. The floor of 16.25 is 16 and the ceiling of 2.89 is 3. Real problems decide which one makes sense.

How long is each investigation?

The study design sets each mathematical investigation at 4 to 6 hours over 1 to 2 weeks, addressing content from two or more areas of study.

Is Foundation Maths a good choice for me?

It suits students who want practical, everyday maths for work, TAFE or apprenticeships. Course and pathway choices are a conversation with your school and family; we can help with the maths once you have chosen.

Will you help with my investigation?

We teach the maths and the investigation process with our own problems, and never plan, calculate, write, check or edit any part of a student's investigation.

Is online tutoring suitable for students who find maths hard?

Often, yes. A patient tutor, a small group or one-to-one setting, and problems from real life tend to rebuild confidence. The free lesson is a low-risk way to find out.

When do lessons run?

Late afternoon in Victoria suits most students and sits within our tutors' working day. The weekly time is agreed after the free lesson.
