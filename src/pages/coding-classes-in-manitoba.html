Coding classes in Manitoba

Where can a Manitoba student learn coding with a live teacher?

Manitoba students from Grade 1 to adult learn coding, Python and AI with Modern Age Coders in live online classes, either with a private teacher or in a group batch of fifteen to twenty. Typical starting points are Python and AI for Kids, Python for Teens and, for Grade 12, our Pre-Calculus companion. Classes run on Manitoba time and the first one is free.

Manitoba has had a provincial computer science framework since 2004, with three courses a school may offer in Senior 2, 3 and 4, and it leaves the programming language to the teacher and the class. That freedom is generous, and it means two Winnipeg schools can teach the same course in different languages, and a school may decide not to offer it at all. This page sets out what each course is meant to cover, in the department's words where they matter; the Grade 12 math tests on the 2026 to 2027 calendar; a Python project built on fish-stocking records the province publishes itself; and the clock change Manitoba is skipping this November.

Computer science in Manitoba schools

The three senior computer science courses, as Manitoba's framework describes them

A single 2004 framework from the department, now called Manitoba Education and Early Childhood Learning, covers Senior 2 (20S), Senior 3 (30S) and Senior 4 (40S) Computer Science. Quoted words below are the department's; the topic lists are taken from its suggested course outlines and shortened by us.

Manitoba computer science courses from the 2004 Curriculum Framework of Outcomes (suggested outlines; wording in quotation marks is the department's, topic lists shortened by us)
CourseHow the framework describes itProgramming topics in the outline
Senior 2 Computer Science (20S)“This introductory course in computer programming is of interest to a broad audience.”Variables and data types, input and output, debugging, branching and nested branching, definite and indefinite loops, built-in graphics, subprograms without parameters
Senior 3 Computer Science (30S)“This course takes an in-depth look at problem solving through computer programming.”Multiple branching, subprograms with parameters, strings, one-dimensional and parallel arrays, simple sorts
Senior 4 Computer Science (40S)“This project-based computer programming course reflects the way projects are completed in industry.”Files, one program in two languages, object-oriented programming, recursion, two-dimensional arrays, analyzing algorithms, sorting and searching, a team computer simulation

No set language

The framework says it “does not prescribe a specific language” and that the teacher and students decide which modern languages to learn. In 40S, students are even asked to learn a new language on their own and to write one program in two different languages.

Credits are optional

Schools may offer one, two or three full credits in computer science, and the framework sets no prerequisites. Where a school skips 20S, its outcomes move into 30S, and the same happens between 30S and 40S. The department's course code for all three is 0280.

Code has to run

The department is plain that “many of the computer science learning outcomes can be achieved only by programming a computer.” Students trace an algorithm by hand to confirm its logic, then program sorts, searches and a random number generator.

The framework replaced the older Computer Science 205 and 305 documents, and its age shows mostly in small things, such as the department's former name, Manitoba Education, Citizenship and Youth, on the cover. The ideas have not aged. Hand-tracing, two-dimensional arrays, recursion and comparing the efficiency of two sorts still separate a student who can type code from one who can reason about it, and in our view they remain the core of programming. The framework also notes that students are not required to create their own classes until S4, which tells a parent roughly when object-oriented programming arrives.

Alongside the school course

Our classes support a student taking 20S, 30S or 40S, or a student whose school does not offer them, and we do not grant Manitoba credits. Credits and marks come from the school. We are not connected with Manitoba Education and Early Childhood Learning or any school division.

Grade 12 math tests

Manitoba's Grade 12 math tests in 2026 to 2027, and the fifth of the mark they carry

Manitoba sets provincial tests at the end of each semester for the three main Grade 12 math courses. Dates are the department's 2026/27 schedule, read on 22 October 2026.

Grade 12 Mathematics (40S) provincial tests, 2026 to 2027 (Manitoba Education and Early Childhood Learning assessment schedule)
TestSemester 1Semester 2
Grade 12 Applied Mathematics Achievement TestTuesday 26 January 2027Thursday 17 June 2027
Grade 12 Essential Mathematics Achievement TestWednesday 27 January 2027Wednesday 16 June 2027
Grade 12 Pre-Calculus Mathematics Achievement TestThursday 28 January 2027Tuesday 15 June 2027

The department says these tests count for 20 per cent of a student's final grade in Applied, Pre-Calculus or Essential Mathematics, and that they are marked locally by teachers using its marking guidelines. The schedule also names the courses at 40S, which places 40S in Grade 12; by the same pattern 30S is the Grade 11 level and 20S the Grade 10 level, though schools decide which grade takes each computer science course.

Pre-Calculus 40S

Grade 12 Pre-Calculus Mathematics carries course code 3939 and one credit. Its overview covers angles in standard position, the unit circle and six trigonometric ratios, transformations and inverses, logarithms, and permutations, combinations and the binomial expansion.

Earlier assessments

Manitoba also collects classroom results province-wide: Grade 3 reading and numeracy between 19 October and 27 November 2026, and Grade 7 mathematics between 18 January and 26 February 2027. These are formative, used to plan teaching rather than to grade.

How we help

For Pre-Calculus 40S we teach the topics in the order a Manitoba school does, with original practice written by us. We do not reproduce provincial test items; the department publishes its own archived tests and support documents.

Manitoba data project

More than a century of fish stocking: a project on Manitoba's open data

The Manitoba government publishes its waterbody stocking records as open data, one row per stocking event back to 1917. Our students pull the whole file through the web service, group it in Python and then question it. Every figure below is our own count.

Our script reads the seven saved pages and groups the events. It runs on Python 3 with no extra packages:import glob import json rows = [] for name in glob.glob("stocking/page-*.json"): with open(name, encoding="utf-8") as f: rows += [feature["attributes"] for feature in json.load(f)["features"]] by_decade = {} ids_for_name = {} for row in rows: decade = row["YEAR"] // 10 * 10 by_decade[decade] = by_decade.get(decade, 0) + 1 ids_for_name.setdefault(row["WATERBODY_NAME"], set()).add(row["WATERBODY_ID"]) print("events", len(rows)) for decade in sorted(by_decade): print(decade, by_decade[decade]) shared = [n for n, ids in ids_for_name.items() if len(ids) > 1] print("names", len(ids_for_name), "shared by 2+ waterbodies", len(shared))

1. Page through the service

The feature service hands back at most 2,000 records per request, so the 12,880 events arrive in seven pages, asked for with a growing resultOffset. Students check that the pages hold 12,880 distinct OBJECTID values, which proves no page was fetched twice.

2. Group by a key

Integer division turns a year into its decade, and a dictionary counts events per decade. The busiest single year in the file is 1994, with 291 events. By species, walleye leads with 4,404 events, ahead of rainbow trout at 3,408 and brook trout at 1,643.

3. Find the trap

There are 594 waterbody IDs but only 576 names, because 23 names belong to more than one lake or river. Gull Lake alone covers four separate waterbodies. A student who groups by name merges different lakes; grouping by WATERBODY_ID keeps them apart.

What the counts do not say

An event is one stocking, of any size, so more events in the 1990s does not mean more fish. We never add up the QUANTITY column, which the province describes as an approximate number of fish. And 2,930 events have no exact date, only a year, which is why the script groups by YEAR rather than DATE.

A good follow-up question for a Grade 11 or 12 student: the SIZE column holds 13 different labels, from Fry and Fingerling to 18+ cm, and includes 70 rows marked 0 and 3 marked SEE COMMENTS. How should those be cleaned before anyone charts fish size over time? Manitoba keeps its clocks still from November 2026

On 17 September 2026 the Manitoba government announced permanent daylight saving time. For families booking online classes, that makes the arithmetic easier, not harder.

What changed

The release said Manitobans' clocks will not revert to standard time on Nov. 1. The Official Time Amendment Act of 2023 already defines Manitoba Standard Time as five hours behind UTC, so the province simply stays at UTC-5 all year.

Winnipeg and Toronto

In winter Winnipeg and Toronto now show the same time. From 14 March 2027, when Ontario moves forward, Toronto is an hour ahead again. Every class time we send names Manitoba time, so no one has to work this out.

Manitoba phone numbers

Numbers with the 204, 431 and 584 area codes all work in our form. Type the ten digits; the +1 country code is already set.

There is a coding lesson inside the news. A reminder program that stores Winnipeg as a fixed offset will be right this winter by luck, while one that relies on an out-of-date time zone database will move the class an hour on 1 November 2026. Our students compare both against the America/Winnipeg zone and see why software teams update time zone data instead of hard-coding it.

Coding and AI

Why hand-tracing still matters when AI can write the sort

Manitoba's framework asks students to confirm an algorithm's logic by hand. That habit is the best protection against confident, wrong AI output.

An AI assistant will write a quicksort in seconds. It will not tell a student whether the version it wrote handles an empty list, or why grouping fish records by lake name quietly merges four different Gull Lakes. Those checks come from tracing code line by line, which is exactly what the 2004 framework asks for and what we practise in every class.

So students write the first version themselves, test it on small cases they worked out on paper, and only then compare it with what a tool suggests. Teenagers use AI tools within each tool's own age limits, and with a parent's consent wherever a tool asks for it. For children under 13 we skip the tools and build tiny guessing programs that show how AI makes a prediction.

A course with no prescribed language is a course about thinking, and thinking is the part no tool can do for the student. The longer argument is in is coding still worth learning in 2026.

FAQ

Manitoba coding questions

The questions Manitoba parents and students ask us before starting.

Do you have coding classes for Manitoba students?

Yes. Every lesson is live on video with a teacher. There is no Manitoba classroom to visit; students log in from home and learn either in a shared class or with a teacher of their own.

Is computer science taught in Manitoba high schools?

Manitoba has a framework for Senior 2 (20S), Senior 3 (30S) and Senior 4 (40S) Computer Science, and schools may offer one, two or three of these full-credit courses. Whether a given school offers them is the school's choice.

Which programming language do Manitoba computer science courses use?

The framework does not set one. The teacher and students decide which modern language to learn, and the 40S outline asks students to learn a new language on their own as well.

Will your classes give my child a Manitoba credit?

No. We do not grant Manitoba credits. Credits for 20S, 30S and 40S courses are earned at school; our classes run alongside them.

When are the Grade 12 Pre-Calculus provincial tests?

In 2026 to 2027 the department lists Thursday 28 January 2027 for semester 1 and Tuesday 15 June 2027 for semester 2. The tests count for 20 per cent of the final grade.

Is Manitoba changing its clocks in November 2026?

No. The Manitoba government announced that clocks will not go back on 1 November 2026, so Manitoba stays at UTC-5 all year. We confirm every class in Manitoba time.

What time are classes for Manitoba families?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays.

Can a Manitoba student enter the Canadian Computing Competition?

Yes, through a school that orders it. The 2027 CCC is on Wednesday 17 February 2027, according to CEMC, and each student picks Junior or Senior by programming skill, not by grade.

Do Manitoba phone numbers work in your form?

Yes. Numbers with the 204, 431 and 584 area codes work. Enter all ten digits without the leading 1.

How young can a child start?

Around age 6, in Grade 1, with block coding. Most children move to typed Python between Grades 4 and 6, and adults can start at any point.

Where can I see the fees?

In the fees block on this page: the free first lesson, the monthly group fee and the monthly private fee, each with its currency. You pay nothing until after the free lesson.
