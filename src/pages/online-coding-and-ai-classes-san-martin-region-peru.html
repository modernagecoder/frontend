Online coding and AI classes in the San Martín region

Moyobamba is the capital, Tarapoto is the busiest town, and families from Rioja and Nueva Cajamarca down to Juanjuí and Tocache live along the same valleys. San Martín has been publishing good news about learning and connectivity. Here we set out the census picture, those results, what a home needs for a live class, and a mapping lesson built on the region's list of waterfalls, caves and viewpoints.

Tarapoto, Moyobamba and the valley towns

Town figures for 2025 are not out yet. INEI's 2017 count gave the capital, Moyobamba, 50,073 people. Its 2017 directory of populated centres lists Tarapoto at 74,804, with La Banda de Shilcayo at 36,201 and Morales at 30,708 next door.

Further along the valleys the same directory lists Juanjuí 28,453, Nueva Cajamarca 25,812, Rioja 22,080 and Tocache 15,752. Tarapoto, Morales and La Banda touch one another, but INEI lists them separately and so do we.

Young people in the count

The census reached 949,469 people in San Martín in person. Of them, 107,708 were aged 6 to 11 and 114,383 were 12 to 17, the primaria and secundaria years.

Whether that learner lives on a cacao farm near Juanjuí or a street in Tarapoto, the class is the same: live, over video, with a mentor who knows them.

School

The regional education office reports clear gains in reading and maths, and the region has approved a large ICT classrooms project. Programming, however, is still not a school subject anywhere in Peru.

"Conectando y Educando"

In January 2026 San Martín's regional council prioritised an investment project called "Conectando y Educando". The DRE San Martín describes it as covering 944 classrooms in 57 public schools, across 37 districts in all ten provinces, for more than 24 thousand pupils.

The plan includes ICT classrooms, technology equipment, digital platforms and teacher training. When it is delivered, those pupils will have better machines. Learning to program on them is a separate step.

What the curriculum asks

Peru's national curriculum, the CNEB, has one technology competency, number 28, and makes it transversal, so every area shares it. The regular secundaria timetable of 35 hours sets aside four for Matemática and none for programming.

Grades follow Law 28044, six years of primaria and five of secundaria, with MINEDU fixing each child's grade by age on 31 March.

Do San Martín homes have the internet and computers for live classes?

Internet, increasingly yes; computers, still often no. INEI's household survey shows home internet in San Martín rising from under one home in ten in 2015 to over half in 2025.

The trend

Between 2015 and 2025 the share of San Martín homes with internet rose from 8.8 to 52.6 per cent, close to the national 56.8. Internet use among people aged six and over reached 78.4 per cent.

Computer ownership grew more slowly, to about one home in four. For coding that is the real constraint, because a learner over nine needs to type, run and correct programs.

What families do about it

Young children can start block coding on a tablet. Older learners use a shared family laptop, a relative's computer, or a parent's office machine outside working hours.

The free first class tests sound, screen sharing and the connection, so that nothing is paid before we know it works.

English

Are classes given in Spanish or English?

English only. Mentors teach from India in live video lessons, go at the learner's speed, and keep the lesson in English throughout.

San Martín's English, measured

On EF's English Proficiency Index 2025 Peru scores 519 and ranks 52nd of 123 countries. The same page lists San Martín at 460, in EF's low band and below the national score.

So most San Martín children are early English learners, and we plan lessons on that basis rather than hoping for the best.

How lessons stay clear

One idea per sentence, the same word for the same thing each week, and a live code example for every new word. Mentors check understanding by asking the learner to predict what the program will do.

Spanish answers are fine; the mentor responds with the English phrase and continues. Coding words such as if, while and print become familiar within weeks.

A San Martín data project

Where are San Martín's waterfalls and caves on the map?

The regional government publishes an inventory of 308 tourist resources with latitude and longitude for each. Before drawing them on a map, a careful programmer checks that every point could actually be in San Martín.

The dataset

The Gobierno Regional de San Martín shares "Inventario de recursos turísticos de la región San Martín" through Peru's open data portal under ODC-By, the attribution licence: anyone, a company included, may reuse it provided the regional government is named. Its data is dated 21 June 2026 and the page was last updated on 16 July 2026.

We saved the CSV as recursos_turisticos_san_martin.csv on 9 November 2026. Of its 308 resources, 206 are natural sites, among them 56 rows typed CATARATAS and 25 typed CASCADAS: waterfalls, under two subtype names. Each row has a name, a category, a district and two coordinates.

The question

Where is the centre of San Martín's tourism, on average? A first program simply averages all the latitudes and all the longitudes.

Coordinates in Peru are south of the equator and west of Greenwich, so both numbers should be negative: latitude around minus six or seven, longitude around minus seventy-six or seventy-seven.

The answer looks believable, which is exactly the danger. Yet not one of the 301 points that later pass our check lies as far east as minus 75.332: the average sits outside the very places it is meant to summarise. One bad value can drag an average a long way, and this file has more than one.

Version 2 draws a loose box of our own around the region, deliberately generous, and keeps only points inside it. For a point outside the box, it tries two repairs, without saving them: first make both numbers negative, in case a minus sign was lost; then swap latitude and longitude, in case they were typed in the wrong columns. If neither repair puts the point in the box, the point is reported as unexplained.

Seven of 308 points were off. Two had lost a minus sign, two had latitude and longitude swapped (one of those had also lost its signs), and three could not be explained. Those three are all in the district of Huicungo, with longitudes near minus 82.9, which is far out in the Pacific Ocean, nowhere near San Martín. They might share a typing slip, but the file does not tell us which digit is wrong, so we leave them out rather than invent a fix.

Leaving seven points out moved the average longitude from minus 75.332 to minus 76.72, a shift of 1.388 degrees. At this latitude one degree of longitude is about 110 kilometres, so the first answer was roughly 150 kilometres too far east (our calculation). A map drawn from Version 1 would have put the region's tourist heart in the wrong place.

The box, the repairs we test and the classification are ours; the regional government has not reviewed them. Our box is a rough rectangle, not the region's real border, so a point inside it is plausible, not proven.

  1. Under 8, on squared paper. Draw a box and five dots inside it. Then put one dot far outside and ask: if we wanted the middle of the dots, what would that lonely dot do?
  2. Block coding, around 8 to 10. On Scratch's stage, move a sprite to five x and y positions you choose, then type one with the minus sign missing and watch where it goes.
  3. Python beginners, 10 to 12. Execute both versions and work out in your own words why the averages differ.
  4. Ages 13 and over. Count the waterfalls under CATARATAS and CASCADAS together, then list which provinces have the most. Explain why you had to merge two labels.
  5. Going further. Replace our box with a real check: is each point closer to the centre of its own district than to the centre of any other? Write down what extra data you would need.

Olympiads

Can San Martín pupils compete in the ONEM?

Yes. Entry runs through each colegio, which hosts the opening round. We are a private tutor and have no tie to MINEDU, the DRE San Martín or the olympiad.

ONEM 2026

On Wednesday 8 July 2026 colegios across Peru held the school round of the ONEM, with pupils grouped by year into three levels. According to El Peruano, more than 1.7 million pupils sat the 2026 edition.

We train olympiad thinking with problems we write ourselves, and never reproduce official ones.

A practice problem of ours

Four map points are (2, 3), (4, 5), (6, 7) and (8, 85). What is their average, and what would it be without the last point? (Ours. Answer: (5, 25) with it, and (4, 5) without it. One faulty point moved the second coordinate from 5 to 25.)

It is the map project in miniature, and a question about averages that every olympiad student should be able to answer quickly.

When your family says. San Martín keeps Peru's clock, and your mentor arranges the Indian working day to fit.

The tz database records America/Lima at UTC minus five with no summer time since 1994; INACAL is in charge of Peru's official hour. India runs five and a half hours ahead of UTC, which leaves Tarapoto is ten and a half hours behind India every day of the year.

With no clock changes on either side, the hour you book stays put through the year. Many families start during the long summer break from January to March and keep the same time when school returns.

Coding and AI classes in San Martín: questions and answers

Are the classes in Spanish?

No. Our classes are always live, online and taught only in English. Mentors talk clearly and patiently, adapt to each learner's speed and accent, and turn a Spanish answer into English for the learner.

Can my child join from Tarapoto, Moyobamba or a smaller town?

Yes. Everything happens over live video, so Tarapoto, Moyobamba, Rioja, Juanjuí, Tocache or a village in Lamas all work the same, given a connection and a suitable device.

What time are classes?

Any day, any time, flexible: choose a batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at the hour that suits your family. India is ten and a half hours ahead, so 10:00 in Tarapoto is 20:30 for the mentor.

Does school in San Martín teach coding?

Not as a subject. The national curriculum spreads digital skills across all areas. San Martín's "Conectando y Educando" project will equip 944 classrooms with ICT, but programming lessons are not part of the regular timetable.

Is a phone enough?

For a young child doing blocks, a tablet can work. From about nine a learner needs a laptop or desktop to type and test code. The free class checks your device.

How young can a child start?

Six. Young children start with blocks and puzzles; most move to typed Python at nine or ten, and the mentor judges readiness in the free class.

Can adults in San Martín learn with you?

Yes, in adult groups or one to one. Many start with websites, Python or AI tools that help with work in tourism, farming businesses or offices.

Do you prepare pupils for the ONEM?

We build olympiad reasoning with our own problems, never official papers. Pupils enter through their colegio. We have no link with MINEDU, the DRE San Martín or the ONEM.

How do fees work?

A monthly fee, paid by card in US dollars, converted into soles by your bank. Visitors from India see rupees. The first class is free and no card is asked for.

Do learners get a certificate?

Where the course includes one, learners receive our own Modern Age Coders certificate of completion. It is not a Peruvian qualification, and neither MINEDU, the DRE San Martín nor any school issues or endorses it.
