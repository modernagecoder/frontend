The short answer

A complete AI coding course in 2026 teaches five modules: enough programming fundamentals to read what models write, the craft of directing models precisely, verification habits that catch confident errors, testing and shipping so projects reach real users, and a supervised introduction to coding agents. Courses that teach only prompting produce students who can start anything and finish nothing. Modern Age Coders runs this syllabus live in three age tracks, teens through working professionals, in batches of five to eight at USD 100 a month or one to one at USD 150, with the first class free and the first shipped project inside month one.

The syllabus

## Five modules, and why the skipped ones are the valuable ones

Each module answers a failure we see in students arriving from prompt-only courses. The order matters less than the coverage; the coverage is not optional.

| Module | What it teaches | The failure it prevents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. The floor | Python and web fundamentals, learned by building | Code you cannot read is code you cannot own |
| 2. Direction | Specs, constraints, and precise change requests | Vague prompts compound into unfixable projects |
| 3. Verification | Reading diffs, testing deliberately, spotting confident nonsense | Shipping whatever ran once on your machine |
| 4. Shipping | Version control, deploys, keys kept secret, real users | A folder of demos instead of a track record |
| 5. Agents | Terminal coding agents, supervised, 18 plus tools honestly gated | Meeting agents unprepared, or never |

Module three deserves its extra sentence, because it is the one nobody advertises. Models produce confident, plausible, occasionally wrong code, and the difference between a professional and a liability is the reflex of checking. We teach it the way it sticks: every week includes one exercise where something the model produced is subtly wrong, and finding it is the assignment. Students who have hunted planted flaws for a term read everything differently, including their own work.

Module five is honestly gated. The serious agent tools set their floors at adulthood, so teens meet agents through demonstrations and the teen-permitted subset, and the full terminal workflow waits for the college and professional tracks. The deeper material on what agents are is on [agentic coding](/agentic-coding-classes), and the teen-specific path is on [AI agents for teens](/ai-agents-for-teens).

Why the syllabus changed

## The same course, taught in 2019 and in 2026

Both columns are twelve weeks of a beginner course. Watch where the hours moved, because the movement is the whole story of this field.

***BEGINNER COURSE, 2019**where the twelve weeks went*

```
Weeks 1-3   Syntax. Loops, types,
            brackets, drills.
Weeks 4-5   More syntax. Functions,
            classes, memorised methods.
Week 6      Environment setup rescue
            week (unofficial, always).
Weeks 7-10  Guided project from
            worksheets, everyone
            builds the same thing.
Weeks 11-12 Polish and present.

# Typing was expensive, so typing
# got the hours. Reasonable then.
```

Nothing was wrong with this in its decade. The scarce skill was producing correct syntax, so the course maximised syntax production.

***THE SAME TWELVE WEEKS, 2026**where they go now*

```
Week 1      First build shipped, day
            one. Syntax met in the wild.
Weeks 2-4   Reading. Tracing what the
            model wrote, finding the
            planted flaw of the week.
Weeks 5-7   Direction. Specs, edge
            cases, change requests
            that come back right.
Weeks 8-10  A chosen project with a
            real user, versioned, live.
Weeks 11-12 Break week: attack your own
            app. Then defend and demo.

# Judgement is now the scarce skill,
# so judgement gets the hours.
```

The 2026 column produces a student who ships in week one and spends the term learning to be right about what shipped. That inversion is this whole course.

A fair question is whether the 2019 skills died. They did not; they moved into module one and into the weekly unassisted work, because reading fluency still comes from writing. What died is the idea that a beginner must earn months of syntax before touching anything real. The modern course runs both currents at once, and the students who thrive are the ones taught to switch between them on purpose.

Outcomes

## What a graduate of this course can actually do

Not "understands AI concepts". Abilities, checkable by anyone, with no goodwill required.

### Ship a real tool in a weekend

Given a concrete problem, a graduate scopes it, directs a model through the build, and puts a working version at a live link inside two days, because they have done exactly this at least six times during the course.

### Read code they did not write

Handed an unfamiliar file, they can say where the data lives, what runs on each action, and which part to touch for a given change. This is the module-one floor, and it is tested cold, without the model open.

### Catch a confident error

Shown output that runs but is wrong, they find it, because a term of planted-flaw hunting built the reflex of distrust. This single ability separates our graduates most visibly from prompt-course graduates.

### Leave a paper trail

Their projects live in version control with honest histories, so a reviewer can watch the work happen over time. Nothing about their portfolio requires taking their word for anything.

### Explain every decision out loud

Demo day is a defence, not a slideshow: batchmates ask why, and the builder answers cold. Graduates interview noticeably better for having practised being questioned about their own work.

### Know what they do not know yet

Graduates leave with an honest map: what they can build alone, what needs the model, what needs a senior. That calibration is rarer than any framework knowledge and hiring managers name it unprompted.

The rhythm

## What a week of the course actually contains

### The live session

Ninety minutes, five to eight students, one teacher who stays with the batch for the whole arc. The shape rarely varies: fifteen minutes reviewing what everyone built since last time, an hour of new work on the week's module, and a closing stretch where each student states, out loud, exactly what they will ship before the next session. Saying it aloud sounds like a small thing; it roughly doubles the completion rate, and we have watched enough batches to say that with a straight face.

### The days between

Two to three hours of build work, split deliberately: most of it AI-assisted, on the week's project, and one exercise done with every assistant closed. The teacher reads what comes back before the next session, not to grade it but to choose what that student meets next, a planted flaw, a harder spec, or a week of consolidation. This per-student steering is the thing a recorded course structurally cannot do, and it is most of what the fee buys.

Questions about the course

## What enquirers ask before enrolling

### What is the difference between an AI coding course and a vibe coding course?

Vibe coding names the conversational build loop; AI coding is the broader discipline around it: fundamentals, verification, testing, shipping and the beginnings of agent tools. Our vibe coding tracks are the project-led doorway, and this page describes the full house behind the door. In practice the tracks overlap heavily and a student moves between them based on goals rather than labels.

### Which track fits me or my child?

By age first, because tool terms decide what is even allowed: 13 to 18 in the teen track with parental consent, university students and career changers in the college track, and working professionals in the professionals track, which meets in evening and weekend slots. Within an age band, the free class settles level: it is a working session and doubles as an honest placement.

### Do I need any coding background to join?

No. Module one exists precisely because the course assumes nothing: enough Python and web literacy to read what models produce is built from zero in the early weeks. Students who already code place straight into the later modules after the free class, so nobody pays to resit what they know.

### Is there a certificate, and does it mean anything?

There is a certificate on completion, and we are candid about what certifies whom: the certificate records that you finished; the portfolio of shipped projects is what actually convinces schools, employers or clients. The course is structured so you leave with both, and if you ever have to choose which to lead with, lead with the projects.

### Will this help me get or keep a job?

For adults, the honest framing is that AI-assisted development is rapidly becoming the default way software gets written, and fluency in it is turning from advantage into expectation. This course will not by itself get anyone hired; it makes you the candidate who ships faster and can explain every line, and it removes the quiet fear of being the person in the team who cannot work the new way.

### How long does the course take?

Each module is roughly a month at one live session a week plus build work, so the full five-module arc is about five to six months. Most students see the first shipped project inside month one, because module one is taught through building rather than before it. Faster is possible one to one, where the pace follows the student instead of the batch.

### Which AI tools does the course use, and must I pay for them?

Modules one to four run entirely on tools with capable free tiers, chosen from what each student's age and country permit under the providers' terms. Module five introduces professional coding agents, and where a specific agent tool requires a paid subscription, that requirement is stated on the relevant course page before you enrol, never discovered afterwards.

### Group or one to one for this course?

Groups of five to eight work well for the teen and college tracks, where watching a batchmate's project fail differently from yours is half the education. Professionals with a deadline, or anyone whose schedule fights fixed slots, tend to choose one to one. Both formats cover the same five modules; the difference is pace and scheduling, not syllabus.

### What does the AI coding course cost?

USD 100 a month for a group batch and USD 150 a month for one to one, in US dollars, identical across every country we serve outside India. Billing is monthly with no joining fee, so a five-module arc costs five to six group months and you can stop at any month boundary if life intervenes.

### What happens after I enquire?

A mentor calls to book the free class, which for this course is a compressed module-two session: you will direct a model at a small build, read what it wrote with the teacher, and catch one planted flaw yourself. That last moment is the course's whole argument made in miniature, and it is a better basis for deciding than anything we could write here.
