Khan Academy math vs a live tutor

Khan Academy is one of the most generous resources in education: a large, free math library that a child can use at any hour. Khan Academy now also offers Khanmigo, a paid AI tutor built to guide rather than hand out answers. So why would a family pay for a live teacher? Sometimes they should not. Sometimes the free library is enough, or the AI tutor fills the gap. And sometimes a child needs a person who listens to how they think. This page lays out what Khan Academy says it offers, what a live tutor offers, and how to tell which your child needs.

Hearing reasoning

What can a live tutor catch that self-paced practice might miss?

A wrong rule that sometimes gives right answers. Self-paced exercises check the answer; a teacher listening to a child explain can find the rule behind it. The examples below are ours.

Four common math misconceptions and how a teacher hears them (our examples)
ProblemWrong answerThe hidden ruleRight answer and the fix
1/2 + 1/32/5Add the tops, add the bottoms5/6. Ask: can the sum be smaller than 1/2?
0.3 x 0.20.6Multiply digits, keep one decimal place0.06. Three tenths of two tenths is six hundredths.
52 - 2735Always take the smaller digit from the bigger25. Regroup a ten: 12 - 7 = 5, 4 - 2 = 2.
Area of a 4 by 6 rectangle20Adding sides (that is the perimeter)24 square units; perimeter is 20 units.

Why the answer alone is not enough

A child who uses the "smaller from bigger" subtraction rule gets 58 - 23 right and 52 - 27 wrong. Over a mixed set they may score well enough to move on, carrying the wrong rule into the next unit.

Our teachers ask "how did you get that?" on right answers as well as wrong ones. That one question finds most hidden rules within a lesson or two.

Where an AI tutor fits

Khan Academy says Khanmigo guides learners to the answer with questions rather than giving it, which is the same instinct. Its usefulness depends on the child asking for help and describing what they did.

A live teacher adds the moments a child does not ask: the hesitation, the erased answer, the quick guess. Both approaches can work together.

When each fits

When is Khan Academy enough, and when does a child need a live tutor?

There is no single answer. These are the patterns we see, offered as general guidance from our teaching rather than statistics.

Khan Academy alone may suit

A self-starting child who works steadily, can explain their answers and mainly needs practice or a refresher on one topic.

Adding Khanmigo may suit

A child who uses Khan Academy happily but gets stuck between sessions and is comfortable asking an AI for hints, with a parent watching the chat history.

A live tutor may suit

A child who drifts without a fixed time, guesses rather than explains, or has a hidden rule that keeps returning in new topics.

A three-question test for parents (ours)
Ask your childA reassuring answerA sign to look closer
"Explain how you got this answer."A clear, step-by-step reasonShrugging, or "the video said so"
"Can you do one like it without the hint?"Yes, at a similar speedOnly after watching again
"How many days did you practise this week?"Most days, without remindersOnly when told, or not at all

If two or three answers fall in the right-hand column for a few weeks running, a person at a set time will probably help more than another app. If they fall in the middle column, Khan Academy is doing its job.

Using both

Can a child use Khan Academy and a live tutor together?

Yes, and many of our students do. The free library is ideal for practice between classes; the teacher sets direction and checks the reasoning.

  1. Tell the teacher what your child does on Khan Academy. We can point practice at the same topic we are teaching.
  2. Keep the live class for explaining. The teacher spends class time on reasoning and new ideas, not on drills the library already offers.
  3. Use Khan Academy for repetition. Short practice on most days helps fluency between classes.
  4. Bring the misses to class. Two or three wrong answers from the week make the most useful agenda a teacher can have.
  5. Review after a month. If your child is explaining confidently and practising alone, it may be time to step the live classes down.

If you already use Khanmigo, keep it. Ask your child to show the teacher a chat where they got stuck; it is an excellent window into how they think. For younger children, Khan Academy requires a parent to sign up for Khanmigo, which is a sensible rule we would follow for any AI tool.

StageWhat it looks like
1. PromptedPractises only when reminded; skips hard items
2. PractisingWorks steadily but cannot always explain answers
3. ExplainingCan say why an answer is right
4. Self-directedPicks topics, notices own mistakes, asks good questions

For parents

Khan Academy alone works best from stage 3. A live teacher helps most at stages 1 and 2.

Moving up a stage matters more than moving up a grade level.

For students

If you get an answer right, try explaining it out loud. If you cannot, you are not finished.

Wrong answers are clues. Write them down.

Questions from families

Khan Academy math vs a tutor: common questions

Is Khan Academy enough to learn math?

For a self-motivated child who can explain their answers, it often is. Khan Academy says its content library will always remain free. Children who drift, guess or carry hidden wrong rules usually benefit from a live teacher as well.

Does Khan Academy have free math tutoring?

The Khan Academy library of videos, exercises and articles is free. Khanmigo, its AI tutor, requires a monthly or annual payment from an adult in the United States.

What is Khanmigo?

Khan Academy describes Khanmigo as an AI-powered personal tutor and teaching assistant. It is designed to guide learners to find answers themselves rather than give them, and it works inside Khan Academy's content.

Can my child under 18 use Khanmigo?

Khan Academy says a parent or guardian aged 18 or older must sign up and add the child. A parent account can enable up to 10 children under 18 and can view chat history and safety alerts.

Is an AI math tutor as good as a live tutor?

They do different jobs. An AI tutor is available any time and responds when asked. A live teacher works at a set time and can notice how a child reasons even when the child does not ask. Khan Academy itself notes that language models have had difficulty with math.

Can my child use Khan Academy and your classes together?

Yes. Many families keep Khan Academy for daily practice and use live classes for explanation and direction. Tell the teacher what your child practises so the two line up.

How do I know if my child needs a tutor?

Ask your child to explain an answer, redo a problem without a hint, and say how often they practised this week. Struggling with two or three of these for several weeks suggests a live teacher would help.

How much does Khanmigo cost?

Khan Academy publishes current Khanmigo terms on its own site, so please check there; we never repeat another organization's prices. Our own monthly fees appear in the fees section above.

When are your live math classes?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays.

Are you connected with Khan Academy?

No. Modern Age Coders is an independent online school, not affiliated with Khan Academy. Khan Academy and Khanmigo are its trademarks.
