# For a quarter of this country, online is not the convenient option. It is the only one.

> Modern Age Coders runs a live online twelve-month Python and AI track for learners anywhere in The Bahamas, aged six to sixty-seven. Because the country keeps a single time zone and our teachers are in India, the main sessions are weekend mornings in Bahamian local time, which works identically from Nassau, Grand Bahama or Long Island. Students write real Python, handle real data, train and measure models, and publish on GitHub, with a written update to the parent each month. Group teaching is USD 100 a month, private teaching USD 150, and the first class is free.

## An archipelago is the wrong shape for a building

The Bahamas is an archipelago, and any provider with a physical classroom has to put it somewhere. Wherever they put it, the great majority of Bahamian families are the wrong side of a boat or a flight from it.

Live online teaching is the only format that is genuinely indifferent to this. A student in Freeport, Marsh Harbour, Governor's Harbour, George Town or Nassau sits in exactly the same class, with the same teacher, at the same time, at the same price. That is not a marketing claim about reach; it is a property of the delivery method.

It matters more here than in most countries. In a compact market, an online programme competes with a drivable centre. In an archipelago, for most of the population, there is no drivable centre to compete with.

## Where the country already is

GDP per capita was around USD 39,455 in 2024 and electricity access is effectively universal, which are the two preconditions for this working at all. What follows from wealth plus connectivity is not automatic though: money buys devices, and devices without a reason to use them produce consumers rather than builders.

The Department of Labour's employment portal names AI programmers, coding specialists, automation engineers, cybersecurity specialists and data analysts among the emerging occupations tied to automation. That is a public statement that the jobs are coming. It is not a statement that anyone has built the pipeline for them, and for school-age children in particular the pipeline is thin.

## What we teach, in the order it has to come

| Rung | What the student does | What it is not |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Python typed by hand, in a file, with real errors | Dragging blocks, or copying a tutorial |
| 2 | Messy data, cleaned by them, charted by them | A tidy file the teacher prepared |
| 3 | A model with a stated error and a baseline it beat | An accuracy figure with nothing to compare it to |
| 4 | An AI agent that logs every decision it makes | A chatbot with a friendly voice |
| 5 | The work published, with its limits written down | A certificate, or a folder on a laptop |

The order is a dependency chain rather than a preference. A student who has not handled real data has no way to check what a model told them, and a student who has never measured an error has no instinct for the difference between confident output and correct output.

## Timing, honestly

The Bahamas is nine hours behind India in summer, so Saturday mornings Bahamian time are the strong slots and weekday evenings Bahamian time are the small hours in India. We teach Saturday 08:00 and 10:00 for groups, and 07:00 on weekdays one to one for students who prefer to work before school. Weekday afternoons and evenings Bahamian time we decline, and we say so on every page rather than take the booking and let the quality drift.
