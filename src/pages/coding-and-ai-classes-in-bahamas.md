> A 12-month Python and AI track for students anywhere in The Bahamas, from Nassau to the Family Islands. Saturday mornings, real projects, first class free.

In short

Modern Age Coders runs a live online twelve-month Python and AI track for learners anywhere in The Bahamas, aged six to sixty-seven. Because the country keeps a single time zone and our teachers are in India, the main sessions are weekend mornings in Bahamian local time, which works identically from Nassau, Grand Bahama or Long Island. Students write real Python, handle real data, train and measure models, and publish on GitHub, with a written update to the parent each month. Group teaching is USD 100 a month, private teaching USD 150, and the first class is free.

The shape of the country

## Three quarters of the Bahamas is on one island. The rest is on a boat.

That sentence is the whole argument of this page, and it is the reason an online programme is not merely convenient here but structurally different from the alternative.

The 2022 census recorded **296,522 people on New Providence, 74.26 per cent** of the national population, and that share had grown 4.17 percentage points since 2010. The remaining quarter or so is distributed across the Family Islands, which is a phrase that hides an enormous amount of water.

For a family in Nassau, choosing between a local class and an online one is a choice about traffic. For a family on Eleuthera, Abaco, Exuma or Long Island it is a choice about whether the thing exists at all within a day's travel. Those are not the same decision and we should not pretend the same sales pitch fits both.

The interesting part is that the appetite outside Nassau is not hypothetical. When the government launched **Upskill Bahamas** in November 2025, of the ten thousand Bahamians who registered in the first weeks, **more than two thousand were from the Family Islands**. That is a fifth of national uptake coming from the places with the least in-person provision, and it happened because the delivery mechanism did not care where anybody lived.

The same programme is open only to those aged sixteen and over, which is covered in full on [the Nassau page](/coding-and-ai-classes-in-nassau). A Family Island household with a twelve year old is therefore in the sharpest version of the national gap: high awareness, no local option, and no eligibility either.

What makes delivery work here

## A wealthy, wired, English-speaking country with a provision gap

Every constraint that usually stops online teaching in a small market is already solved in the Bahamas, which is exactly why the remaining problem is worth naming precisely.

### Money is not the obstacle

GDP per head was US$39,455 in 2024, among the highest in the region. A family here is not being asked to choose between this and something essential.

### Power is not the obstacle

Access to electricity is recorded at 100 per cent. That sounds trivial until you have tried to run a weekly video class in a market where it is not true.

### Connectivity is not the obstacle

Internet penetration was 94.8 per cent at the start of 2025, with 381,000 users. For most households the equipment for this is already in the house.

### Language is not the obstacle

The Bahamas is English-speaking and so is this subject: the libraries, the documentation and the error messages are all in English, so nothing gets translated twice.

### Awareness is not the obstacle

The state has spent a year telling the country that AI and digital skills matter, and ten thousand adults acted on it within weeks of the platform opening.

### Provision for under-sixteens is

That is the gap, and it is the only one on this list. It is what a continuous weekly programme with a named teacher and a written record is for.

Put those together and the diagnosis is unusual. This is not a market where families cannot afford specialist teaching, or cannot receive it, or would need it translated. It is a market where a continuous, individually tracked, weekly programme for under-sixteens has simply not been the thing on offer. Volunteer and nonprofit efforts have done real work in Bahamian schools, and we say that without qualification; a robotics kit in a classroom is a genuine good. What a grant-funded volunteer programme cannot easily promise is the same teacher every week for a year, a written record per student, and a portfolio with twelve months of commits in it. Those are the things a paid programme should be judged on, and they are what we sell.

Timing, wherever you are in the country

## One time zone, and a Saturday morning that works from every island

The Bahamas runs a single time zone nationwide, so the scheduling answer is the same on Grand Bahama as it is in Nassau. Our teachers are in India, nine and a half to ten and a half hours ahead depending on the season, which rules out weekday after-school teaching entirely and makes weekend mornings the natural slot.

Eight o'clock on a Saturday morning in the Bahamas is half past six in the evening for the teacher. That is a better hour for both ends than almost any compromise a European or Asian provider can offer a Caribbean family, and it is the reason the offer is shaped the way it is rather than being an apology for it.

The country changes its clocks in March and November and India never does, so the gap moves twice a year. Your slot does not: it stays fixed in Bahamian local time and the shift is absorbed at our end. The full arithmetic, including the hours we will not teach, is set out on [the Nassau page](/coding-and-ai-classes-in-nassau).

The one genuine risk for a Family Island household is bandwidth rather than distance, and the free first class exists partly to test it. If a ninety-minute video call with screen sharing struggles at your house, we will say so rather than take a year's booking that will be miserable by week six.

What a Bahamian student ends up building

## The projects that get finished are the local ones

We do not hand students a dataset. They choose something they can check the answer to, and in an archipelago that turns out to be an advantage rather than a limitation.

The single strongest predictor of a student project being finished rather than abandoned is whether the learner can independently tell that their answer is wrong. A model trained on somebody else's tidy download fails that test: when it produces nonsense the student has no way to know. A model built on something they measured themselves passes it immediately.

Students here have produced work on ferry and flight schedules, on rainfall they recorded, on the timing of their own household electricity use, and on how long a walk takes at different times of day. None of those is impressive as a topic. All of them are checkable, which is the only property that matters in the first year.

There is a rule we apply before any of the technical criteria, and it is about people rather than engineering. Projects must not use data about identifiable individuals who have not agreed to it, must not put personal information into a public repository, and must not scrape anything a site has asked not to be scraped. Students find those constraints more interesting than they expect once they realise professionals argue about the same questions.

What finished looks like, as five testable properties, is on [the portfolio page](/github-portfolio-for-students-bahamas), and how a fixed external deadline gets a project over the line is on [the competitions page](/coding-competitions-for-students-bahamas).

## What Bahamian families ask first

### We are on a Family Island. Is the class any different?

Not in any respect. The country keeps one time zone, so the Saturday morning slot is the same hour for you as for a family in Nassau, and the group a learner joins is assembled on demonstrated ability from an intake across twenty-five countries rather than by location. The only thing worth checking is your connection, and the free first class is partly there to do exactly that.

### How reliable does our internet need to be?

It needs to carry ninety minutes of video with screen sharing, twice a month at minimum and weekly ideally. Nationally, penetration was 94.8 per cent at the start of 2025 and electricity access is recorded at 100 per cent, so for most households this is already in place. If it struggles during the free session we will tell you plainly rather than take a year's booking that will fail by week six.

### Why weekend mornings rather than after school?

Because our teachers are in India, which is nine and a half to ten and a half hours ahead of the Bahamas depending on the season. Four in the afternoon here is half past two in the morning there, and we will not staff that. Eight on a Saturday morning here is half past six in the evening for the teacher, which is a good hour at both ends rather than a compromise at one.

### How much does it cost?

A group batch of five to eight students is USD 100 a month and one to one teaching is USD 150 a month, billed in US dollars, the same flat figures everywhere outside India. There is no enrolment fee, no annual contract, and the first class is free without a card.

### Is this connected to Upskill Bahamas?

No, and it does not overlap with it. Upskill Bahamas is a free government platform launched in November 2025 and open to Bahamians aged sixteen and over. If your child is sixteen or older, use it: it is free and it carries courses from institutions including Harvard, Oxford and Columbia. This is a paid weekly programme for ages six to sixty-seven, and it exists largely because nothing in that platform is available to a younger child.

### Do you have anywhere in the Bahamas we could visit?

No premises anywhere in the country and no representative. Every session is a live video call taught from India, and the telephone number on this page rings in India rather than locally. What you supply is a computer with a keyboard, a connection that holds video, audio the teacher can hear and a browser from the last few years.

### What about the volunteer and school robotics programmes here?

They do real and valuable work and we are not going to argue against them. A robotics kit in a Bahamian classroom is a genuine good, and a child who has used one usually arrives with better instincts than one who has not. What a grant-funded volunteer effort cannot easily promise is the same teacher every week for twelve months, a written record for each student, and a repository with a year of dated commits. That is a different product rather than a better version of the same one.

### Can adults join, or is this only for school students?

Ages six to sixty-seven, and adults are a routine part of the intake. The usual adult pattern here is somebody wanting to automate work they currently do by hand, which suits one to one because the syllabus can bend entirely around the task and usually finishes inside a few months rather than a year.

### Which island do most of your Bahamian students come from?

New Providence, which is unsurprising given that three quarters of the country lives there. What we would point out is that when the national upskilling platform opened, more than two thousand of the first ten thousand registrations came from the Family Islands, which suggests the demand outside Nassau is considerably larger than the local provision would imply.

### What happens after we send the form?

A mentor telephones you at a Bahamian hour to arrange the free session. Nothing is charged and no card is requested. Sending the form opens a conversation rather than an enrolment and holds no place in any batch. The stage, the format and the weekend slot are agreed on that call, ahead of any discussion of money.
