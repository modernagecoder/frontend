## Class 10 AI MCQ with answers: Part A and the project cycle unit

Questions 1 to 10. Try each before opening the answer, then read the reason: it names the option most students wrongly pick.

### Part A: employability skills (one per unit)

- During a group discussion, Arjun nods and keeps eye contact while a classmate speaks. This is
(a) verbal communication (b) written communication (c) non-verbal communication (d) visual communication
Answer (c) No words are used, only body language. Visual communication means charts, signs and pictures.

- In a SMART goal, the letter M stands for
(a) Meaningful (b) Measurable (c) Motivating (d) Manageable
Answer (b) You must be able to track progress. "Manageable" is the trap: being realistic is what the A, achievable, covers.

- Which of these protects a computer from viruses that arrive with downloads?
(a) defragmenting the disk (b) installing and updating antivirus software (c) raising screen brightness (d) deleting temporary files
Answer (b) The others are care and maintenance tasks; none of them stops harmful software.

- Meera sees that students near her school cannot buy healthy snacks, and starts a small stall. Which entrepreneurial quality does she show first?
(a) spotting an opportunity (b) avoiding every risk (c) following instructions (d) waiting for a job offer
Answer (a) An entrepreneur starts by noticing an unmet need. Starting a stall involves risk, so (b) contradicts the story.

- Using compost and crop rotation in place of chemical fertilisers is part of
(a) organic farming (b) monoculture (c) deforestation (d) heavy pesticide use
Answer (a) Organic farming keeps soil healthy without synthetic chemicals; monoculture repeats one crop and drains it.

Every Part A unit, with its short answers as well, is covered on [Part A employability skills](/cbse-class-10-ai-employability-skills-part-a).

### Unit 1: Revisiting AI Project Cycle and Ethical Frameworks for AI

- Which stage of the AI project cycle comes straight after Data Acquisition?
(a) Modelling (b) Data Exploration (c) Problem Scoping (d) Evaluation
Answer (b) Collected data is explored and visualised before a model is chosen. Jumping to (a) is the usual slip.

- In the 4Ws problem canvas, which W asks how solving the problem will benefit the people affected?
(a) Who (b) What (c) Where (d) Why
Answer (d) Why records the value of the solution. What describes the problem itself, which is why it tempts.

- A set of ethical rules written only for hospitals and medical AI is
(a) a value-based framework (b) a sector-based framework (c) a rule-based model (d) a utility-based framework
Answer (b) It is built for one sector, healthcare. Bioethics is the standard example of a sector-based framework.

- A city picks the traffic AI that cuts total travel time for the most people, even though a few roads get slower. Which approach is this?
(a) rights-based (b) virtue-based (c) utility-based (d) sector-based
Answer (c) It weighs the overall benefit against the harm. A rights-based approach would protect every road user first.

- Which of these is NOT one of the four principles of bioethics?
(a) respect for autonomy (b) do not harm (c) ensure maximum benefit (d) maximise the hospital's income
Answer (d) The fourth principle is justice, meaning fair treatment. Income appears nowhere in the four.

## Modeling and evaluating models: class 10 MCQ, including the numericals

Questions 11 to 22. Unit 3 carries the most objective items, and five of its seven below need a quick calculation.

### Unit 2: Advanced Concepts of Modeling in AI

- A spam filter gets better every week as users mark new emails as spam. It follows
(a) a rule-based approach (b) a learning-based approach (c) a fixed if-else checklist (d) no model at all
Answer (b) It learns from fresh labelled data. A rule-based system changes only when a person rewrites the rules.

- A model predicts tomorrow's highest temperature in degrees Celsius. This is
(a) classification (b) clustering (c) regression (d) association
Answer (c) The output is a continuous number. Labels such as "hot" or "cold" would make it classification.

- An online shop groups its customers by buying habits, with no labels given. It uses
(a) supervised learning, classification (b) unsupervised learning, clustering (c) supervised learning, regression (d) reinforcement learning
Answer (b) No labels means unsupervised, and grouping means clustering. Association finds items bought together, not groups of people.

- A perceptron has inputs 1, 0 and 1, weights 0.4, 0.7 and 0.2, and a bias of -0.5. What is the weighted sum plus bias?
(a) 0.6 (b) 0.8 (c) 0.1 (d) 1.3
Answer (c) 0.4 + 0 + 0.2 - 0.5 = 0.1. Option (a) forgets the bias; (b) treats the middle input as 1.

- Which deep learning network is designed for images, using small filters that slide across the picture?
(a) ANN (b) CNN (c) a decision tree (d) a rule-based system
Answer (b) A convolutional neural network. ANN is the general family, so it is close but not the specific answer.

### Unit 3: Evaluating Models

```
A model screens 200 X-ray reports.
It flags 60 as positive; 54 of them
really have the disease. Of the 140
it calls negative, 36 have it.

              Reality: Yes  Reality: No
Predicted Yes   TP = 54      FP = 6
Predicted No    FN = 36      TN = 104
```

- The precision of this model is
(a) 0.60 (b) 0.79 (c) 0.90 (d) 0.72
Answer (c) TP / (TP + FP) = 54 / 60 = 0.90. Option (a) is the recall.

- The recall of this model is
(a) 0.60 (b) 0.90 (c) 0.27 (d) 0.79
Answer (a) TP / (TP + FN) = 54 / 90 = 0.60. Option (c) divides TP by all 200 reports, which no metric does.

- The F1 score is
(a) 0.75 (b) 0.72 (c) 0.79 (d) 0.54
Answer (b) 2 x 0.90 x 0.60 / (0.90 + 0.60) = 1.08 / 1.50 = 0.72. Option (a) is the plain average, the most common mistake.

- The accuracy, and the number of wrong predictions, are
(a) 0.79 and 42 (b) 0.90 and 20 (c) 0.72 and 56 (d) 0.79 and 36
Answer (a) (54 + 104) / 200 = 0.79, and wrong = FP + FN = 6 + 36 = 42. Option (d) counts only the false negatives.

- For a disease test, which error is most dangerous, so which metric matters most?
(a) false positives, precision (b) false negatives, recall (c) true negatives, accuracy (d) true positives, precision
Answer (b) A missed patient goes untreated. For a spam filter the answer flips to precision.

- Of 100 patients, 5 have a rare disease. A lazy model calls everyone healthy. Its accuracy is
(a) 5% (b) 50% (c) 95% (d) 100%
Answer (c) 95 of 100 are right, yet its recall is 0. Accuracy flatters a model when one class is rare.

- Why is a model not tested on the same data it was trained on?
(a) testing would be slower (b) the score would be over-optimistic and hide overfitting (c) training data has no labels (d) the data would be deleted
Answer (b) A model can memorise its training data, so only unseen test data shows how it will really perform.

## Computer vision class 10 MCQ and NLP class 10 MCQ, with the counting questions

Questions 23 to 36. Pixel and token counts are where a careful student and a rushed one part ways.

### Unit 5: Computer Vision

- A self-driving car's camera draws a box around every pedestrian and cyclist in a frame. Which task is this?
(a) image classification (b) object detection (c) instance segmentation (d) image processing only
Answer (b) It finds and locates many objects with boxes. Segmentation would trace each object's exact outline in pixels.

- How many pixels are there in an image of resolution 640 x 480?
(a) 1,120 (b) 307,200 (c) 921,600 (d) 30,720
Answer (b) 640 x 480 = 307,200. Option (c) is three times that, the count of RGB values, not pixels.

- A colour (RGB) image is 200 x 100 pixels. How many numbers does it store?
(a) 20,000 (b) 300 (c) 60,000 (d) 600
Answer (c) Each pixel holds a red, a green and a blue value: 200 x 100 x 3. Option (a) would be the grayscale version.

- In a grayscale byte image, a pixel value of 0 means
(a) pure white (b) pure black (c) mid grey (d) a missing pixel
Answer (b) 0 is no brightness and 255 is full brightness, white. Reversing the two is a frequent slip.

- Why does a byte image allow exactly 256 pixel values?
(a) a byte has 8 bits, and 2 to the power 8 is 256 (b) screens show only 256 colours (c) each pixel uses 256 bytes (d) cameras record 256 shades
Answer (a) Eight bits give 256 patterns, which is why values run from 0 to 255.

### Unit 6: Natural Language Processing

- How many tokens does the sentence "AI helps doctors, and doctors help patients." produce?
(a) 7 (b) 6 (c) 9 (d) 8
Answer (c) Seven words plus the comma plus the full stop. Punctuation marks are tokens too.

- After lowercasing and dropping punctuation, how many unique words does that sentence give a Bag of Words dictionary?
(a) 9 (b) 7 (c) 6 (d) 5
Answer (c) "doctors" repeats: ai, helps, doctors, and, help, patients. Option (d) wrongly merges "help" and "helps", which happens only after stemming.

- With the dictionary [rain, go, away, come], the vector for "rain rain go away" is
(a) [1, 1, 1, 0] (b) [2, 1, 1, 0] (c) [2, 1, 1, 1] (d) [1, 2, 1, 0]
Answer (b) Bag of Words counts occurrences in dictionary order. Option (a) only marks presence.

- "studies" becomes "studi" with one technique and "study" with another. Which pairing is right?
(a) stemming gives studi, lemmatisation gives study (b) lemmatisation gives studi, stemming gives study (c) both give study (d) tokenisation gives studi
Answer (a) Stemming chops the ending and may leave a non-word; lemmatisation returns a real dictionary word.

- A word appears in all 4 documents of a corpus. With IDF taken as log(N / documents containing the word), its IDF is
(a) 4 (b) 1 (c) 0 (d) 0.25
Answer (c) log(4 / 4) = log 1 = 0, so its TFIDF is 0: a word found everywhere tells the documents apart not at all.

- "The chair sang a song" is grammatically correct but makes no sense. At which stage does it fail?
(a) lexical analysis (b) syntactic analysis (c) semantic analysis (d) tokenisation
Answer (c) The grammar passes the syntactic check; the meaning fails the semantic one.

### Units 4 and 7: practical only, not in the theory paper

These units carry no Section A items, but the practical exam and viva ask students to predict what a program prints.

- For the data 9, 3, 10, 4, 4, the median is
(a) 10 (b) 6 (c) 4 (d) 3
Answer (c) Sort first: 3, 4, 4, 9, 10, so the middle value is 4. Option (a) is the middle of the unsorted list and (b) is the mean.

- What does print(np.median([1, 4, 9, 7])) show?
(a) 9 (b) 5.5 (c) 5.25 (d) 4
Answer (b) Sorted, the list is 1, 4, 7, 9; with an even count the median is (4 + 7) / 2. Option (c) is the mean.

- Which Matplotlib call draws a scatter chart of x against y?
(a) plt.plot(x, y) (b) plt.scatter(x, y) (c) plt.bar(x, y) (d) plt.hist(x)
Answer (b) plt.plot joins the points into a line chart by default.

## MCQ technique for 417, and the traps that cost the most

Objective marks are rarely lost on unstudied topics. They go to distractors built from half-remembered ideas. These are the patterns we see most when marking class sets.

- Trap | What it looks like | The habit that beats it

- Swapped cells | Precision and recall both appear as options, from FP and FN reversed | Draw the matrix with Reality across the top every time, even for a one-mark item

- Average for F1 | An option equal to (P + R) / 2 | Write 2PR / (P + R) beside the question before choosing

- Forgotten punctuation | Token counts one or two short | Circle each comma and full stop before counting

- Pixels versus values | A figure three times too large or too small | Ask: does the question want pixels, or numbers stored?

- Family versus member | ANN when the answer is CNN; supervised when it is classification | Pick the most specific option that is still true

- Close definitions | Clustering against association; stemming against lemmatisation | Learn one example of each that cannot belong to the other

- The NOT question | Three true statements and one odd one out | Underline NOT, then mark each option true or false on its own

### Read all four first
It catches the option that is true but less specific than the one the examiner wants.

### Eliminate, then decide
Strike the plainly wrong ones. With no negative marking, a choice between the last two is always worth making.

### Choose your skips early
On a first read, mark the four AI items you are least sure of, answer the rest in order, then return if time allows.
