MATHCOUNTS is the middle-school contest that runs in every state, from a school round in the autumn to Chapter competitions in February, State in March and the national final in May. It is four different tests in one morning: a fast Sprint without a calculator, a Target round in timed pairs, a Team round, and a head-to-head Countdown with 45 seconds a problem. Most students prepare for one of them. Our batches of five to ten teach all four, in the shape of each round, and we tell families plainly which of the two entry routes applies to them, because one of them lets a parent register a child directly.

Ans. The short version

The MATHCOUNTS Competition Series is a United States contest for students in grades 6 to 8, run by the MATHCOUNTS Foundation in all fifty states, the District of Columbia, Puerto Rico, the US Virgin Islands, Guam and in schools abroad affiliated with the Departments of Defense and State. A school registers up to fourteen competitors; where a student's school will not register, the parent can register the student as a non-school competitor online. The competition has four rounds: Sprint, thirty problems in forty minutes with no calculator; Target, four pairs of problems with six minutes a pair and a calculator; Team, ten problems in twenty minutes for the four official team members; and the optional Countdown, head to head at up to forty-five seconds a problem. For 2026-27 the early-bird deadline is 3 November 2026, non-school registration opens 5 November, the regular deadline is 15 December, Chapter competitions run 1 to 28 February 2027, State 1 to 31 March 2027 and the national final is 9 to 10 May 2027 in Washington DC. Modern Age Coders prepares students live online in batches of five to ten: one class a week on the mathematics and the reasoning, one on timed practice in the shape of each round. The first class is a free demo, and the group batch fee is shown on this page in your own currency.

Q2. What does each round actually test?

Four rounds, four different skills

A student who is excellent at one round is often average at another. The Sprint rewards pace without a calculator; the Target rewards careful multi-step reasoning with one; the Team rewards dividing work; the Countdown rewards nerve. We train each in its own shape.

Sprint

Thirty problems, forty minutes, no calculator

Eighty seconds a problem, and the arithmetic must be done by hand. The skills are fluency, recognising the standard problem types instantly, and the discipline to skip a problem that is not yielding and come back. We drill mental arithmetic and the common types at speed, with a clock, every week.

Target

Four pairs, six minutes a pair, calculator allowed

Harder problems with time to think and a calculator to remove the arithmetic. The skill is reading the problem into a plan, then executing it cleanly: organised cases, working backwards, setting up an equation from a story. This is where the base course and the contest course meet.

Team

Ten problems, twenty minutes, four students

Only the school's official team of four takes it for a team score. The skill is dividing the problems by strength, checking each other, and not all attacking the same one. We rehearse it in the batch as a team exercise, because it teaches every student which problems are theirs.

Countdown

Head to head, forty-five seconds a problem

Optional at the lower levels, decisive where it runs. The skill is composure: seeing a problem, deciding the method in seconds, and buzzing in with the answer, not the working. We run it as a game at the end of class, because that is what it is, and the students who dread it in October enjoy it by February.

The Foundation publishes past competitions and a school handbook, which we use in class rather than reproduce here. Every student sees a real Chapter Sprint and Target from a past year in the first week, in the real shape, with the real clock.

Q3. Why the Sprint punishes the school method

The same problem, two students, eighty seconds

A Sprint-style problem: "What is the sum of all the positive two-digit numbers that are multiples of 7?" No calculator, eighty seconds. Both students know their 7 times table.

Every round has its versions of this. The plan below teaches the ideas by name, then rehearses each round in its own shape until the shape is familiar.

Q4. How does the batch prepare?

Five steps, from the school round to the State competition

Two live classes a week. One on the mathematics and the reasoning, one on timed practice in the shape of each round, reviewed problem by problem the next class.

  1. A placement on a real past Sprint and Target in the first week

    Sat alone with the real clock, then walked through together. It tells the teacher whether the gap is fluency, reasoning or nerve, and it tells you honestly where your child stands before any preparation.

  2. The mathematics of grades 6 to 8, taught for understanding

    Ratio, percentages, geometry, counting, probability, early algebra. MATHCOUNTS asks the middle-school syllabus at speed and sideways, so a gap in the base becomes a lost Sprint mark. The base course runs alongside for students who need it.

  3. Sprint fluency and the standard types, with a clock

    Mental arithmetic, the common problem families, skipping and returning. Every week a timed Sprint set, every set reviewed, every slip named.

  4. Target reasoning and Team habits

    Multi-step problems with a calculator and six minutes a pair, then the batch working as a team of four on a Team round, learning which problems are whose.

  5. Countdown as a game, then two full rehearsals

    Head-to-head practice at the end of class from the second month, so the nerve is built by February. The last four weeks are full Chapter-style competitions under contest conditions, reviewed round by round.

Batches for American families run in US evening and weekend slots; the mentor fixes the time on the call once we know the grade and the time zone. The fee for families in the United States is shown in dollars on this page.

Q5. Is this batch right for my child?

Who it suits, and who it does not

It suits

A grade 6 to 8 student in the United States whose school runs MATHCOUNTS, or whose school will not, in which case the non-school route from 5 November applies. A student strong at school maths who has never been timed. A family that wants the preparation to leave real reasoning behind, whatever the placing.

It also suits

A student who reached Chapter last year and wants State this year. A student sitting AMC 8 in January and MATHCOUNTS in February, which share most of their mathematics. A home-schooled student, who enters through the Foundation's Home School Participation Form rather than as a non-school competitor.

It does not suit

A family outside the United States whose child is not at a Department of Defense or State Department school, because the Foundation's rules give no other way in and we will not pretend otherwise; our Math Kangaroo and AMC pages cover contests with wider reach. A student in grade 9 or above, who has aged out. A family looking for a crash session the week before Chapter.

Questions parents ask

About MATHCOUNTS and the preparation batch

Can a parent register a child for MATHCOUNTS directly?

Only as a non-school competitor, and only when the child's school of record will not register for the Competition Series and will not support participation through the school. In that case the Foundation says "the parent/guardian of an NSC must register online and pay with a credit card," from 5 November 2026, at $75 by the regular deadline or $85 late. Otherwise entry is through the school, which can register up to fourteen competitors. Home-schooled students use the Foundation's Home School Participation Form instead.

What are the MATHCOUNTS dates for 2026-27?

From the Foundation's critical dates page: early-bird registration by 3 November 2026, non-school competitor registration opening 5 November, the regular deadline 15 December 2026, Chapter competitions 1 to 28 February 2027, State competitions 1 to 31 March 2027 and the national competition 9 to 10 May 2027 in Washington DC. The school round runs inside the school between August and January.

What are the four rounds?

Sprint: thirty problems in forty minutes with no calculator. Target: four pairs of problems with six minutes per pair and a calculator. Team: ten problems in twenty minutes, taken by the four official team members. Countdown: head to head at up to forty-five seconds a problem without a calculator, optional at the school, chapter and state levels. We train each round in its own shape, because the skills differ.

We live outside the United States. Can my child take part?

Only if the child attends a school abroad affiliated with the US Departments of Defense or State; the Foundation lists those alongside the fifty states and territories and no other route. For a family in India, the UAE, the UK or Singapore, MATHCOUNTS is not an option, and we say so before anyone pays. Our Math Kangaroo, AMC and CEMC pages cover contests with wider reach.

How much does it cost to enter?

The Foundation publishes school fees of $42 per student early bird by 3 November 2026, $48 regular by 15 December and $53 late, with a 50% discount for Title I schools, and non-school competitor fees of $75 regular and $85 late, with no Title I discount. Full refunds carry a $50 fee and non-school registrations are not refundable in 2026-27. Those are the Foundation's figures, paid to it; our own monthly fee is on this page.

Is MATHCOUNTS the same as AMC 8?

No, but they share most of their mathematics and the same age group. AMC 8 is one 40-minute multiple-choice paper in late January run by the MAA; MATHCOUNTS is four rounds across a season run by the MATHCOUNTS Foundation, with a team element and a head-to-head final. Most of our middle-school competition students sit both, and the preparation overlaps heavily; our AMC page covers the other.

Do you use past MATHCOUNTS competitions?

Yes. The Foundation publishes past competitions and a coach handbook, and we use them for placement, weekly timed sets and the two full rehearsals, then send families to the Foundation's own resources rather than reproducing papers here. Every set a student sits is reviewed problem by problem in the next class.

Can my child practise the Team round without a school team?

Yes, as training. Only a school's official team of four takes the Team round for a team score, so a non-school competitor does not score on it, but we rehearse it in the batch anyway because dividing problems by strength teaches every student which problems are theirs in the Sprint.

What happens after the Chapter competition?

The top four individuals in each chapter advance to the State competition in March, and the top four in each state, including non-school competitors advancing as individuals, earn a trip to the National competition in May, where they form four-person state teams and compete for the individual title. Our batch continues through State for students who advance.

What happens after I send the form?

A mentor calls within a few hours, asks which grade your child is in and whether the school is registering, and books a free demo class at a time that suits you. The demo is a real session: a few Sprint problems with a clock, one Target pair, and a Countdown question for fun, with the teacher showing the reasoning rather than the answer key. There is no card, no enrolment fee and no pressure on the call.
