The best coding classes for kids in Minneapolis have a live teacher, a group of children at the same level, and real projects a child finishes. Watch for screen time dressed as coding

Many apps let a child tap through puzzles for hours without writing a single line of their own. Ask what your child will make, not how many levels they will clear.

Expect the teacher to explain

A good teacher asks a child to explain why a program works. If your child can say it out loud, the idea has stuck; if they cannot, it has not yet.

Think about the long road

The strongest choice is a class with a next step: Scratch should lead somewhere, usually to Python, without starting over each time.

Signature project

Scooter or e-bike? A pivot table of a whole Minneapolis summer

The City of Minneapolis publishes every shared scooter and e-bike trip as open data. For 2024, we asked the city’s server to count rides by month and vehicle type, which is exactly what a pivot table does.

1. Rows and columns

A pivot table puts one category down the side (months) and another across the top (vehicle type), then counts what falls in each box. Kids build it first in a spreadsheet, then in Python with a dictionary of dictionaries.

2. Do the parts add up?

The server reported 804,546 trips in all. Our seven monthly totals add to exactly 804,546, and so do the two vehicle columns. When the parts match the whole, the table is trustworthy; when they do not, something is being missed.

3. Read the pattern

Rides climb from April to a September peak of 172,763, then drop sharply in October. There are no rides at all from November to March, and children can explain why from the city’s own rule about snow and ice.

A rule you can find in the data

The city says operations may be suspended when it becomes snowy and icy, and the 2024 file runs from April 4 to October 15, with nothing outside that window. Children who notice the empty winter months and connect them to that rule are reading data like scientists.

We ran these queries ourselves on 18 October 2026, so the counts are our results, not official statistics; Minneapolis waives copyright on this open data and offers it as reference material. The file also gives distance in meters and duration in seconds, so older children can go further: our averages show scooter rides around 2,288 meters and bicycle rides around 2,802, while the 15,824 trips that started on a trail averaged about 4,567 meters, almost twice the street average.

One honest caveat belongs in every write-up. Start times are rounded to the nearest half hour and appear to be stored in UTC, so a ride just after midnight on the last day of a month could land in the next month. The effect on our table is tiny, but saying so is part of the skill.

Midnight, in code

The midnight shut-off is a rule a program can check. Older kids write an if statement that flags any ride starting after midnight, then discover why time zones make that harder than it looks.

Miles of lanes

Trails, protected bikeways and painted lanes are three kinds of route. Children can draw them as a bar chart and ask which one most of those trail-start rides used.

Minnesota schools

Where coding fits for a Minnesota child

A short note on the state picture; the full detail lives on our Minnesota material and the state’s own sources.

Minnesota’s 2023 Computer Science Education Advancement Program law, Minnesota Statutes section 120B.241, focuses on building capacity and on reporting: among other things it requires every high school to report its computer science course offerings and enrollment. It does not make coding a subject every elementary child takes, so for most Minneapolis kids, coding happens in clubs, camps or classes like ours. In the Code.org (2025) data, 275 of 799 Minnesota high schools, 34.4%, offered foundational computer science in 2023 to 2024 (Code.org (2025). 2025 State of AI + CS Education).

Coding and AI

Should a Minneapolis child still learn to code when AI can write programs?

Yes, because coding teaches a child to check an answer, not just to get one.

Ask an AI tool for the busiest scooter month and it might say July, because summer sounds right. The city’s data says September. A child who built the pivot table knows the answer and also knows how to prove it, which is a habit no tool can hand them.

For younger children we keep AI simple and safe: they learn what it is, where it makes mistakes and why a person should check its work. They are never asked to sign up for adult-only services, and the coding itself is theirs from the first block.

Learning to code at eight or nine is less about becoming a programmer than about learning to ask "how do I know that?", a question that serves a child in every subject. The longer argument is in is coding still worth learning in 2026.

FAQ

Minneapolis parents’ questions

What families in Minneapolis ask before a first class.

How do I choose the best coding class for my child in Minneapolis?

Look for a live teacher in every session, grouping by level, a group size you are comfortable with, finished projects and a free trial lesson. Our checklist on this page applies to any provider.

What age can my child start coding?

From age 6 with Scratch and block coding. Most children move to typed Python between 9 and 11, and the free lesson tells us when.

What is the scooter ride project?

Children count the City of Minneapolis 2024 scooter and e-bike trips by month and vehicle type in a pivot table, then check that the parts add up to the 804,546 total.

Which month had the most shared rides in 2024?

September, with 172,763 rides in our count of the city data, followed by August with 152,575. The season ran from April 4 to October 15, 2024.

Does Minnesota require coding in elementary school?

No. Minnesota Statutes 120B.241 builds capacity and requires high schools to report CS offerings and enrollment, but it does not add coding as a required elementary subject.

What time are classes for Minneapolis families?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Times are confirmed in Central Time.

Will my child be using AI chatbots?

Not adult-only ones. Younger children learn what AI is and why its answers need checking, and they write their own code from the first lesson.

Is there a Modern Age Coders center in Minneapolis?

No. Every class is live online from India; a child needs a computer, a headset and a reliable connection at home.

Can siblings learn together?

Siblings at a similar level can share a group, and siblings at different levels are placed separately so each one moves at the right pace.

Where do I find the prices?

Just above the booking form there is a short fees block: the trial lesson costs nothing, then you see one monthly figure for a group place and one for private lessons, in US dollars.
