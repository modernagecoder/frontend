Online coding summer camp in Canada, live from home

For many Canadian families a summer coding camp means one week in a hot room in July, a lanyard and a game that nobody opens again in September. It can mean more. Two months is long enough to move a child from blocks to typed code, to get ahead of the coding their next grade will ask for, or to finish a project a teenager is proud to show. This guide sets out the summer window under Ontario's school-year rules, compares the camp formats on offer across Canada, lists what each grade's curriculum asks next, and sets out our own live online summer camp, with an eight-week plan, one to one at your convenience or with a weekly batch.

Which shape suits your family

The online summer camp suits a child of 6 to 16 who has the whole summer and benefits from spacing: two classes a week, with the same mentor, around trips and day camps.

The intensive tracks suit a student who wants a concentrated burst of two to three weeks, including teenagers building a portfolio and adults who want Python and data skills in one stretch.

What the camp is not

It is not childcare and not a full-day program. Our own camp page says so plainly: an online camp is taught hours plus practice, not a place to drop children off for the day.

If your family needs full days covered, pair it with an in-person day camp for a week or two; the formats compared further down show what each is good at.

The summer window

How long the summer really is

In Ontario the gap between the last school day and the first is about nine weeks. Allow a week at each end for rest and getting ready, and roughly seven or eight weeks are open for a plan.

That is the length our plan further down assumes: eight weeks from Monday 5 July to Friday 27 August 2027. Families on other calendars can slide it earlier or later.

How small a summer is next to a school year

British Columbia sets the minimum a board must offer at "952 hours of instruction for students in grades 8 to 12" and "878 hours of instruction for students in grades 1 to 7".

Our arithmetic: a batch summer of about 16 live one-hour classes is under 2 percent of a Grade 8 to 12 year. A summer cannot do everything, so it should do one thing well: one new language, one next-grade skill or one finished project.

For Toronto families comparing summer camps

A city the size of Toronto has campus camps, franchise centres and community programs within reach, and many families combine one in-person week with something steadier for the rest of the summer.

Live online classes fit around those weeks rather than replacing them. A child can spend the second week of July at a day camp and still keep a weekly class with the same teacher.

Outside the big cities the choice is narrower. A family in Whitehorse, Iqaluit or a small Prairie town may have few or no coding camps within easy reach.

Why getting ahead works

A child who meets nested loops in August hears the word in October as a reminder rather than a surprise. The school lesson becomes practice, which is far less stressful than a first meeting.

This is not about racing through the curriculum. One idea from next year, understood well, is enough.

For students heading into ICS3U or ICD2O

Ontario's Grade 11 ICS3U has no prerequisite, so some students arrive having never typed code. A summer of Python closes that gap before the course starts.

Our ICS3U and ICS4U companion course supports the school course in Python or Java. We teach alongside it and do not grant Ontario credits.

A summer project

A summer project to start with: the secret-message wheel

Our project, for students of about 10 and up who know a little Python. It turns a message into code by shifting every letter along the alphabet, the oldest trick in cryptography, and it teaches one idea that returns again and again in math and computing: wrapping round.

The idea inside it. The line (ALPHABET.index(ch) + key) % 26 is what makes Z wrap round to A. The % sign gives the remainder after dividing by 26, so position 25 (Z) plus 3 is 28, and 28 % 26 is 2, which is C. Clock arithmetic works the same way: three hours after 11 o'clock is 2 o'clock, because 14 % 12 is 2.

Week by week with this project

Week 1: run it and explain every line. Week 2: let the user type the message and the key. Week 3: crack a message without knowing the key, by trying all 26 and spotting the one that reads as English.

Week 4: invent a stronger cipher, such as a different shift for each letter, and explain why it is harder to crack.

What it connects to

The remainder idea comes back in Grade 9 algebra, in contest problems about days of the week, and in every program that cycles through a list.

Ontario's ICD2O names cybersecurity among the technologies students investigate, so a Grade 10 student meets this kind of idea again in September.

Eight weeks

An eight-week live online summer plan for July and August 2027

Our summer camp is live online and runs on our regular courses, without a fixed daily timetable: a batch meets for eight live classes a month, about two a week, and one-to-one students book classes at times that suit the family. This plan is ours and follows the camp's own arc of foundations, a build and a final family demo. It assumes two live classes a week and twenty minutes of practice on most other days, from Monday 5 July to Friday 27 August 2027.

  1. Week 1 (5 to 9 July): a first class to agree one summer goal: a new language, a next-grade skill, or one finished project. Canada Day falls the Thursday before, so the week starts clean.
  2. Week 2 (12 to 16 July): the basics the goal needs, taught live and practised in short daily sessions at home.
  3. Week 3 (19 to 23 July): the first small version of the project working, however rough.
  4. Week 4 (26 to 30 July): a pause week if the family travels; otherwise, testing and fixing, with a list of every error met so far.
  5. Week 5 (2 to 6 August): the hardest part of the project, with the teacher guiding by questions rather than answers.
  6. Week 6 (9 to 13 August): a rule change: the student adapts the project to a new requirement, which shows whether they understand it.
  7. Week 7 (16 to 20 August): polish and a short written or spoken explanation of how the project works.
  8. Week 8 (23 to 27 August): the student presents the project to the family, and the teacher writes a short note on what to carry into September.

Ages 6 to 9

Scratch projects, shorter classes, a parent nearby. The goal is a finished game and wanting more.

Ages 10 to 13

The move to Python, or a next-grade skill from the table above. The cipher project fits here.

Ages 14 to 18

A real project, ICS3U preparation, or a first look at contest problems before the school year.

Our promise holds through the summer as in term time: we teach students how to reason through a problem themselves, never to copy an answer or paste what an AI tool produced. That is why week 6 is a rule change. A project the student cannot change is a project someone else understood.

For parents

Agree the one goal in the first class and write it down. In August, ask your child to show you the result and change one thing while you watch.

Summer coding in Canada: questions and answers

Do you run an online coding summer camp in Canada?

Yes. Our summer camp is live online for ages 6 to 16, with eight live classes a month, two a week, in a batch or one to one, and a finished project shown to the family at the end. Canadian families join from any province.

What tracks does your summer camp have?

The online camp has four tracks: First Games in Scratch (6 to 9), Game Builders (9 to 12), Python Start (10 to 14) and AI Explorers (11 to 16). There are also two to three week intensive tracks for kids 6 to 12, teens 12 to 17 and adults 18 and over.

When does summer break start in Ontario in 2027?

Ontario Regulation 304 says the school year ends on or before 30 June, and the next starts on or after 1 September. In 2027, 30 June is a Wednesday. Your board's calendar gives the exact last day.

Is there a class on Canada Day?

Canada Day 2027 is Thursday 1 July under the Holidays Act. We plan around it and around any other holiday your family keeps; a missed class is moved, not lost.

Is an online summer camp as good as an in-person one?

They do different jobs. An in-person camp gives a week out with friends. Live online classes give steady progress for the whole summer, from home, with a teacher who continues in September. Many families do both.

What should my child practise over the summer?

One idea from next year. In Ontario, Grade 4 math adds nested events in code, Grade 5 adds conditional statements and Grade 7 adds subprograms. Meeting that idea in August makes the autumn easier.

What age is summer coding for?

We teach from age 6. Scratch suits ages 6 to 9, the move to Python suits 10 to 13, and teens can build a real project or prepare for a Grade 10 or 11 computer science course.

Can we pause for a family trip?

Yes. Our eight-week plan includes a possible pause week, and one-to-one classes are booked around your summer. Fees are monthly, so a travel month can be skipped.

What times are summer classes across Canada?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. In summer 2027 Vancouver is three hours behind Toronto, and we set each time in your clock.

Are there free summer coding resources in Canada?

Yes. CEMC at the University of Waterloo offers Computer Science Circles, free Python tutorials, plus past contests from the last ten years and math courseware, with no registration needed.

Is there a separate summer camp fee?

No. July and August are billed like any other month of classes, and the fees panel on this page lists the monthly amount for where you live.
