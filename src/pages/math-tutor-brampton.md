## A hundred trillion trees: cleaning Brampton&rsquo;s tree tracker before averaging it

The City of Brampton runs an online tracker where residents and groups record trees they have planted, and publishes the records as open data. We downloaded every record and tried to answer a simple question: how many trees does a typical record report? The cleaning decisions and every figure here are ours.

The layer belongs to the Brampton One Million Trees programme, which the City describes as a framework to realize the target of one million trees by 2040. The City&rsquo;s description explains where the records come from: it has launched an online tree planting tracker tool and invites anyone who has recently planted a tree to record it. That single sentence tells a careful student what to expect. These are self-reported entries, typed in by people, so some will be wrong.

**Our checks on the City of Brampton tree planting tracker layer, downloaded 22 October 2026 (1,228 records)**

| Check | What we found |
|---|---|
| Records and distinct record numbers | 1,228 records, 1,228 distinct objectid values, no blank tree counts or dates |
| Median and most common value | 1 tree per record; 845 records report exactly one tree |
| Raw mean, all records | about 81,433,224,775 trees per record |
| The record behind that mean | objectid 1291, dated 11 September 2025, Ward 1, reporting 100,000,000,000,000 trees |
| A date that has not happened yet | objectid 1292 is dated 24 June 2027, eight months after our download |
| Mean after removing objectid 1291 | 19.21 trees per record, across 1,227 records |
| Trees in the cleaned records | 23,576, with the largest single record at 5,000 |
| Records of 100 trees or more | 60 records, holding 76.6 percent of the cleaned trees |

One typed number moved the mean from about nineteen trees to about eighty-one billion. The median did not move at all, because it only cares about the middle of the sorted list. But the lesson is not simply &ldquo;use the median&rdquo;. The impossible value has to be found, named and removed with a stated reason before any summary is honest, and the future-dated record has to be flagged too, even though its count of 2 is perfectly plausible. This short program does the first part:

```
import json, statistics

records = []
for page in ("trees_page_0.json", "trees_page_1000.json"):
    with open(page) as f:
        records += [feature["attributes"] for feature in json.load(f)["features"]]

counts = [r["NumTreesPlanted"] for r in records]
print("records:", len(counts), "raw mean:", round(statistics.mean(counts)))

plausible = [c for c in counts if c <= 10_000]
print("dropped:", len(counts) - len(plausible))
print("clean mean:", round(statistics.mean(plausible), 2))
print("median:", statistics.median(plausible))
print("trees in clean records:", sum(plausible))
```

### Why our cut-off is 10,000

The largest believable record is 5,000 trees, plausibly a big organised planting. We set the line at 10,000, above every believable record and far below the impossible one, and we say so, because a cut-off is a decision, not a fact.

### When the textbook rule fails

The usual outlier fence is the upper quartile plus 1.5 times the interquartile range. Here the quartiles are 1, 1 and 2, so the fence sits at 3.5 and flags 212 records, almost all of them honest group plantings. A rule built for measurements breaks on counts like these.

### Who records the trees

Records marked as planted by an organization number 838 and hold 22,515 of the cleaned trees; the other 389 records hold 1,061. Most entries are small, but most trees come from a few large ones.

## Does a pace of 50,000 trees a year reach one million by 2040?

The City&rsquo;s description of the programme gives a rate, so a Grade 7 to 9 student can test it with a linear model. The question and the working are ours; the two numbers in it are the City&rsquo;s.

The City writes that, to achieve one million trees by 2040, it and its partners must aim to plant, on average, an additional 7000 trees per year for a total of 50,000 trees per year starting in 2020.

**Part (a).** Write a model for the trees planted from the start of 2020, if the pace is exactly 50,000 a year. Let t be the number of full years since the start of 2020. Then T = 50,000t. This is a direct variation: no starting amount, constant rate, a straight line through the origin.

**Part (b).** When does T reach 1,000,000? Solve 50,000t = 1,000,000, so t = 20. Twenty full years from the start of 2020 ends at the start of 2040, which matches the City&rsquo;s target date. The City&rsquo;s two numbers are consistent with each other.

**Part (c).** What does the word &ldquo;additional&rdquo; tell you? If 7,000 is the extra amount and 50,000 the total, the planting already under way before the programme must be about 50,000 &minus; 7,000 = 43,000 trees a year. That is our reading of the City&rsquo;s sentence, not a figure the City states, and a good answer says so.

**Part (d), the trap.** Can you use the 23,576 trees in the open tracker layer to judge whether the programme is on track? No. The tracker holds only trees that someone chose to record in it, so it is a sample of unknown size from a larger total. Comparing it with 50,000 a year would mix two different quantities. Saying what a dataset cannot answer is part of the answer.

**Try it yourself.** Suppose planting started at 43,000 a year in 2020 and rose by 1,000 each year after that. Write the number planted in year t, then use the formula for an arithmetic series to find the total after ten years.

## If AI can average a column, why should a Brampton student learn how?

Because the average was the easy part. Knowing the column was broken was the math.

Ask an AI tool for the mean of the tree tracker and it may well return eighty-one billion without comment, or quietly drop the bad row without telling you it did. Either way the student learns nothing about why. Spotting the hundred trillion, deciding what to do with it and explaining the decision is reasoning only the student can own.

That is how we teach: estimate first, calculate by hand where it matters, check with technology afterwards, and always explain the result in a sentence. Marked school work stays the student&rsquo;s own.

MTH1W asks students to model and reason. A tool can speed up arithmetic, but it cannot take over the judgement. The longer argument is in [the case for learning to reason in an age of AI tools](/blog/is-coding-worth-learning-2026).
