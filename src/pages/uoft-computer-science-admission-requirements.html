U of T computer science admission requirements, campus by campus

The University of Toronto runs computer science on three campuses, and an Ontario student applies to each one separately, with its own OUAC code. The prerequisites overlap but are not identical, the supplementary application is described differently on different U of T pages, and only two campuses publish an average. This page lays out what each source said when we read it on 22 October 2026, side by side and unreconciled where the sources disagree. Admission rules change between cycles, so check every line on U of T’s own pages in the autumn your teen applies. We teach the math and programming; we do not forecast decisions.

The rule behind every column

For all three campuses the central page states the base requirement the same way: an “Ontario Secondary School Diploma (OSSD) with six subjects at the 4U/M level, including English”. It adds a line that catches some families out: “The co-op component of Grade 12 courses will not be used for admission purposes”.

The course codes are Ontario’s, and U of T labels them itself: English is ENG4U, with EAE4U accepted as the alternative for that line; Calculus & Vectors is MCV4U; Advanced Functions is MHF4U; Math of Data Management is MDM4U.

One difference worth noticing

On the central page, St. George lists English and Calculus and Vectors only, while Mississauga and Scarborough each list Advanced Functions as well. We report that as printed and draw no conclusion about admission from it. The Ontario mathematics curriculum (revised 2007) settles the practical side: it says Advanced Functions must be taken before or at the same time as Calculus and Vectors, so a St. George applicant with MCV4U has MHF4U anyway.

What it does show is that a student cannot read one campus’s list and assume it covers the others. Each OUAC code is a separate application with its own published list.

Sources: U of T Future Students, Computer Science (Ontario requirements, all three campuses), U of T Mississauga, Computer Science and U of T Scarborough, Computer Science, read 22 October 2026. The central page states no admission year, so we give the date we read it. We have no affiliation with the University of Toronto.

What that means for planning

On the two campus pages we read, a student cannot take a year of another program and then apply into computer science as a transfer. The Grade 12 application is the entry point, so the Grade 12 courses and the supplementary application are where the effort belongs.

Families sometimes assume a gap year of college or university courses can only help. On these two pages, any post-secondary study makes a student ineligible for the computer science admission category.

What a program at Scarborough contains

Scarborough’s page says that in the Specialist, “More than half of the credits needed for your degree will be in Computer Science.” It lists five streams: Comprehensive, Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning, Entrepreneurship, Information Systems and Software Engineering.

Mississauga’s page describes computer science research areas including human-computer interaction, software engineering, numerical analysis, machine learning and cryptography. Both are real computer science degrees with heavy mathematics, which is why the math prerequisites exist.

How to read the disagreement

St. George is described as requiring one on every central page we read. Mississauga’s own page says one is required even though the central program page shows no such row. For Scarborough, the deadlines page says its form is required for some program areas without naming them on that page.

We are not in a position to resolve that, and guessing would not help a family. The applicant portal lists each student’s required items after they apply, and that list is the one to follow.

What a supplementary application can look like

U of T’s supplemental applications page says “Most programs only require your academic results for admission.” Where a program does ask for more, the page says applicants may need to “answer short-answer or essay questions, provide a personal profile or even submit a video response.”

Whatever the format, the answers must be the student’s own. We teach coding and math, and we do not produce or revise application material of any kind.

Sources: U of T Future Students, Computer Science, U of T Dates and Deadlines, U of T Supplemental Applications and U of T Mississauga, Computer Science, read 22 October 2026.

Why we stop at the quotation

A published range describes past applicants in general terms. It is not a threshold, and a campus that also requires a supplementary application reads more than grades. Turning these numbers into advice about any one student’s chances would be guesswork presented as fact, so we do not do it, and no one on our team will.

Scarborough distinguishes the co-op version of the program from the regular one, and lists a separate minimum for English. Families should read that whole block on the campus page rather than our summary of it.

What the numbers do tell a Grade 11 student

They say the required Grade 12 courses carry weight, which is not news but is worth acting on early. Advanced Functions and Calculus and Vectors are demanding courses, and marks in them come from understanding, not from last-minute drilling.

That is the part we can help with. Our Grade 12 math teaching aims at a student who can explain why a derivative rule works, check a vector answer geometrically, and recover when a question is phrased in an unfamiliar way.

A worked example, ours

Checking a Grade 12 plan against all three lists, in Python

Requirements written as “ENG4U / EAE4U” are really sets of alternatives: any one course from the set satisfies that line. That makes a campus list easy to check in code, and writing the checker is a good exercise in sets. The plans below are invented. The program only compares course codes with the lists as published; it says nothing about admission.

  1. Model one requirement as a set. The English line becomes the set of ENG4U and EAE4U; the calculus line becomes a set with one member, MCV4U.
  2. Model a campus as a list of those sets. St. George has two; Mississauga and Scarborough have three each.
  3. Model a student’s plan as a set of course codes. Order does not matter, and a course appears once.
  4. A requirement is covered if the two sets overlap. In Python, alts & plan is the intersection; an empty intersection means nothing in the plan satisfies that line.
  5. Collect what is not covered, rather than returning true or false. A list of missing lines is more useful to a student than a yes or no.
  6. Test a plan built to fail. A plan that breaks one rule on purpose proves the checker can see the gap.
What each invented plan shows (ours; outputs from Python 3.13)
PlanWhat is differentWhat the checker reports
Plan AEAE4U instead of ENG4U, plus MHF4U and MCV4UEvery listed line covered on all three campuses: EAE4U satisfies the English set
Plan BENG4C, a college-preparation (C) English, instead of a U EnglishThe English line uncovered everywhere: a C course is not in the set
Test CA plan built to fail, with no MHF4UCovered at St. George on the central page’s list; MHF4U missing at the other two

What the exercise teaches

The intersection test is one line, but the modelling decision behind it is the real lesson: a slash in a requirement means or, and a list of requirements means and. Students who see that structure can read any university’s requirements, and any software specification, more carefully.

Test C is the kind of input good programmers write first. It is deliberately unrealistic, a plan without Advanced Functions, and its only job is to prove the checker notices a difference between campuses that a quick reading might miss.

What it cannot do

The checker knows the course lists we copied on one day. It does not know the six-course rule, marks, the supplementary application or any later change U of T makes. A green line from it means only that the codes match.

Treat it as a reading aid. The authoritative check is U of T’s own page and, after applying, the applicant portal.

Early consideration

U of T says applicants who apply by November 7 and complete the next steps in the Join U of T portal by December 1 can be considered in the first admission round as well as later ones. It also says decisions “will be released beginning in January and continue through to late May.”

For an Ontario Grade 12 student in the 2026 to 2027 school year, that makes November the month to finish the application itself, so that December and January can go to schoolwork and any supplementary application.

Applying to more than one campus

Each campus has its own OUAC code, so a student interested in computer science at two or three campuses lists each one. The prerequisites table above shows why that is worth doing carefully: a plan that matches one campus’s list may leave a line uncovered at another.

The application goes through the Ontario Universities’ Application Centre. U of T says that about a week after applying, students receive an email explaining how to log into their applicant portal.

Source: U of T Dates and Deadlines, 2027 admissions tables and FAQ, read 22 October 2026.

Choosing, ours

Questions to ask before choosing campuses

These questions are ours. None of them is about which campus is easier, because nothing we read supports an answer to that, and it is the wrong question anyway.

  1. Does my Grade 12 plan cover every listed line on every campus I will apply to? Run the checker above, or do it by hand, then confirm on each campus page.
  2. Which supplementary items will I owe? Read each page, then trust the portal checklist once it appears.
  3. Do I want co-op or work-integrated learning? All three list a work-integrated learning option; the campus pages describe how it works.
  4. Which stream or specialization interests me? Scarborough lists five streams; read the program descriptions rather than the names alone.
  5. Where would I live and study for four years? The three campuses are in different parts of the Toronto area, and that changes daily life more than any course list.
  6. Am I ready for first-year math? Every campus lists Calculus and Vectors. If MCV4U feels shaky in October of Grade 12, that is the most useful thing to fix.

Grade 12 math, honestly assessed

Four stages in Calculus and Vectors

Our own stages, from teaching MCV4U to students who went on to university computer science. They describe understanding, which is what first-year math tests.

Stages in Calculus and Vectors (ours)
StageA sign you are here
1. Pattern matcherDifferentiates correctly when the question looks like the last one
2. ExplainerSays why the chain rule works, using a picture or a small example
3. CheckerVerifies an answer independently, by graph, estimate or substitution
4. TransfererUses rates of change and vectors on a problem from physics or code

For parents

Ask your teen how they know an answer is right. A stage 3 student has a method; a stage 1 student says it matched the back of the book.

Moving from stage 1 to stage 2 takes patient questioning, and it is the step a busy classroom has least time for.

For students

After every calculus question, spend thirty seconds on a sanity check. It is the cheapest habit in mathematics.

Write a short program that estimates a derivative numerically and compare it with your exact answer. You will understand both better.

Common questions

U of T computer science admission: questions families ask

What are the U of T computer science prerequisites for Ontario students?

On 22 October 2026, U of T's Future Students page listed ENG4U or EAE4U and MCV4U for St. George (TAD); ENG4U or EAE4U, MHF4U and MCV4U for Mississauga (TMX), with MDM4U recommended; and ENG4U or EAE4U, MCV4U and MHF4U for Scarborough (TXC). All three also need an OSSD with six 4U or M courses including English.

What do TAD, TMX and TXC mean?

They are the OUAC codes students use to apply. TAD is computer science in the Faculty of Arts and Science at St. George, TMX is U of T Mississauga and TXC is U of T Scarborough. Each is a separate application.

Does U of T computer science need a supplementary application?

U of T's central page listed one as required for St. George, and its deadlines page gave the Computer Science Supplementary Application a February 1 date for 2027 admissions. Mississauga's own page also said one is required. Scarborough's form was described as required for some program areas. Follow the applicant portal checklist.

What average does U of T publish for computer science?

The central program page lists none. Mississauga's campus page gave a competitive average of mid to high 80s, and Scarborough's gave approximate requirements of low 90s overall, high 90s for co-op and at least 70 per cent in English. We quote these and make no prediction about any applicant.

Can I transfer into computer science at UTM or UTSC later?

Not according to the campus pages we read. Mississauga says entry to its computer science admission category is directly from high school and transfer students with post-secondary study are not eligible. Scarborough says computer science is direct from high school only.

Is ICS4U required for U of T computer science?

None of the three campus lists on U of T's central page names a computer science course. The listed requirements are English and math courses. A computer science course is still useful preparation for the work.

When is the U of T application deadline?

For 2027 admissions, U of T's deadlines table listed January 15 as the application deadline for Arts and Science at St. George, Mississauga and Scarborough, with November 7 as the recommended early date and February 1 for supporting documents.

Do co-op credits count toward the U of T average?

U of T's central page says the co-op component of Grade 12 courses will not be used for admission purposes. The base requirement is six subjects at the 4U or M level, including English.

Will you help with the U of T supplementary application?

No. We do not produce or revise application material of any kind. We teach Grade 12 math and programming so that a student's own skills and projects are real.

What class times are available?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Classes run on Eastern time, and the hour is reconfirmed after the 1 November 2026 change and again after 14 March 2027.
