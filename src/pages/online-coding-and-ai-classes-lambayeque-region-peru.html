The region in figures

What is the population of the Lambayeque region, province by province?

The cities people name

In the 2017 census, the last one with city figures, INEI counted 552,508 people in the city of Chiclayo, a built-up area that runs across several districts. INEI has not yet published city totals from the 2025 census, so that 2017 figure is still the newest one for the city as a whole.

INEI's 2017 directory of centros poblados also lists the separate places inside and around it: Chiclayo itself with 270,208 people at 28 metres above sea level, José Leonardo Ortiz with 156,498, La Victoria with 86,024, the town of Lambayeque with 58,276, Ferreñafe with 33,526 and Monsefú with 23,969. Each of those is one centro poblado, and none of them is a total for the metropolitan area.

School-age children in 2025

The 2025 census counted 142,542 people aged 6 to 11 in the department and 147,164 aged 12 to 17. Those two age bands line up closely with primaria and secundaria, so together they give a feel for how many young learners the region has right now.

These are people counted in person (población censada), which INEI publishes by age; the department total above also includes an estimate for people the census missed. That is why the age figures look a little lower than a share of 1,404,633 would suggest.

Lambayeque is usually pictured as coast: rice fields, the sea at Pimentel and the pyramids of Túcume. The regional school list used later on this page tells another story, because its highest school sits at 3,467 metres in the district of Incahuasi, in the Ferreñafe highlands. A live online class reaches both ends of the department the same way, which is one reason families outside Chiclayo city ask about it.

What a class actually needs

A live class needs a screen big enough to see code, a microphone, and a connection that can hold a video call for an hour. A laptop or desktop is ideal. A tablet with a keyboard works for Scratch. A phone is fine for the free first class and for listening, but too small for typing Python week after week.

If your home has internet but no computer, tell us before the first class. The mentor will plan the first weeks around what you have, and we will be honest if the course you want needs more.

Where online beats travel

From Olmos or Mórrope to central Chiclayo is a long trip for an evening class, and Incahuasi is further still. Online, the same mentor reaches a learner in La Victoria and one in Ferreñafe at the same moment, with nobody on the road after dark.

Because everything happens on screen, every learner also practises a skill school rarely teaches on purpose: managing files, sharing a screen, and explaining a problem clearly to someone who is not in the room.

What school covers

Do schools in the Lambayeque region teach programming?

Not as a subject. Lambayeque's schools follow the national curriculum, the CNEB, which has no computing or programming area; digital skills are expected inside every subject instead. The regional education office is putting its effort into maths, with technology as a tool.

What the regional office reports

On 22 July 2026, the Gerencia Regional de Educación de Lambayeque reported the close of its "IV Programa de Actualización en Matemática y TIC 2026", run with the Universidad Nacional Pedro Ruiz Gallo. The programme lasted 120 pedagogical hours over eight weeks and trained 84 maths teachers from the UGEL of Chiclayo, Lambayeque and Ferreñafe, with emphasis on GeoGebra and artificial intelligence.

That is a sign of where the region is heading: teachers using software and AI to teach maths better. It is not the same as pupils learning to write programs themselves, which is the part we add.

The size of the system

INEI's compendium, using MINEDU's school census, counts 115.8 thousand pupils in public primaria and 87.5 thousand in public secundaria in Lambayeque in 2025, and 21,826 teachers across all levels and sectors. Private schools add more pupils on top of the public figures.

Across Peru, MINEDU counted 53,631 schools and 6,225,000 students when the 2026 school year began on 16 March 2026. Lambayeque follows the same national curriculum, so what is missing from the timetable in Lima is missing here too.

Our classes are not a school and lead to no Peruvian qualification. A child's colegio stays in charge of marks, promotion and certificates. What we add is regular, hands-on practice: the learner writes a program, runs it, finds out why it is wrong, and explains the fix out loud to a mentor. A real Lambayeque project

Why do four honest percentages add up to 101?

The Gobierno Regional de Lambayeque publishes the list of every school service in the region as open data. Our students use it to meet a problem that every newspaper, school report and chart maker faces: round each share to a whole number and the total may not come out at 100.

The data

The Gobierno Regional de Lambayeque releases "Centros Educativos en la Región Lambayeque actualizados a junio 2025" through the national open-data portal (Plataforma Nacional de Datos Abiertos), with ODC-By, the Open Data Commons Attribution Licence, as its terms. Our copy, data_iiee_rl.csv, taken on 9 November 2026, holds 3,616 rows dated 1 July 2025.

Each row is one school service, not one building: a primaria and a secundaria that share a site are two rows with the same Código Local. The column "Nivel / Modalidad" says which kind of service it is, from Inicial No Escolarizado to Superior Tecnológica.

The question

What share of the region's school services are inicial, primaria, secundaria and everything else? A student groups the sixteen kinds of service into those four stages, counts them, divides by the total and rounds each share to a whole percentage, the way a newspaper would print it.

Then they add the four percentages up as a check, expecting 100. The program below does exactly that.

Nothing is wrong with the arithmetic. The exact shares are 49.585% for inicial, 30.725% for primaria, 13.772% for secundaria and 5.918% for the rest. Every one of them rounds up, so each gains a little, and the little gains add up to a whole extra point. Change the grouping and the total can just as easily come out at 99. Rounded parts do not have to add up to a rounded whole.

There are two honest ways out. One is to print one decimal place and say that the parts may not sum exactly because of rounding. The other, when a chart or a table must show whole numbers that total 100, is the largest-remainder method: round every share down, see how many points are missing, and give them one at a time to the shares that lost the most when they were cut.

Rounded down, the four shares are 49, 30, 13 and 5, which make 97. Three points are missing. The leftovers are 0.918 for "other", 0.772 for secundaria, 0.725 for primaria and 0.585 for inicial, so the first three get a point each and inicial stays at 49. The total is now exactly 100. The price is that inicial shows as 49 even though 49.585 rounds to 50 on its own: no method can make every part look rounded the usual way and still hit 100, so a careful writer chooses one and says which.

  1. Under 9, with counters. Share 10 sweets between 3 children as fairly as possible. Who gets the extra one, and why? That is the largest-remainder idea with no computer at all.
  2. Scratch, around 9 to 11. Make a list of four numbers, divide each by the total, round, and show the sum on screen. Try lists until the sum is 99.
  3. First Python, around 11 to 13. Run Version 1, then explain in your own words why 50 + 6 + 31 + 14 makes 101.
  4. Ages 13 and over. Write Version 2 yourself before reading ours, then repeat the shares for each province separately. Which provinces add up to 100 without any help?
  5. Going further. Count distinct buildings instead of services, using Código Local, and decide what to do with the 737 zeros before you trust the answer.

The ONEM in Lambayeque, 2026

The Gerencia Regional de Educación reported on 21 August 2026 that more than 1,700 students from the provinces of Chiclayo, Lambayeque and Ferreñafe took part in the UGEL stage of Peru's national school maths olympiad, the ONEM. More than 950 secundaria students sat it in the UGEL Chiclayo and 752 in the UGEL Lambayeque.

Nationally, the 2026 school stage was held on Wednesday 8 July 2026, with three levels (1° and 2°, 3° and 4°, and 5° de secundaria) and a paper of ten questions in two hours. Students enter through their own school; we are not connected with MINEDU, the GRE or the ONEM.

What we do about it

A satisfactory result in maths depends on reading a problem carefully, choosing a method and checking the answer. Those are the same habits a good programmer uses, which is why our maths and coding classes share them.

For olympiad preparation we write our own problems in the ONEM spirit and never copy the organiser's papers. The percentages project above is one example: a short question that rewards thinking before calculating. Families who want maths taught in English as its own subject can read about our maths teachers in English for Peru.

A practice problem of ours. Three classes have 11, 13 and 16 pupils, so the shares of the total are 27.5%, 32.5% and 40%. Rounded to whole numbers, what do they add up to? Using the largest-remainder method, which class gives up its rounding? (Ours, written for this page. Answer: 28 + 33 + 40 = 101 when each rounds half up; rounding down gives 27, 32 and 40, one point is missing, and the two leftovers of 0.5 tie, so a rule for ties must be chosen and stated, for example the larger class first, giving 27, 33 and 40.)

Whenever suits you. Chiclayo shares one clock with the rest of Peru, five hours behind UTC in every month, and mentors fit their day to yours.

Peru keeps UTC minus 5 hours with no summer time (tz database zone America/Lima; Peru last changed its clocks in 1994). India sits at UTC+5:30 and never changes its clocks either, which keeps it 10 hours 30 minutes ahead of Chiclayo on every date.

A slot agreed in the summer holidays is still the same slot in the school year, because neither country moves its clocks. Online coding and AI classes in Lambayeque: questions and answers

Are the classes in Spanish?

No. All teaching is in English, live and online, with no Spanish-language groups. Mentors keep their English slow and clear, follow the learner's accent and speed, and when a child slips into Spanish they reply with the English word.

Do you have a centre in Chiclayo?

No. All our classes are online, so there is no centre to visit. A learner in Chiclayo, José Leonardo Ortiz, Lambayeque, Ferreñafe or Incahuasi joins from home in exactly the same way.

From what age can my child start?

Six is the youngest, using Scratch blocks with no typing. Typed Python usually follows at nine or ten. A teenager with no experience can begin at thirteen, and adults are welcome at any point in life.

Do schools in Lambayeque teach coding?

Not as a subject. The national curriculum treats digital skills as a competency inside every area, and the secundaria timetable has no computing subject. The regional office has trained maths teachers in GeoGebra and AI, which helps teaching, but pupils rarely write programs of their own at school.

What time are the classes?

Any day, any time, flexible: choose a batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at the hour that suits your family. Peru is always 10 hours 30 minutes behind India, so 08:00 in Chiclayo is 18:30 for the mentor.

Is our internet good enough?

Usually. In 2025, INEI found an internet service in 58.8% of Lambayeque households. A class needs a connection that holds a video call for an hour; a laptop or desktop is best, and the free class is a good test of your set-up.

Can you help with the ONEM?

Yes, by training careful reasoning on problems our own team writes; the organiser's papers are never copied. Entry is through the student's colegio, and the 2026 school round took place on 8 July. Modern Age Coders has no link with MINEDU, the GRE Lambayeque or the olympiad itself.

Can university students and adults join?

Yes. Adults have their own groups. Most begin with Python, then branch into spreadsheets and data or AI tools, scheduled around lectures or a working day.

How are fees charged?

Once a month, by card, in US dollars, with your bank doing the conversion to soles. Prices shown to someone browsing from India appear in rupees instead. Nothing is charged for the first class.

Will my child get a certificate?

Where a course includes one, it is a Modern Age Coders completion certificate that records the work done. No Peruvian body issues or backs it: not MINEDU, a UGEL, a university or a school, and it carries no school credit.

What is the Lambayeque project on this page?

An original Python lesson on the regional government's open list of school services: rounding four shares gives 101, and the largest-remainder method fixes the total at 100. Older children and teenagers work through it in class.
