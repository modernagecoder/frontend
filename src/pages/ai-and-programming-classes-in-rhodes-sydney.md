Signature project

## Borrowing a year from 337 stations

Two forecasters predict Rhodes Station's entries for March to August 2026. One sees only the last few months of Rhodes. The other learns the seasonal shape from every other station with a complete record, then adjusts it to Rhodes.

| Months of Rhodes data seen | Rhodes alone | With the borrowed shape |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 12.81% | 10.96% |
| 3 | 16.60% | 11.03% |
| 6 | 8.96% | 8.65% |
| 12 | 7.10% | 6.97% |

### Why three months is worst

December 2025 to February 2026 were among Rhodes's quietest months, so a model that sees only them guesses far too low for autumn and winter.

### What transfer adds

The other stations already know that March is busy and January is quiet. Rhodes only has to supply its own level.

### When it stops helping

With a full year of its own data the gap almost closes, 7.10% against 6.97%, because Rhodes can now see its own seasons.

| Months seen | Transfer better at | Median error alone | Median error with transfer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3 | 295 stations | 14.78% | 10.11% |
| 12 | 269 stations | 9.42% | 9.05% |

### The baseline that beat both

Simply using each month's value from 2025 gave Rhodes an error of 5.25%, better than either model, and a median of 8.69% across all stations. Learners must report that. A clever method that loses to the obvious one is a finding, not a failure.

Entries are Transport for NSW's published monthly counts; the 338 stations are those with a number for every one of the 23 months. Models, errors and station counts are our own calculation, and they forecast counts only, never reasons.

## The idea inside modern AI

Large AI models are pretrained on huge general data, then adapted to a narrow task with little data of its own. The station project is that idea at a size a learner can check by hand.

| Step | Station project | Large AI model |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Learn general patterns | Seasonal shape from 337 stations | Language or images from vast data |
| Adapt to one case | Level from a few Rhodes months | Fine-tuning on a small task set |
| Test honestly | Six unseen months | Held-out test examples |

Learners code both forecasters on logged counts, slide the number of months seen from one to seventeen, and compare against the same-month-last-year baseline. They then write a short note on when transfer is worth it and when it is not, which is the judgement a data team is paid for.

## Can a Rhodes learner tell when an AI forecast is worse than a simple guess?

Only by running the simple guess too.

An assistant asked for a forecasting model will usually return something impressive and rarely a baseline. On Rhodes Station the transfer model beat the model that saw only Rhodes, yet both lost to last year's numbers.

A learner who has built all three knows to ask for the baseline first and to judge every model against it. That habit is what separates a working AI system from an expensive demo.

In 2026 the Rhodes learners who check AI results against honest baselines will be the ones whose models get used. The longer argument is in [why teenagers should still learn to code in 2026](/blog/is-coding-worth-learning-2026).

### What is the population of Rhodes?

Rhodes had 11,453 residents at the 2021 Census, with 3,314 families, 5,871 private dwellings and a median age of 32.

### What is transfer learning?

Transfer learning means training a model on a large general data set and then adapting it to a specific task with little data. It lets a model perform well where its own data is scarce.

### What is the Rhodes station project?

Learners forecast Rhodes Station's monthly entries for March to August 2026. With three months of its own data the error was 16.60%; borrowing the seasonal shape from 337 other stations cut it to 11.03%.

### Did the AI model beat a simple method?

No. Using the same month from 2025 gave 5.25% error for Rhodes, better than both models, and learners are taught to report that plainly.
