# Coding Classes in Illinois

> The Education and Workforce Equity Act gives every Illinois student the opportunity to take at least one computer science course from the 2023 to 2024 school year. That is a floor, not a ceiling, and many students clear it in a term. Modern Age Coders teaches what comes next, live, for ages 6 to 67, in batches of five to eight or privately, at USD 100 and USD 150 a month for eight classes. The opening class is free.

## What HB 2170 requires

HB 2170, the **Education and Workforce Equity Act**, was signed in March 2021 and championed by the **Illinois Legislative Black Caucus**.

| Requirement | From |
|---|---|
| Every student offered the opportunity to take at least one computer science course aligned to rigorous learning standards | 2023 to 2024 school year |
| High school students take one year of a course including **intensive instruction in computer literacy** | 2022 to 2023 school year |
| Districts ensure students gain computer literacy skills beginning in elementary school | Ongoing |
| State Board of Education develops computer science learning standards | Delivered by end of 2021 |

Read the first row carefully. The obligation is to **offer the opportunity**. That was a deliberate and defensible equity choice, removing the situation where a child in one district has computer science and a child twenty minutes away has none.

But a floor is a floor. The law does not say the course must be taught by somebody who has written software, does not say what happens in year two for a student who finished year one in October, and does not say the course leads anywhere.

**Chicago went further, earlier.** Chicago Public Schools made computer science a high school graduation requirement in 2016, ahead of the state and ahead of almost every large district in the country. That is a district rule, not a state one. Students in Naperville, Schaumburg or Aurora fall under the state provisions instead.

**On AP computer science and the maths requirement:** it is widely reported that an AP CS course may count toward the three years of mathematics required for graduation. We could not confirm the current provision at primary source, so we do not state it as settled. Ask your counsellor for the rule in force for your child's cohort.

## When you probably do not need us

Illinois provision is uneven, and several suburban districts with large South Asian communities run genuinely strong programmes. If that is your school, duplicating it would waste your money.

Four questions, asked of the school:

| Ask | What the answer tells you |
|---|---|
| Which CS courses run this year, and which ran last year? | A course that appears and disappears is a staffing gap, not a programme |
| What does a student take the year after the introductory course? | If the answer is nothing, that is the year we exist for |
| Has the teacher written software outside a classroom? | Not disqualifying, but it changes how far a keen student goes |
| Does any course reach data not invented for the lesson? | The biggest difference between a course and an exercise |

If the answers are good, use the school and come to us for mathematics, or later.

## The Illinois student data project

Each market gets a project on local data containing one specific way of being wrong. Illinois gets the one that fools people who already know some machine learning.

The **Chicago Park District** monitors Lake Michigan beach water quality, testing for E. coli, and issues a swim advisory when the predicted level reaches at least 235 **Colony Forming Units** per 100 millilitres. The Chicago Data Portal publishes lab results, advisories and model predictions daily through the season, and the City has run the forecasting problem publicly with volunteer data scientists and university students.

A student builds a classifier: given today's conditions, will this beach need an advisory? It reports around ninety-five percent accuracy.

**The trap.** Most beach-days are fine. If advisories occur on roughly one day in twenty, a model that ignores every input and answers "safe" every time is also about ninety-five percent accurate. It has learned nothing and will never issue a warning. Accuracy on an imbalanced problem measures the imbalance, not the model.

This forces the confusion matrix, then precision and recall, not as vocabulary but as the answer to a question the student actually has. And it forces the point we most want a fourteen year old to keep: a false alarm closes a beach on a good day, a missed advisory sends children into contaminated water, and deciding how to weigh those is not a mathematical question. There is a step in every serious machine learning problem where the mathematics stops and somebody must judge consequences.

Students pull current data from the Chicago portal themselves.
