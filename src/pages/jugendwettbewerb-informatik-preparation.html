Jugendwettbewerb Informatik preparation

The Jugendwettbewerb Informatik is where many pupils in Germany write their first real programs under a clock. Its online rounds use a block editor, and from 2027 Python as well, with three levels that pupils choose for themselves. This page sets out the 2027 dates, what each level tests, how the new 26-point rule for round 3 works, and the one habit that helps most in every level: tracing a program by hand before pressing run.

The contest

What is the Jugendwettbewerb Informatik?

A national programming contest from BWINF for pupils with little or no programming experience, run in three rounds, with training material free all year on jwinf.de.

How it works

Rounds 1 and 2 are online on jwinf.de. Unlike the Informatik-Biber, BWINF says, the pupil programs here, in a visual environment: blocks that move a figure, repeat, decide and remember. From 2027 the same tasks can also be solved in Python.

Round 3 is different. Pupils who qualify solve two harder tasks at home with their own programming tools, alone or in a team of up to four. These are the two junior tasks of the Bundeswettbewerb Informatik, and the work can be handed in to both contests.

Who takes part

BWINF recommends the contest from Klasse 5 and allows it from Klasse 3. In rounds 1 and 2 pupils may work alone or in pairs.

The 2026 edition was the 10th. BWINF reports that 73,272 pupils took part in round 1 of 2026. In round 2, 25,777 pupils took the Silver level and 4,725 the Gold level.

The 2026 round 3 is running now, together with round 1 of the 45th Bundeswettbewerb, and closes on 23 November 2026. BWINF's rules keep solution ideas private until that deadline, so those two tasks stay out of our classes until it has passed.

What changes in 2027

Two things. Python joins Blockly as a language for the online rounds. And the step-by-step unlocking of levels is gone: in 2027 every level of rounds 1 and 2 is open to everyone, including pupils who missed round 1.

BWINF says the recommended school years, the number of tasks, the times and the maximum points stay as they were.

A sensible plan from now (ours)

Bronze, Silver, Gold

Which level should my child choose, and what does each one test?

The levels build on each other: Silver includes everything in Bronze, and Gold everything in Silver. BWINF links each to a school year as a guide, not a rule.

Because a pupil may now try more than one level, the choice is less risky than it looks. A child in Klasse 6 who is comfortable with nested loops can attempt Bronze and then Silver in the same round. Our advice is to start a level below where you think you are, finish it calmly, and climb. Every task also comes in three versions, so even a hard level has an easy first step.

Points and round 3

How many points does a pupil need for round 3 in 2027?

Twenty-six, from two levels added together. Here is how the counting works, and what it means in practice.

The rule, from BWINF

Each task has an easy, a medium and a hard version, worth 2, 3 and 4 points, and only the hardest version a pupil solves counts. For 2027, round 3 needs at least 26 points from two levels: either the highest-scoring level of round 1 plus the highest-scoring level of round 2, or the Silver and Gold levels of round 2 together.

What that adds up to (our arithmetic)

With 4 points as the top score per task, Bronze can give at most 12 points, Silver 16 and Gold 24. No single level reaches 26, which is why two levels count. A pupil who does Bronze in round 1 and Silver in round 2 can reach 28 at most, so needs almost every hard version. Two Gold levels allow up to 48.

The table explains our advice for an ambitious pupil: aim to be ready for Silver in round 1 and Gold in round 2. That route leaves room for mistakes; the Bronze route leaves almost none. For a younger child the point is different. Bronze is a real achievement, and round 3 can wait a year.

Practice, ours

How should a pupil practise? Trace the program before running it

The two programs and four exercises below are our own, made for this page; none comes from a BWINF contest. Every one is solved the same way: write down what each variable holds after each step, predict the result, and only then run it. BWINF's own platform now shows variable values while a program runs; a pupil who can do that in their head is ready for Silver and beyond.

Program 1: the spiral robot (ours)

A robot starts at (0, 0) facing east. On side 1 it moves 1 step, on side 2 it moves 2, and so on to side 4, turning left after each side. Before running the code, fill in the robot's position after every side.

Worked solution. Side 1: one step east to (1, 0), turn to face north. Side 2: two steps north to (1, 2), face west. Side 3: three steps west to (-2, 2), face south. Side 4: four steps south to (-2, -2), face east again.

Why the turn works

A direction is stored as a pair: east is (1, 0), north is (0, 1). Turning left swaps the two numbers and changes the sign of the new first one, so (1, 0) becomes (0, 1), and (0, 1) becomes (-1, 0), which is west.

The nested loop is the Bronze idea: the outer loop counts sides, the inner loop repeats steps, and the inner count depends on the outer one.

Program 2 uses three variables that change at different moments, the Silver and Gold idea in miniature. The common mistake is to forget that streak goes back to zero on an empty cell, which would make longest come out as 4. The trace table catches that before the computer does, and in a timed round that saves minutes.

  1. Exercise 1 (Bronze idea). Change Program 1 to run for 6 sides. Where does the robot end, and which way does it face? (Answer: at (3, 4) facing west. Side 5 takes five steps east to (3, -2) and turns north; side 6 takes six steps north to (3, 4) and turns west.)
  2. Exercise 2 (Silver idea). In Program 2, change the path to "**.***.*". Predict coins and longest run. (Answer: coins 6, longest run 3.)
  3. Exercise 3 (round 2 Silver idea). Turn Program 2 into a function that takes the path as a parameter and returns both numbers. Call it on two different paths.
  4. Exercise 4 (round 2 Gold idea). Store the position of every coin in a list while you walk, then print the list. For the original path it should be [2, 4, 5, 8].

Signing up

How does a pupil sign up, with or without the school?

Either way works. Unlike the Informatik-Biber, the Jugendwettbewerb lets pupils register themselves.

Through the school

A teacher registers on BWINF's system and creates a group on jwinf.de. Pupils join with a group code once, then always log in with their personal login code, which BWINF advises them to note down and keep safe.

There is no separate entry form: a pupil with a login code simply takes part during the contest dates.

On your own

A pupil creates a BWINF account at login.bwinf.de and logs in to jwinf.de with it. BWINF says pupils who did not take part through a school must enter their postal address in that account to receive a certificate and prize.

For a team of two, each member needs their own login code.

We are not connected with BWINF and we do not register pupils or enter anyone in the contest. A pupil who prepares with us signs up in one of the two ways above, and does every contest task alone or with their own teammate, exactly as BWINF's rules expect.

Jugendwettbewerb Informatik preparation: common questions

Are the classes in German?

No. Lessons are live and given in English only; the mentor follows your child's pace and accent. Blockly and Python both use simple English words, so the contest itself is easy to follow.

When is round 1 of the Jugendwettbewerb Informatik 2027?

BWINF gives 22 February to 7 March 2027, with a practice contest from 1 February 2027. Round 2 runs from 12 to 25 April 2027 and round 3 from 1 September to 15 November 2027.

Can my child use Python in the contest?

From 2027, yes. BWINF says rounds 1 and 2 can be solved in Blockly or in Python. Round 3 is solved at home with any programming tools.

How many points are needed for round 3?

For 2027, at least 26 points from two levels: the top-scoring level of round 1 plus the top-scoring level of round 2, or Silver and Gold of round 2 together. Each task gives 2, 3 or 4 points depending on the hardest version solved.

Does my child have to enter through school?

No. Pupils can create their own BWINF account, or join a group their teacher sets up. Pupils who enter on their own add their postal address to receive a certificate.

Which level should my child start with?

BWINF suggests Bronze for Klasse 5 and 6, Silver for 7 to 9 and Gold from Klasse 10, and all levels are open. We usually advise starting one level lower and climbing, since several levels may be attempted.

Can you go through this year's round 3 tasks with my child?

Not before the deadline of 23 November 2026, because BWINF wants solution ideas kept private until then. Until that date we teach with tasks we write ourselves. We are not connected with BWINF.

When are classes in German time?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Most pupils come after school or on a weekend morning.

What currency are the fees in?

The euro figure on this page comes from the European Central Bank's reference rate; the actual charge is in US dollars, and your bank does the conversion. The trial class is free and asks for no card.

Is the Jugendwettbewerb a good step after the Biber?

Yes. BWINF describes it as the programming contest for pupils with little or no experience, and its round 3 shares tasks with the Bundeswettbewerb Informatik, the next step up.
