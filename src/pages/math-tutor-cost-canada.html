How to read the spread

The table runs from nothing at all to four times the low end of the private market, and every row is honest. The difference is mostly about who is teaching and how much of the hour belongs to your child. A free provincial service answers the question in front of the student tonight. A registry tutor is usually a university student who did well in the course. A firm adds matching, scheduling and a manager to call when something goes wrong.

The rows also price different things. Mathify and Alloprof are help when a student is stuck. A tutor or a class is a plan that runs week after week. A self-paced subscription is a library of lessons with nobody watching the work. Comparing their figures directly is like comparing a bus fare with a car lease.

Why the registry range is so wide

On the Alberta listing, the cheapest high school math tutors list $15 CAD and the dearest $80 CAD an hour. Of the 238 profiles under High School Math that day, 46 gave no figure at all, and the most common stated rate, on 41 profiles, was $30 CAD. The middle of the 192 numbers also fell at that figure. Those counts are ours, made from the page as it stood.

The Calgary students’ union takes a different approach and publishes a guideline instead of a list. Its high school band is lower than most Alberta listings, which tells you something useful: a guideline is a floor for negotiation, not a quote. Calgary says so itself, noting that the tutor chooses the price and that there is often room to negotiate.

Before you pay anything

Free math help in Canada, and what free covers

Two provinces fund a free service large enough to matter. If your child lives in Ontario or Quebec, try these first: a week of free help tells you a great deal about what kind of paid help, if any, is worth buying.

Ontario: TVO Learn Mathify

TVO’s own one-pager describes “FREE 1:1 on demand math tutoring with Ontario Certified Math Teachers” for “Grade 4-12 students in Ontario”. Sessions happen on a shared whiteboard with audio and text, and the leaflet lists “recorded sessions, and ability to choose preferred tutors”.

The hours printed on it are “Monday to Friday: 9am - 9pm ET” and “Saturday and Sunday: 3pm - 9pm ET”. Registration has one condition worth knowing before a crisis evening: “Students require an OEN.” That is the Ontario Education Number on the report card, and a Toronto District School Board secondary school reminds its students that it is not the board student number.

Quebec: Alloprof

Alloprof’s About page says that “Since 1996, Alloprof has been developing professional services and digital academic support resources and making them available free of charge to all Quebec students and their parents.” Its team, the same page says, numbers “over 200 employees, including teachers”.

Its site lists help from Grade 1 to Secondary 5, which covers the whole of Quebec’s school math, including the Secondary 4 and 5 sequences families worry about most. An English version of the site exists; check which services run in English before relying on it.

What free help does well

On-demand help is excellent at one thing: getting a student unstuck on a specific question while the homework is still open. A certified teacher on a whiteboard for fifteen minutes can save an evening of frustration, and it costs the family nothing but the time to log in.

Free help also gives you evidence. If your child uses Mathify three times in a week on the same kind of question, you have learned which idea is shaky. Bring that list to any paid tutor and the first session starts in the right place.

What free help is not designed to do

An on-demand service sees a different student every few minutes. It cannot follow one child from September to June, notice that fractions are the real problem behind a Grade 9 algebra mark, or build a plan for an MHF4U final. Nobody on the other end knows what last week looked like.

Outside Ontario and Quebec, we could not confirm a province-wide free one-to-one math service on official pages that would load for us on 31 October 2026. Ask your school about extra-help periods and your public library about homework help; many libraries list it, and the details differ by city.

A wage is not a fee

These are the hourly earnings of people employed as tutors, not the price a parent pays. When a centre or an agency employs the tutor, the fee also has to cover the room, the manager, the scheduling, the advertising and the hours the tutor is paid but not teaching. That is why a firm’s hourly price normally sits well above the wage of the person in the chair.

A self-employed tutor keeps the whole fee, but carries the unpaid time: travel, preparation, marking and the empty slot when a family cancels. So the gap between the wage table and the marketplace range is not profit alone. Much of it is the cost of an hour of teaching that someone has to absorb.

Why the median barely moves between cities

Searches like “math tutor Toronto price” and “private tutor cost Vancouver” assume the big cities cost far more. On the wage side, Job Bank’s medians sit close together: the Toronto Region median is $20.62 CAD an hour, the Lower Mainland and Southwest region of BC $20.00 CAD, the Edmonton Region $22.02 CAD. The gap between low and high inside one city is far wider than the gap between cities.

City still matters for in-person tutoring, because rent and travel time differ, but the person teaching is paid on a similar scale across the country. When a quote in one city is double another, ask what is different about the teacher, the format or the extras, rather than putting it down to the postcode.

Course codes and exam seasons

Families do not usually search for a tutor in October of Grade 9. They search the week after an Advanced Functions unit test, in the run-up to the Alberta Math 30-1 diploma exam, or when a Quebec Secondary 5 student is choosing between sequences. Demand bunches around those moments, and so does the tutor’s calendar.

If your child is heading into a senior course, booking help at the start of the semester is cheaper in every sense. A tutor found in September can work at a normal pace; one found the week before a final has to compress a term into a few sessions, and the family pays for every one of them.

Credentials you can check

TVO describes Mathify’s tutors as Ontario Certified Teachers, which is the standard a public service can set. For a private tutor, ask what training they have and how many students they have taught in the exact course. A student tutor who scored well in MCV4U last year may explain it more clearly than someone who has not touched calculus in a decade.

University registries do not vet teaching quality in the way an employer might. Alberta’s registry lists graduates of its own tutor training program, while Calgary’s states plainly that registry tutors are not its employees, contractors or agents. Both are honest about what a listing is: an introduction, not a guarantee.

A method, ours

How to work out a tutor’s real hourly cost

Quotes arrive in different units: per session, per hour, per month, per term, sometimes with a one-off fee in front. These six steps turn any of them into one comparable figure. The method is ours; it is the unit-rate idea from Grade 7 math, applied to a family decision.

  1. Write down the unit of the quote. Is the figure for a session, an hour, a month or a package? A “session” may be 45 minutes, 60 or 90. Ask, and write the length in minutes beside the figure.
  2. List every one-off charge. Assessment, registration, materials, a deposit. Ask for the full fee schedule in writing before the first session, not after it.
  3. Decide how long you expect to stay. Choose a realistic number of weeks, such as one school term. Spread the one-off charges across that period rather than ignoring them.
  4. Total the cost for the period. Session fee times number of sessions, plus the one-off charges. This total is your own calculation for your own plan, not a published price.
  5. Total the live teaching minutes. Session length times number of sessions. Leave out anything that is not a teacher working with your child: travel, setup, the minutes spent waiting for a login to work.
  6. Divide the cost by the hours. Cost for the period divided by teaching minutes, times 60, gives the real hourly figure. For a group, also divide the teaching minutes by the number of students to see how much attention each child can expect.

What to ask before you agree

What happens if we cancel the day before? Is there a minimum number of sessions or months? Who is the teacher, and will it be the same person each week? How will you report progress, and how often? A good tutor or firm answers these in one email.

Prep Academy, for one, states on its own site that families “never pre-pay” and are invoiced at the end of each month for the hours attended. Billing style is a fair thing to compare: paying in arrears, paying monthly ahead, and buying a package all carry different risks if the fit turns out to be wrong.

Why per hour is still not the whole story

Two tutors with the same hourly figure can deliver very different value. One spends the hour working last night’s homework for the student; another makes the student do the thinking and finds the gap underneath. The second hour is worth more, and no calculator will show it.

So use the real hourly figure to rule options in or out, then judge what remains on teaching. Ask for a trial session and watch who holds the pencil. If the tutor talks for most of it, the student is paying to watch someone else do math.

Worked example, ours

A worked example: three quotes for a Grade 12 student

This example is ours and every number in it is invented. Money is written as plain numbers with no currency, so the arithmetic is the point, not the prices. Imagine an Ontario student taking MHF4U who wants help once a week for a ten-week stretch before the final.

The three quotes (invented). Quote A charges 48 per 45-minute session, plus a one-off assessment of 120. Quote B charges 55 per 60-minute session, with nothing else. Quote C is a monthly plan of 150 for four 60-minute sessions, sold in whole months, so ten weeks means paying for three months and attending ten of the twelve sessions.

  1. Units first. A is per 45 minutes, B per 60 minutes, C per month of four sessions. Nothing can be compared yet.
  2. One-off charges. Only A has one: the assessment of 120. It belongs inside the total.
  3. The period. Ten weeks, one session a week, so ten sessions for A and B. For C, ten weeks spans three billed months.
  4. Totals for the period. A: 10 × 48 + 120 = 600. B: 10 × 55 = 550. C: 3 × 150 = 450.
  5. Teaching hours. A: 10 × 45 minutes = 450 minutes, which is 7.5 hours. B: 10 × 60 = 600 minutes, 10 hours. C: the student attends 10 sessions of 60 minutes, so 10 hours, although 12 were paid for.
  6. Real hourly figure. A: 600 ÷ 7.5 = 80. B: 550 ÷ 10 = 55. C: 450 ÷ 10 = 45, or 37.5 if all twelve paid sessions are used.

What the example shows

Quote A looked cheapest per session and turned out dearest per hour. Two things did it: the shorter session and the assessment charge. Neither is unfair, but neither is visible in the headline figure, which is why step one and step two come before any division.

Quote C looked like the largest number on the page and is the lowest per hour, provided the family actually uses the sessions it buys. A plan sold in whole months rewards steady attendance and penalises a family that drifts. That is worth knowing about yourselves before signing.

Try it with your own quotes

Take the quotes you have, apply the same six steps, and set out a table like the one above. Then add one more column that no calculation fills in: what each tutor said they would do in the first session. The figure in the last column decides which options are affordable; that extra column decides which one to choose.

A version of this is a fair Grade 7 or 8 practice problem, too. Ask your child to work out the three hourly figures without looking at our answers. Unit rates are on every provincial curriculum, and a real decision makes them stick.

Why many centres do not print a price

Where centres are locally run, as Sylvan’s own page explains for its locations, one national figure cannot be true in every city, so the website sends families to a local centre or an assessment instead. That is a reasonable reason, but it does mean the family learns the price late, often after an assessment visit.

When you do get a quote, run it through the six steps. Ask how long each visit lasts, how many students one instructor supervises at a time, and whether there is a minimum term. Those three answers turn a monthly figure into something you can compare with a private tutor.

Questions specific to centres

Does the child work on the school course, or on the centre’s own sequence? Some centres follow their own curriculum, which can be good for foundations and less helpful the week before an MHF4U test. Ask which it is.

How many students share one instructor? A centre session may be two hours long with one adult moving between several students. That can be good value per hour of practice and less so per minute of teaching. Both measures are worth having before you decide.

What a batch hour is, honestly

In a group of fifteen, an hour shared evenly is four minutes of individual attention each. A batch is not a cheaper version of one-to-one; it is a class, built around explanation, worked examples and practice, with questions answered for everyone. Many students learn well that way, and the price reflects it.

If your child needs someone to sit with their own test paper, find the exact misunderstanding and rebuild it, choose one to one. If they need steady teaching and practice alongside the school course, a batch is often enough. We will say which we think fits after the free class.

What we teach, and what we do not

We teach students to think through a problem, not to copy an answer, whether that answer comes from a tutor, a solutions site or an AI tool. In practice that means the student holds the pen, explains each step, and checks the result by substitution or estimate before calling it done.

We teach alongside school. For senior courses such as MHF4U, MCV4U or Math 30-1 we follow the school’s own outline and unit order, and we do not grant Ontario credits, or any other province’s: the credit and the mark stay with the school.

Times that fit a Canadian evening

Our teachers work from India, so families never pay for a tutor’s travel and nobody comes to the house. We fix class times in your own province’s clock, whether that is Eastern time in most of Ontario and Quebec or the clocks of British Columbia and Alberta.

Clocks change soon. Ontario and Quebec move back to UTC-5 on 1 November 2026 and forward again on 14 March 2027; British Columbia stays on UTC-7 all year; Alberta and Saskatchewan stay on UTC-6. We confirm every class time with each family in the week the clocks move.

How much does a math tutor cost per hour in Canada?

It ranges from free to the upper end of the private market. Ontario and Quebec fund free services, TVO Learn Mathify and Alloprof. Student tutors on university registries in Alberta set their own hourly rates, and one Canadian marketplace publishes the range most of its tutors charge. The table near the top of this page gives each published figure with its source and the date we checked it.

How much does a tutor cost per hour in Ontario?

Ontario students in Grades 4 to 12 can use TVO Learn Mathify free, with an Ontario Education Number. For paid tutoring, prices depend on the tutor's training, the course and the format. Job Bank also publishes what employed tutors in Ontario earn, which sits below what families are usually charged because a fee also covers the provider's overheads.

Is a math tutor more expensive in Toronto or Vancouver?

Not by as much as people expect, on the wage side. Job Bank's median hourly wage for secondary school tutors in the Toronto Region and in the Lower Mainland and Southwest region of BC are close to each other and to the national median. In-person tutoring in a large city can cost more because of travel and rent, so compare quotes using the real hourly method on this page.

How much does Mathnasium cost in Canada?

We could not read Mathnasium's Canadian site when we checked on 31 October 2026, so we do not print a figure and we do not repeat figures from articles about the brand. Fees at tutoring centres often differ from one location to the next. Ask the nearest centre for its fee schedule in writing, including any assessment charge and minimum commitment.

Is there free math tutoring in Canada?

Yes, in some provinces. TVO Learn Mathify offers free one-to-one online help with Ontario Certified Teachers to Ontario students in Grades 4 to 12. Alloprof has offered free academic support to Quebec students and their parents since 1996. Elsewhere, start with your school's extra-help periods and your public library's homework help pages.

Why does an MHF4U or Math 30-1 tutor cost more?

Senior courses that count towards university admission need a tutor who knows that exact course outline, and fewer tutors can teach them well. Demand also bunches before unit tests and finals. Booking help at the start of the semester lets the work run at a normal pace instead of being squeezed into the last few weeks.

How do I compare tutoring quotes fairly?

Convert every quote to the same unit. Note the session length, add any one-off charges, choose a realistic period such as a term, total the cost and the teaching hours, then divide. A cheap-looking session can turn out to be expensive per hour once a short session and an assessment charge are included. Our worked example shows the steps.

No. Our lessons sit beside the course the school runs, and we do not grant Ontario credits or any other province's credits. The school that teaches and evaluates the course awards the credit and the mark; our classes are the teaching and practice around it.
