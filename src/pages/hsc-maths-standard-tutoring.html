In short

HSC Mathematics Standard is the NSW applied mathematics course set by the Mathematics Standard 11-12 Syllabus (2024). Every student completes the Year 11 Standard course, then takes Standard 1 or Standard 2 in Year 12. It covers algebra, financial mathematics, measurement, networks and statistics. Standard 2 ends in a 100-mark HSC paper; the Standard 1 exam is optional and worth 80 marks.

One course, two endings

A shared Year 11, then Standard 1 or Standard 2

NESA's 2024 course structure says all Year 11 Standard focus areas are completed first, then the focus areas of either Standard 1 or Standard 2. The lists below are NESA's, checked on 7 October 2026.

Mathematics Standard 11-12 Syllabus (2024): focus areas
Area of studyYear 11 (all students)Year 12 Standard 1Year 12 Standard 2
AlgebraFormulas and equations; Linear relationshipsAlgebraic relationshipsAlgebraic relationships
Financial mathematicsEarning money; Managing moneyInvestment; Depreciation and loansInvestment and loans; Annuities
MeasurementApplications of measurement; Time and locationRight-angled triangles; Ratios and ratesTrigonometry; Ratios and rates
NetworksNetworks, paths and treesNot in Standard 1Network flow; Critical path analysis
StatisticsData analysisBivariate data analysis; Relative frequency and probabilityBivariate data analysis; Relative frequency and probability; The normal distribution

The columns show where the two Year 12 courses part ways. Standard 2 carries networks into network flow and critical path analysis, adds annuities to the financial work, and finishes statistics with the normal distribution. Standard 1 keeps a narrower focus, with right-angled triangles in place of full trigonometry.

NESA records the Year 11 course as 11236, and the Year 12 courses as 15231 for Standard 1 and 15236 for Standard 2, each 120 hours and 2 units. The two Year 12 courses exclude each other, so a student is in one or the other.

The changeover matches the other NSW mathematics courses. Implementation began in 2026; Year 12 classes move to the new content from Term 4 that year, and 2027 brings the first HSC paper written for it.

Choosing between Standard 1 and Standard 2 is a school and family decision, usually made at the end of Year 11. What we can do is make sure Year 11 is secure, so that whichever course a student takes, they arrive in Year 12 able to handle it.

Two different papers, one of them optional

The Standard 1 exam is the unusual one. NESA says it "is not compulsory" and that students may choose to sit it. Those who want an ATAR and sit the paper may have the mark used by UAC towards it. NESA also lists a separate enrolment, Mathematics Standard 1 (Examination), course 15232, for students who intend to sit.

NESA's 2026 key dates list work samples and assessment grades for Mathematics Standard 1 as due on 15 September 2026, which reflects how much of that course is judged through school work rather than the exam.

Standard 2 is a full HSC paper. Eighty-five marks over 35 to 40 items means short, practical questions, many built on a table, a network diagram or a graph. Reading a stimulus carefully earns as many marks as the arithmetic that follows it.

At two and a half hours for 100 marks, a student has about one and a half minutes per mark. We teach students to read every stimulus once, fully, before writing, because most lost marks in applied papers come from misreading the situation rather than miscalculating.

Annuities, one year at a time

Build the table before trusting the formula

Annuities sit in Standard 2 financial mathematics, and they are where many students switch off. A formula with powers and fractions looks like magic until the same answer is built one year at a time.

A worked lesson, written by us. A student puts 100 dollars into an account at the end of every year for five years. The account earns 5% interest per year, compounded yearly. How much is in the account straight after the fifth deposit?

The annuity built as a recurrence: last balance times 1.05, plus the new 100
End of yearBalance before depositPlus depositBalance after deposit
10100100
2105100205
3215.25100315.25
4331.0125100431.0125
5452.563125100552.563125

Each row follows one rule: grow last year's balance by 5%, then add the new deposit. After five years the account holds 552.563125 dollars, or 552.56 to the nearest cent. Of that, 500 dollars came from deposits and the rest, about 52.56, is interest earned on interest as well as on the deposits.

The future value formula for an annuity gives the same number in one line: 100 times (1.05 to the power 5, minus 1), divided by 0.05. Because 1.05 to the power 5 is 1.2762815625, the formula gives 100 times 5.52563125, which is 552.563125.

Seeing the table first changes how students treat the formula. They can now explain every symbol in it, check a calculator slip against a rough row-by-row estimate, and handle variations, such as a deposit at the start of each year, by adjusting the table instead of hunting for a different formula.

The row-by-row rule is also a tiny program: a loop that multiplies and adds. Students who code can write it in three lines of Python, and those who use spreadsheets can fill it down a column. Both are honest ways to check a formula answer before writing it in an exam.

The take-home limit means most marks are earned in class or under exam conditions, so the student needs the method in their own head. In Standard that usually means reading a context carefully, choosing the right tool, and writing the answer with units and a sentence of interpretation.

We teach every topic with original problems drawn from everyday situations. We never prepare, write or check a school task, because the mark has to reflect what the student can do alone.

Questions about Standard

What Standard students and parents ask

What is HSC Mathematics Standard?

The NSW applied mathematics course set by the Mathematics Standard 11-12 Syllabus (2024). Students take one Year 11 Standard course, then either Standard 1 or Standard 2 in Year 12, studying algebra, financial mathematics, measurement, networks and statistics.

What is the difference between Standard 1 and Standard 2?

Standard 2 covers more: network flow, critical path analysis, annuities, trigonometry and the normal distribution are in Standard 2 but not Standard 1. Standard 2 has a compulsory 100-mark HSC paper; the Standard 1 paper is optional and worth 80 marks.

Is the Standard 1 exam compulsory?

No. NESA states the Standard 1 HSC examination is not compulsory. Students who want an ATAR may sit it, and UAC may use the exam mark in calculating the ATAR.

How long is the Standard 2 exam?

2 hours and 30 minutes plus 10 minutes reading time, for a paper worth 100 marks with a reference sheet and approved calculators.

What is an annuity?

A series of equal payments made at regular intervals, such as a fixed deposit every year. Its future value adds up each payment together with the interest it earns, which can be built row by row or found with a formula.

When does the 2024 syllabus start?

NESA says it is implemented from 2026, with Year 12 teaching from Term 4 of 2026 and the first HSC examination in 2027.

Can a Standard student move to Advanced?

Course changes are decided by the school under NESA rules. We can help a student strengthen the algebra and functions that Advanced needs if a change is being considered.

How is school assessment weighted?

Half the school mark comes from what students know about the course and half from how they work mathematically, and a school program may include just one take-home task, capped at 15%.

Will you promise a particular band?

No. We teach the course thoroughly; the result depends on the student's work.

Do you help with school tasks?

We teach the mathematics with our own problems, and we never write, prepare or check work a school will assess.
