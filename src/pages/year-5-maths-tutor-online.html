Year 5 maths tutor online

Year 5 is the year primary maths changes gear. Numbers grow to a million, children meet prime numbers and cube numbers, long multiplication arrives, and fractions start to behave like numbers that can be added, compared and turned into decimals and percentages. It is also a year of quiet deadlines. A child in Year 5 now will sit the KS2 tests in May 2028, and in areas with grammar schools, an 11+ test will come round before the end of October 2027. This page sets out what the national curriculum adds in Year 5, how the year connects to what follows, what the School Admissions Code says about 11+ tests, and how we tutor Year 5 maths, with four worked examples of our own.

In short

Year 5 maths is the first year of upper Key Stage 2 in the national curriculum for England, taught to children aged 9 to 10. It extends place value to at least 1 000 000 and adds prime numbers, square and cube numbers, long multiplication by a two-digit number, thousandths, and percentages as "number of parts per hundred". Children in Year 5 in 2026 to 2027 sit the KS2 tests in May 2028. Year 5 content

What do children learn in Year 5 maths?

The Department for Education's programme of study lists what must be taught in Year 5. These are the statutory requirements, grouped by area and summarised from the document.

The curriculum describes the aim of upper Key Stage 2 like this: "The principal focus of mathematics teaching in upper key stage 2 is to ensure that pupils extend their understanding of the number system and place value to include larger integers." It also signals a new direction: "With this foundation in arithmetic, pupils are introduced to the language of algebra as a means for solving a variety of problems." The table shows what that means in Year 5 specifically.

Year 5 maths in the national curriculum for England (statutory content, summarised)
AreaWhat Year 5 adds
Place valueNumbers to at least 1 000 000; rounding to the nearest 10, 100, 1000, 10 000 and 100 000; negative numbers in context; Roman numerals to 1000 (M)
Properties of numberFactors, factor pairs and common factors; prime numbers, prime factors and composite numbers; primes up to 19 recalled; square and cube numbers
CalculationColumn addition and subtraction with more than 4 digits; long multiplication for two-digit numbers; short division with remainders interpreted in context
FractionsCompare and order; equivalent fractions; mixed numbers and improper fractions; add and subtract with related denominators; multiply by whole numbers
Decimals and percentagesThousandths; numbers with up to three decimal places; per cent as "number of parts per hundred"; percentage equivalents of halves, quarters, fifths and tenths
MeasurementMetric conversions; approximate imperial equivalents such as inches, pounds and pints; perimeter of composite shapes; area of rectangles; estimating volume
GeometryAcute, obtuse and reflex angles, measured with a protractor; angles at a point (360°) and on a line (180°); regular and irregular polygons; reflection and translation
StatisticsProblems using line graphs; reading and interpreting tables, including timetables

Two notes in the programme of study explain why Year 5 can feel hard. The first is about division: the curriculum wants children to express "non-integer answers to division" in more than one way, and gives an example in which 98 divided by 4 is written as 24 remainder 2, as 24 and a half, as 24.5, and rounded to 25. The second is about connections: "Pupils should be taught throughout that percentages, decimals and fractions are different ways of expressing proportions." A Year 5 child is no longer learning separate topics. They are learning that one quantity can wear several notations.

Taken from the Year 5 section of the DfE's primary mathematics curriculum, including its non-statutory notes, checked on 31 October 2026.

Year 5 to Year 6

How does Year 5 maths lead into Year 6 and the KS2 SATs?

Year 5 has no national test of its own, but it sits between two that do. The dates below come from the Department for Education's published primary assessment calendar.

Where Year 5 sits for a child in Year 5 during 2026 to 2027 (dates from DfE, Primary assessments: future dates; the year mapping is ours)
WhenWhatWho sits it

Year 5 is therefore the last full school year before every deadline. The Year 6 curriculum goes further (ratio and proportion, algebra, long division, and multiplying and dividing fractions), and the curriculum's stated target is that "By the end of year 6, pupils should be fluent in written methods for all four operations, including long multiplication and division". Long multiplication for two-digit numbers starts in Year 5. A child who leaves Year 5 secure in it, and in short division with remainders, starts Year 6 with the hardest written methods half learned.

Times tables still matter

The Year 4 multiplication tables check is the last national check of times tables. Year 5 assumes them: the curriculum says pupils "apply all the multiplication tables and related division facts frequently, commit them to memory and use them confidently".

Gaps here show up immediately in long multiplication, factors and fractions. Our Year 4 times tables page covers the check.

Assessment this year

For this school year the DfE has confirmed that nothing changes, although arrangements from 2027 to 2028 onwards are being reconsidered after the Curriculum and Assessment Review.

The 11+ year

Is Year 5 the right time to prepare for the 11+?

For families in grammar school areas, usually yes, because the test comes early in Year 6. What the test contains is decided locally, so the honest preparation is the Year 5 and 6 curriculum done well, plus familiarity with the local format.

There is no single national 11+. The School Admissions Code says "It is for the admission authority to decide the content of the test", and requires that tests "must be clear, objective, and give an accurate reflection of the child's ability or aptitude". It also sets the timing: admission authorities must "take all reasonable steps to inform parents of the outcome of selection tests before the closing date for secondary applications on 31 October", "while making clear that this does not equate to a guarantee of a selective place".

What that means for a Year 5 family

Because results must reach parents before 31 October of Year 6, the test itself falls before that date. For a child in Year 5 in 2026 to 2027, that is a test in 2027 (our reading of the Code's timing, not a local date).

Check the exact date and registration deadline with your own local authority or grammar school consortium, since each sets its own.

Who can select

The Code is clear that "Only designated grammar schools are permitted to select their entire intake on the basis of high academic ability". Outside those areas there is no 11+ to prepare for, and the Year 6 SATs are the next milestone.

Our 11+ maths page covers preparation itself, and area pages such as Kent and Buckinghamshire cover local tests.

Whatever the local test, most 11+ maths questions draw on Key Stage 2 content, and some of it is taught in Year 6, after the test has been sat. That is the real reason Year 5 matters: a child who is secure in Year 5 work, and has met a little Year 6 content such as ratio and simple algebra, is not meeting new topics for the first time under exam conditions.

Source: Department for Education, School Admissions Code 2021, paragraphs 1.18 and 1.31 to 1.32, read on 31 October 2026.

Worked examples

Four Year 5 examples we wrote, worked step by step

Our own questions on statutory Year 5 content. None is taken from a test paper.

1. Long multiplication by a two-digit number

Question (ours). 346 × 27.

Split the 27. 27 is 20 + 7, so work out 346 × 7 and 346 × 20.

Two rows. 346 × 7 = 2422. 346 × 20 = 6920 (346 × 2 = 692, then × 10).

Add. 2422 + 6920 = 9342. Check by estimating: 350 × 30 = 10 500, so an answer a little under 10 000 makes sense.

2. A remainder that depends on the question

Question (ours). 158 children are going on a trip. Each minibus holds 9. How many minibuses are needed?

Divide. 158 ÷ 9 = 17 remainder 5, because 9 × 17 = 153.

Think about the context. 17 minibuses leave 5 children behind, so we need one more.

Answer. 18 minibuses. The curriculum asks children to "interpret remainders appropriately for the context", and here that means rounding up.

3. Adding fractions with related denominators

Question (ours). 2/3 + 5/6.

Same denominator first. 6 is a multiple of 3, so write 2/3 as 4/6.

Add. 4/6 + 5/6 = 9/6.

Mixed number. 9/6 = 1 and 3/6 = 1 and a half. Year 5 asks for exactly this: adding fractions whose denominators are multiples of the same number, and writing answers over 1 as mixed numbers.

4. Fractions, decimals and percentages

Question (ours). Write two fifths as a decimal and a percentage.

Hundredths. One fifth is 20 hundredths, so two fifths is 40 hundredths.

Decimal. 40 hundredths is 0.40, usually written 0.4.

Percentage. Per cent means "number of parts per hundred", so 40 hundredths is 40%. One quantity, three notations.

Practice set (ours, with answers). (1) Round 486 251 to the nearest 10 000. (2) Is 91 prime? (3) Write MCMXC as a number. (4) 2.4 m in centimetres. (5) Find the perimeter of an L-shape made from a 6 cm by 4 cm rectangle with a 2 cm by 2 cm square cut from one corner. (6) What is 3/4 as a percentage? Answers: (1) 490 000; (2) no, 91 = 7 × 13; (3) 1990; (4) 240 cm; (5) 20 cm, because cutting a square from a corner leaves the perimeter unchanged; (6) 75%.

Our tutoring

What does a Year 5 maths tutor actually do?

Find the gaps from Years 3 and 4, teach the new Year 5 content properly, and, for families in selective areas, add reasoning and a little Year 6 content before the test. The steps are ours.

  1. A free first class. The tutor checks times tables, place value and written methods with your child, and listens to how they explain a problem.
  2. Close the old gaps first. Times tables, column subtraction and simple fractions from Years 3 and 4 are fixed before the new content is built on them.
  3. Teach Year 5 in the curriculum's spirit. One quantity in several forms: fractions, decimals and percentages taught together, remainders discussed in context.
  4. Written methods with estimates. Every long multiplication and short division checked by a rough estimate, a habit that lasts into secondary school.
  5. For 11+ families, reasoning and a little Year 6. Ratio, simple algebra and multi-step problems added in the summer term of Year 5.
  6. Short practice between classes. Ten minutes most days, never hours of papers.

Year 5 questions

What parents ask about Year 5 maths

What maths do children learn in Year 5?

The national curriculum adds numbers to at least a million, Roman numerals to 1000, prime, square and cube numbers, long multiplication by a two-digit number, short division with remainders, thousandths and percentages. Angles, area, perimeter and line graphs are covered too.

Is there a test in Year 5?

There is no national test in Year 5. The multiplication tables check is in Year 4 and the KS2 tests are in Year 6; for children in Year 5 now, those are in May 2028.

When will my Year 5 child sit the KS2 SATs?

The DfE lists the 2028 KS2 tests from Monday 8 May to Thursday 11 May 2028, which is when children in Year 5 during 2026 to 2027 will sit them.

Is Year 5 too early to start 11+ preparation?

In grammar school areas, Year 5 is usually the right time, because results must reach parents before 31 October of Year 6. Check your local test date with your local authority or consortium.

What maths is in the 11+?

It varies, because the School Admissions Code leaves the content of the test to each admission authority. Most maths questions draw on Key Stage 2 content, including some taught in Year 6.

My child is struggling with Year 5 maths. Where do we start?

Usually with times tables and place value. Year 5 builds long multiplication, factors and fractions on them, so closing those gaps first makes the new work far easier.

Why do Year 5 fractions feel so hard?

Because the curriculum asks children to treat fractions, decimals and percentages as different ways of writing the same amount, and to add and compare fractions for the first time with different denominators.

When are lessons?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. All times are UK time.

Which course should a Year 5 child take?

11 Plus Maths Prep if a selective test is ahead, otherwise Elementary Maths. The free first class helps decide.

Are you connected with any school or 11+ test provider?

No. We are an independent online school with no link to schools, local authorities or test providers, and we never promise a place or a score.
