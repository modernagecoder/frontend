Best online coding classes in the UK

A UK parent looking for coding help meets a long list: a free Code Club at school, a holiday camp, a tutor paid by the hour, a recorded course, and for adults a Skills Bootcamp. This page does four things. It shows what the latest entry figures say about computing at school, sets out seven checks any class should pass, explains what each option is genuinely for, and answers the checks for our own classes, including the places where we are not the right fit.

In short

The best online coding class in the UK is one that fits the learner's school system and stage and teaches them to reason about code rather than copy it. Interest at school is moving: Ofqual's provisional figures for England show GCSE computing entries falling from 89,610 in 2025 to 83,330 in 2026, down 7.0 percent and the largest fall of any EBacc subject, while A level computing grew by 3.5 percent. A good class works around school rather than against it: a live teacher at a UK hour, a prediction before any typing, code read by a person every week, AI used openly and recorded, and evidence you can check yourself. We run classes like that for learners from 6 to 67, either in a small group of five to ten or with a teacher to themselves. Trying one costs nothing, and staying on costs USD 100 a month in a group or USD 150 for private lessons.

What the numbers say

Computing at school in England, 2025 against 2026

Every June, Ofqual publishes provisional exam entries for England. They are the freshest official signal of where computing is heading before a family picks a class.

Provisional entries in England, from Ofqual's release of 9 June 2026
QualificationSummer 2025Summer 2026Change
GCSE computing89,61083,330Down 7.0 percent, the largest fall among EBacc subjects
A level computing19,01019,685Up 3.5 percent

Two things are true at once. Fewer pupils in England were entered for GCSE computing in 2026 than in 2025, and more students were entered for the A level. The figures do not say why, and this page does not guess. They do say that plenty of students now reach sixth form, and some reach A level Computer Science, without having sat the GCSE.

For a family, that matters in one practical way: a class for a Year 12 student cannot assume GCSE programming, and a class for a Year 9 pupil deciding on options should show what the subject is really like before the choice is made.

Beneath the exams sits the curriculum. England's computing programmes of study, published on 11 September 2013, cover key stages 1 to 4. At Key Stage 1 pupils should understand that programs run by following precise and unambiguous instructions; at Key Stage 2 they should use logical reasoning to explain how simple algorithms work and to detect and correct errors; at Key Stage 3 they should use two or more programming languages, at least one of which is textual.

Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland run their own systems and exams, set out on our UK coding page.

Sources: Ofqual, Provisional entries for GCSE, AS and A level: summer 2026 exam series (England); Department for Education, National curriculum in England: computing programmes of study. Read 20 September 2026. Figures are provisional entries, not results.

The seven checks

Seven things the best online coding class in the UK should do

Put each question to every provider on your list, and to us. Three of the checks rest on research summarised on the best coding class in the world; the other four are about the UK in particular.

Seven checks and one question for each
CheckThe reason, here in the UKQuestion to put to them
1. A teacher who knows the learnerA video can explain a loop. It cannot notice that a child stopped following three minutes ago.Will one named person teach my child every week?
2. Prediction before typingEngland's curriculum asks pupils to explain how algorithms work and to find their own errors, which starts with saying what code will do.When a new idea arrives, does my child predict the output first or copy a template?
3. Code read by a personSchools mark finished work. Learning happens when someone reads the attempt in the middle.How often does a teacher read the code, and what comes back?
4. The right system and boardKey Stage 3, S3 and Year 10 in Northern Ireland are not the same, and neither are the exam boards.Which curriculum, board or course will you teach towards?
5. UK hours and the UK calendarHalf terms, inset days and holidays that each council sets for its own schools.What time is the class in UK time, and what happens in exam weeks?
6. AI used in the openCoursework rules require AI help to be acknowledged, and in the end the work has to be the student's own.When may my child use AI tools, and how will they record what the tool did?
7. Proof you can look upStars on a website say nothing until you know who gave them and how many there are.Can I read your reviews somewhere independent, and sit in on a real lesson first?

None of these is unusual, and a good provider can answer each one in a sentence. What they screen out is common enough: videos sold as teaching, a lesson that starts by copying finished code, a slot at an hour no UK child is free, or a promise about grades nobody can keep.

The sixth check has grown in importance as AI tools reach every homework. What the rules for coursework say, and how a learner keeps work honestly their own, is on our page about GCSE Computer Science and will have a page of its own.

Your options

Code Club, camps, tutors, recorded courses, bootcamps and live classes: what each is for

These are not rivals so much as tools for different moments. Plenty of families use two of them in the same year, a club for fun and a class for progress.

Six routes into coding in the UK, compared on what they are built for
RouteBuilt forLeaves to someone else

Holiday campsA burst of making things while schools are closedThe weekly habit that turns a burst into a skill

For a primary-age child

A club or camp to light the interest, and a weekly class once one project is no longer enough. Blocks come first, and typed code usually arrives around Year 4 or P5.

For the secondary years

Something that goes past the timetable, a real project to finish, and a straight conversation about what GCSE or National 5 computing involves before options are chosen.

For sixth form and adults

Depth next to an A level, Higher or CCEA course, or for an adult, a steadier and more thorough route than a bootcamp while still working.

Questions from UK families

The questions UK families put to us first

Which online coding class in the UK is the right one for my child?

The one that suits their school system and stage and passes seven checks: a teacher who knows them, a prediction before typing, code read by a person, the right curriculum or board, UK hours, AI used openly, and proof you can look up.

Are fewer students taking GCSE Computer Science?

In England, yes, on Ofqual's provisional figures: GCSE computing entries fell from 89,610 in 2025 to 83,330 in 2026, down 7.0 percent, while A level computing entries rose by 3.5 percent.

What does England's computing curriculum expect at primary school?

By Key Stage 1, pupils should understand that programs execute by following precise and unambiguous instructions; by Key Stage 2, they should explain how simple algorithms work and detect and correct errors in algorithms and programs.

Is a free Code Club enough?

As a starting point, often yes: Code Clubs are free, part of the Raspberry Pi Foundation and open to all school-aged young people. Moving up level by level over years usually needs a regular class with a teacher who follows the learner's work.

Should an adult do a Skills Bootcamp or a weekly class?

A Skills Bootcamp, for people aged 19 or over, takes up to 16 weeks and ends with a guaranteed job interview, which suits someone ready for an intensive switch. A weekly class suits someone learning alongside a job at a slower pace. Some people do one after the other.

Do you teach Scottish, Welsh and Northern Irish courses?

Yes. Teachers work with National 5, Higher and Advanced Higher Computing Science, WJEC's GCSEs in Wales and CCEA's courses in Northern Ireland, as well as the English key stages and boards.

May my child use ChatGPT during lessons?

Once they have said what the code ought to do, yes. Whatever the tool proposes goes into a short log alongside what the learner actually used, which is the kind of record coursework rules in the UK now ask for.

How much are the classes?

The opening lesson is free. Staying on costs USD 100 a month in a group or USD 150 a month for private lessons, in US dollars, with nothing to pay up front and no twelve-month tie-in.

What time would lessons be?

Whichever regular slot you settle on in the free lesson, most often late afternoon, evening or a weekend morning. India is five and a half hours ahead of the UK in winter and four and a half in summer, so times are always given in UK time.

What happens once the form is sent?

We contact you at a UK time that works for you and arrange the free lesson. You pay nothing unless the learner decides to continue.
