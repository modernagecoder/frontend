Coding and AI classes in Indiana

Do most Indiana high schools offer computer science?

Yes. Code.org (2025) reports 368 of 414 Indiana high schools, 88.9%, offering foundational computer science in 2023 to 2024, but only 5.57% of high school students enrolled in it. Modern Age Coders closes that gap from the other side: live online coding and AI lessons for grades 3 to 12, on Eastern or Central time.

Indiana is a state where the course is usually there and the students mostly are not. Close to nine in ten of its high schools offered computer science in 2023 to 2024, well above the 60% national figure in the same Code.org sheet, yet barely one high school student in eighteen was enrolled in it. Indiana is also, by the account of the people who maintain the world’s time zone database, the most complicated state in the country to keep a clock in. This page sets out the published access and enrollment figures, explains Indiana’s two time zones the way the database records them, and walks through a project on Indianapolis greenway data that teaches one of the cleanest ideas in programming: sets, and what they share.

The course is there

From 62% in 2018 to 2019 the offering share rose every year to a peak of 90.6% in 2022 to 2023. By then a student at most Indiana high schools could find computer science somewhere on the schedule.

The seats are not full

Enrollment never passed 6.6% in the four years shown. Offering a course and filling it are different problems, and a single elective competes with everything else a schedule must hold.

What families can do

Ask early. A student who has already written real programs in middle school is far more likely to choose the high school course, and to stay in it.

Signature project

Built, planned, or both? Indianapolis greenways as Python sets

The City of Indianapolis and Marion County publish their greenway network as open data. Every segment carries a name and a status, and the two statuses make two sets.

1. Build the sets

Students read each segment’s FULL_NAME and STATUS and add the name to one of two Python sets. Duplicates vanish on their own: Fall Creek GW has eight existing segments but is one member of the set.

2. Ask set questions

Intersection, difference and union each answer a plain-English question about the network. Checking that 19 plus 25 minus 11 equals 33 is a first lesson in testing your own code.

3. Spot the traps

Monon Trail is existing and Monon Rail is proposed; Vandalia Tr and Vandalia Trail both appear. Sets match exact strings, so students must decide whether those are the same path before trusting the answer.

Credit, and what the numbers are

Data from the City of Indianapolis/Marion County, IN, whose terms ask that derived data acknowledge them and which provides it as is, without warranty. The counts above are our own from one download. A name appearing in both sets suggests a greenway that is partly built, but only the city can say what is actually planned.

Once the idea clicks, students reuse it on Indiana’s clocks. The time zone database quotes the federal rule that moved eight counties, Starke, Pulaski, Knox, Daviess, Martin, Pike, Dubois and Perry, from Eastern to Central time on April 2, 2006, and then records six of them returning to Eastern in 2007. The difference of those two sets is Perry and Starke, exactly the two Indiana counties the database still lists on Central time with their own zones.

345 regions

The database records that an earlier time zone reference partitioned Indiana into 345 regions, each with its own time history. Its maintainers note that most of that complexity happened before 1970.

Daylight saving arrived late

The America/Indiana/Indianapolis entry stays on Eastern Standard Time all year until 2006 and only then follows the US daylight saving rule. Separately, Switzerland County did not observe DST from 1973 through 2005.

Coding and AI

An AI model can write the set code. Can it tell you what Monon Rail is?

Writing three lines of Python is easy now; deciding whether the answer is right is still the job.

Ask an assistant which Indianapolis greenways are both built and planned and it will produce correct set code in seconds. It will not stop to ask whether Monon Rail and Monon Trail are one path, or whether a blank name hides a real segment. Those questions only occur to someone who has handled the data.

That is the case for learning to code even when AI writes much of it. A student who understands intersection and difference can read generated code, test it on a small example and spot when the data, not the code, is the problem.

Indiana already offers the course in most high schools; the students who take it with real practice behind them are the ones who will use AI well. The longer argument is in why we still teach students to write code themselves.

FAQ

Indiana parents ask

Answers drawn from the sources on this page; for school rules, your counselor is the authority.

What share of Indiana high schools offered computer science in 2023 to 2024?

Code.org (2025) reports 368 of 414 Indiana high schools, or 88.9%, using state education agency data.

How many Indiana high school students took computer science?

Code.org (2025) lists 18,146 of 325,663 high school students, or 5.57%, the value it gives for 2023 to 2024.

Why does this page not list Indiana graduation rules?

We print state rules only from the state's own site, and the Indiana Department of Education site could not be reached from our network on 17 and 18 October 2026. Ask your school counselor for the current rules.

Is all of Indiana on Eastern time?

No. Most of the state is on Eastern time, but the time zone database lists counties such as Lake, Porter, Vanderburgh, Perry and Starke on Central time.

Which time zone will you use for my lessons?

Your county's zone. Families in Central counties get Central times, and everyone else gets Eastern times.

When are lessons held?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays.

How many Indiana schools give AP exams?

The College Board counts 418 Indiana schools with AP exams in 2023 and 433 in 2024, across all AP subjects.

Do your lessons give Indiana school credit?

No. Credit comes from your school's own courses. Our lessons build skills and projects alongside them, and assessed school work stays the student's own.

Can my child start in elementary school?

Yes. Many begin in third grade with block-based games, then move to Python in middle school.

Where do I see the fees?
