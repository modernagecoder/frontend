The Canadian Computing Olympiad: the road from CCC Senior to the IOI

Canada does not hold an open informatics olympiad that students sign up for. Its olympiad is an invitation: CEMC, at the University of Waterloo, reads the results of the Canadian Computing Competition Senior paper each February, invites about twenty students to the Canadian Computing Olympiad on campus in the spring, and uses that week to choose the team that represents Canada at the International Olympiad in Informatics. This guide sets out each step in CEMC's own words, what an invited student needs to have done in February, why the CCO runs only in C++, and how IOI-style subtasks work, with a practice task we wrote and solved twice.

In short

The Canadian Computing Olympiad (CCO) is CEMC's invitation-only week at the University of Waterloo each spring, for about 20 top official CCC Senior writers from schools in Canada; it is written in C++ and used to select Canada's IOI team. Who decides at each step

CEMC states it directly: "The selection of CCO participants and the Canadian IOI team members is at the discretion of the CEMC." CEMC lists no application form and no essay: the invitation follows from results.

CEMC also leaves room beyond its main rule: "Outstanding participants, including those who are Canadian citizens or permanent residents attending school outside of Canada, may also be invited."

The search term that confuses people

Many families search for a "Canadian informatics olympiad". The olympiad CEMC runs for Canadian school students is called the Canadian Computing Olympiad, and the national contest that feeds it is the Canadian Computing Competition.

The International Olympiad in Informatics is the world level. CEMC calls it "one of the most recognized computer science competitions in the world".

Screen recording on CCC day

CEMC asks something specific of the schools of students hoping for an invitation. For "participants aiming to be invited to the Canadian Computing Olympiad (CCO)", it says "we strongly recommend that Contest Supervisors arrange to record their screen for the entire duration of the competition".

The school keeps the recording, not CEMC, "for a month after the contest", because students being considered may be asked for it before formal invitations go out.

What a recording does and does not do

CEMC is careful on both sides of this. It says that "being asked to provide a screen recording does not guarantee an invitation, and without a screen recording, evidence of broken rules is more likely to lead to disqualification."

Our advice to a CCO hopeful: ask the CCC supervisor in January whether the school can record, and practise one mock paper with recording running so it is routine on the day.

Put the numbers side by side and the shape of the road is clear. Thousands write the CCC each year; CEMC printed 2439 Senior contestants for 2026. About 20 reach the CCO. Four go to the IOI. That is why we talk to families about the CCO as a direction to grow in, never as a target to be promised.

C++ only

Why the CCO is C++ only, and how a Python student switches

The CCC accepts five languages; the CCO accepts one. CEMC gives the rule without a reason. The practical effect is about speed: CEMC already warns that Python or Java may not reach a perfect score at the CCC, and the CCO is harder still. The table maps the Python a CCC student already knows onto the C++ they will need. It is our teaching table.

When to switch

A student aiming at the CCO should write CCC Senior in C++, because the invitation comes from that paper and the student will need C++ the moment it arrives. The year before is the right time to move: the autumn of Grade 9 or 10 for a student who expects to be strong at Senior by Grade 11.

In our classes the switch usually takes several weeks of steady practice before C++ feels as natural as Python did, and the first fortnight is the hardest.

How we teach it

We re-solve problems the student already solved in Python, in C++. The algorithm is familiar, so all the attention goes to the language: types, references, the standard library.

After that, every new Senior problem is attempted in C++ first. Python stays as a scratchpad for checking ideas, not as the contest language.

Worked example

How IOI-style subtasks work: one task, two solutions

CCC Senior and the olympiads above it split problems into subtasks: a small version that a simple method can solve, and a large version that needs the real idea. This task is ours, written for our students; it is not a CEMC, CCO or IOI problem. Both solutions are in C++, the CCO's language, and both were checked against a brute-force count on 400 random cases with no mismatches.

Our task: Signal Towers. N radio towers stand along a straight highway at positions x (whole numbers, kilometres from a marker). Two towers can talk if they are at most D kilometres apart. Count the pairs of towers that can talk. Input: N and D on the first line, then the N positions on one line. Output: one integer. Subtask 1 (40 percent of the marks): N is at most 2,000. Subtask 2 (60 percent): N is at most 200,000, and positions and D are at most one billion.

The habit this teaches

Write the subtask 1 solution first, always. It takes minutes, it earns marks, and it becomes the brute-force checker for the clever version. Students who skip this step tend to lose an afternoon to a bug it would have caught in seconds.

Then ask what structure the simple version wastes. Here, it compares towers that are obviously too far apart. Sorting is what lets the program stop looking.

Reading the sample by hand

Sorted, the positions are 1, 2, 4, 7, 10 and 13, with D = 3. The pairs that can talk are 1 and 2, 1 and 4, 2 and 4, 4 and 7, 7 and 10, and 10 and 13: six, as both programs print.

A student who cannot list the six pairs on paper is not ready to code either solution. At the olympiad end, the paper work comes first just as it does at Junior.

A longer view

Very few students reach the CCO in their first CCC year. This is the kind of path we sketch with a strong, keen student who starts in Grade 9. It is ours, and it is a direction rather than a promise.

What sustains a student over four years

Enjoyment of the problems for their own sake. Students who stay with algorithms through Grade 12 are the ones who like the moment an idea clicks, not the ones chasing a medal.

Steady weekly work. Two classes and a few problems a week, every week, beats intense bursts before February.

What to tell a student who is not invited

That about twenty places a year across Canada says nothing about whether a student is good at computer science. The skills built on the way, algorithms, efficient code, careful testing, are the ones university courses and software work ask for.

And that CEMC runs the CCC every year, so a student who is still an official participant can write Senior again next February.

For parents

Ask your child's teacher which stage they are at, not whether they will be invited. Stages can be worked on; invitations cannot.

Stage 2 is the one families often skip. Without C++ fluency, a strong Senior result cannot carry into the CCO.

For students

Questions from Canadian families

The Canadian Computing Olympiad: common questions

What is the Canadian Computing Olympiad?

It is an invitation-only week run by CEMC at the University of Waterloo each spring, with workshops, a contest and other activities. CEMC also uses it to select Canada's team for the International Olympiad in Informatics.

How does a student qualify for the CCO?

Through the Senior paper of the Canadian Computing Competition. CEMC invites about 20 of the top official CCC Senior participants from schools in Canada, and may also invite outstanding participants such as Canadian citizens or permanent residents at school abroad. Selection is at CEMC's discretion.

Can a student apply to the CCO directly?

No. CEMC lists no application. Invitations come from CCC Senior results, and only official participants are eligible, which for a school in Canada means born on or after 1 July 2007 and living in Canada.

What programming language is used at the CCO?

C++ only. CEMC says CCO participants must use C++, even though the CCC itself also accepts C, Python and Java. Students hoping for an invitation should switch to C++ before writing CCC Senior.

How is Canada's IOI team chosen?

CEMC says Canada's IOI entry is determined by performance on the Canadian Computing Competition, and that top scorers on the CCO contest are then selected for the team. CEMC trains the team and leads the delegation.

How did Canada do at IOI 2026?

CEMC reports that the team returned from Tashkent, Uzbekistan with four medals in 2026, two silver and two bronze.

Does my child need to screen-record the CCC to be invited?

It is not required, but CEMC strongly recommends that schools record the screens of students aiming for the CCO, and keep the recordings for a month. CEMC adds that being asked for one does not guarantee an invitation.

When is the 2027 CCO?

CEMC says the CCO is held at the University of Waterloo each spring, but it had not posted a 2027 date when we checked on 22 October 2026. The CCC Senior paper that leads to it is on 17 February 2027.

Can Modern Age Coders get my child into the CCO?

No. Invitations are decided by CEMC from CCC results, at its discretion, and we are not connected with CEMC. We teach the algorithms and C++ that strong Senior writers need.

When are your olympiad-track classes for Canadian families?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Times are agreed in Eastern time and converted to your province.
