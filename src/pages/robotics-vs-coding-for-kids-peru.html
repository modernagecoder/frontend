Robotics vs coding for kids in Peru

In Peru, "robotics" is often the first word a parent reaches for when they want a child to learn technology. It is a good instinct: robots are exciting, physical and easy to show off. But every robot that does something clever is running a program, and that program is where most of the thinking lives. This page explains the difference between robotics and coding, what real robotics competitions expect of children, how to choose for your child, and what we teach.

Robotics or coding?

Robotics is building machines that sense, decide and act; coding is writing the instructions, and in any robot the deciding is done by code. For most children in Peru coding is the better first step: it needs only a computer, it teaches the logic every robot depends on, and it carries straight into Python, games and AI. The difference

What is the difference between robotics and coding for kids?

Robotics is the whole machine: a body, motors, sensors and a program. Coding is the program. A robot without code is a model; code without a robot can still run games, apps, websites and AI.

Sense

A robot reads the world through sensors: light, distance, touch, a camera. In code, each reading is just a number arriving many times a second.

Decide

The program compares those numbers with rules: if the line is to the left, steer left; if something is close, stop. This is the part that takes real thinking.

Act

Motors turn, wheels move, an arm lifts. The action is only as good as the decision before it, which is why the decision is where children learn most.

What robotics adds

Building teaches mechanical sense: gears, balance, why a robot tips over on a slope. Children see their code act on the real world, which is motivating, and team builds teach cooperation.

It also brings costs and limits: parts to buy, a kit that must be at home or at a centre, and lessons where much of the time goes on assembly rather than on the program.

What coding adds

Coding goes deeper into the decide step and much further beyond it. The same if, loop and variable that drive a robot also build games, apps and data projects, and lead to Python, Java and AI.

It needs only a computer. INEI found a computer in 36.4% of Peruvian households in the second quarter of 2026, so a family that has one already has everything a coding class needs.

Look at the World Robot Olympiad, which runs categories for students from 8 to 19, and to 22 in one category. Its own rules show how much of robotics is programming.

Notice the word autonomous

In RoboMission, the WRO explains, "the field is set up randomly each round", so "the robot needs to be able to make its own decisions during the run". Nobody steers it with a remote. Every decision is a line of code written before the run.

Future Engineers goes further: a car-like robot must drive itself round a track. That is a problem in control, the subject of the lesson further down this page.

What that means for a parent

A child who reaches the senior categories will spend far more hours on the program than on the build. In our view, the teams whose robots cope with surprises are usually the teams with strong coders.

Learning to code first is therefore not a detour from robotics. It is the part of robotics that takes longest to learn and transfers to everything else.

Robotics in Peruvian schools

What has MINEDU done with robotics in schools?

More than many parents realise, and with a clear message: in MINEDU's own words, "la robótica en educación no es el objetivo, es el medio", robotics in education is not the goal but the means.

A long record

A document from MINEDU's directorate for technological innovation (DITE) says the ministry has used educational robotics "desde el año 1996 con el proyecto Infoescuela", and that in 2009 and 2010 it supplied LEGO WeDo kits to almost every public primary school in the country.

Since 2022 the same directorate has organised a Hackathon on educational robotics and digital technologies. MINEDU reported that its 2025 edition rewarded "proyectos de IA y robótica", and that between 2023 and 2025 the number of girls taking part rose by 28%.

Peruvian students at the top

On 2 October 2023 MINEDU announced that a 4th-grade team from COAR Lima, Peru's public high-achievement school network, had won a national robotics category and would represent Peru at the World Robot Olympiad 2023 in Panama. Their project was a 3D-printed drone with artificial intelligence that detects and counts ships.

Look at what made that project stand out: detecting and counting ships is software, an AI model running on the drone. The build mattered, but the intelligence was code.

The national curriculum, the CNEB, has no robotics or computing subject of its own. Its science and technology area includes the competency "Diseña y construye soluciones tecnológicas para resolver problemas", designing and building technological solutions to problems. Robotics is one way schools work on it; coding is another. Modern Age Coders has no link with MINEDU, COAR or any school.

A steering lesson

How does a robot follow a line? A lesson in code, no robot needed

Here is a lesson we teach to children of about 10 to 12, simulated in Python. A small robot starts 10 millimetres to the right of a black line and must steer back onto it. The numbers and the simple model are ours; real robots add speed and friction, but the idea is the same.

The obvious idea

Most children first write: if the robot is right of the line, turn left by a fixed amount; if it is left, turn right. In our model a fixed turn moves the robot 3 millimetres sideways each step.

Before running it, the mentor asks: "Where will the robot be after eight steps?" Children usually say "on the line". The program disagrees.

What actually happens

The robot reaches 1 millimetre right of the line, turns 3, and lands 2 millimetres left. Then it turns 3 back and lands 1 right. It never settles: it wobbles across the line for ever.

Real line-followers built this way do exactly that, zig-zagging along the tape. The child has found the problem with their own eyes before anyone names it.

The better idea: steer by how far off you are. Far from the line, turn hard; close to it, turn gently. In code the correction is the error times a number called the gain. Engineers call this proportional control, and it is the first idea behind self-driving robots like those in the Future Engineers category.

Children then explain the four rows in their own words, in English. Too gentle is slow but safe. A bit stronger is ideal. Too strong overshoots. Much too strong is worse than doing nothing, because each correction is bigger than the error it fixes. A correction bigger than the mistake makes the mistake grow, and that sentence is as true of a robot as of many things outside robotics.

  1. Ages 8 and 9. On a paper number line, start a counter at 10 and move it 3 towards zero each turn. Where does it end up after eight turns?
  2. Ages 10 and 11. Run both versions. Find, by testing, the largest gain that still settles without crossing the line at all.
  3. Ages 12 and up. Change the gain from 2.2 to 2.0 and predict the result before running. Explain why 2 is the turning point in this model.

What we teach

Do you teach robotics online without a kit?

We teach the programming side of robotics, and we say so plainly: we do not sell robots, kits or parts, and children need none for our classes.

What children do with us

Younger children learn exact sequences, loops and conditions in blocks, often by guiding a character or a simulated robot round a grid. Older children write Python that reads simulated sensor numbers, decides and repeats, like the steering lesson above.

Teenagers can go on to control ideas, data from sensors and machine learning. If they later join a school robotics team, they arrive as the person who can write the program.

The right age

At what age should a child start robotics or coding?

Coding can start at 6 with blocks. Competitive robotics, by the WRO's own rules, starts at 8, and the senior categories run to 19. A child who codes from 6 or 7 arrives at 8 already thinking in steps and conditions.

A sensible order

6 to 8: blocks, sequences and simple conditions on screen. 9 to 11: typed Python, simulated sensors, games. 12 and up: control, data and AI, and a robotics team if the child wants one.

Our page on online coding classes for kids in Peru explains how to judge a live class for children of 6 to 12, and the Spanish-language guide to coding at 8, in 3rd grade compares Scratch, Roblox and robotics at that age.

For teenagers

Teenagers who want robotics competitions benefit most from solid Python and some mathematics: angles, rates, averages of noisy readings. Our coding classes for teens in Peru page sets out those paths.

Whatever the age, AI tools come after understanding, and only those whose own terms allow the child's age. Anthropic sets 18 as the floor for Claude; OpenAI asks for a parent's consent before a minor uses ChatGPT.

Progress

How do I know my child is learning to think like a robot programmer?

By how they plan and explain, not by what they assemble. Our own stages:

Our stages in control and decision logic
StageCan doCan explain
1. Exact stepsGuide a character round a grid with no wasted moves"Forward two, turn right, forward one."
2. RepeatUse a loop instead of copying the same step"Repeat four times makes a square."
3. Sense and decideReact to a number with if and else"If it is dark, the light turns on."
4. Keep checkingLoop over readings and decide each time"It checks again every step."
5. ControlCorrect in proportion to an error"Big error, big turn; small error, small turn."
6. Test the edgesFind the setting where a system fails"Above 2 it runs away, so I keep it below."

For parents

Ask your child to explain a decision their program makes. If they can say the rule in a sentence, they understand it.

Stage 5 is the steering lesson. It is often the point where children see that maths is useful.

For young engineers

Predict what your program will do before you run it, especially at the extremes.

When something wobbles or overshoots, ask whether your correction is too big.

Parents in Peru ask

Robotics vs coding: questions from families in Peru

Are the classes in Spanish?

No. Teaching happens live, over video, and solely in English. Our mentors keep their speech calm and distinct, follow the speed and accent of each child, and display each step on screen as they say it. You will find Spanish-language pages from us for Peruvian parents, yet the classes themselves never switch to Spanish.

Is coding or robotics better for kids?

For most children, coding first. It needs only a computer, it teaches the decision-making part of every robot, and it leads on to games, apps and AI. Robotics adds building and teamwork and suits children who later join a school or club team.

Do you teach robotics?

We teach the programming side of robotics: sequences, sensing, decisions and control, using blocks, Python and simulations. We do not teach mechanical building and we do not sell or require any robot, kit or parts.

Does my child need a robot kit for your classes?

No. A laptop or desktop computer and an internet connection are enough. Robots in our lessons are simulated on screen, like the line-follower on this page.

What age can a child start?

Coding with blocks from 6. The World Robot Olympiad's youngest categories start at 8. A child who has coded from 6 or 7 arrives at 8 already able to plan exact steps and conditions.

Will coding help my child in robotics competitions later?

It helps with the part that takes longest to learn. In the WRO's RoboMission category the robot must make its own decisions on a randomly set field, and Future Engineers asks for a robot that drives a track by itself. Both are programming problems, though we prepare no team for any specific competition.

What did MINEDU do with robotics in schools?

A MINEDU document says the ministry has used educational robotics since 1996, supplied LEGO WeDo kits to almost all public primary schools in 2009 and 2010, and has run a robotics and digital technology Hackathon since 2022. It describes robotics as a means for learning, not the goal.

When are classes on Peru time?

Any day, any time, flexible: choose a batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at the hour that suits your family. Because India leads Peru by ten and a half hours on every date, 16:45 in Peru matches 03:15 the following day in India.

Group or one to one?

A group of fifteen to twenty of the same age works for children who like comparing answers. Private lessons work for a quiet child, a child still settling into English, or one who would race past a group.

Are you linked with the WRO, MINEDU or a school?

No. We run on our own, unconnected to the WRO, MINEDU, COAR, any school in Peru or any robotics academy. The only certificate a learner gets is the completion certificate we issue.
