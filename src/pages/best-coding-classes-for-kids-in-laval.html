Coding classes for kids in Laval

Can a Laval child who goes to school in French take a coding class in English?

Yes, and it often works well, because coding is learned by doing and Python’s keywords are the same English words in every country. Check that the teacher speaks slowly, connects the French terms your child knows, and groups children by age. Modern Age Coders teaches Laval children aged 6 to 12 live online, in English, with classes of 15 to 20 or one to one. Your first class is free.

For many Laval families the first question about a coding class is not about price or projects but language. French is the official language of Quebec, and many Laval children spend their school day in French. Our classes are taught in English. That can be a strength rather than a barrier: programming languages borrow their keywords from English, so a child who learns that a boucle is a loop and that si is if has two words for one idea, and understands both better. This page sets out a checklist written for Laval families, a short French-English coding glossary of our own, and a Ville de Laval open data file that our ten-to-twelve-year-olds work through: it is in French, full of accented letters that a careless program will mangle. We do not rank other providers, and the checklist applies to us as much as anyone.

A note on schools

Quebec’s ministry is the Ministère de l’Éducation. We have not found a primary source that sets out how Quebec elementary schools teach coding, so we make no claim about it here. Ask your child’s teacher what they do in class.

A note on the law

The Charter of the French language states that French is the official language of Québec. A coding class in English sits outside school, as an activity a family chooses, and does not replace any part of a child’s schooling.

When French is the better choice

If English is a struggle that makes your child unhappy, a French-language class may serve them better for now. We would rather say that than lose a child’s interest in coding.

Names in any language

Python lets a child name things in French: pointage = 0 works as well as score = 0. We let younger children choose, so the logic stays theirs.

Accents in names

Python 3 even accepts accented letters in names. We still suggest plain letters, because some tools and keyboards handle accents badly, which the project below shows.

Reading aloud

We ask children to read their code aloud. A Laval child may do it half in French, half in English, and that is fine. What matters is that they can explain each line.

Laval data project

Splash pads, play structures and accents: a Ville de Laval open data project

The Ville de Laval publishes its recreation sites, Sites de loisirs, as open data on Données Québec. The file is in French, column names included. Before any counting, children in our upper classes learn to open it without breaking the accents. All numbers here are ours.

Step 1: the accent trap. The file is saved as UTF-8, where é takes two bytes. Read those bytes with the wrong encoding and every é turns into two strange characters. This four-line demonstration, ours, shows the damage and the repair:word = "Régional" broken = word.encode("utf-8").decode("latin-1") print(word, broken) print(broken.encode("latin-1").decode("utf-8"))It prints Régional RÃ©gional and then Régional again. On some Windows computers Python may default to a different encoding, so we teach children to write encoding="utf-8" every time they open a file.

This is our counting script. A set holds the four facility types we want, and a dictionary inside a dictionary keeps the count for each sector:import csv wanted = {"Modules de jeux", "Jeu d'eau", "Pataugeoire", "Butte de glisse"} by_sector = {} with open("sites-de-loisirs.csv", encoding="utf-8") as f: for row in csv.DictReader(f): kind = row["descr_type_plateau"] if kind in wanted: sector = row["nom_secteur"] by_sector.setdefault(sector, {}) by_sector[sector][kind] = by_sector[sector].get(kind, 0) + 1 for sector in sorted(by_sector): print(sector, by_sector[sector])

A label used in two sectors only

All 28 Modules de jeux rows sit in Secteur 2 and Secteur 3. It is very unlikely that four sectors have no play structures at all; more likely they are recorded under another type, or not at all. Students learn to say the file is uneven rather than that the sectors are.

Reading the column names

nom_secteur is the sector name, descr_type_plateau the facility type and ind_sans_reserv a yes or no flag, O or N, which we read as whether booking is needed. That reading is ours; the file does not define it.

Blank rows

Twenty-four rows are blank in every column. The script skips them naturally, because a blank type is not in the wanted set, but a careful student counts them and says so.

What the file cannot tell you

The CSV on Données Québec was last modified in January 2021, so it is a snapshot from several years ago. A splash pad built since then will not appear, and one listed may have changed. Students write the file date beside every result.

An English contest at a French school

Because the Beaver Computing Challenge is offered in English only, a child in a French-language school may find the reading harder than the logic. Practising logic puzzles in English, as we do in class, helps.

Entry is by school

Families cannot sign a child up directly; CEMC routes every entry through a school. Where a school does not order the contests, CEMC suggests finding another local school willing to let the child join.

Original puzzles

CEMC’s past papers stay on CEMC’s own free archive, and we leave them there. The practice puzzles in our lessons are written by our teachers and marked as ours.

Coding and AI

Can an AI tool replace learning to code for a Laval child?

No. It can write code, but it cannot tell your child whether the accents survived.

Ask an AI assistant to count Laval splash pads and it may open the file with the wrong encoding, so that Jeu d’eau never matches anything and the answer is zero. It will present that zero calmly. A child who has seen RÃ©gional on their own screen knows to check the text first, and knows why the count was wrong.

So our classes for children teach thinking first: look at the data, understand it, write the code, test it, explain it, in whichever of their languages helps. Children learn how AI works and where it goes wrong, but they do their own reasoning. If ChatGPT is used at home, OpenAI’s terms say a parent must agree for any child under 18.

A bilingual child already knows that one idea can wear two sets of words; checking that an answer means what it seems to mean is the same skill. The longer argument is in whether learning to code still pays off in 2026.

Questions from Laval parents

What Laval families ask us before a first class.

Do you offer coding classes for kids in Laval?

Yes. They are live, online and in English. There is no Laval building: your child logs in from home, and a teacher works with them in a group of 15 to 20 or alone.

My child goes to school in French. Can they follow a class in English?

Usually, yes. Coding is learned by doing, and Python keywords are English everywhere. Private lessons help if English is still new, and the free class shows how your child copes.

Are your classes in French?

No, our classes are taught in English. Children can use French words when stuck, and the teacher helps connect them to the English terms.

What age can a Laval child start?

From age 6, with picture-based blocks. These courses for children go up to age 12.

Will learning in English confuse my child?

It rarely does. Most children enjoy having two words for one idea, such as boucle and loop, and understand the idea better for it.

Can my child write a CEMC contest?

Through their school. The Beaver Computing Challenge is offered in English only and needs no coding; the later Canadian Computing Competition is offered in English and French.

When are classes held for Laval families?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Times are confirmed in Eastern time.

Does my child need special software?

No. A computer with a browser is enough to start, and a French or English keyboard both work.

Do children use AI in your Laval classes?

Only as a subject. They study how AI tools reach answers and where those answers go wrong, while every line of their own projects is written by them.
