Alabama, United States

Coding and AI classes in Alabama

Can an Alabama student take computer science at school, and what can they add outside it?

Very likely at school: Code.org (2025) counts 351 of 382 Alabama public high schools, or 91.9%, offering computer science in 2024 to 2025, after the Computer Science for Alabama Act (HB216) phased in course offerings. Modern Age Coders adds live online classes for grades K to 12, in a small batch or 1-on-1, from Scratch to Python data projects and AP Computer Science.

Few states changed course as fast. In 2018 to 2019, fewer than half of Alabama's public high schools offered a computer science class; four school years later the published share was 95%. Along the way, the governor's office announced a 2017 advisory council, a national governors' partnership and a 2019 law. This page sets out what those official announcements say, the access and enrollment counts as published, a Birmingham open data project that teaches the least glamorous and most useful skill in data work, and how our live classes run on Central Time for families anywhere in the state.

The state push

How Alabama set out to put computer science in every high school

Alabama's own education department site could not be reached on 17 or 18 October 2026, so this timeline uses the governor's official announcements, read on 18 October 2026.

Computer science milestones announced by the Office of the Governor of Alabama
Date announcedWhat the governor's office said
September 27, 2017Creation of the Governor's Advisory Council for Computer Science Education, to make recommendations for the expansion of computer science education, including teacher training
December 4, 2017Alabama joined the Governors' Partnership for K-12 Computer Science, whose members work toward K-12 standards, teacher professional learning and computer science in every high school
July 26, 2019The Computer Science for Alabama Act (HB216) phases in requirements for public schools to offer courses in computer science
July 26, 2019The same law funds evidence-based professional learning for K-12 computer science teachers and a clearer route to certification
October 23, 2025Students invited to the 2025-2026 Alabama Governor's App Challenge for grades K through 12

An offering law, not a seat law

As the 2019 announcement describes it, HB216 phases in a duty on public schools to offer computer science. It is about whether a course exists, which is exactly what the access figures below measure.

Teachers first

Both the 2017 council and the 2019 law put weight on training. A course on the timetable needs a qualified teacher in the room, and that supply takes years to build in rural districts.

Part of a wider plan

The governor's office filed computer science under its "Strong Start, Strong Finish" education initiative, alongside early reading and workforce training, as one of three focus areas.

What we could not confirm: the current Alabama computer science standards document, its adoption year, and whether a computer science credit now counts toward a diploma. Those live on the department's site, which returned no answer when we checked. We would rather leave a gap than print a rule we have not read at its source.

The jump between 2019 to 2020 and 2020 to 2021, from 57% to 82%, is the largest single-year move in Alabama's series and lines up with the period after HB216 was signed. Correlation is not proof, and the sheet does not say why access dipped slightly after its 2022 to 2023 peak; a few schools closing, merging or changing their course lists could all move a share by a point or two.

The harder number is enrollment. Even with a course in more than nine of ten high schools, 7.87% of high school students were taking one in 2023 to 2024, and the sheet has no 2024 to 2025 enrollment figure for Alabama yet. Signature project

Cleaning Birmingham's park list before trusting a single total

The City of Birmingham publishes its parks layer as open data under a Creative Commons Attribution licence. Before any chart, students audit it for what is missing, repeated or unexplained.

What our audit of the Birmingham City Parks layer found (our counts, from one download on 18 October 2026)
CheckWhat we foundWhy it matters
Records in the layer130, matching the service's own count queryA download that silently stops at a page limit gives wrong totals
Distinct park names120Some parks are stored as several pieces
Blank address field7 recordsA blank is honest about being missing
Acreage recorded as 08 records, 6 of them pieces of Rotary TrailA zero can quietly mean "not measured"
Rows repeating a name and acreage10Adding them double counts the same land
Coded columns with no labelsDEDICATED, MANAGED, PARK_TYPEA 10 or a 20 means nothing without the codebook

1. Count before you calculate

Students ask the service how many records exist, then check the download holds all 130. It is a one-line habit that catches truncated files before they mislead anyone.

2. Find the quiet gaps

Blank fields are easy to spot. Zeros are harder: Railroad Park and Henley Park both carry 0 in the acreage column, which is plainly a missing measurement, not an empty park.

3. Decide, then document

Drop repeats, mark zeros as unknown, leave codes uninterpreted. Every choice goes in a short note so another student could repeat the work and get the same answer.

Two totals, both ours, neither official

Summing the acreage column as downloaded gives 3,026.67. Dropping the 10 rows that repeat a name and acreage already counted gives 2,815.34. One record alone holds 1,051.74, about 34.7% of the naive total. These are our calculations from one download, not the City of Birmingham's published park acreage, and the gap between them is the lesson.

Eighteen records with a real, nonzero acreage are under one acre, which raises a fair question for a follow-up: are those pocket parks, or pieces of larger parks stored separately? The data cannot answer it alone. A student who writes that sentence instead of guessing has learned more about data science than one who produced a tidy chart from untidy rows.

Class times

Central Time, with a twist on the eastern border

Alabama runs on US Central Time, and our teachers work from India, so the conversion matters.

The official zone

Alabama sits in US Central time; in the IANA tz files its zone is America/Chicago. We confirm every class slot on that clock.

The informal exception

The same database's notes record that Phenix City, Lanett, Smiths Station and Valley, near the state's eastern edge, keep Eastern time by local habit. Families there should tell us which clock they live by.

India time, our arithmetic

By our calculation, India is 10 hours 30 minutes ahead of Central Daylight Time and 11 hours 30 minutes ahead of Central Standard Time. The next two clock changes fall on the first Sunday of November 2026 (November 1) and the second Sunday of March 2027 (March 14).

A bug worth making on purpose

A reminder app that stores "UTC minus 6" for a Birmingham student is right all winter and an hour wrong all summer, and wrong all year for a family in Phenix City that keeps Eastern time. Students fix it by storing the zone name, America/Chicago or America/New_York, and letting the tz database do the work.

Coding and AI

Why an AI assistant will not clean the Birmingham parks data for you

It will clean it, quickly. Whether the result is right is a separate question only a person who understands the data can answer.

Ask an AI tool to total the acreage in the Birmingham parks file and it will happily return a number. It will not, unless told, notice that six pieces of one trail carry zero acres or that one park appears twice. The answer looks finished and is quietly wrong.

Students who have done the audit by hand have a checklist in their heads for every total they meet, whether a classmate or a model produced it. That judgment is what turns AI from a shortcut into a useful assistant, and it is learned by doing the slow version first.

Alabama put a computer science course within reach of nearly every high schooler; the edge goes to the students who learn to check an answer, not just generate one. The longer argument is in our essay on learning to code when AI writes code.

FAQ

Alabama questions, answered

The questions Alabama parents and students most often send us.

What does the Computer Science for Alabama Act do?

According to the governor's office in July 2019, HB216 phases in requirements for public schools to offer computer science courses and funds professional learning and certification routes for computer science teachers.

How many Alabama high schools offer computer science?

In the 2025 Code.org data sheet, 351 of 382 Alabama public high schools, or 91.9%, had a computer science course in 2024 to 2025, with the state education agency named as the data source.

How many Alabama students actually take computer science?

The latest published figure is 17,488 of 222,089 high school students, or 7.87%, for 2023 to 2024. The student total behind that share comes from NCES for fall 2022.

Does a computer science credit count toward an Alabama diploma?

We could not confirm the current rule because the Alabama State Department of Education site did not respond when we checked in October 2026. Ask your school counselor for the rule that applies to your student.

Can my child enter the Alabama Governor's App Challenge?

The 2025 to 2026 challenge was open to students in grades K through 12, alone or in teams of up to three, with registration through a teacher. Check the official challenge site for the current cycle.

Will you build my child's app challenge entry?

No. We teach the skills and help a student plan and debug their own project, but the entry is their work. We are not connected with the challenge organizers.

What time zone do you schedule Alabama classes in?

Central Time, America/Chicago. Families in the few eastern border towns that keep Eastern time by habit should tell us, and we will confirm times on their clock.

When can classes take place?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays.

Do you teach in person anywhere in Alabama?

No. Every class is live online with a teacher in India, and we do not claim any Alabama location. A computer, sound and a reliable connection are all a student needs.

Where are the prices?

The fees section of this page lists the free first lesson, the monthly batch and the monthly 1-on-1 option, all in US dollars, with billing only after the free lesson.
