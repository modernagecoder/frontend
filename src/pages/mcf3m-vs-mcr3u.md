# MCF3M vs MCR3U: choosing Grade 11 math in Ontario

Every year, Ontario Grade 10 students pick between two Grade 11 math courses with similar names: Functions and Applications (MCF3M) and Functions (MCR3U). The choice looks small and decides which Grade 12 math courses are open a year later. This page compares the two from the 2007 curriculum, maps where each leads, gives a decision table, and shows one problem asked the MCF3M way and the MCR3U way. We teach both courses alongside school and do not grant Ontario credits; the school grants the credit.

### How the curriculum describes the difference

The programme overview is unusually direct. Functions and Applications explores functions &ldquo;using a more applied approach with less emphasis on abstract concepts than in the Grade 11 university preparation course, Functions&rdquo;. Both courses cover quadratics, exponentials and trigonometry; MCR3U goes further into inverses, transformations, rational expressions and sequences.

The two course types are defined differently too. The document says &ldquo;University/college preparation courses are designed to equip students with the knowledge and skills they need to meet the entrance requirements for specific programs offered at universities and colleges.&rdquo; University preparation courses aim at university entrance requirements generally.

### Who MCF3M was written for

The overview says MCF3M &ldquo;provides preparation for students who plan to pursue technology-related programs in college, while also leaving the option open for some students to pursue postsecondary programs that require the Grade 12 university preparation course Mathematics of Data Management&rdquo;.

It is also the course with the wider entry. Its prerequisite accepts either Grade 10 course, Academic or Applied, while MCR3U lists only the Academic one. Grade 10 course offerings have changed in some schools since 2007, so check your school&rsquo;s current calendar for the Grade 10 course it treats as the prerequisite.

Source: [2007 Ontario mathematics curriculum for Grades 11 and 12](https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/math1112currb.pdf) (programme overview, course types, the MCF3M and MCR3U course pages), read 22 October 2026, and the ministry&rsquo;s online records for both courses, checked that day. This comparison is ours; the ministry has not reviewed it.

### The longer road from MCF3M to Advanced Functions

Advanced Functions lists two prerequisites: Grade 11 Functions, or Mathematics for College Technology, a Grade 12 college course. So an MCF3M student who later wants university math has a route, but it adds a course: MCT4C first, then MHF4U.

The overview describes MCT4C in exactly that role: it &ldquo;may help students who decide to pursue certain university programs to prepare for the Grade 12 university preparation course Advanced Functions&rdquo;. In timetable terms, that can mean an extra semester, summer school, or a fifth year for a student who also needs Calculus and Vectors.

### Data Management is open from both

Mathematics of Data Management accepts either Grade 11 course. For university programs that need some Grade 12 U math but not calculus, MCF3M followed by MDM4U can be enough; the overview names this option itself.

What MCF3M does not open directly is the calculus route. A student heading for engineering, computer science, the physical sciences or most mathematics programs needs MCR3U in Grade 11 to keep that route at its normal length.

Source: [2007 Ontario mathematics curriculum for Grades 11 and 12](https://www.edu.gov.on.ca/eng/curriculum/secondary/math1112currb.pdf), course pages for MHF4U, MCV4U, MDM4U, MCT4C and MAP4C, read 22 October 2026. Schools decide which courses they offer each year and may have their own rules on changing course types, so confirm with the school&rsquo;s guidance office.

A student who scraped through Grade 10 Academic math with weak algebra may do better in MCF3M, earn a solid mark and take MDM4U, than fail MCR3U. A failed course costs more time than the MCT4C route does.

## One quadratic, asked the MCF3M way and the MCR3U way

The clearest way to see the difference between the courses is the same function asked about twice. Both versions are our own, written at Grade 11 test level, and every value was checked with SymPy.

The model. A ball is thrown upward from a balcony. Its height in metres after t seconds is h(t) = &minus;4.9t2 + 19.6t + 1.5.

Version A, the MCF3M way. Find the maximum height and when it occurs, when the ball reaches 15 m, and when it lands.

1. Find the vertex. The axis of symmetry is t = &minus;b/(2a) = &minus;19.6/(2 &times; &minus;4.9) = 2. So the maximum is at t = 2 s, and h(2) = &minus;19.6 + 39.2 + 1.5 = 21.1 m.
2. Write the vertex form as a check. h(t) = &minus;4.9(t &minus; 2)2 + 21.1. Expanding returns the original, so the vertex is right.
3. Solve for 15 m. &minus;4.9t2 + 19.6t + 1.5 = 15 rearranges to 4.9t2 &minus; 19.6t + 13.5 = 0. The quadratic formula gives t of about 0.88 s on the way up and about 3.12 s on the way down. Two answers, both meaningful.
4. Solve for landing. Set h(t) = 0. The formula gives t of about &minus;0.08 or about 4.08. A negative time is before the throw, so it is rejected: the ball lands after about 4.08 s.
5. Interpret. The two 15 m times are equally spaced around t = 2 (2 &minus; 1.12 and 2 + 1.12), which is the symmetry of the parabola showing up in the story.

Version B, the MCR3U way. State a domain and range that fit the context. Then restrict h to the falling part of the flight, find its inverse, and explain what the inverse tells you.

1. Domain and range from the context. The ball is in the air from t = 0 to about t = 4.08, so the domain is 0 &le; t &le; 4.08 (approximately). Its height runs from 0 up to the maximum of 21.1, so the range is 0 &le; h &le; 21.1.
2. Why restrict before inverting. On the whole flight, most heights happen twice, once rising and once falling, so the inverse would not be a function. On the falling part, t &ge; 2, each height happens once.
3. Undo the vertex form in reverse. From h = &minus;4.9(t &minus; 2)2 + 21.1: subtract 21.1, divide by &minus;4.9, take the positive square root (because t &ge; 2), add 2. So t = 2 + &radic;((21.1 &minus; h)/4.9).
4. State the inverse&rsquo;s domain and range. On the falling part, heights run from 21.1 down to 0, so the inverse takes 0 &le; h &le; 21.1 and returns times from 2 to about 4.08.
5. Check and interpret. At h = 1.5, the balcony height, the inverse gives 2 + &radic;(19.6/4.9) = 2 + 2 = 4 s: the ball falls past the balcony exactly 4 seconds after the throw. The inverse answers &ldquo;when is it at this height?&rdquo; for any height at all, in one formula.

**What each version asked of the student (ours)**

|  | Version A (MCF3M style) | Version B (MCR3U style) |
|---|---|---|
| Main skill | Solving: vertex, quadratic formula, rejecting a negative time | Reasoning about the function: domain, range, one-to-one, inverse |
| Typical output | Numbers with units | A new function and an explanation |
| Algebra | Substitution and the quadratic formula | Rearranging a formula in reverse order, choosing a root |
| Where marks are lost | Keeping the negative root | Inverting without restricting the domain |

Students who enjoy version B, or at least follow it, are usually ready for MCR3U. Students who find version A comfortable and version B baffling are not wrong to choose MCF3M, but they should know which Grade 12 courses that choice leaves one step further away.

## Ten placement questions: MCF3M-ready, MCR3U-ready

Our own short check, written for this page, for a Grade 10 student deciding. Questions 1 to 5 test the base both courses assume. Questions 6 to 10 preview ideas that only MCR3U treats in depth. Answers follow; try them without a calculator except for question 5.

1. Expand. (2x &minus; 3)(x + 4).
2. Solve by factoring. x2 &minus; 5x &minus; 14 = 0.
3. Exponent laws. Evaluate 23 &times; 2&minus;1.
4. Growth. A phone game has 1000 players and the number grows by 4% each week. About how many players will it have after 5 weeks?
5. Sine law. In a triangle, A = 50&deg;, B = 60&deg; and side a = 12 cm. Find side b to one decimal place.
6. Inverse. Find the inverse of f(x) = (x &minus; 4)/2.
7. Rational expressions. Simplify (x2 &minus; 4)/(x2 &minus; x &minus; 2) and state the restrictions.
8. Fractional exponents. What is 163/4?
9. Series. Add the first 8 terms of 3 + 6 + 12 + &hellip;
10. Angles beyond 90&deg;. Find every angle from 0&deg; to 360&deg; with sin x = &minus;1/2.

## Placement answers, and what they say

Mark each answer right only if the reason is right too. Then read the note after the list.

1. 2x2 + 5x &minus; 12. 2x &times; x = 2x2; the middle terms are 8x &minus; 3x = 5x; the constant is &minus;12.
2. x = 7 or x = &minus;2. Find two numbers that multiply to &minus;14 and add to &minus;5: &minus;7 and 2. So (x &minus; 7)(x + 2) = 0.
3. 4. Add the exponents: 23 + (&minus;1) = 22 = 4.
4. About 1217. Multiply by 1.04 five times: 1000 &times; 1.045 is about 1216.7. Growth by a fixed percentage is exponential, not linear, so adding 40 each week is wrong.
5. About 13.6 cm. b = 12 sin 60&deg; / sin 50&deg;, about 13.57. The longer side faces the larger angle, which is a quick check.
6. f&minus;1(x) = 2x + 4. f subtracts 4 then halves; the inverse doubles then adds 4.
7. (x + 2)/(x + 1), with x &ne; 2 and x &ne; &minus;1. Factor: (x &minus; 2)(x + 2) over (x &minus; 2)(x + 1). The restrictions come from the original denominator, before cancelling.
8. 8. The fourth root of 16 is 2, and 2 cubed is 8.
9. 765. Each term doubles, so it is geometric with first term 3 and ratio 2. The sum is 3(28 &minus; 1)/(2 &minus; 1) = 3 &times; 255 = 765.
10. 210&deg; and 330&deg;. The reference angle is 30&deg;, and sine is negative in the third and fourth quadrants: 180&deg; + 30&deg; and 360&deg; &minus; 30&deg;.

### Reading the result

Four or five right in 1 to 5: the base for either course is there. Fewer than three: rebuild Grade 10 algebra first, whichever course is chosen, because both assume it.

Questions 6 to 10 are MCR3U topics that MCF3M treats lightly or not at all. A student who manages two or three of them before Grade 11 starts is in good shape for MCR3U.

### What the check cannot tell you

Ten questions are a snapshot, not a verdict. Motivation and the Grade 12 plan matter as much as one evening of algebra. A student set on engineering can close a gap over the summer; a student without a plan may prefer the course where they can do well.

This check is ours and is not used by any school. The school&rsquo;s guidance staff and the Grade 10 teacher know the student&rsquo;s work and should have the final word.

The curriculum gives an MCF3M student two ways into Advanced Functions: take MCT4C first, or take MCR3U as an additional course, for example in summer school. Which of these a school offers, and whether it accepts other routes, is the school&rsquo;s decision.

## MCF3M vs MCR3U: common questions

### What is the difference between MCF3M and MCR3U?

MCF3M is Functions and Applications, a Grade 11 university/college preparation course with three strands: quadratic, exponential and trigonometric functions, taught with an applied approach. MCR3U is Functions, the Grade 11 university preparation course, with four strands that add inverses, transformations, rational expressions, and sequences and series.

### Which is harder, MCF3M or MCR3U?

MCR3U is more abstract. The 2007 curriculum describes MCF3M as using a more applied approach with less emphasis on abstract concepts than MCR3U. Difficulty depends on the student, though: a strong algebra base matters more than the course name.

### Can I take MHF4U after MCF3M?

Not directly. Advanced Functions lists Grade 11 Functions (MCR3U) or Mathematics for College Technology (MCT4C) as its prerequisite. From MCF3M, the curriculum route is MCT4C first, then MHF4U. Some students take MCR3U in summer school instead; ask the school which routes it accepts.

### Can I take Data Management after MCF3M?

Yes. Mathematics of Data Management (MDM4U) lists either Grade 11 Functions or Grade 11 Functions and Applications as its prerequisite, so both MCF3M and MCR3U lead to it.

### Which Grade 11 math do I need for university?

It depends on the program. Programs that need Advanced Functions or Calculus and Vectors, such as engineering, computer science and most mathematics and science programs, are simplest from MCR3U. Some social science programs accept MDM4U, which MCF3M also leads to. Check the program pages before choosing.

### Do you grant the MCF3M or MCR3U credit?

No. Our lessons support whichever course the student takes at school, and we do not grant Ontario credits; those come only from the institution teaching and evaluating it.

### Can a student in Grade 10 Applied math take MCR3U?

The MCR3U course page lists Principles of Mathematics, Grade 10, Academic as its prerequisite, while MCF3M accepts either the Academic or the Applied course. Grade 10 offerings have changed in some schools, so check how your school treats its current Grade 10 course.

### Can you help my teen decide?

We can give a placement check and an honest view of readiness in the free first class, and explain the prerequisite routes. The decision belongs to the family with the school's guidance staff, who know the student's record and the school's course offerings.
