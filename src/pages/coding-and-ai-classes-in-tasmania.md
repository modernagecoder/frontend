## What day of the week does each 2027 term start?

Conway's Doomsday rule applied by hand and in code to DECYP's 2027 dates, then checked against the weekdays the department prints. Our calculation, 7 October 2026.

| Date | Nearest doomsday in 2027 | Weekday |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4 February, Term 1 starts | 28 February (Sunday), 24 days later | Thursday |
| 9 April, Term 1 ends | 4 April (Sunday), 5 days earlier | Friday |
| 26 April, Term 2 starts | 4 April (Sunday), 22 days earlier | Monday |
| 2 July, Term 2 ends | 11 July (Sunday), 9 days later | Friday |
| 19 July, Term 3 starts | 11 July (Sunday), 8 days earlier | Monday |
| 24 September, Term 3 ends | 5 September (Sunday), 19 days earlier | Friday |
| 11 October, Term 4 starts | 10 October (Sunday), 1 day earlier | Monday |
| 16 December, Term 4 ends | 12 December (Sunday), 4 days earlier | Thursday |

### 1. Find the year's doomsday

For the 2000s start from Tuesday. 27 divided by 12 is 2 remainder 3, and 3 divided by 4 is 0 remainder 3. Add 2, 3 and 0 to get 5, and five days after Tuesday is Sunday. Every doomsday in 2027 is a Sunday.

### 2. Count from a landmark

Every month has an easy doomsday: 4 April, 6 June, 8 August, 10 October and 12 December, plus the last day of February. From 10 October, a Sunday, the next day, 11 October, must be Monday.

### 3. Check it

The rule agrees with all eight weekdays the department prints, from Monday 25 January to Friday 17 December, and with Python's calendar on all 22 dates we tested. Every holiday starts on a Saturday and ends on a Sunday.

### A Monday that is also a day off

The published first day of Term 3, Monday 19 July 2027, is also listed as a K to 10 statewide professional learning day, when students do not attend. Spotting that took nothing more than putting two lists side by side once both had weekdays. Learners discover that working out the dates is only half the job; checking them against everything else on the page is the other half.

The weekdays in this section are our calculations from the department's published dates. The dates themselves belong to DECYP and can change; check its page before planning around them.

## Five habits for turning a clever trick into reliable code

Learned on a calendar rule, then applied to any shortcut someone hands you, including one an AI tool writes.

| Habit | In practice | What it prevents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Write the rule down exactly | Spell out every step, including the century anchor | A method that only works in your head |
| Test against a library | Compare with Python's calendar on many dates | Silent errors on the dates you did not try |
| Test the awkward cases | Leap years, 29 February and the first days of January | Bugs hiding in rare dates |
| Use outside evidence | Check against weekdays published by someone else | Agreeing with yourself and calling it proof |
| Read the whole source | Compare every list on the page, not just one | Missing a clash like a term start that is a day off |

The fourth habit is the easiest to skip and the most valuable. Agreeing with Python's calendar shows the code matches Python; agreeing with the eight weekdays the department printed shows both match the real world.

## An AI tool can tell you the weekday of any date. Why should a Tasmanian student learn the rule?

Because the habit of checking an answer against independent evidence is worth more than any single answer.

Ask an assistant what day 4 February 2027 falls on and it will probably be right. Ask it to write a weekday function from scratch and it may hand back something that works for most dates and slips on leap years or the first days of January. The output looks authoritative either way. The only defence is a learner who knows how to test it: against a library, against tricky dates and against evidence printed by someone else.

A student who has derived 2027's weekdays with the Doomsday rule and matched them to the department's eight printed weekdays has practised exactly that discipline. It applies to every generated answer, not just dates: what independent evidence would confirm this, and have I looked at it?

That is why a Tasmanian student should still learn to code in 2026: to be the person who checks the answer, not just the person who receives it. The longer argument is in [why teenagers should still learn to code in 2026](/blog/is-coding-worth-learning-2026).

## Tasmanian coding class questions

What Tasmanian families usually ask before booking.

### How many people live in Tasmania?

The 2021 Census counted 557,571 people in Tasmania, with a median age of 42, the highest of any state, and 247,086 of them in Greater Hobart.

### What is the Doomsday rule?

The Doomsday rule is a method devised by the mathematician John Conway for working out the day of the week of any date mentally. A set of easy dates, such as 4 April, 6 June and 12 December, always share one weekday within a year, and every other date is found by counting from them.

### What is the Tasmanian weekday project?

Learners use the Doomsday rule to work out the weekday of every 2027 Tasmanian term and holiday date, which the department lists without weekdays, then check the method against the eight dates it does print with weekdays. All of them agree.

### When are the 2027 Tasmanian school terms?

Term 1 runs 4 February to 9 April, Term 2 26 April to 2 July, Term 3 19 July to 24 September and Term 4 11 October to 16 December, according to the Department for Education, Children and Young People.
