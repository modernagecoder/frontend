When will Bathurst's population double, and why do three honest methods disagree?

The ABS estimated resident population for the Bathurst council area, 2001 to 2025, read through the ABS Data API.

Doubling time by data window (our calculation from ABS estimates)
Years usedAverage yearly growthExact doubling timeRule of 70
2001 to 20251.026%67.9 years68.2 years
2001 to 20101.188%58.7 years58.9 years
2010 to 20200.960%72.5 years72.9 years
2016 to 20250.794%87.7 years88.2 years
2020 to 20250.866%80.3 years80.8 years

1. Compound growth

The average yearly rate is the 24th root of 45,359 divided by 35,504, minus one: 1.026% a year, not the simple change divided by 24.

2. The exact formula

Doubling time is the natural log of 2 divided by the natural log of 1 plus the rate. For 1.026% that is 67.9 years.

3. The rule of 70

Dividing 70 by the percentage rate gives 68.2 years, within half of one per cent of the exact answer at this low rate.

The window matters more than the method

The three formulas agree to within two years. The choice of years does not: growth averaged 1.19% a year in the first decade, with a fastest single year of 2.49% in 2009, and only 0.79% a year from 2016 to 2025. That spread, 58.7 to 87.7 years, is the honest answer to "when will Bathurst double". Any single year printed without its window is a choice dressed up as a fact. None of these figures is a forecast; they describe what the past rates would imply if continued.

Two geographies are in play and the page keeps them apart. The census table describes the Bathurst urban area, 37,396 people in 2021; the growth series is the ABS estimate for the whole Bathurst council area, which includes villages beyond the city and stood at 43,674 in 2021. Neither number is derived from the other.

Rules of thumb in code

When a shortcut is good enough, and when it breaks

Learners code the exact doubling formula and the shortcuts, then find where the shortcuts fail.

Rule of 70 against the exact doubling time (our calculation)
Growth rateExactRule of 70Error
1%69.66 years70.0 years+0.49%
5%14.21 years14.0 years-1.45%
10%7.27 years7.0 years-3.75%
50%1.71 years1.4 years-18.11%

The rule of 70 works because the natural log of 2 is about 0.693, and for small rates the log of 1 plus r is close to r. As rates rise that approximation fails, and by 50% a year the shortcut is out by almost a fifth. Writing both versions in Python and plotting the gap teaches more about logarithms than a page of algebra.

Coding and AI

Why does an AI give one confident doubling year when the data supports a thirty-year range?

Because the question did not ask for the range, and nothing forces the model to volunteer it.

Ask a chatbot when Bathurst's population will double and it may pick a growth rate, apply the rule of 70 and name a year, all in one fluent paragraph. Each step can be correct while the answer still misleads, because the rate depends on which years it assumed. From this official series the doubling time ranges from 58.7 to 87.7 years depending on the window.

Our Bathurst learners build the window table themselves, then use an assistant to draft the explanation and check that every number it prints names its years. Learning to ask "over which period?" is the part no tool supplies on its own.

A Bathurst teenager who has watched a doubling date slide by three decades with the data window will read every AI forecast more carefully in 2026, and that habit starts with writing the code. The longer argument is in why teenagers should still learn to code in 2026.

FAQ

Bathurst AI and programming questions

Questions Bathurst learners and parents raise most often.

How many people live in Bathurst?

The Bathurst urban area counted 37,396 people at the 2021 Census. The ABS estimate for the wider council area was 45,359 in 2025.

What is the rule of 70?

The rule of 70 is a shortcut for doubling time: divide 70 by the yearly growth rate in per cent. At 1% growth it gives 70 years against an exact 69.66, and it becomes less accurate as rates rise.

What is compound annual growth?

Compound annual growth is the single yearly rate that turns a starting value into an ending value over a number of years. For Bathurst's estimates from 2001 to 2025 it is 1.026% a year.

What is the Bathurst doubling project?

Learners download 25 years of ABS population estimates, compute growth and doubling time with the exact formula and two shortcuts, and show how the answer moves from 58.7 to 87.7 years as the window changes.

Is this a population forecast?

No. The figures show what past growth rates would imply if they continued. They are a programming and maths exercise, not a prediction for Bathurst.
