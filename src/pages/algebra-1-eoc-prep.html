Algebra 1 EOC prep, state by state

“The Algebra 1 EOC” sounds like one exam. It is really a family of state exams that share a subject and little else. Texas gives 50 questions and ties it to graduation. North Carolina gives 60 items and counts the result in the course grade. Pennsylvania splits it into two modules. New York calls it a Regents. This page lays four of them side by side, each fact taken from that state’s own education department, then shows what they all test and how to prepare for whichever one your child faces.

In short

An Algebra 1 EOC (end-of-course) exam is a state test taken when a student finishes Algebra I. Its form depends on the state: Texas’s STAAR Algebra I has 50 questions, North Carolina’s NC Math 1 has 60 items, Pennsylvania’s Keystone Algebra I has two modules worth 60 points, and New York’s Algebra I Regents has 35 questions. Modern Age Coders teaches Algebra I for any state’s EOC, live online, in a batch or 1-on-1, through our Algebra Foundations course.

Four states

How many questions are on the Algebra 1 EOC?

It depends on the state: 35 in New York, 50 in Texas, 60 items in North Carolina, and two modules worth 60 points in Pennsylvania. The table sets out what each state publishes.

Algebra I end-of-course exams in four states (each row from that state’s education department, read 31 October 2026)
State and examLengthTimeCalculator
Texas: STAAR Algebra I50 questions, 59 pointsNot given in the blueprint; ask the schoolGraphing, required throughout; solver functions disabled
North Carolina: NC Math 1 EOC60 items (50 operational, 10 field test), one point eachAbout 3 hours for most; up to 4 hoursGraphing; 15 operational items calculator inactive, 35 active
Pennsylvania: Keystone Algebra ITwo modules of 30 points, 60 points in allNo time limit; typically 2 to 3 hoursSee PDE’s administration materials
New York: Algebra I Regents35 questions, 82 creditsThree hoursGraphing, for the whole exam

Why the numbers differ

Each state writes its own standards and its own test. Texas tests the Texas Essential Knowledge and Skills; North Carolina its Standard Course of Study, adopted in June 2016; Pennsylvania its Core Standards; New York its Next Generation standards.

Field-test questions also vary. North Carolina says plainly that 10 of its 60 items are field test items that do not count toward the score.

What does not differ

All four require or provide a graphing calculator, all four mix multiple choice with questions where the student produces the answer, and all four are built around linear functions.

A student who truly understands Algebra I is ready for any of them with a few weeks spent on the local format.

What it counts for

Does the Algebra 1 EOC count toward graduation or grades?

In some states it is a graduation test, in others part of the course grade. Here is what each of the four says about itself.

What the Algebra I EOC counts for (each state’s own wording, read 31 October 2026)
StateWhat the state says
TexasAlgebra I is among the five EOCs TEA lists for graduation testing, with approved substitute tests as an alternative
North CarolinaPer State Board policy TEST-003, EOC results count as at least 20 percent of the student’s final course grade
PennsylvaniaFrom the class of 2022 to 2023, proficiency on the Algebra I, Literature and Biology Keystones or another of five Pathways to Graduation
New YorkUnder current rules, one math Regents or approved alternative for a Regents or local diploma; a proposal to end this from fall 2027 awaits approval

Pennsylvania’s pathways

Pennsylvania’s spring 2026 parent FAQ says that beginning with the class of 2022 to 2023, students “must demonstrate proficiency on the Algebra I, Literature, and Biology Keystone Exams to graduate or fulfill one of the other pathways provided to graduate.”

The same FAQ says results are banked until senior year for graduation purposes. PDE’s graduation page adds that students who do not reach proficiency or a minimum composite score can show proficiency through locally set requirements under the pathways.

North Carolina’s grade weight

NCDPI’s state tests page: “schools are to use results from all EOC assessments as at least twenty (20) percent of the student’s final grade for each respective course.”

So a North Carolina student who does well all year but poorly on the EOC sees it in the report card, even though the test is not a stand-alone graduation hurdle on that page.

Sources: TEA graduation requirements; NCDPI State Tests; PDE Pathways to Graduation; NYSED diploma requirements. Read 31 October 2026.

Shared content

What is on the Algebra 1 EOC in every state?

Linear functions, everywhere. Beyond that, the states group the content differently, but the same ideas keep appearing.

How four states organize Algebra I content (each state’s blueprint or test definition, read 31 October 2026)
StateHow content is grouped
TexasFive reporting categories, two of them linear (one on graphing and describing, one on writing and solving), plus number and algebraic methods, quadratics and exponentials
North CarolinaDomain weights: Number and Quantity and Algebra 36 to 40%; Functions 32 to 36%; Statistics and Probability 18 to 20%; Geometry 8 to 12%
PennsylvaniaModule 1, Operations and Linear Equations and Inequalities; Module 2, Linear Functions and Data Organization; 50 percent of points each
New YorkAlgebra 48 to 61%; Functions 24 to 32%; Statistics and Probability 7 to 15%; Number and Quantity 4 to 10%

The common core of the subject

Our reading across the four: every exam tests writing and solving linear equations and inequalities, slope and rate of change, graphing lines, systems of two equations, and interpreting data. That is the part of Algebra I a student cannot afford to be shaky on anywhere.

Texas and New York also give real weight to quadratic and exponential functions. North Carolina’s Math 1 includes a geometry domain worth 8 to 12 percent.

Statistics matters more than students expect

North Carolina puts 18 to 20 percent of NC Math 1 on statistics and probability, and Pennsylvania gives half a module to data organization. Scatter plots, lines of fit and reading box plots are worth practising early.

These are also some of the quickest topics to improve, because they reward careful reading more than long calculation.

Worked example

Linear or exponential? The comparison every Algebra 1 EOC asks

Our own problem. Some version of it appears in every state’s content, because it tests whether a student understands what the two kinds of growth mean.

  1. The problem (ours). A sunflower is 20 cm tall and grows 5 cm each week. A vine is 2 cm long and grows by 50 percent each week. In which week does the vine first become longer than the sunflower?
  2. Name the growth. Adding 5 each week is linear: S = 20 + 5w. Multiplying by 1.5 each week is exponential: V = 2(1.5)w.
  3. Build a table before any algebra. Week 0: 20 and 2. Week 4: 40 and about 10.1. Week 6: 50 and about 22.8. Week 8: 60 and about 51.3. Week 9: 65 and about 76.9.
  4. Read it. The vine is still shorter in week 8 and longer in week 9, so it first passes the sunflower in week 9.
  5. Check with the graphing calculator. Graph y = 20 + 5x and y = 2(1.5)x; they cross between x = 8 and x = 9. The table and the graph agree.

A second skill: reading slope in context (ours)

A bike rental costs C = 12 + 3h for h hours. The 3 is the rate of change, 3 per hour; the 12 is the starting amount when h = 0, a fixed fee. Every state’s exam asks students to say what slope and intercept mean in words.

Answer with units and with the situation, not just “slope is 3”.

Why we teach tables first

A table shows the difference between adding and multiplying before the formula hides it. Students who see that pattern rarely confuse 20 + 5w with 20(5)w on test day.

It also works without a calculator, which matters in North Carolina’s calculator-inactive section.

Any other state

How to find your own state’s Algebra 1 EOC documents

If your state is not one of the four above, these five steps find the documents that matter. It is the method we use ourselves.

  1. Get the exam’s exact name. Ask the school or check the course syllabus: it might be an EOC, a Keystone, a Regents or a state-specific name.
  2. Go to the state education department, not a prep site. Search the department’s own website for that name plus “blueprint” or “test specifications”.
  3. Find three documents. The blueprint or test specifications (questions and weights), the calculator policy, and the testing calendar for 2026 to 2027.
  4. Find the official practice. Most states release items or a practice form, often inside the online testing platform.
  5. Map it to the class. Line up the blueprint categories with the units in your child’s course so revision follows the weights.

We could not read several state department sites for this page on 31 October 2026, so it says nothing about their exams. If your family is in one of those states, bring the documents to the first class and we will read them with you.

Algebra 1 EOC questions

Algebra 1 EOC: what families ask

How many questions are on the Algebra 1 EOC?

It varies by state. Texas's STAAR Algebra I has 50 questions, North Carolina's NC Math 1 has 60 items of which 50 count, Pennsylvania's Keystone Algebra I has two modules worth 60 points in all, and New York's Algebra I Regents has 35 questions.

How long is the Algebra 1 EOC?

North Carolina estimates about three hours for most students, with four as the maximum; New York allows three hours; Pennsylvania sets no time limit and expects two to three hours. Check your own state's administration rules.

Can you use a calculator on the Algebra 1 EOC?

In all four states compared here, a graphing calculator is part of the exam. North Carolina also has a calculator-inactive section, and Texas requires solver functions to be switched off.

What happens if you fail the Algebra 1 EOC?

It depends on the state. Texas offers December and June retakes and approved substitutes, Pennsylvania has other pathways to graduation, and in North Carolina the score counts as at least 20 percent of the course grade. Ask the school counselor what applies.

What is on the Algebra 1 EOC?

Every version tests linear equations, inequalities and functions heavily. Texas and New York add quadratic and exponential functions, North Carolina includes some geometry and a large statistics share, and Pennsylvania focuses its two modules on linear work and data.

Is the Algebra 1 EOC hard?

It is a fair test of the course: students who understand linear functions and practise the format usually find it manageable. The hardest part is often the written or typed answers, where guessing does not help.

Do you cover the Florida Algebra 1 EOC?

We teach Algebra I to students in Florida and every other state. This page does not describe Florida's exam because we could not read the state department's site when writing it; bring the official documents and we will plan from them.

Where can I find Algebra 1 EOC practice tests?

On your state education department's site: Texas, North Carolina, Pennsylvania and New York all publish released items or practice forms. Use your own state's, since formats differ.

When are your Algebra I classes?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. We schedule in ET, CT, MT or PT.

Are you connected with any state education department?

No. Modern Age Coders is an independent online school. We quote each state's public documents and link to them, and we write our own practice problems.
