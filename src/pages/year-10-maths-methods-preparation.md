# Year 10 maths for Methods

The jump from Year 10 maths to a calculus-based senior course is real, and most of it is not about calculus. Students stumble in Year 11 Methods because algebra is slow, function notation is unfamiliar and graphs feel like pictures rather than information. Year 10 is the year to fix those things calmly, before the senior course starts moving.

At a glance: Victorian course: VCE Mathematical Methods, Units 1 to 4; Unit length: At least 50 hours each; Units 3 and 4 exams: Examination 1 (no technology) and 2; NSW equivalent route: Stage 5 Paths into Mathematics Advanced; Assumed background: Number, algebra, function, geometry, probability, statistics; Common gap: Algebra speed and function notation; We teach: The Year 10 base, done thoroughly; We never: Promise a study score. ## Three courses that build a Methods-ready base

Algebra first, functions and graphs second, probability third.

- [Algebra at speed](/courses/algebra-foundations-masterclass): Expanding, factorising, solving and rearranging until they are fast and accurate, because Methods uses them on every page.
- [Functions and graphs](/courses/complete-high-school-mathematics-mastery): Function notation, graph sketching and transformations, the language Units 1 and 2 of Methods are written in.
- [Probability and statistics](/courses/statistics-probability-maths-course): Probability rules and data handling, the strand Methods returns to in both Year 11 and Year 12.

## No prerequisites, but a lot is assumed

These points are quoted from the VCE Mathematics Study Design, accredited from 2023, linked from VCAA's Mathematical Methods page and read on 7 October 2026.

**VCE Mathematical Methods: entry and scope, in VCAA's words**

| Topic | What the study design says |
|---|---|
| Entry | "There are no prerequisites for entry to Units 1, 2 and 3" |
| Assumed background | Students in Mathematical Methods Units 1 and 2 "are assumed to have a sound background in number, algebra, function, geometry, probability and statistics" |
| What the study covers | "simple elementary functions, transformations and combinations of these functions, algebra, calculus, probability and statistics, and their applications" |
| Sequence | Units 1 and 2 cover "assumed key knowledge and key skills as preparation for Units 3 and 4" |
| Time | Each unit involves at least 50 hours of scheduled classroom instruction |
| Specialist link | Specialist Mathematics Units 3 and 4 assume current enrolment in, or completion of, Mathematical Methods Units 3 and 4 |

Read the assumed background line carefully: number, algebra, function, geometry, probability and statistics. That is effectively a checklist for Year 10. A student who is shaky on any of the six will meet it again early in Units 1 and 2, usually at speed.

The phrase "combinations of these functions" in the study's description is worth noticing too. Combining functions, by adding them, multiplying them or feeding one into another, is a central idea in Methods, and it is the subject of the lesson further down this page.

VCAA designs Units 1 to 4 as a sequence. Units 1 and 2 are not a separate course students can skim through; they deliberately lay down what Units 3 and 4 rely on, and the final examinations can draw on it.

For students also considering Specialist Mathematics, the dependence runs one way: Specialist Units 3 and 4 assume Methods Units 3 and 4. Our [guide to choosing computing subjects](/choosing-year-11-computing-subjects-australia) covers the computing side of senior choices.

## Coursework, a technology-free exam, and an exam with technology

The weightings and exam rules below are from the VCE Mathematics Study Design and VCAA's 2026 examination timetable, checked on 7 October 2026.

**Mathematical Methods Units 3 and 4 assessment**

| Component | Weighting | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Unit 3 School-assessed Coursework | 20% | Set and marked by the school |
| Unit 4 School-assessed Coursework | 20% | Set and marked by the school |
| Units 3 and 4 Examination 1 | 20% | Technology-free; 2026 sitting Thursday 5 November, 9:00 to 10:15 am including reading |
| Units 3 and 4 Examination 2 | 40% | Assumes VCAA-approved technology; 2026 sitting Friday 6 November, 11:45 am to 2:00 pm including reading |

Examination 1 is technology-free, which is the single strongest argument for working on algebra in Year 10. Every manipulation in that paper is done by hand, under time pressure, and the marks go to students for whom expanding and factorising is automatic.

Examination 2 carries the largest single weighting at 40% and assumes access to approved technology. It rewards students who understand what their calculator output means and can interpret it in context, not just produce it.

Coursework in Units 3 and 4 adds another 40% between them. Schools design those tasks, and they are the student's own work. We teach mathematics with our own problems and never help with a task a school will mark.

At the end of Units 3 and 4, VCAA reports a study score on a scale of 0 to 50, measuring how a student performed relative to everyone else who took the study. Like any rank, it cannot honestly be promised by anyone.

## Stage 5 Paths lead towards Mathematics Advanced

NSW does not use the name Methods; its calculus-based course is Mathematics Advanced. The Year 7 to 10 structure that leads there is described in the Mathematics K-10 Syllabus (2022), read on 7 October 2026.

**NSW Mathematics K-10 (2022): Stage 5 Paths outcome groups**

| Path outcome group | Why it matters for Advanced |
|---|---|
| Further algebra and equations | The algebra every Advanced topic relies on |
| Variation and rates of change | The idea underneath the derivative |
| Functions and graphs | The language of Year 11 Advanced |
| Further trigonometry | A head start on the trigonometric functions |
| Further probability | Groundwork for random variables in Year 12 |

The syllabus describes a Core that typically covers learning up to the middle of Stage 5, with Paths that teachers program "as appropriate to the needs of the students by the end of Stage 5". It also states that "The Core outcomes and content provide students with the foundation for Mathematics Standard 11-12."

The implication for a student aiming at Advanced is clear: the Paths content, especially further algebra, functions and graphs, is where the preparation happens. The right-hand column of the table above is our reading of how each group connects; the group names are NESA's.

The Paths also include further area and volume, geometrical figures and proof, introduction to networks and data analysis and statistical enquiry. They are all useful; the five in the table are the ones we prioritise for a student heading into Advanced.

NESA notes that the Core-Paths structure is not meant to lock students into a pathway at the end of Stage 4. A student who has missed some Paths content can catch up in Year 10, and that is often where our lessons begin. ## Composite functions do not commute

Feeding one function into another is a core Methods idea, and new students often assume the order does not matter. A small table settles it.

A worked lesson, written by us. Let f(x) = x + 3 and g(x) = x squared. The composite f(g(x)) means "square first, then add 3"; the composite g(f(x)) means "add 3 first, then square".

**f(g(x)) and g(f(x)) for f(x) = x + 3 and g(x) = x squared**

| x | f(g(x)) = x squared + 3 | g(f(x)) = (x + 3) squared | Equal? |
|---|---|---|---|
| -2 | 7 | 1 | No |
| -1 | 4 | 4 | Yes |
| 0 | 3 | 9 | No |
| 2 | 7 | 25 | No |
| 5 | 28 | 64 | No |

The two composites agree at exactly one value. Expanding gives f(g(x)) = x squared + 3 and g(f(x)) = x squared + 6x + 9. Setting them equal leaves 6x + 6 = 0, so x = -1, which matches the table. Everywhere else, the order changes the answer.

The lesson for Year 10 is the habit, not the result: read a composite from the inside out, work out which function acts first, and test a value or two before trusting any algebra. The same habit prevents errors with transformations of graphs later on.

Programmers meet the same idea constantly. Calling one function on the result of another is composition, and swapping the order of two steps in a data pipeline usually changes the output. Students who code can write f and g in Python and print both composites for a range of x in a few lines.

Function notation itself, f(x), is the other hurdle. Students who are comfortable reading f(2) as "the output when the input is 2" find Methods far less foreign. We spend real time on that reading skill in Year 10.

## What Year 10 families ask

### What is Maths Methods?

In Victoria, Mathematical Methods is the VCE calculus-based mathematics study, covering functions and their combinations, algebra, calculus, probability and statistics across Units 1 to 4.

### Are there prerequisites for VCE Mathematical Methods?

VCAA says there are no prerequisites for entry to Units 1, 2 and 3, but students starting Methods Units 1 and 2 are assumed to have a sound background in number, algebra, function, geometry, probability and statistics.

### How is Methods assessed in Year 12?

For Units 3 and 4: school-assessed coursework worth 20% in each unit, Examination 1 worth 20% and Examination 2 worth 40%. Examination 1 is technology-free.

### When are the 2026 Methods exams?

From the VCAA timetable: Examination 1 on Thursday 5 November 2026, 9:00 to 10:15 am, and Examination 2 on Friday 6 November 2026, 11:45 am to 2:00 pm, with reading time included.

### What is the NSW equivalent of Methods?

NSW calls its calculus-based senior course Mathematics Advanced. In Years 9 and 10, the Stage 5 Paths of the Mathematics K-10 Syllabus (2022) build towards it.

### What is a composite function?

A function made by feeding the output of one function into another, such as f(g(x)). The order matters: with f(x) = x + 3 and g(x) = x squared, f(g(2)) = 7 but g(f(2)) = 25.

### What should my child focus on in Year 10?

Algebra by hand, function notation, reading graphs, and probability. Those match the background VCAA says Methods students are assumed to have.

### Does Specialist Mathematics need Methods?

VCAA states that enrolment in Specialist Mathematics Units 3 and 4 assumes current enrolment in, or completion of, Mathematical Methods Units 3 and 4.

### Can you promise a good study score?

No. A study score measures performance relative to everyone else that year, and nobody can honestly promise it.

### Do you teach Queensland, WA or SA Methods too?

We teach the shared mathematics any senior calculus course needs. For the details of a specific state's course, please check that state's curriculum authority.
