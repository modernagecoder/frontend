### How it was counted

Code.org lists the primary source for the New Hampshire figure as school course catalogs and teacher surveys, not a state education agency data return.

### The six schools

In 2024 to 2025, six of the 95 high schools in the count were not recorded as offering a course. The data does not name them and neither do we.

### The missing column

Every year of the participation tab is blank for New Hampshire, so there is no published count of how many high schoolers took computer science.

A catalog listing proves a course exists on paper. It does not prove the course ran that year, how many sections it had, or whether it was introductory or advanced. That is why a near-universal access figure can sit alongside families who tell us their student could not fit the only computer science section into a timetable already full of required classes.

## Schools giving AP exams, and what that count does and does not say

The College Board&rsquo;s state-by-state school report counts schools where students sat at least one AP exam, across every AP subject.

**New Hampshire schools with AP exams (College Board, School Report of AP Exams by State, 2023-24)**

| Year | All schools | Public | Non-public |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2023 | 108 | 81 | 27 |
| 2024 | 108 | 81 | 27 |

### Flat on both lines

The total, the public count and the non-public count were identical in 2023 and 2024, which is unusual in a report where most states move by a few schools each year.

### All subjects, not just CS

A school appears here if students took any AP exam, so this is a ceiling on where AP computer science could be tested, not a count of where it was.

### More schools than the CS count

The AP report covers 108 schools while the access count covers 95 high schools, because it includes non-public schools and uses its own rules.

For a New Hampshire student aiming at AP Computer Science Principles or AP Computer Science A, the practical question is simpler than either dataset: does my school offer the course and the exam this year, and if not, can I sit the exam elsewhere? ## How many trails can you hike and ski? Sets on the NH GRANIT trail inventory

NH GRANIT, the state&rsquo;s geographic data clearinghouse, publishes NH Recreational Trails as open data. Its attribute table is a natural first lesson in sets: distinct values, unions, intersections and differences.

**Set results on named trails, case-folded (our calculation from NH Recreational Trails, downloaded 17 October 2026)**

| Set expression | Meaning | Distinct trail names |
|---|---|---|
| H | Trails with a hiking segment marked Y | 3,547 |
| S | Trails with a cross-country ski segment marked Y | 817 |
| B | Trails with a mountain bike segment marked Y | 886 |
| H & S | Hike and ski | 726 |
| S - H | Ski but never marked for hiking | 91 |
| H | S | Hike or ski | 3,638 |
| H & S & B | All three activities | 437 |

### 1. Count distinct

The layer has 15,791 segment records but 6,364 different trail names. Trimming spaces and upper-casing cuts that to 6,306, the first lesson in why you normalise before you deduplicate.

### 2. Inspect a flag

The hiking column holds five distinct values, not two: Y, N, NA, UNKNOWN and a blank. Students decide which ones mean yes before building any set.

### 3. Combine sets

With Python sets, hiking & ski and ski minus hiking are one line each. The table above is the result; checking that 3,547 + 817 - 726 = 3,638 proves the union.

### A name is not a trail

There are 831 named trails that appear in more than one town, and some are not one trail at all: BLUE TRL turns up in 24 towns and RED TRL in 20. Blank names cover 3,413 segments. The counts above are ours, from one download, and the data publisher describes the layer as an approximation for planning use only whose coverage should not be treated as complete.

One more distinct-values surprise makes a good discussion: the county column lists the ten New Hampshire counties plus (Maine), (Vermont) and ALBANY, which is a town rather than a county. Spotting values that do not belong to the set you expected is a skill students use in every data project afterwards, and in SQL it becomes a SELECT DISTINCT query.

## Why a New Hampshire student who can code checks the AI&rsquo;s count

A distinct-count question is easy to ask an AI tool and easy for it to get wrong.

Ask a chatbot how many trails in New Hampshire allow both hiking and skiing and it will produce a tidy number. It cannot know that the state layer has five versions of yes and no, a few hundred names that differ only in capitals, and thousands of unnamed segments. A student who built the sets can.

That is the practical meaning of AI literacy: knowing which assumptions sit under an answer. Students who have cleaned and combined the data themselves can read an AI response, see what it must have assumed, and test it, rather than copying the figure into a report.

High schools across New Hampshire list computer science; the habit worth building on top is questioning a count before trusting it. The longer argument is in [should students still learn to code](/blog/is-coding-worth-learning-2026).
