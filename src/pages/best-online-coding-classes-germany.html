What the checklist leaves out on purpose

It has no row for the number of courses on offer, the size of a video library or the number of countries a company works in. None of those tells you whether your child will learn. A provider with three good courses and a teacher who knows your child is worth more than one with three hundred courses and a different stranger every week.

It also has no row for awards, badges and logos. They are easy to put on a website and hard for a parent to check, and none of them shows you how a teacher reacts when your child is stuck. A trial class does.

How to use it

Write the eight questions down and ask each provider the same ones, by email or on a call. Notice which answers come quickly and plainly and which ones turn into a sales pitch.

Then book a trial with the two providers whose answers you liked, and let your child decide between them. A child who looks forward to the second lesson has told you more than any comparison table.

Question 1

Are live online coding classes better than recorded courses?

Recorded courses are cheaper to make and to buy, and for a self-driven adult they can work. For most children they stop working at the first error message.

Where recorded lessons fail

Programming is mostly getting stuck. A missing bracket, a variable spelled two ways, a loop that never ends: each one needs a person who can look at this child's code, now, and ask the right question. A video can show how the teacher's program works. It cannot see why the child's does not.

A recorded course also has no way to notice that a learner copied the answer instead of understanding it. That matters more than ever now that an AI chat can produce a working program in seconds.

What to look for in a live class

The test is simple: during a live class, does the teacher look at the learner's own screen or code and talk about it? If the class is a lecture on video with the cameras off, it is a recorded course that happens to be on time.

Ask how many learners share one teacher, and how a learner gets help when stuck in the middle of a task. Every one of our classes is live, with a mentor who reads the learner's code, in a group of fifteen to twenty or one to one.

Question 2

Should an online coding class for a child in Germany be in German or in English?

Either can be right. What is never right is finding out in the third lesson. We teach in English only, live, and we say so first.

Who learns well in English

Families for whom English is the language of school, home or work: pupils at international schools, which the association of international schools in Germany (AGIS) describes as bringing together children from up to 65 nations with English as the shared language of lessons; families who moved to Germany for a job; pupils in bilingual streams such as Berlin's Staatliche Europa-Schule, which the KMK describes as a network of bilingual streams at 33 schools with about 6,000 pupils.

And many pupils at ordinary German schools, who get two subjects for the price of one hour: they practise programming and they practise English. Python's keywords, if, while, def and return, are English words in every country.

Who should choose German

A child who is still a beginner in English and finds instructions in it tiring. Learning two hard things at once can be motivating for some children and exhausting for others; the trial class shows which.

Our mentors teach in English and adapt to the learner: they speak clearly, slow down, rephrase rather than repeat, and get used to each learner's accent. German is not a language we teach in, and we would rather lose a family at this question than after a month.

English also travels beyond the classroom. The Bundeswettbewerb Künstliche Intelligenz, a national AI competition for pupils in Germany, accepts projects in German or in English, according to its rules, and the documentation that programmers use every day is written in English first.

Question 3

How big should an online coding group be?

There is no single right number, but there should always be a stated one. A provider who cannot tell you how many children share a teacher is telling you something.

One to one

The mentor follows one learner's pace completely. Best suited to a learner preparing for something specific, a learner who is shy in groups, or an adult with a project of their own.

A group

Learners see how others solve the same problem, explain their ideas aloud and keep each other going. It suits children who enjoy company and families who want regular classes at a lower monthly fee.

What to ask

How are learners placed into a group: by age, by level, or both? What happens if a learner is clearly faster or slower than the rest? Can they move?

Our groups have fifteen to twenty learners, placed by age and level, with one regular mentor; the alternative is one to one. Learners who outgrow a group move up. We say the number here because it is the first thing a careful parent should ask anyone.

Does my child get coding at school in Germany?

It depends on the Land, the school type and the Klasse, and it is changing every year. A good provider asks where you live before suggesting a course.

What that means for choosing a class

A ten year old in Hamburg will meet Informatik at school within a few years; a ten year old in Hessen may never meet it as a compulsory subject. The same online class can be enrichment for one and the only coding lesson for the other.

At the top of school the gap widens. Across Germany, the monitor finds, only about 15 percent of Oberstufe pupils have Informatik as a course in their final two years.

The question to ask

Ask any provider: "My child is in Klasse 6 at a Gymnasium in Niedersachsen. What will the school teach in computing, and how does your course relate to it?" A provider who answers with a list of programming languages and nothing about the school has not understood your situation.

We do not replace school Informatik and we award no German grades. A Land-by-Land table, written in German, is on our Informatik-Nachhilfe page.

Question 4

What time are online coding classes in Germany, and what happens when the clocks change?

A class time is only useful in your own clock. Many online teachers work from countries that never change their clocks, and that is where the confusion of March and October comes from.

Example class times in Germany and the same moment in India, on either side of the 2027 clock change
In GermanyIndia, until 27 March 2027India, from 28 March 2027
15:1519:4518:45
16:4521:1520:15
Saturday 11:3016:0015:00
Sunday 10:1514:4513:45

Germany's legal time is set by the Einheitenzeitgesetz: Central European Time, UTC+1, and Central European Summer Time, UTC+2. Under the Sommerzeitverordnung, summer time runs from the last Sunday in March to the last Sunday in October, so it ended on 25 October 2026 and begins again on 28 March 2027. India keeps UTC+5:30 all year. The rows are examples, not a timetable.

The problem in one sentence

India runs four and a half hours ahead of German winter time and three and a half ahead of German summer time, so a class pinned to India time jumps by an hour on a German afternoon twice a year, while a class pinned to German time jumps for the mentor instead.

What a good provider does

It keeps the class at the same German time all year and lets the mentor's side move, and it tells you the time in your clock when you book. Our booking page shows demo times in your own time zone, and each class is agreed in German time.

Our students turn that sentence into a program, and the first version contains the very mistake many timetables make.

Both lines say 21:15, and the second one is wrong. On 1 April 2027 Germany is already on summer time, so India is only three and a half hours ahead and the mentor's clock reads 20:15. The bug is that german is a time with no zone attached: Python has no idea it belongs to Germany, so it cannot know that Germany changed its clocks on 28 March. Adding a fixed offset bakes one season's gap into the program for ever.

  1. Ages 9 to 11, on paper. Draw two clocks, Berlin and Kolkata. Move the Berlin clock forward one hour for summer and leave Kolkata alone. How far apart are they now?
  2. Ages 11 to 14, Python. Run Version 1, then explain in one sentence why both lines agree. Add the date 2026-10-22, the Thursday before the autumn change, and predict its output before running Version 2.
  3. Ages 14 and up. Print every Thursday from January to June 2027 with the India time of a 16:45 German class, and mark the week where it changes.

The programs and their output are our own work, run with Python 3 on 3 November 2026, using the Europe/Berlin and Asia/Kolkata zones of the standard time-zone database.

Question 5

Which AI tools may children use in an online coding class in Germany?

AI now appears in almost every coding course advertisement. The age limits are set by each tool's own terms, and several of them are higher in Germany than in other countries.

For a family the question is not whether a course mentions AI, but whether it asks a twelve year old to create accounts on tools whose own terms do not allow twelve year olds. In our classes for under-18s we do not use or recommend tools set at 18, and for tools that need a parent's permission we ask the parent first. A good class can teach how AI works, with small models the learner builds, without signing a child up anywhere.

There is a second question underneath: does the class teach a child to think, or to prompt? A learner who pastes an AI answer they cannot explain has learned to copy. In our classes the learner writes the code and explains it aloud, and AI comes in once they can judge what it produces.

Questions 7 and 8

What should a trial class show you, and how easy should stopping be?

The trial is the one moment when you see the product instead of the advertising. Use it well, and settle how stopping works before the first payment, not after.

  1. Let your child do the work. A good trial gives the learner a small task at their level and watches how they think. If your child spends most of the time watching a presentation, it was a sales meeting.
  2. Listen for questions. A good teacher asks the learner why their code does something before telling them how to fix it.
  3. Check the language and the pace. Could your child follow every instruction? Did the teacher slow down when needed?
  4. Ask for a recommendation in writing. Which course, which group, which times in German time, and why.
  5. Ask how stopping works. Is the plan monthly? How much notice is needed? Is it written down somewhere you can read before paying?

Choosing an online coding class in Germany: common questions

What is the best online coding class in Germany?

The one that passes the eight questions for your child: live teaching, a language your child follows, a stated group size, times in German time across the clock change, no AI tools above your child's age, a sense of what the school teaches, a real trial and a simple way to stop. We do not rank providers, including ourselves.

Are the classes in German?

No. Our classes are live and taught in English only, by mentors who match the learner's pace and accent. We do not offer lessons in German, and we say so before any trial so that no family is surprised.

Are live classes worth it compared with recorded courses?

For most children, yes. Learning to code means getting stuck, and a live teacher can look at a child's own code and ask the question that unblocks it. A recorded video cannot see the mistake the learner made.

What time are classes for families in Germany?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Classes are agreed in German time and stay there when Germany changes between UTC+1 and UTC+2.

How many children are in a group?

Our groups have fifteen to twenty learners, placed by age and level, with one regular mentor. The alternative is one to one, where the mentor teaches only your child.

Can children use ChatGPT in an online coding class?

OpenAI's terms allow ChatGPT from 13 with a parent's or guardian's permission under 18. Claude requires users to be 18, and Google and Microsoft set higher minimum ages in Germany for Gemini and Copilot. In our classes for under-18s we never use tools set at 18.

Is coding taught at German schools?

In some Länder and some grades. In its 2026/27 monitor the Gesellschaft für Informatik lists Berlin, Brandenburg, Bremen, Hessen, Rheinland-Pfalz and Sachsen-Anhalt as the six Länder where Informatik is not yet a compulsory subject for every pupil. Hamburg, by contrast, requires four weekly hours across grades 7 to 10.

At what age should a child start an online coding class?

Many children start happily at 6 or 7 with block coding in Scratch, and move to typed Python from about 9. What matters more than age is whether the child enjoys puzzles and can concentrate for an hour with a mentor.

Is there a free trial?

Yes. You pay nothing for the first class and give no card details. It is a real lesson at the learner's level, after which the mentor suggests a course and a group or one-to-one plan.

Which currency do I pay in?

Fees are shown in euro at the European Central Bank reference rate; the payment is taken in US dollars and converted by your bank, so the statement amount can differ slightly.
