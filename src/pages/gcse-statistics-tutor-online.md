# GCSE Statistics tutor online: AQA 8382 and Edexcel 1ST0

GCSE Statistics is often the GCSE nobody planned for. It is offered as a second maths qualification, sometimes in an options block and sometimes after school, and it is usually taught in fewer hours than GCSE Maths. ## What is the difference between AQA and Edexcel GCSE Statistics?

Less than for GCSE Maths. Ofqual sets the same assessment objectives for both, and both sit in June only. The main differences are paper length, calculator expectations and how the content is laid out.

**AQA GCSE Statistics 8382 and Pearson Edexcel GCSE Statistics 1ST0, from each board's specification and June 2027 timetable**

| Feature | AQA 8382 | Pearson Edexcel 1ST0 |
|---|---|---|
| Specification read | Version 1.2, June 2026 | Issue 2, December 2017 |
| Papers | Two, same tier, same series | Two, same tier, same series |
| Length of each paper | 1 hour 45 minutes | 1 hour 30 minutes |
| Marks per paper | 80 | 80 |
| Tiers | Foundation 1 to 5; Higher 4 to 9, allowed grade 3 | Foundation 1 to 5; Higher 4 to 9 |
| Exam series | Every May/June | May/June only |
| June 2027, Paper 1 | Monday 7 June, afternoon | Monday 7 June, afternoon |
| June 2027, Paper 2 | Wednesday 16 June, morning | Wednesday 16 June, morning |
| Qualification number | 603/1177/0 | 603/1084/4 |

The two boards sit on the same afternoon and morning in June 2027, because the GCSE timetable is shared across boards. AQA's papers are fifteen minutes longer for the same 80 marks, which gives a little more time per mark; AQA describes its question styles as "Multiple choice, short answer and a Statistical Enquiry Cycle (SEC) question", while Pearson describes "short response, medium response and extended response questions". AQA's specification also states plainly that "A student sitting the Higher tier who just fails to achieve grade 4 will be awarded an allowed grade 3."

**Assessment objectives for GCSE Statistics, the same on both boards (AQA and Pearson specifications; percentages approximate, plus or minus 3)**

| Objective | What it asks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|
| AO1 | Knowledge and understanding of standard techniques to collect and represent data, and to calculate summary statistics and probabilities | 55% |
| AO2 | Interpret statistical information in context and reason statistically to draw conclusions | 25% |
| AO3 | Assess the appropriateness of methods and conclusions through the statistical enquiry cycle | 20% |

AQA explains why the table is the same for both: "Assessment objectives (AOs) are set by Ofqual and are the same across all GCSE Statistics specifications and all exam boards." Pearson splits each objective evenly across its papers, printing 27.5% AO1, 12.5% AO2 and 10% AO3 for each one. Compared with GCSE Maths, technique (AO1) carries more weight, but a fifth of the marks still go to judging whether a method or a conclusion is sound, which is where students who only drill calculations lose ground.

## What is the statistical enquiry cycle in GCSE Statistics?

It is the five-stage process both boards build the course on, from asking a question to reviewing the answer. AQA says it "underpins the study of Statistics", and both boards set exam questions on its stages.

AQA lists the five stages as "initial planning", "data collection", "data processing and presentation", "interpretation of results" and "evaluation and review". Pearson goes further in how it lays out the content: "The order of the content, for each tier, follows the order of the statistical enquiry cycle." Every topic, from sampling to Spearman's rank, belongs to one of the stages, and a typical extended question walks through several of them.

**The five stages and what each asks of a student (stage names from the AQA 8382 specification; the right-hand column is our checklist)**

| Stage | From the AQA specification | Our question to ask at that stage |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Initial planning | Defining a question or hypothesis; deciding what data to collect and how | Is the hypothesis testable, and what would count as evidence against it? |
| 2. Data collection | Constraints on primary and secondary data; acknowledging sources | Who is missing from this sample, and why? |
| 3. Processing and presentation | Organising data, diagrams, summary measures, the role of technology | Does this diagram suit this kind of data? |
| 4. Interpretation | Conclusions that relate to the question; inferences; reliability | Does the conclusion answer the original question, in context? |
| 5. Evaluation and review | Weaknesses in approach; improvements; refining the hypothesis | What one change would make the result more trustworthy? |

The cycle is why GCSE Statistics feels different from the statistics strand of GCSE Maths. In Maths, a question usually hands over the data and asks for a mean or a box plot. In Statistics, a question may ask why a sample of shoppers outside one shop at 9 am is a poor sample, what a hypothesis should be, or whether a conclusion follows from the diagram. Those are AO3 marks, and they reward a student who has carried out an enquiry, not just read about one. AQA says as much: "Practical experience of the cycle is integral to their understanding of the principles of the SEC."

### Content beyond GCSE Maths

AQA's specification includes topics a Maths student will not meet: Spearman's rank correlation, weighted index numbers, moving averages for time series, Petersen capture and recapture, binomial situations and quality assurance sampling.

Pearson organises its content under three headings: "The collection of data", "Processing, representing and analysing data", and "Probability".

### Formulae, given and not

Pearson states "There is no formulae sheet for Foundation tier"; the one formula Foundation students may need, a rate of change such as a birth rate, is given in the question. Higher papers carry a formulae sheet on page 2 with skew, standard deviation and Spearman's rank.

AQA's notes say that formulae for skewness, and for rates of change, "will be given" when needed.

## Can you use a calculator in GCSE Statistics, and which functions matter?

Yes, on every paper of both boards. The boards differ on one point: whether students are expected to use the calculator's statistics mode.

AQA sets a minimum: the calculator must have "four rules", "square", "square root", "reciprocal function", "power function", "brackets" and "a memory facility". It then adds that "Students may use the statistical functions on their calculator, where appropriate, if they wish but this is not a requirement of the specification." Pearson is firmer: students "are expected to be familiar with the statistical function keys on their scientific calculators."

### Mean and standard deviation

Entering a list into statistics mode gives the mean and standard deviation at once. A Higher student who can do this saves minutes on a long data question.

### Check, do not replace

Write the method line first, such as the sum of the d squared terms in Spearman's rank, then use the calculator. Method marks need the written step.

### Memory for accuracy

Store intermediate results, especially in standard deviation and Spearman's rank, so rounding happens once at the end.

In our classes we teach the statistics mode on the student's own calculator model in the first weeks, because the keys differ between models and a student should not discover that in the exam room. We teach the hand method too, since the papers reward seeing the structure of a calculation and not only its answer.

## Three GCSE Statistics problems we wrote, solved step by step

Original problems, not board questions, on three topics unique to GCSE Statistics. Each ends with the interpretation sentence that AO2 and AO3 marks depend on.

### Example 1, capture and recapture

**Question (ours).** Volunteers catch and mark 40 newts in a pond and release them. A week later they catch 50 newts, of which 8 are marked. Estimate the number of newts in the pond, and state one assumption.

**Method.** The Petersen estimate is N = M × n ÷ m, where M is the number first marked, n the second catch and m the marked newts in it.

**Working.** N = 40 × 50 ÷ 8 = 250 newts.

**Assumption.** The population did not change between the two visits, and marked newts mixed back in and were as likely to be caught as unmarked ones. An answer without an assumption is unfinished.

### Example 2, Spearman's rank correlation

**Question (ours).** Five students are ranked on a maths test and a statistics test: A (1, 2), B (2, 1), C (3, 4), D (4, 3), E (5, 5). Calculate Spearman's rank correlation coefficient and interpret it.

**Differences.** d = &minus;1, 1, &minus;1, 1, 0, so the sum of d² is 1 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 0 = 4.

**Working.** rs = 1 &minus; (6 × 4) ÷ (5 × (25 &minus; 1)) = 1 &minus; 24 ÷ 120 = 0.8.

**Interpretation.** Strong positive correlation between the rankings: students who ranked high in one test tended to rank high in the other. With only five students, the result is weak evidence; a larger sample would be more reliable.

### Example 3, judging a sample (AO3)

**Question (ours).** A student wants to know how far pupils at her school travel to school. She asks the first 30 pupils who arrive at the front gate one morning. Give two reasons the sample may be biased and suggest a better method.

**Reasons.** Early arrivals may differ from the rest, for example pupils dropped off by car on the way to work. Pupils who arrive at another entrance, such as by school bus at the back, are left out entirely.

**Better method.** A stratified sample by year group, chosen at random from the school register, with the sample in each year proportional to its size.

**Why it matters.** This is a stage 2 question in the cycle. No calculation is needed, but a full answer names the bias and says why it distorts the result.

### What links the three

Each problem has a number in it, and each ends with a sentence about what the number means and how far it can be trusted.

That closing sentence is the habit we drill most in GCSE Statistics. Students who stop at 250 or 0.8 leave the interpretation marks behind.

**Practice set (ours, answers at the end).** (1) Quarterly sales are 12, 18, 15, 21 and 16; find the two 4-point moving averages. (2) An item cost 80 units in the base year and costs 92 now; find its index number. (3) A data set has lower quartile 14 and upper quartile 29; find the interquartile range. (4) A distribution has mean 52, median 49 and standard deviation 6; use skew = 3(mean &minus; median) ÷ standard deviation and describe the skew. (5) Give one advantage of a pilot survey. **Answers:** (1) 16.5 and 17.5; (2) 115; (3) 15; (4) 1.5, positive skew; (5) it shows up unclear or leading questions before the full survey is run.

## How do you study GCSE Statistics alongside GCSE Maths?

AQA says its specification "is designed to be taken over two years", but many schools teach it in less time than that. Our plan shares the work with GCSE Maths where the two overlap and keeps separate time for what only Statistics covers.

1. **Find the overlap.** Averages, spread, box plots, scatter graphs, tree and Venn diagrams appear in both GCSEs. Learn them once, carefully, and use them in both.
2. **List what is Statistics only.** Spearman's rank, index numbers, moving averages, capture and recapture, standard deviation and skew, sampling methods, quality assurance. These need their own lessons.
3. **Run one small enquiry.** Pick a question, collect a little real data, process it and write a conclusion and a review. One enquiry teaches all five stages faster than any worksheet.
4. **Learn the calculator mode.** Mean and standard deviation from a list, on the student's own model, practised until it is automatic.
5. **Past papers from the right board.** AQA papers for 8382 and Pearson papers for 1ST0, timed to 1 hour 45 minutes or 1 hour 30 minutes accordingly.
6. **Revise after GCSE Maths ends.** In June 2027, the Statistics papers on 7 and 16 June fall after the second GCSE Maths paper on 27 May, so the last fortnight before 7 June can belong to Statistics.

This plan is ours. It assumes Statistics is the second maths GCSE and that GCSE Maths is the priority, which is usually the right order for a student's next step after 16. For a student already strong in maths and drawn to data, Statistics can be the more enjoyable of the two, and it leads naturally to [A level Maths](/a-level-maths-tuition-online), whose statistics strand builds on it.

## What families ask about GCSE Statistics

### Is GCSE Statistics the same as GCSE Maths?

No. It is a separate GCSE with its own specification and papers. It overlaps with the statistics and probability in GCSE Maths but adds topics such as Spearman's rank correlation, index numbers, moving averages and capture and recapture.

### How many papers are in GCSE Statistics?

Two, at either Foundation or Higher tier, each worth 80 marks and half of the GCSE. AQA 8382 papers last 1 hour 45 minutes and Pearson Edexcel 1ST0 papers last 1 hour 30 minutes.

### When are the GCSE Statistics exams in 2027?

Both AQA and Pearson Edexcel list Paper 1 on the afternoon of Monday 7 June 2027 and Paper 2 on the morning of Wednesday 16 June 2027. Check the school's own timetable as well.

### Is GCSE Statistics hard?

The algebra is lighter than in Higher GCSE Maths, but the challenge is different: a fifth of the marks are for judging methods and conclusions, and those reward practice at explaining rather than calculating.

### What is the statistical enquiry cycle?

It is the five-stage process both boards use: initial planning, data collection, processing and presentation, interpretation of results, and evaluation and review. Exam questions can target any stage.

### Can you use a calculator in GCSE Statistics?

Yes, on every paper. AQA lists a minimum set of functions and says statistical functions may be used but are not required; Pearson expects students to be familiar with the statistical function keys.

### Is there a formulae sheet in GCSE Statistics?

On Pearson Edexcel, only at Higher tier, printed on page 2 with skew, standard deviation and Spearman's rank. Pearson's Foundation papers have no sheet. AQA gives formulae such as skewness and rates of change within the questions when they are needed.

### Can my child take GCSE Statistics as a private candidate?

AQA says its specification is available to private candidates, including those who are self-taught, home schooled or privately tutored, by entering through an AQA-approved school or college that agrees to host them.

### When are classes held?
