Before anything else

What can an online coding course honestly promise a career changer?

Less than many advertisements suggest, and more than nothing. Here is our side of it, in full.

What we do

Teach programming properly, live and in English, with one mentor who knows your work. Set projects that resemble real work, review your code line by line, and tell you honestly where it is weak. Help you write up and present what you built.

What we do not do

Place you in a job, promise a salary, or speak to employers for you. Award any German qualification. Take part in any public funding or approval scheme. Advise on residence permits, visas, employment contracts or tax. For those, ask the competent German authority or a qualified adviser.

What it takes from you

Time, steadily. Two live classes a week plus practice in between, for many months. The course is planned for adults who keep working while they learn.

Why say all this so early? Because a career change is a large decision with your income attached, and a page that sells a dream is no help in making it. If after the free first class you decide that a full-time training route suits you better, that is a good outcome too.

The official route

What is the state-recognised route into software work in Germany?

For software developers it is the training occupation Fachinformatiker or Fachinformatikerin. Its rules are federal law, published in full and free to read, and they are the clearest statement of what a German examiner expects a developer to be able to do.

How to read the table

One and a half times three years is four and a half years of work in the occupation; that is our arithmetic on the two texts, not advice on any individual case. Whether someone is admitted is decided by the competent body (the zuständige Stelle) for that occupation. Ask them, not us.

A university degree is a separate path again. We are neither of these, and we do not pretend to be.

Why we quote it at all

Because the exam rules read like a job description. Section 12 lists six things a candidate must show in a software project: analyse what a customer needs, plan the project, consider its cost, build or adapt the software, test it and prepare its rollout, and document it all.

That list is as good a standard for a self-built portfolio as any we know. We teach towards it, without claiming that our course is that training.

What a portfolio cannot do is replace judgement. A recruiter or an examiner will ask why you built it that way, what you would change, and what happens if the input is wrong. You can only answer those questions about code you wrote and understood yourself, which is why our mentors let AI tools into a career-change course only after the learner can do the work without them, and only as something to check rather than to trust.

Testing, from the first month

How do you write a test that catches your own mistake?

Section 14 of the training rules asks a candidate to choose test scenarios and generate test data. It sounds dry. In practice it is the difference between code that works on your examples and code that works. Our career-change learners start with this small project on official German data.

The data

The Federal Statistical Office gives every Land and every municipality an Amtlicher Regionalschlüssel, a code whose first two digits name the Land: 01 is Schleswig-Holstein, 09 is Bayern, 16 is Thüringen. Kiel has the code 010020000000 and Köln 053150000000.

We saved the codes of the 16 Länder and of the 80 cities of 100,000 or more people from the Destatis Gemeindeverzeichnis (reference date 31 December 2024) as gemeinden.csv.

The task

Write a function that takes a city and returns its Land. Then, before trusting it, write six tests with the right answers looked up by hand, and choose them on purpose: cities from Länder whose code starts with 0, and some whose code does not.

A good test set is one that is designed to break the code, not to confirm it.

Turning 010020000000 into a number throws away the leading zero, so Kiel's code becomes 10020000000. Its first two digits are now 10, which is Saarland: a wrong answer with no error at all. For Köln the first two digits become 53, which is no Land, so the program would have crashed without the try. Had we tested only Leipzig, Erfurt and Saarbrücken, every test would have passed and the bug would have shipped.

A Regionalschlüssel, a postcode, a phone number or a tax number looks like a number but is a label: nobody adds two of them together. Keep labels as text. The last line counts how many of the 80 cities would have been affected: 69, every city in Länder 01 to 09. This one habit, choosing test data at the edges, comes up again in every web form, every database import and every interview question about input handling.

  1. Run Version 1 with only the three passing tests. Notice that it looks finished.
  2. Add the other three and explain each failure in one sentence.
  3. Write a test for bad input. What should land_of("Atlantis") do? Decide, write the test, then make it pass.
  4. Going further. Put the function and tests in a public repository with a README that a stranger could follow. That is your first portfolio piece.

Data: Statistisches Bundesamt, Gemeindeverzeichnis GV-ISys, Länder and cities of 100,000 or more, reference date 31 December 2024. Rules: FIAusbV section 14(1). The code, the test choice, the counts and the exercises are our own.

Career changers ask

Career change into coding from Germany: common questions

Are the classes in German?

No. The course is live and in English only. Your mentor matches your pace and accent, and English is also the language of most programming documentation and many development teams.

Will this course get me a job?

We cannot promise that, and we do not. We teach the skills, review your work honestly and help you build projects you can show and explain. Hiring decisions are made by employers.

Do I get a German qualification?

No. We award no German qualification and hold no German accreditation. The state-recognised training for developers is the three-year Fachinformatiker Ausbildung under the FIAusbV of 2020.

Can public funding pay for this course?

No. Our course is not part of any German public funding or approval scheme, so please do not plan on that.

Can you advise me on visas or work permits?

No. We give no advice on residence, visas, employment law or tax. Please ask the competent German authority or a qualified adviser.

I have no experience at all. Can I start?

Yes. The course begins with Python from the first line, then moves to web development. The free first class shows where to begin.

How long does a career change take?

It depends on your time and your starting point. Our own milestones put a first portfolio of three projects around month ten or later for someone with two classes a week and regular practice.

Will I use AI tools like Claude?

Later, and only as something to check. Our AI coding agents course is for adults of 18 and over, in line with Anthropic's terms for Claude. First you learn to write and test code yourself.

When are classes in German time?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Early mornings and lunch breaks in Germany are ordinary hours for our mentors in India.

How do I pay from Germany?

We list fees in euro at the European Central Bank reference rate and take the payment in US dollars; your bank converts it. The first class is free.
