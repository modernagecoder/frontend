## Which primaria grade is ready for which kind of coding?

Every primaria grade can code; what changes is the tool. MINEDU fixes each grade's normative age on 31 March of the school year, and the CNEB timetable sets how many hours of English the grade has each week, so both are useful clues to where a child should begin.

### What the national timetable shows

Primaria lasts six years under Ley 28044, and its regulations say it serves children from age 6. On the CNEB timetable every primaria grade has 30 hours a week. English gets 2 hours in 1° and 2° and 3 hours from 3° to 6°, while Matemática and Comunicación get 5 hours each in the first two grades and 4 after that.

There is no computing or programming area in the table. The CNEB itself also notes that English in primaria depends on a specialist teacher being hired; where none is, those hours become free-use hours. So two children in the same grade can have had very different amounts of English.

### How we use those clues

A child's age matters less than two things: whether they can read a short sentence, and whether they can use a mouse and keyboard. A confident reader in 3° may start Python early; a quieter 5° pupil may enjoy a term of Scratch first.

The free first class sorts this out in twenty minutes. The mentor asks the child to drag a few blocks, read a few words aloud and explain what they built, then suggests a starting point.

If you are looking at one particular age, our Spanish pages go deeper: [programación para niños de 6 años](/programacion-para-ninos-de-6-anos) covers coding before a child reads fluently, and [programación para niños de 10 años](/programacion-para-ninos-de-10-anos) covers the step from blocks to Python.

## How much English does my child need to start coding classes?

Less than most parents fear. A child who can follow "click here", "drag this block" and "what happens next?" is ready. Everything else is taught inside the lesson, one word at a time, alongside the code.

### Ages 6 and 7

Classes lean on pictures, gestures and the shared screen. A child learns perhaps one new word per class, such as "move", "turn" or "repeat", and uses it straight away in a block. ScratchJr, built for young children of 5 to 7, works the same way: almost everything is a picture.

### Ages 8 and 9

Children start reading block labels and short instructions. The mentor keeps a word list on screen and asks the child to say each new word aloud. By this age most children have had two years of English at school, at least on paper.

### Ages 10 to 12

Typed code is itself English: print, if, else, while. Children now explain their programs in short sentences, which is often the first time their school English is used for something they care about.

1. **The mentor speaks slowly and shows everything.** Every instruction is said and done on screen at the same time, so a child who misses a word still sees what to do.
2. **Repeating is normal.** Children learn "Can you say that again, please?" in the first class and use it freely. No one is embarrassed to ask.
3. **Spanish stays at home.** The class itself is in English only, but parents can message us in Spanish and children are never scolded for a Spanish word when they are stuck.
4. **Progress is checked in both directions.** Every few weeks the mentor tells you which new coding ideas and which new English words your child can use.

Families at bilingual or international schools, where English is already strong, can start faster, and [our Lima page for English-medium pupils](/english-medium-coding-classes-lima) is written for them. For maths in English at the same ages, see [clases de matemáticas en inglés para niños](/clases-de-matematicas-en-ingles-para-ninos).

## What does a coding class in English look like for a ten-year-old?

Here is a lesson our mentors teach to children of about 10 to 12 who have started Python. The game is rock, paper, scissors, which every child in Peru knows as piedra, papel o tijera. The code is ours. The surprise is that a spelling mistake in English breaks the game without any error message.

**Version 1: a chain of if statements** (Python)
```python
def judge(a, b):
    if a == b:
        return 'draw'
    if a == 'rock' and b == 'scissors':
        return 'player 1'
    if a == 'paper' and b == 'rock':
        return 'player 1'
    if a == 'scissors' and b == 'paper':
        return 'player 1'
    return 'player 2'

rounds = [('rock', 'scissors'), ('paper', 'paper'), ('scissors', 'rock'),
          ('sissors', 'paper'), ('Rock', 'scissors')]
for a, b in rounds:
    print(a, 'vs', b, '->', judge(a, b))
```
The child writes the rules as they would say them: if it is a draw, say draw; if rock meets scissors, player 1 wins; and so on. Anything else means player 2 wins. Before running it, the mentor asks: who wins each of the five rounds?

**What Version 1 prints** (Output)
```output
rock vs scissors -> player 1
paper vs paper -> draw
scissors vs rock -> player 2
sissors vs paper -> player 2
Rock vs scissors -> player 2
```
Exact output from our own run with Python 3. The first three rounds are right. Round four is a misspelling of "scissors", and round five has a capital R. Both are reported as wins for player 2, and the program never complains.

Children usually spot round five first: rock beats scissors, so why did player 2 win? Then someone notices "sissors". The program did not fail; it quietly took the last line, "otherwise player 2 wins", for any word it did not recognise. The lesson now has two parts: write the rules more clearly, and make the program say when it does not understand.

**Version 2: the rules as a table** (Python)
```python
beats = {'rock': 'scissors', 'paper': 'rock', 'scissors': 'paper'}

for move in beats:
    print(move, 'beats', beats[move])
print('Words the game knows:', len(beats))
```
A dictionary pairs each move with the move it beats. The loop reads the table back as English sentences, and len counts how many words the game knows.

**What Version 2 prints** (Output)
```output
rock beats scissors
paper beats rock
scissors beats paper
Words the game knows: 3
```
Exact output from our own run. The child reads the three sentences aloud. If one sounds wrong, the table is wrong, which is much easier to check than nine separate lines of if statements.

**Version 3: judge with the table, and say what you do not know** (Python)
```python
def judge(a, b):
    a = a.lower()
    b = b.lower()
    if a not in beats:
        return 'I do not know the word ' + a
    if b not in beats:
        return 'I do not know the word ' + b
    if a == b:
        return 'draw'
    if beats[a] == b:
        return 'player 1'
    return 'player 2'
```
Uses the same beats table and the same five rounds as before. lower() turns "Rock" into "rock"; not in beats catches any word that is not in the table.

**What Version 3 prints** (Output)
```output
rock vs scissors -> player 1
paper vs paper -> draw
scissors vs rock -> player 2
sissors vs paper -> I do not know the word sissors
Rock vs scissors -> player 1
```
Exact output from our own run. The capital R no longer matters, and the misspelt word is reported by name instead of silently losing the round.

In about forty minutes the child has met a dictionary, the words "beats", "draw" and "scissors" (and how to spell it), and the idea that a good program says when it is confused. An extension for fast finishers: add "lizard" and "Spock" to the table and find out why one value per key is no longer enough.

## Do Peruvian primary schools teach coding?

Not as a subject. The CNEB has no computing area in primaria; digital skills appear as a competency shared across all areas, and science and technology work may include building things. Some schools add computing or robotics workshops in their free-use hours.

### Robotics kits, then and now

MINEDU has used educational robotics since the 1990s. In 2009 and 2010 it supplied LEGO WeDo kits to almost all public primary schools, according to its own robotics document. Many parents therefore think of "robótica" as the natural first step in technology for children.

Our classes are about code, not hardware: no kit arrives at your door. What a child learns with us, sequences, loops and decisions, is exactly what makes a school robot move, so the two fit together.

### National tests in primaria

MINEDU's national assessment, the ENLA, tests reading and maths rather than computing. ENLA 2024 was a census test of 532,000 pupils of 4° de primaria in 18,112 schools, and ENLA 2025, applied from 11 to 13 November 2025, again tested 4° de primaria in reading and maths.

Coding does not prepare a child for the ENLA directly, and we do not claim that it does. It does give daily practice in reading instructions carefully and in step-by-step reasoning, which is useful in any subject.

The scale is national. MINEDU said on 14 October 2026 that its materials for the 2027 school year will reach 2.7 million primaria pupils. We are not connected with MINEDU or any school; our classes run beside school, and a child who completes a course receives our own certificate of completion, not a school grade.

## What does a child in Peru need at home for online coding classes?

A laptop or desktop with a webcam, a microphone and a steady connection, plus a quiet corner and, for the youngest, an adult nearby for the first few classes. Typing code on a tablet or phone does not work well.

### What INEI found in 2026

According to INEI's survey of technology in households, April to June 2026, only 36.4% of Peruvian households owned at least one computer. It also showed that children of 6 to 11 were the age group least likely to use the internet, at 68.2%.

So for many primaria children, a coding class is their first regular, purposeful use of a computer. That is one reason we start young children with blocks and mouse skills before typing.

### A short checklist

One computer shared by the whole family is fine. A headset with a built-in microphone cuts out kitchen and street noise, so your child catches every word the mentor says.

Keep the same seat for every class, open the link a few minutes early and have a notebook ready for new words. Scratch works straight from the browser, and in the first Python lesson the mentor talks your child through installing a free editor.

If the family computer is busy at certain times, choose class hours around it. That flexibility is one reason we never fix a timetable.

## Are the classes taught in Spanish?

No. All teaching is in English, live and online, by mentors who work from India. With children they speak simply and slowly, say things again in other words, and adjust to how fast the child follows and how they pronounce English.

### Why English, for a Spanish-speaking child

Programming languages are built from English words, so a child who learns to code in English learns the words and the ideas at once. A Spanish-taught class still ends up typing if and while; ours explains them in the language they come from.

Children also get regular spoken English with an adult who is patient and interested in what they made, which school groups of thirty rarely allow.

### When to wait

A child who finds any English stressful, or who is struggling with reading in Spanish, may be happier waiting a year. We would rather tell you that in the free class than see a child unhappy.

Parents who would rather read about this in Spanish will find the questions they usually ask us first answered on [our Spanish guide for parents of young coders](/clases-de-programacion-para-ninos-en-ingles).

## What time are the classes for children in Peru?

At an hour your family picks, often after school or homework. Mentors are in India, and the difference between the two clocks is fixed all year.

**Peru time and India time for common after-school hours (conversions only, not a schedule)**

| Peru time | India time | Day in India |
|---|---|---|
| 8:00 a.m. | 6:30 p.m. | Same day |
| 10:30 a.m. | 9:00 p.m. | Same day |
| 12:00 noon | 10:30 p.m. | Same day |
| 3:30 p.m. | 2:00 a.m. | Next day |
| 5:00 p.m. | 3:30 a.m. | Next day |
| 7:00 p.m. | 5:30 a.m. | Next day |

Peruvian clocks stay on UTC minus 5 through the whole year, with no daylight saving (tz database, zone America/Lima). Indian clocks stay on UTC plus 5:30, which puts India a constant 10 hours 30 minutes ahead.

Children who go to school in the afternoon shift often take class in the morning, and morning-shift pupils in the late afternoon. If the school changes your child's shift, you simply move the class.

## How will I know my child is learning, in code and in English?

Five steps we use with primaria children in Peru. They are our own, and each one has a coding side and an English side.

**Five steps in code and English for children of 6 to 12 (ours)**

| Step | In code, your child can | In English, your child can |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Follow | drag blocks in order so a character reaches a goal | follow "click", "drag", "move" and "turn" |
| 2. Repeat | use a loop instead of copying the same block | say "repeat ten times" and "again, please" |
| 3. Decide | add an if block that changes what happens | explain a rule: "if it touches the wall, it stops" |
| 4. Type | write a short Python program and guess its output first | read error messages and name the problem |
| 5. Build and show | finish a game or page of their own | present it to the family in a few English sentences |

### For parents

Once a week, let your child give you a tour of what they built, in Spanish, English or a mix. Being able to explain it is the clearest sign that it was understood.
Most children move one step every couple of months. Step 4, typing, is the slowest and the most important.

### For children

Say what you think will happen before you press the green flag or run.
Keep a list of new English words next to your keyboard.

## Coding classes in English for Peruvian kids: questions and answers

### Are the classes in Spanish?

No. Classes are live, online and taught only in English. With children our mentors in India slow down, use short sentences, repeat with simpler words and follow each child's pace and accent. You can still message us in Spanish on WhatsApp.

### My child speaks very little English. Can they still join?

Usually yes. If your child can follow simple instructions such as "click" and "drag", they can start. Young children work mostly with pictures and blocks, and the mentor shows every step on screen. The free first class will tell you honestly whether to start now or wait.

### From what age can a child begin?

From 6, in 1° de primaria, with Scratch blocks. Most children move to typed Python around 9 or 10. The free class places each child by reading level and confidence, not only by age.

### Which course should a child in 4° de primaria take?

Often Roblox game coding or web pages, and Python for confident readers. Children of that age usually have three hours of school English a week on the national timetable, which is enough to begin.

### Does school in Peru already teach coding?

Not as a subject. The national curriculum has no computing area in primaria; digital skills are shared across other areas, and some schools run computing or robotics workshops. Our classes run alongside school.

### Is this the same as robótica?

No. We teach programming only and never post out robot kits. Still, the loops and decisions a child practises with us are the same instructions that drive a school robot.

### What time are the classes?

Any day, any time, flexible: choose a batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at the hour that suits your family. India is 10 hours 30 minutes ahead of Peru all year, so 5:00 p.m. in Lima is 3:30 a.m. the next day in India.

### Does my child need a computer?

Yes, a laptop or desktop with a keyboard, camera and microphone. A tablet is not enough for typing code. A shared family computer is fine if the class hour is kept free.

### Will the classes affect my child's school grades?

They do not count towards Peruvian grades; we are not connected with MINEDU or any school. When a child finishes a course they receive our own certificate of completion.

### Do children use ChatGPT in class?

No. Children under 13 do not use chatbots with us. They learn how AI works through small projects that need no account.
