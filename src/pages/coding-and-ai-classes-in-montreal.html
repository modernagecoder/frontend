Coding and AI classes in Montreal

Where can a Montreal learner study coding and AI live, in English?

Montreal learners aged 6 to adult can study coding, Python and AI with Modern Age Coders, live online and in English, one to one or in groups of fifteen to twenty. Children begin with Python and AI for Kids, teens with Python for Teens, and CEGEP students and adults with our Python Masterclass. A free first class sets the level.

A Montreal student does not count school years in grades for long. Quebec numbers its secondary years Secondary I through Secondary V, and the usual road to a Quebec university then passes through a two-year pre-university program at a CEGEP, while technical programs there take three. That changes how coding should be planned here: a teenager the same age as an Ontario Grade 12 student can already be a college student in Montreal, and the right class for them is an adult-paced one. This page lays out where code does and does not appear in the Quebec program, which CEMC contests are open to CEGEP students, what a Montreal family should know about our teaching language, and a project we built on a year of the city's own bike counter readings, a file that quietly contains a missing hour.

There is one gap worth naming. The list of secondary programs of study on quebec.ca, read on 22 October 2026, includes mathematics, several science and technology courses, financial education and more, but no separate computer science or programming subject. Code can appear inside other subjects at a school's choice, yet nothing on that list guarantees a Montreal teenager writes a program before CEGEP. That is the space our classes fill: the steady practice of writing, testing and explaining code, which school timetables here leave to chance.

Montreal data project

When does Montreal ride? A year of the city's bike counters, and the missing hour

The Ville de Montréal publishes readings from its permanent bike counters as open data, every quarter-hour, under a Creative Commons licence. Our students take the 2025 file, group it by month and test whether the counters are complete enough to compare. Every number below is our calculation from the file as downloaded on 22 October 2026.

Most students guess July for the peak. At these 26 counters it is September, by a short head over June, and February is the quietest month at roughly one sixteenth of September. Commuting after the summer holidays is a plausible reason, but the file records bikes, not reasons, so we teach students to write that sentence as a guess. The program that produced the table is below; it uses only Python's standard library and expects the downloaded file in the same folder:import csv EXPECTED = 365 * 96 - 4 # 9 March 2025 lost one hour to the clock change rows_per_counter = {} monthly = {} march_9 = {} with open("comptage_velo_2025.csv", encoding="utf-8") as counts_file: for row in csv.DictReader(counts_file): counter = row["id_compteur"] rows_per_counter[counter] = rows_per_counter.get(counter, 0) + 1 if row["date"] == "2025-03-09": march_9[counter] = march_9.get(counter, 0) + 1 if row["nb_passages"] == "": continue key = (counter, row["date"][:7]) monthly[key] = monthly.get(key, 0) + int(row["nb_passages"]) complete = {c for c, n in rows_per_counter.items() if n >= EXPECTED} print(len(rows_per_counter), "counters,", len(complete), "complete") print("rows on 9 March:", sorted(march_9[c] for c in complete)) for month in sorted({m for (c, m) in monthly}): total = sum(n for (c, m), n in monthly.items() if c in complete and m == month) print(month, total)

1. Read the French headers

The columns are date, heure, id_compteur and nb_passages. A student who has never studied French still works out that nb_passages is the count, from the numbers alone, then checks the guess against the dataset page.

2. Check completeness first

The 2025 file has 1,404,316 rows from 45 counters, but only 26 report every quarter-hour of the year. Comparing months across all 45 would mix counters that were switched on, moved or offline, so the table uses the complete 26 only.

3. Find the missing hour

A full year should give 365 days of 96 quarter-hours. On 9 March 2025, 25 of the complete counters have 92 rows, because clocks jumped from 2 am to 3 am; one keeps 96 rows with the vanished hour stored as blanks. That is why the script expects 4 fewer rows.

What a passage count cannot prove

A rider who passes the same counter twice is counted twice, and counters marked dir nord and dir sud count each direction separately, so no row here is a number of cyclists. The 434 at 23:15 might be a night ride, a group or a sensor fault; the file does not say, and a good analyst leaves it at that.

Registration runs through schools

CEMC's rule is plain: all students must participate through their school, or through another local school willing to host them. A CEGEP student asks their college, not CEMC.

The CCC, in either language

The 2027 Canadian Computing Competition is set for Wednesday 17 February 2027, according to CEMC, and is offered in French as well as English. Students may use C, C++, Python or Java.

Free papers, original lessons

CEMC posts its last ten years of contests online at no cost. We point students to those papers and teach with problems we write, never by reproducing CEMC's questions or solutions.

The counter project is closer to contest work than it looks: reading structured input, grouping by a key and refusing to compare incomplete groups are exactly the habits a CCC problem rewards. For contest training, see our CCC preparation page; for the math side, Waterloo contest preparation. Neither CEMC nor the University of Waterloo is connected with Modern Age Coders.

Island and suburb numbers

Numbers on the island use 514, 438 and 263; Laval, Longueuil and the other suburbs use 450, 579 and 354. The form takes any of them as ten digits after the +1.

The same gap, in code

The 92-row day in the bike file is the spring change seen from the data side. Students who plot the file with a timezone-aware date type watch the gap explain itself, which teaches more than a rule about clocks.

Coding and AI

An AI assistant can sum the bike file. Can it tell you the sum is unfair?

Only if the person asking already knows to check.

Ask a chatbot for monthly totals from the Montreal counter file and it will produce code that runs. Whether it warns you that 19 of the 45 counters are missing parts of the year, or that 9 March is four rows short, depends entirely on whether you thought to ask. Most beginners do not, and the chart they publish quietly compares counters that were not all switched on.

We teach the other way round. Students plan the steps, write the grouping themselves and test the result before trusting it. As they grow older they use AI tools under age rules we follow, as a second pair of eyes on work they understand, not as a source of answers to copy.

Thinking clearly about a problem is the skill that survives every new tool; code is how a student proves they have done it. The longer argument is in our longer piece on why learning to code still matters when AI can write it.

FAQ

Montreal coding and AI questions

Questions Montreal families and learners ask before a first class.

Do you offer coding classes for kids in Montreal?

Yes, live online with a real teacher, from age 6. Children join from home in a group of fifteen to twenty or one to one; we have no classroom in Montreal.

Are your Montreal classes taught in French?

No. Every class, every handout and all written feedback is in English. Many bilingual Montreal learners are comfortable with that, but a family wanting French-language teaching should look elsewhere.

Does a Quebec secondary school teach coding?

The secondary program list on quebec.ca names no separate computer science or programming subject, so coding depends on the school. Our classes give a Secondary student regular, structured programming practice.

My child is 17 and at CEGEP. Which class fits?

An adult-paced one. A CEGEP student joins our Python Masterclass or an adult data course, alongside university students and working adults, rather than a teen group.

Can you help with Python classes for a Montreal teen?

Yes. Python for Teens runs from a first program to files, functions and data, and it is the base for our AI and machine learning course.

Do you teach robotics classes in Montreal?

We teach the software side only: Python, logic, reading sensor data and debugging. Building a physical robot needs a school team or a local club, and our classes sit well beside one.

Is there an AI class suitable for a Montreal teenager?

Yes, AI and Machine Learning for Teens, once Python is solid. Tools with an 18-plus age rating stay out of under-18 lessons, and ChatGPT for a teenager requires a parent's permission, which we confirm before any lesson uses it.

Can a CEGEP student enter a CEMC contest?

Yes. CEMC lists CEGEP students for the Euclid, on Tuesday 6 April 2027, and the CSMC. Entry goes through the student's school or college, not through us.

What are class times for Montreal learners?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Times are confirmed in Eastern time.

Will a 514 or 450 number work in the form?

Yes. Island numbers with 514, 438 or 263 and suburban numbers with 450, 579 or 354 are accepted. Type the ten digits after the +1.

How do Montreal families see the fees?

The fees section of this page lists the free lesson, the monthly group fee and the monthly private fee, each with its currency code. Nothing is charged before the free lesson.
