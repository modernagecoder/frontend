The test

What is the TACHS, and what does it test?

The TACHS tests academic achievement in reading, written expression and mathematics, plus general reasoning. Its results go to three high schools the student chooses, together with school records.

TACHS content areas (official TACHS site and 2026 Student Handbook, read 31 October 2026)
AreaWhat the organizers say it covers
ReadingFactual, interpretative and evaluative comprehension of informational and literary texts
Written ExpressionStandard English: organization, sentence structure, clarity, and finding the line with an error
MathematicsNumber sense and operations, algebraic patterns and connections, data analysis, probability and statistics, geometry, and measurement
AbilityLearned reasoning with somewhat novel questions using spatial and figural content

How the day is built

The 2026 Parent Guide says "There are 6 timed subtests". After each of the first three a student may take a 5-minute break; after the fourth and fifth there is only a one-minute pause.

Testing time is about 2 hours 10 minutes for most students, or about 3 hours 15 minutes with approved extended time, which is the only accommodation offered. Each question has four answer choices.

What is not published

The organizers do not publish how many math questions there are or how long the math subtest lasts, and they say they cannot give tutors or families more detail than the site and official materials.

So any prep course claiming to mirror the exact TACHS format is guessing. The site warns that outside practice tests may not match "the number of questions, exam format, and allotted testing time".

Sources: the official TACHS site (exam FAQs) with its 2026 Student Handbook and 2026 Parent Guide, read 31 October 2026. Modern Age Coders is not connected with Riverside Insights, TACHS or any diocese or school.

A straight answer

Do TACHS tutoring and prep courses help?

The organizers say plainly that they do not encourage or endorse coaching for the TACHS. Their view is that students who do well in school math can expect to do well on the test, and we build our classes around that.

On its practice-test FAQ the official site states that Riverside Insights, together with the Archdiocese of New York, the Diocese of Brooklyn/Queens and the Diocese of Rockville Centre, "does not encourage or endorse coaching or tutoring programs for the TACHS Examination." It adds that "Official TACHS practice tests are not published or provided" and that outside practice tests "are not fully representative of the actual TACHS Examination".

We think families deserve to read that before paying anyone, including us. So we do not sell TACHS question drills or claim inside knowledge of the test. What we teach is the middle school math the organizers list, the estimation habits that make the new calculator safe to use, and the pacing that keeps a student moving across a timed subtest. Those skills serve a student in ninth grade math too, whatever the result.

Math strands

What math is on the TACHS?

The Mathematics subtest draws on five strands: number sense and operations; algebraic patterns and connections; data analysis, probability and statistics; geometry; and measurement. All five sit inside a normal grade 6 to 8 course.

The five TACHS math strands and what they usually mean at grade 8 (strand names from the TACHS Student Handbook; the middle column is ours)
StrandTypical grade 8 skills (our reading)One habit we drill
Number sense and operationsFractions, decimals, percents, integers, rounding, order of operationsEstimate before computing
Algebraic patterns and connectionsNumber and shape patterns, expressions, one-step and two-step equationsWrite the rule as a formula in n
Data analysis, probability and statisticsMean, median, range, reading tables and graphs, simple probabilitySort the data first
GeometryPerimeter, area, angles, properties of shapesLabel every known length
MeasurementUnits of length, time, weight and capacity, and converting between themWrite units on every line

Worked example 1, a pattern turned into a rule (ours). A row of tile squares uses 4 tiles for one square, 7 for two joined squares and 10 for three. How many tiles for 20 joined squares? Each new square adds 3 tiles, so the count is 3n + 1, where n is the number of squares. Check: n = 1 gives 4. For n = 20 the answer is 3 x 20 + 1 = 61 tiles. A student who extends the list 4, 7, 10, 13 by hand gets there too, but the rule takes ten seconds and cannot drift.

Patterns are the strand to watch

“Algebraic patterns and connections” can get less direct attention in grade 8 classes than equations do. Some students can solve 2n + 9 = 31 but freeze when asked for the 12th term of a pattern.

We teach one move for both: find what changes each step, write it as a number times n, then fix the starting value.

Measurement needs facts

Converting 2.75 hours to minutes or 3 feet 8 inches to inches is easy only when the unit facts are automatic: 60 minutes, 12 inches, 3 feet, 16 ounces, 4 quarts.

We test those facts in the first minutes of class until a student never has to pause on them.

New in 2026

Can you use a calculator on the TACHS?

Yes, from 2026, but only the basic 4-function calculator built into the testing platform. A student found using their own calculator is disqualified.

The official FAQ says: "New in 2026: A basic 4-function calculator will be provided within the testing platform for use on applicable questions." It opens from the calculator icon in the Proctorio toolbar. The site points families to a free basic calculator practice page so students can get used to it before test day.

When to reach for a basic calculator, and when not to (our classroom rule of thumb)
SituationOur adviceWhy
Multiplying decimals such as 19.8 x 3.1Estimate first (20 x 3 = 60), then use the calculatorThe estimate catches a mistyped decimal point
A mixed expression such as 3 + 4 x 5Do the multiplication first, then addMany basic calculators work left to right and would show 35
Fractions such as 3/5 against 5/8Convert by division, or cross-multiply by handA 4-function calculator has no fraction key
Easy facts such as 25 percent of 48Mental math: a quarter of 48 is 12Opening the calculator takes longer than the answer
Choosing between four answersEstimate and eliminate before computingTwo choices are often far from the estimate

Worked example 2, order of operations (ours). Evaluate 3 + 4 x 5. The correct value is 23: multiply 4 x 5 = 20, then add 3. On a calculator that applies each key as it is pressed, typing 3 + 4 x 5 in that order gives 7 x 5 = 35. The fix is to enter 4 x 5 first and add 3 afterwards. Try this on the practice calculator before test day to see how it behaves.

Worked example 3, estimation as a check (ours). A rectangle is 19.8 cm by 3.1 cm. What is its area? Estimate first: about 20 x 3 = 60 square centimeters. The exact product is 61.38. If the calculator shows 6.138 or 613.8, a decimal point slipped, and the estimate exposes it at once. Among four answer choices, only one is likely to sit near 60.

Sources: TACHS site FAQ “Are students allowed to use calculators?” and the 2026 Parent Guide, page on the in-exam calculator, read 31 October 2026. Our advice table and examples are our own.

One attempt only

The Student Handbook is firm: "A student may take the TACHS examination only once." It also says results are final, with no reviewing, rescoring or retesting.

That makes grade 7 the real preparation year. A student who strengthens all five math strands then sits the test once, in the fall of grade 8.

How results are used

The site says high schools use a combination of TACHS results, the Applicant Record from the student’s school and state testing results. Families see local and national percentile ranks in the Home Report.

Scholarship and financial aid decisions are made by each high school. Questions about admission go to the high schools directly, not to the testing company.

Sources: the TACHS site registration and exam FAQs and the 2026 Student Handbook calendar, read 31 October 2026. Confirm every date on the official site; it is the organizers’ to change.

Practice set

Twelve original TACHS-style math practice items

Our own items, across the five strands, with four-choice thinking in mind. They are not TACHS items. Use the calculator only where our table above says it helps.

  1. Which is greater, 3/5 or 5/8?
  2. Round 4,768 to the nearest hundred.
  3. A pattern starts 5, 9, 13, 17. What is the 12th term?
  4. If 2n + 9 = 31, what is n?
  5. A spinner has 8 equal sections and 3 are blue. What is the probability of landing on blue?
  6. What is the range of 14, 9, 21, 17 and 9?
  7. What is the perimeter of a rectangle 7.5 units long and 4 units wide?
  8. A triangle has a base of 10 cm and a height of 6 cm. What is its area?
  9. How many minutes are in 2.75 hours?
  10. How many inches are in 3 feet 8 inches?
  11. What is 25 percent of 48? Try it without the calculator.
  12. In a table, x = 1, 2, 3 gives y = 4, 7, 10. What is y when x = 8?

The off-by-one trap

A common wrong answer to item 3 is 53, from multiplying 12 x 4 and add 5. The 12th term is only eleven steps past the first, so it is 5 + 11 x 4 = 49.

Writing the rule as 4n + 1 and checking n = 1 gives 5 avoids the slip every time.

For parents

Look at your child’s school math grades and state test results first; the site says high schools consider them alongside the TACHS.

If the school math is solid, a few weeks on estimation and the calculator may be all that is needed. If it is shaky, fix the school math.

For students

Practice the basic calculator before test day, so it is a tool and not a surprise.

On the day, you can skip and mark items inside a subtest. Once you finish a subtest you cannot go back, so use the marks.

TACHS math: what New York parents ask

What math is on the TACHS?

The official site lists number sense and operations, algebraic patterns and connections, data analysis, probability and statistics, geometry, and measurement. These are standard middle school topics.

Is a calculator allowed on the TACHS in 2026?

Yes. New in 2026, a basic 4-function calculator is provided inside the testing platform for applicable questions. Personal calculators are not allowed, and a student using one is disqualified.

When is the TACHS in 2026?

The Archdiocese of New York tests on Friday, November 6, 2026, and the Diocese of Brooklyn/Queens and the Diocese of Rockville Centre on Saturday, November 7, 2026. Sessions begin at 8:30 AM or 10:00 AM.

How long is the TACHS?

About 2 hours 10 minutes for most students, including directions, across 6 timed subtests. With approved extended time it is about 3 hours 15 minutes.

Is the TACHS taken at home?

Yes. The 2026 TACHS is an online, remote, at-home test with Proctorio proctoring and a webcam, a format the site says has been used since 2020.

Is there an official TACHS practice test?

No. The site says official practice tests are not published or provided, though the Student Handbook includes sample questions and an answer key.

Do the organizers recommend TACHS tutoring?

No. The site says Riverside Insights and the three dioceses do not encourage or endorse coaching or tutoring programs, and that students who do well in school coursework can expect to do well. We teach school math, not TACHS drills.

Can my child take the TACHS twice?

No. The Student Handbook says a student may take the TACHS only once, and results are final with no rescoring or retesting.

When do we get TACHS results?

Scores go to the three high school choices on or before December 7, 2026. Families can view the Home Report online from 3:00 PM on January 22, 2027.

What days and times are classes?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays.

Are you connected with TACHS or the dioceses?

No. Modern Age Coders is independent and has no tie to Riverside Insights, the TACHS program, any diocese or any high school.
