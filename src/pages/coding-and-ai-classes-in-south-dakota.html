South Dakota, United States

Coding and AI classes in South Dakota

Does South Dakota have computer science standards, and does CS count toward graduation?

Yes to both, with limits. South Dakota adopted K-8 computer science standards on April 14, 2025, and a state-approved advanced CS course such as AP Computer Science Principles may replace one science elective, though never Biology. Modern Age Coders teaches South Dakota students live online, ages 6 to 18, on Central or Mountain time.

South Dakota is new to formal computer science standards, and the timing shows in what they ask for. Written by a committee the Department of Education formed in the spring of 2024, the K-8 standards say outright that one of their goals is to mark where artificial intelligence comes in. At high school, the state already gives computer science a foothold in the diploma: an approved advanced course can take the place of a science elective. Access is still uneven, with a little over half of high schools offering CS in the latest count, and families in the east and the west of the state live on two different clocks. This page covers the standards, the substitution rule, the numbers, a binning project on Sioux Falls recreation data and how our live classes work across both time zones.

New in 2025

South Dakota’s first K-8 computer science standards

From the standards document published by the South Dakota Department of Education (SD DOE), adopted April 14, 2025, read on 18 October 2026.

South Dakota Computer Science Standards at a glance, as SD DOE describes them
FeatureWhat the document says
AdoptionThe document is dated Adopted April 14, 2025
Who wrote themA Computer Science Standards Committee the department created in the spring of 2024
Grades coveredKindergarten to grade 8, in bands K-2, 3-5 and 6-8
What they drew onThe 2017 CSTA K-12 standards and the 2016 K-12 Computer Science Framework; 11 state standards were referenced, with most adaptations from Montana, Idaho and North Dakota
Artificial intelligenceOne goal is to explicitly indicate where AI is referenced or where skills build toward an understanding of it
Relationship to technology standardsOverlap slightly with the Educational Technology Standards adopted in 2021, which focus more on using computers appropriately and safely

Not a curriculum

SD DOE says content standards do not mandate a specific curriculum and are reviewed every five to seven years. Districts decide how to teach them.

Concepts and practices

Following CSTA, each standard pairs a concept, such as algorithms and programming or data and analysis, with a practice, such as computational thinking.

From unplugged to code

The document describes kindergarten work as simple sequencing, often without a device, rising by eighth grade to designing algorithms and beginning to write code.

High school

Swapping a science elective for advanced computer science

South Dakota’s graduation requirements were passed by the Board of Education Standards on July 16, 2018, under South Dakota Administrative Rule 24:43:11. They include one specific route for computer science.

What it replaces

One unit of a science elective, for the base High School Diploma and the Advanced Career Endorsement.

What it cannot replace

Biology. The substitution also does not count for the Advanced or Advanced Honors Endorsements.

Robotics

Robotics is reviewed case by case, on an application from the school, to decide whether it qualifies.

Where outside classes fit

Only the school’s own approved course earns the substitution. A student who has already built projects in Python or studied for AP outside school simply walks into that course better prepared.

Signature project

Binning five years of Sioux Falls recreation programs

The City of Sioux Falls publishes its Parks and Recreation activity points under a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 licence. Each record is a program, from toddler sports to the municipal band, with a season, a year and a number in a field called Frequency.

1. Choose the bins

Students pick bin edges, count records in each, and draw a histogram. Then they try other edges and see how the picture changes, which is the whole art of binning.

2. Mean against median

The median Frequency is 1 but the mean is 2.11, because a walking program reaches 65. One long tail drags the average, a lesson that applies to every skewed dataset a student will meet.

3. Clean the labels

Seven records say Weeday Morning, and 126 have a blank category. Students fix the typo, decide what to do with blanks, and record each choice.

Coding and AI

South Dakota wrote AI into its CS standards. Here is why that needs coding.

The standards say AI leverages core computer science ideas such as algorithms and data structures. Understanding those ideas is what lets a student judge an AI answer.

Ask an AI tool for the typical number of sessions in a Sioux Falls recreation program and it may answer with the mean, 2.11, which describes almost no actual program. The median is 1. A student who binned the data knows which number to trust and can explain why.

That is the habit we build: compute it yourself first, then use AI to speed up work you understand and can check. Students under 18 use only AI tools whose terms allow their age, and school work is always their own.

Standards that name AI from kindergarten up are a signal that judgment, not just tool use, is the skill that lasts. The longer argument is in why coding is still worth it.

FAQ

South Dakota coding and AI questions

Answers from SD DOE, Code.org and the College Board. Our own suggestions say so.

Does South Dakota have computer science standards?

Yes. SD DOE published K-8 computer science standards adopted April 14, 2025, in bands for grades K-2, 3-5 and 6-8.

Can computer science count toward a South Dakota diploma?

Yes, in one way. A state-approved advanced computer science course may replace one unit of a science elective for the base diploma and the Advanced Career Endorsement. It may not replace Biology.

Which courses can replace a science elective?

SD DOE lists Networking Technologies, Computer Programming I and II, PLTW Computer Science Principles, PLTW Computer Science A or AP Computer Science A, PLTW Cybersecurity, and AP Computer Science Principles. Robotics is reviewed case by case.

Does the substitution count for the Advanced Honors Endorsement?

No. SD DOE says the advanced CS substitution may not count for the Advanced or Advanced Honors Endorsements.

Do the SD standards mention AI?

Yes. The 2025 standards document says one goal is to explicitly indicate where artificial intelligence is referenced or where skills build toward understanding it.

How many South Dakota high schools offer computer science?

Code.org (2025) reports 97 of 178 South Dakota high schools, about 54.5%, offering foundational CS in 2023 to 2024.

Which time zone are South Dakota classes in?

Whichever your town keeps. IANA places eastern South Dakota on Central time and western South Dakota on Mountain time, and we confirm lessons in your zone.

When can South Dakota students take classes?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays.

Is there a South Dakota center?

No. There is no Modern Age Coders center in South Dakota. Every lesson is live and online, taught from India.

Do your classes earn the science-elective substitution?

No. Only a state-approved school course does. Our classes build the skills and projects that make that course easier, and graded school work stays the student's own.
