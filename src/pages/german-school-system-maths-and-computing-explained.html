In short

German school runs from Grundschule (Klasse 1 to 4, or 1 to 6 in Berlin and Brandenburg) into one of several secondary schools, then to the Mittlerer Schulabschluss after Klasse 10 or on through the Oberstufe to the Abitur after 12 or 13 years. Each Land names its schools differently. Modern Age Coders teaches maths and coding alongside it, live online in English, in a group or one to one.

The stages

How does the German school system work, from Grundschule to Abitur?

Education in Germany is run by the sixteen Länder, not by Berlin. Their education ministers agree a common frame in the Kultusministerkonferenz (KMK), and the frame below is the KMK's own.

Starting school and how long it lasts

The school year in Germany legally begins on 1 August and ends on 31 July, says the KMK dossier, although the first day of lessons depends on the Land's summer holidays. Compulsory schooling normally begins in the year a child turns six; each Land sets a cut-off birthday somewhere between 30 June and 30 September.

Full-time schooling is compulsory for nine years in most Länder. The dossier names Berlin, Brandenburg, Bremen and Thüringen as Länder with ten years, and Nordrhein-Westfalen with nine or ten depending on the course a pupil follows.

The big decision after Grundschule

There is no exam at the end of Grundschule. Instead, the KMK dossier describes a "Votum der abgebenden Schule", the primary school's recommendation, which always comes with a counselling talk for parents. Depending on the Land, the final choice of secondary school rests either with the parents or with the school and the school authority.

The dossier is also clear that families have no legal right to a place at one particular school. If you are arriving mid-year, ask the local Schulamt early which schools have room.

Two KMK rules soften the early choice. Pupils can still switch to another track in Klasse 5 and 6, and the KMK says that, in principle, every Sekundarstufe I certificate can be reached at every school type. General certificates can also be gained later at a vocational school. A choice made at age ten is important, but it is not a life sentence.

School names

Why does every Land call its schools something different?

Lower secondary school has three tracks everywhere: Hauptschule level, Realschule level and Gymnasium level. The Länder combine them into school types with their own names, which is why a school that sounds unfamiliar in Hamburg may be ordinary in Berlin.

The one name that never changes

The KMK overview makes one exception to the patchwork: the Gymnasium exists in every Land. It is the school type that leads most directly to the Abitur, and the KMK overview shows it running from the end of Grundschule into its own Oberstufe.

The two Oberschulen in the table are a good warning about names. In Niedersachsen and Bremen an Oberschule combines all three tracks; in Sachsen and Brandenburg the same word means a school with two.

Where the Oberstufe can be

The gymnasiale Oberstufe, the upper school that leads to the Abitur, is not only found at a Gymnasium. The KMK overview places it at the Gymnasium (Klassen 10 to 12 or 11 to 13) and at schools with three tracks (as a rule Klassen 11 to 13), and the KMK dossier adds the Berufliches Gymnasium, a vocational upper school.

Entry to any of them needs the Mittlerer Schulabschluss with the entitlement to the Oberstufe, which a pupil earns with results above the plain MSA level.

G8 or G9

Is my child's Gymnasium G8 or G9?

G8 means the Abitur after eight years of Gymnasium (Klasse 5 to 12), G9 after nine (Klasse 5 to 13). The KMK allows both, as long as pupils reach at least 265 weekly lesson hours, counted year by year, from Klasse 5. Several Länder have switched back to G9 in recent years, so check the route for your child's own year group.

Why it matters for maths: on a G8 route, the Oberstufe begins a year earlier, in Klasse 10, so the Einführungsphase overlaps with what G9 pupils still treat as lower secondary. A pupil arriving from abroad at age fifteen can land in quite different maths depending on the route. We place new pupils by what they can already do, not by their age, and then follow their school's pace.

Marks and points

How do German school marks work, and why is 1 the top mark?

German schools use two scales. Up to the Oberstufe, the KMK's six-step scale runs the opposite way from many countries: 1 is the top mark and 6 the lowest. In the Qualifikationsphase, a 0 to 15 point scale takes over, and there higher is better.

The six marks and the matching Oberstufe points
NoteGerman wordPunkte in the Qualifikationsphase

2gut12, 11 or 10
3befriedigend9, 8 or 7
4ausreichend6, 5 or 4
5mangelhaft3, 2 or 1
6ungenügend0

Source: KMK Eurydice dossier 2024/2025, sections on assessment in Sekundarstufe I and in the allgemeinbildende Sekundarstufe II. The three points within one Note reflect the "Notentendenz", a plus or minus.

What a 4 means

A 4, ausreichend, is a pass. The KMK dossier says that moving up a year generally needs at least ausreichend in every subject that counts for promotion, and that a mangelhaft or ungenügend in one subject can usually be balanced, within limits, by at least befriedigend elsewhere. The class conference of all the pupil's teachers decides.

For the Abitur, the KMK says the Allgemeine Hochschulreife is awarded when the overall qualification reaches at least an average of 4.

Reports twice a year

Pupils receive a Zeugnis, a school report, at the end of each half-year and at the end of the school year. The Länder may use written reports instead of marks in some years, says the dossier, but never on a leaving certificate.

A family used to percentages should read a German report the other way round: a row of 2s is good news.

Maths

Where is maths tested in German schools?

Maths is taught every year of every school type, and the KMK sets binding national standards for it at three points. The Länder check progress with comparison tests in between.

VERA is the test that confuses new families most. The IQB says plainly that VERA results are not graded and that no rankings of schools or Länder are produced; the tests tell teachers, a year before the standards are due, how far a class has come. Some Länder call them KERMIT, Kompetenztests or Lernstandserhebungen. There is no reason to drill for VERA, but a weak VERA-8 result is a useful early signal before the MSA.

At the Abitur, maths is close to unavoidable: the KMK says the exam covers four or five subjects, at least two at raised level, and two of them must come from German, a foreign language and maths. Our page on Abitur maths in English explains the pool exam itself.

Computing

Do German schools teach computer science?

Some do from Klasse 5, some only as an option, and the answer depends entirely on the Land.

Uneven by design

Informatik is the newest subject in German schools and the least settled. The Gesellschaft für Informatik counts six Länder in its Informatik-Monitor 2026/27 where no Informatik lesson is compulsory for every pupil: Berlin, Brandenburg, Bremen, Hessen, Rheinland-Pfalz and Sachsen-Anhalt.

In the other ten it is compulsory at some point in lower secondary, from Klasse 5 and 6 in Nordrhein-Westfalen to Klasse 9 and 10 in Niedersachsen.

Thin at the top

In the Oberstufe Informatik is an option, and the same monitor puts the national share of pupils who take it in the Qualifikationsphase at about 15 percent.

Our English guide for families in Germany and our German page on Informatik-Nachhilfe go further into the rules Land by Land.

A program you can run

How many routes lead to the Abitur? A short Python program

Families often hear that only the Gymnasium leads to the Abitur. The KMK rules above say otherwise, and a short program makes the point better than a paragraph. The map below is our own simplification of the KMK overview, not an official chart.

What the program says

Of the fourteen routes, only one is the classic straight line: Gymnasium, then the Oberstufe at the same school. Three never touch a Gymnasium of any kind: a pupil goes from a school with several tracks or a Realschule to the MSA and then into the Oberstufe of a school with three tracks. Our map is simplified (a Land may restrict some of these steps), but the shape is the KMK's.

The Realschule and Gymnasium point at each other because the KMK allows a change of track in Klasse 5 and 6.

The line that stops a loop

That two-way arrow is the trap in the code. Delete the line if here in path and the function walks Gymnasium, Realschule, Gymnasium, Realschule for ever; Python gives up with RecursionError: maximum recursion depth exceeded.

Real route planners, from train timetables to the maps in your phone, guard against the same circle. Spotting it is the kind of thinking we teach before any syntax.

  1. Ages 10 to 12. Draw the map on paper as boxes and arrows. Trace three routes with a finger and count the arrows on each.
  2. Ages 12 to 14. Run the program, then add a box for the Erster Schulabschluss after Klasse 9. Does the number of routes to the Abitur change? Explain why.
  3. Ages 14 and up. Change the program so it prints the shortest route first. Then remove the loop guard on purpose and read the error message slowly.

The German school system: common questions

At what age do children start school in Germany?

Compulsory school normally starts in the year a child turns six. Each Land sets a cut-off birthday between 30 June and 30 September, and children who are six by then start on 1 August, the legal start of the school year, according to the KMK dossier.

How many years is school compulsory in Germany?

Nine years of full-time school in most Länder. The KMK dossier names Berlin, Brandenburg, Bremen and Thüringen with ten years, and Nordrhein-Westfalen with nine or ten depending on the course.

Is there an exam to get into the Gymnasium?

There is no leaving exam at the end of Grundschule. The primary school gives a recommendation after a counselling talk with parents, and depending on the Land the final decision lies with the parents or with the school and school authority. Some Länder use a trial period or entrance test for certain schools.

Can my child reach the Abitur without going to a Gymnasium?

Yes. The KMK places the gymnasiale Oberstufe at the Gymnasium, at schools with three tracks and at the Berufliches Gymnasium. Entry needs the Mittlerer Schulabschluss with the Oberstufe entitlement, which can be earned at any lower secondary school type.

What do G8 and G9 mean?

G8 is the Abitur after eight years of Gymnasium, at the end of Klasse 12; G9 after nine, at the end of Klasse 13. Several Länder, Bayern among them, have returned to G9. Our table above shows the route each Land's Gymnasium follows in 2026/27, as far as our sources describe it.

Is a 1 or a 6 the best mark in Germany?

A 1 (sehr gut) is the top mark and a 6 (ungenügend) the lowest. In the last two years before the Abitur, marks become points from 0 to 15, where 15 is the top.

What is VERA and does my child need to prepare?

VERA is a set of comparison tests in Klasse 3 and Klasse 8. The IQB says the results are not graded and no rankings are made. The 2027 window for VERA-8 is 8 February to 25 March 2027. No special preparation is needed, but the result can point to gaps before the MSA.

Are the classes in German?

No. Every class is live and taught in English only, by mentors based in India who match your child's pace and accent. We do keep the German school terms beside the English, because your child's tests will use them.

When do classes run in German time?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Germany is 4 hours 30 minutes behind India in winter and 3 hours 30 minutes behind in summer, so German afternoons suit our mentors well.

Do you give German school marks or certificates?

No. We teach alongside school and are not connected to the KMK, the IQB or any ministry. Your child's school sets the marks that count; our job is to make the maths and computing behind them clear.
