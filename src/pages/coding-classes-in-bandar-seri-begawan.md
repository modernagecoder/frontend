## What is Bandar Seri Begawan, administratively?

Worth reading before you compare providers, because it explains why an address in the capital tells you very little.

The city sits inside **Brunei-Muara**, the smallest of the country's four districts and by a wide margin the most populous, with **332,462** residents. The municipality itself is estimated at **72,133** people, and it is composed of thirteen mukims:

Berakas 'A' · Berakas 'B' · Burong Pingai Ayer · Gadong 'A' · Gadong 'B' · Kianggeh · Kilanas · Kota Batu · Peramu · Saba · Sungai Kebun · Sungai Kedayan · Tamoi

**Six of those thirteen are Kampong Ayer**, the settlement of stilt villages standing over the Brunei River in the middle of the capital. It is not a preserved quarter or an attraction with a fence round it. It is part of the city's administration, it has been inhabited since long before the city had a name, and **Awang Semaun Secondary School** is described as the only school of its kind whose buildings are built on water.

### Why "a class in Bandar Seri Begawan" is a weak promise

On **1 August 2007** the municipal area went from 12.87 square kilometres to **100.36**, roughly eight times larger, without anybody moving house. So a physical class advertised as being in the capital can sit most of an hour's drive from a family who also live in the capital, and if the family live over the river the first part of that journey is not a drive at all.

Online, the distance between a learner and the teacher is the same from every mukim in the list above, and it is nil.

## The Bandar Seri Begawan student data project

Every page in this cluster works through one analytical mistake. The capital's is the one that ruins more spreadsheets than any other: **you cannot add a part to its whole.**

The question is deliberately ordinary. How many people live in Bandar Seri Begawan and its water village? Two figures are available: **72,133** for the city and **10,250** for Kampong Ayer at the 2016 census.

Almost everybody adds them. 82,383. It is the obvious move, the units match, and nothing about the two numbers looks incompatible.

**Every one of those 10,250 people has now been counted twice.**

Kampong Ayer is not next to the capital. It is six of the capital's thirteen mukims: Burong Pingai Ayer, Peramu, Saba, Sungai Kebun, Sungai Kedayan and Tamoi. Every name on that list appears on the list of thirteen. So the water village is a subset of the city, and the 10,250 are already inside the 72,133.

The learner writes the union properly. For two sets the size of the union is the size of the first plus the size of the second minus the size of the overlap. Here the second set is entirely contained in the first, so the overlap is the whole of the second set, and the arithmetic collapses: **the union is just 72,133.** The addition did not approximate the answer. It overstated it by exactly the size of the smaller set, 14.2 per cent of the true figure.

The rule the learner takes away is one line. **Before adding two counts, name one person who is in the first and not the second.** If you cannot describe that person, the sets are nested and addition is not an estimate, it is a category error. The size of the mistake does not depend on how big the numbers are. It depends entirely on how much they overlap, which is the one thing the numbers themselves never tell you.

Then the same discipline is turned on the two census columns. Between 2016 and 2021 the water village total falls by 1,348, and it would be easy to write "Kampong Ayer is emptying". Check the parts and two of the six went up: Peramu from 1,111 to 1,151, Sungai Kedayan from 230 to 241. A direction that is true of a total can be false of the things the total is made of.

Every figure is public and linked above. No household, school or family is described, and the learner does the arithmetic themselves.

## Does coding still matter when AI can write it?

Try this before you decide. Ask any assistant how many people live in Kampong Ayer. There is a good chance you are told about thirty thousand, confidently, in a well-shaped sentence.

The censuses say **10,250 in 2016 and 8,902 in 2021.** The thirty thousand figure is not invented: it was roughly right in 1981, when the settlement held about 28,000 people, and it has been copied from travel page to travel page ever since. The model is reproducing a consensus faithfully. The consensus is forty-five years old.

That is the shape of the problem, and a better model does not fix it. The output was fluent, plausible and drawn from real text. What it lacked was somebody asking **as of when**. A learner who has spent a term being caught out by their own arithmetic asks that question unprompted, and that habit is the actual product.

## Practical questions

**Which days?** Whichever the household is free. The capital is the seat of government, so a great many families here keep the public sector pattern, in which Saturday is a working day. Others keep a Monday to Friday week. We ask rather than assume. What the Brunei working week actually looks like is set out on the [country page](https://learn.modernagecoders.com/coding-classes-in-brunei).

**What time?** Brunei is on UTC plus 8 with no daylight saving, and Indian Standard Time does not shift either, so the offset to the teacher is fixed at **two hours thirty minutes** all year. An early evening here is a mid-afternoon there. Agree a time once and it stays right.

**Format?** Groups of **five to eight** or **one to one**, **twice a week**, normally eight lessons a month, in **English**. A laptop or desktop with stable internet, working audio and a current browser is all that is needed.
