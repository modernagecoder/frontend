Coding classes for kids in Dortmund

In North Rhine-Westphalia every pupil gets Informatik in grades 5 and 6, so a Dortmund child meets computing at about ten, earlier than in most of Germany. The four Grundschule years before that have no computing lesson of their own. This page sets out what the NRW subject covers, how our live classes in English fill the primary years, a free maths contest open from grade 3, and a project in which children read the building year of every Dortmund playground and find out how messy real data can be.

School in North Rhine-Westphalia

When do Dortmund children first learn computing at school?

In grade 5. NRW is one of the Länder that makes Informatik compulsory for every pupil, and it starts at the beginning of secondary school. The full path below is drawn from the GI monitor and the KMK.

Grades 1 to 4: Grundschule, no Informatik

Dortmund children spend grades 1 to 4 in Grundschule, as in almost every Land: the KMK describes a primary stage running "von der ersten bis zur vierten" Jahrgangsstufe, with Berlin and Brandenburg as the only exceptions. There is no leaving exam at the end of it, and computing has no lesson of its own.

That makes the years from 6 to about 10 free time for coding in the best sense: nothing is being examined, so a child can play, build and get things wrong without a mark attached.

Grades 5 and 6: Informatik for everyone

The Informatik-Monitor 2026/27 of the GI (Gesellschaft für Informatik) records that NRW teaches "das Pflichtfach Informatik in den Jahrgangsstufen 5 und 6" at all general school types, with a Kernlehrplan written for those two years. Whether your child goes to a Gymnasium, a Gesamtschule, a Realschule or a Hauptschule in Dortmund, Informatik is on the grade 5 timetable.

After grade 6 it becomes a choice, and the size of that choice depends on the school type, as the table shows.

What this means for a Dortmund family: the school will teach computing, but it starts at ten and, for many pupils, stops being compulsory at twelve. A child who has spent a few years building things before grade 5 tends to enjoy the Informatik lessons rather than struggle through them, and a child who enjoys grades 5 and 6 can keep going with us after the elective choice narrows.

Language of the classes

Are classes taught in German or in English?

In English, always. We say so before the first class because it matters for a family choosing between options in Dortmund.

English, not German

Every lesson is live and in English. We do not run German-language groups. Families at German schools often treat this as two subjects at once: a child learns to program and hears an hour of clear spoken English twice a week.

Speed set by the child

The mentor slows down, rephrases and waits. A child who mixes German words into an answer is understood, and the mentor offers the English word back without fuss. Pace and accent are matched to the learner, not to a script.

The screen does the talking

In Scratch and Python most of the lesson is visible: blocks snap, a turtle draws, a number prints. Children follow what they can see, and the English they need grows week by week from a small, repeated set of words.

Every mentor works from India over live video, which is why a Dortmund afternoon lines up with an Indian evening. The same mentor stays with a group, which means that after a month they know which child needs a harder puzzle and which one needs a minute of quiet before answering.

A real Dortmund project

How old are Dortmund's playgrounds? A project in reading numbers out of text

The City of Dortmund publishes a register of its playgrounds as open data. One column says, in words, how big each playground is and when it was built. Getting a computer to read that column is a lesson every young programmer needs.

The file

The register is called Spielplätze and comes from the city's open-data portal, published under the Datenlizenz Deutschland Zero 2.0, which lets anyone reuse it. We downloaded it on 2 November 2026: 366 rows, one per playground, with the name, the Stadtbezirk and a column called Information.

That column holds short notes such as "2300 qm, Baujahr 1983": the area in square metres and the year the playground was built. It is written for people. A computer sees only a string of characters.

The question

In which decade did Dortmund build most of its playgrounds? Children usually guess "recently", because the playground they know looks new. The register says otherwise.

The first program does the obvious thing: cut each note at the word "Baujahr" and turn what is left into a number. It works on the first 38 rows and then stops.

A crash is a good teacher. The child opens the file and finds that not every row follows the pattern: ten cells are empty, four just say "Westfalenpark", one says "Träger: Jugendamt", and three say "Baujahr k.A.", the German short form for "no information". A program that only works on tidy rows is a program that has not met real data yet.

The 1960s win clearly, with 80 playgrounds. The second program also says, without being asked twice, that 18 rows could not be read. That last line is the habit we want: a result that admits its own gaps is a result you can trust. And one row stands out by itself: a playground in Brackel listed with the building year 1878. It may be real, or it may be a typing slip for 1978; the file cannot tell us, so a careful programmer reports it and does not quietly drop it.

  1. Ages 6 to 8, on paper. Write ten cards like "800 qm, built 2008". Sort them by year by hand. Then add a card that just says "park". Where does it go?
  2. Ages 8 to 10, in Scratch. Keep a list of years and count how many fall in each decade. Then feed it the word "unknown" and see what Scratch does.
  3. Ages 10 to 12, in Python. Run Version 1, read the error, find row 39 in the file, and write your own fix before you look at Version 2.
  4. Going further. The inner-city districts hold most of the old playgrounds, the outer ones far fewer. Use the area figure in the same notes to ask a second question: are the older playgrounds bigger or smaller?

A free contest from grade 3

Can a Dortmund primary pupil take part in the Mathematik-Olympiade?

Yes. The Mathematik-Olympiade has been open to primary schools since 2005, and it is entered through the school, free of charge for the family.

What the contest is

The Mathematik-Olympiade is a national maths competition with about 200,000 pupils a year, from grade 3 to grade 13. It is not a speed quiz: children write out solutions to a small number of problems and explain their reasoning, which is exactly the habit that makes a good programmer.

For grades 5 to 7 it runs in three rounds, and from grade 8 in four. Primary pupils in grades 3 and 4 take part in three rounds as well.

The 2026 to 2027 calendar

The school round of the 66th Olympiad runs in the autumn of 2026 at each school. The national association Mathematik-Olympiaden e.V. lists the Landesrunde for 26 to 28 February 2027 and the Bundesrunde for 30 May to 2 June 2027, hosted in Kaiserslautern.

Ask your child's maths teacher whether the school takes part this year; the problems are set and marked through the school.

We are not connected with the Olympiad or its organisers, and we never copy its problems. In class we write our own problems in the same spirit: a short story, a pattern to explain, a claim to check for every case. A child who has met a "Could not read: 18" line in a program already knows the most important olympiad question: have I covered every case?

Dortmund parents ask

Coding classes for kids in Dortmund: questions and answers

Are the classes in German?

No. All classes are live and taught only in English. The mentor speaks clearly, adapts to your child's pace and accent, and is used to children who are still learning English. Code itself looks the same in every language.

Do Dortmund schools teach Informatik?

Yes, from grade 5. The Gesellschaft für Informatik records that North Rhine-Westphalia teaches Informatik as a compulsory subject in grades 5 and 6 at all general school types. After grade 6 it becomes an elective, and how much is offered depends on the school type.

Is there any computing in Dortmund Grundschulen?

There is no Informatik subject in grades 1 to 4 in NRW. Some schools do media work or clubs, but there is no compulsory computing lesson before grade 5, which is why many families start at home or online.

From what age can my child start?

From 6. The youngest children start with puzzles, ordering games and Scratch blocks, so no typing or reading of long instructions is needed. Typed Python usually starts at about 9.

When are classes in Dortmund time?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Most Dortmund children join after school, between mid-afternoon and early evening.

Where are the teachers?

Our mentors are based in India and teach live over video. A class at 16:25 in Dortmund starts at 20:55 in India during the winter months and at 19:55 in summer. Your child keeps the same mentor.

Can my child enter the Mathematik-Olympiade?

Usually yes, through the school. The Olympiad is open from grade 3 and has been offered to primary schools since 2005. Ask the maths teacher whether the school takes part; we are not connected with the organisers.

What is the playground project?

An original Python project on the City of Dortmund playground register. Children read the building year out of a text note, count playgrounds by decade and learn to report the 18 rows the program could not read.

How big are the groups?

Groups have fifteen to twenty children of a similar age. If you prefer, one-to-one classes give your child a mentor of their own at times you agree.

What does it cost, and in which currency?

The fee is monthly and shown on this page in euro at the European Central Bank reference rate. The payment is taken in US dollars and your bank converts it. The first class is free.
