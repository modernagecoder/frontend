> "Should my child learn AI" is three questions. Should they learn to judge AI output, literacy: yes, essentially every child, starting as young as 6 with concepts and no accounts, because they already live among these systems. Should they learn to build with AI, vibe coding and app building: if they enjoy making things, from 6 with age-appropriate tools, and it should follow interest rather than fear. Should they learn to build AI itself, machine learning: only the genuinely curious, typically from the teen years. The falling-behind panic is a sales device; a calm start beats a scared one in every outcome we can measure. ## The three meanings, kept apart

Judging it: knowing what AI is, why it errs, when to check. Who needs it: essentially every child. Sensible start: 6 to 8, concepts only, no accounts. Driven by necessity, like road safety; there is no wrong reason to start.

Building with it: making apps, games and tools by directing AI. Who: children who love making things. Start: 6 in block environments, 13 for the real tools with consent. Driven by interest, and only interest; a parent's fear of falling behind is the wrong reason.

Building it: training and understanding models themselves. Who: the persistently curious minority. Start: usually 13 plus, with maths tolerance helping. Driven by fascination, self-selected; prestige or someone else's child is the wrong reason.

Conflating the three is how families buy the wrong course, and how the wrong course convinces a child that AI is not for them.

## The pitch versus the truth

The pitch: AI will run the world by 2030, children who start now will lead, the rest fall behind, limited seats, price ends Sunday. Note what it never mentions: your actual child. Any child fits the ad, and that is the tell.

The true version: AI systems will sit inside most work and daily life; judging their output is becoming a basic skill; children who learn it calmly do fine whenever they start; the building tracks are optional and wonderful for children who like building; watch your child, then choose; there is time.

On falling behind specifically: in six years of teaching, eleven year old starters draw level with eight year old starters within months, every time, because the real prerequisites are reading, logic and curiosity. The gap that does not close easily is between children taught to check machine output and children taught to accept it.

## Ready signals and honest reasons to wait

Green lights: for literacy, being six and able to sit with a group. For building with AI, the child who modifies rather than consumes and says "what if it could" at the screen. For building AI, the question behind the question: not "how do I win" but "how did it decide", asked unprompted more than once. The strongest signal: the child asks to continue after the free class.

Honest reasons to wait: a term already at capacity, a sibling's enthusiasm doing the choosing, a recent string of purchases that did not stick. What should never drive timing: a viral clip of somebody's nine year old shipping an app.

## Frequently asked questions

**Falling behind?** No. A child starting literacy at 11 catches a panicked 8 year old starter within months. The framing sells courses and ruins the fun.

**Can it wait a year?** The building tracks wait beautifully. Literacy we would not delay, for the road-safety reason: the child is already walking beside the road.

**No interest shown?** Push literacy gently like swimming; never push the building tracks, which run on wanting to build.

**Is 6 too young?** Right for literacy concepts, no accounts. Too young for mainstream tools, whose own terms say 13, and a programme that ignores that has failed your vetting.

**For daughters?** Emphatically, and early: girls opt out through accumulated signals, not ability, and a girl who has shipped something real has evidence no stereotype survives.

**Will AI replace the careers anyway?** No honest promises about 2040 jobs; directionally, the person who can judge and direct machine output is employable across far more futures.
