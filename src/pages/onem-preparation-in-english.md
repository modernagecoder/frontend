# ONEM preparation in English

ONEM, the Olimpiada Nacional Escolar de Matemática, is Peru's national school maths olympiad and one of the largest school contests in the country. Its papers are in Spanish, but plenty of the students who sit it learn their maths in English, at bilingual, Cambridge and IB schools. ## What is ONEM, in plain English?

A national maths competition for every secundaria student in Peru's regular school system, run by the Ministry of Education (MINEDU) with the steel company Aceros Arequipa. 2026 was its twenty-second edition, the XXII ONEM.

### Who runs it

MINEDU, which made the olympiad a permanent national contest by ministerial resolution in 2004, with Aceros Arequipa as its private partner under an agreement numbered 004-2025-MINEDU. Schools, local education offices (UGEL) and regional offices (DRE or GRE) run the early stages.

### How big it is

El Peruano, the official gazette, put ONEM 2026 at over 1.7 million participating secundaria students. In one region alone, La Libertad, 177,797 students from 936 schools sat the school stage.

### What it rewards

Problem solving rather than recall. The bases say the early papers use problems set in context, with special weight on analysis, heuristic strategies and mathematical procedures, and that later stages move towards international olympiad style.

At the top, the 2026 national stage brought 237 students together at the Centro Vacacional Huampaní on 11 October 2026 (the bases place the national stage in Lima), and El Peruano reported 72 medals: 12 gold, 24 silver and 36 bronze. We are not connected with MINEDU, any UGEL or DRE, or Aceros Arequipa, and nothing on this page is official ONEM material. For the Spanish-language overview of how we teach maths in English, see our [profesor de matemáticas en inglés](/profesor-de-matematicas-en-ingles-peru) page.

## Can a student at a private, bilingual or international school take part?

Yes, if the school is a secundaria school in Peru's regular basic education (EBR) and takes part. ONEM 2026 was open to boys and girls in 1° to 5° de secundaria at public and private schools, in two categories and three levels.

**ONEM 2026 categories and levels**

|  | What it means | Normative age on 31 March (MINEDU) |
|---|---|---|
| Category Alfa | Students at public schools | n/a |
| Category Beta | Students at private schools | n/a |
| Nivel 1 | 1° and 2° de secundaria | 12 and 13 |
| Nivel 2 | 3° and 4° de secundaria | 14 and 15 |
| Nivel 3 | 5° de secundaria | 16 |

Sources: ONEM-AA 2026 bases, issued to schools by UGEL 06 (MINEDU) in Oficio Múltiple 00164-2026, read on 8 November 2026; MINEDU, Edades normativas, which counts a student's normative age on 31 March of each school year.

### Entry runs through the school

A family cannot enter a child directly. The bases put registration in the hands of the school, which logs its students in MINEDU's competitions platform using its SIAGIE account, the ministry's school records system.

So the first step for an interested student is a conversation with the maths teacher or head of department. If the school does not take part, there is no individual route into ONEM.

### Private schools compete with private schools

From the UGEL stage onwards, Alfa and Beta students qualify separately within their own category. A student at a private bilingual school in Lima therefore competes for Beta places, against other private-school students.

Whether a particular Cambridge, IB or international school enters ONEM is the school's decision; we do not hold a list and name no school. Families at those schools often ask, so it is worth asking early in the year, before the school stage in July.

## How do the ONEM stages and scoring work?

Four stages, each harder than the last, all on paper and in person. Every paper in every stage is out of 100 points, but the way those points are earned changes halfway up.

**ONEM 2026: the four stages and their papers**

| Stage | Who sits it | Paper | Time | Scoring |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| School (IE) | Students the school registers; 8 July 2026, morning or afternoon shift | 10 closed-answer items: 2 per competency, 2 mixed | 2 hours | 10 points per correct answer, 0 for wrong or blank |
| UGEL | Two boys and two girls per level from each mixed school | 10 closed-answer items, same structure | 2 hours | 10 points per correct answer |
| DRE or GRE (regional) | Top four per level and category from each UGEL | 8 closed-answer items and 2 open problems | 2 hours | 10 points per item |
| National, in Lima | The top students of each region, by quota | 4 open problems needing full working | 4 hours | Up to 25 points per problem |

Source: ONEM-AA 2026 bases (UGEL 06, Oficio Múltiple 00164-2026), sections on stages, papers and scoring, read on 8 November 2026. "Closed-answer" translates "respuesta construida cerrada": the student writes a single exact answer, with no options to choose from. The four competencies are the CNEB's: quantity; regularity, equivalence and change; data and uncertainty; and shape, movement and location. Single-sex schools send their top four per level instead of two boys and two girls.

### Three scoring rules that change strategy

**A wrong answer costs nothing.** At the school, UGEL and regional stages an incorrect or blank answer scores zero, never minus. Every item should have an answer written in, even a careful estimate.

**Time breaks ties.** Where students tie on points in those stages, the bases give priority to the time taken; only if time also ties are two extra questions used. Finishing cleanly and early has real value.

**Zero stops you.** A student who scores zero cannot go through to the next stage, whatever the quota.

### What changes at the top

The regional paper adds two open problems, described as similar in style to international olympiads, and the national paper is entirely open: four problems, four hours, marks for development and justification.

A student who has only trained for quick exact answers meets a different sport at that point. Preparing well means building both skills from the start: fast accurate answers for the early rounds, and the patience to write a full solution for the later ones.

The bases also say the papers at the school, UGEL and regional stages are sat at the same time across the country and cannot be rearranged, so a family trip in early July needs to be planned around the school's date. The 2026 school stage was on Wednesday 8 July, with a morning shift from 8:00 to 10:00 and an afternoon shift from 13:00 to 15:00.

## My child learns maths in English. Can they still do well in a Spanish paper?

Yes, if they can read a Spanish statement precisely. The mathematics is identical; the risk is a single word that changes the answer. These are the words we drill with English-medium students.

**Spanish contest words that change an answer (our classroom list)**

| In the Spanish statement | In English | The trap |
|---|---|---|
| cifras | digits | Not "figures" in the sense of amounts; "¿cuántas cifras?" asks how many digits |
| entero positivo | positive integer | Excludes zero; "entero no negativo" includes it |
| residuo | remainder | Not "residue" as in leftovers in general |
| a lo sumo, como máximo | at most | The boundary value is allowed |
| MCD, MCM | HCF (or GCD), LCM | The letters do not match the English abbreviations |
| capicúa | palindromic number | No everyday English word; 1221 is capicúa |
| razón | ratio | Also means "reason" in ordinary Spanish |
| consecutivos | consecutive | Consecutive even numbers differ by 2, not 1 |

Our own list from teaching bilingual students. It is not an official ONEM glossary, and ONEM papers are set and marked in Spanish by MINEDU's contractors, not by us.

In class we put a Spanish statement and our English version side by side, and ask the student to find the word that matters before solving anything. Then we remove the English. Within a few weeks most students read the Spanish directly and stop translating, which is faster in an exam where time decides ties.

There is a benefit the other way too. A student who has to explain a solution in English, slowly and in full sentences, writes clearer solutions in Spanish, and clear writing is what the open problems in the regional and national papers reward.

## What is the pigeonhole principle? A lesson with a Python check

If more objects than boxes go into boxes, some box holds two. It sounds too obvious to be useful, yet it settles a whole family of contest problems, provided you choose the right boxes. We wrote the problem below.

### The problem

Choose some of the whole numbers from 1 to 8. How many must you choose to be certain that two of them add up to 9?

Most students guess and test a few choices. The olympiad answer needs two halves: a reason why a certain number always works, and an example showing that one fewer can fail.

### The boxes

Put the eight numbers into four boxes: {1, 8}, {2, 7}, {3, 6} and {4, 5}. Each box is a pair that adds up to 9. Choose five numbers and, with only four boxes, two must come from the same box, so they add up to 9.

Choose four and you can dodge it: take 1, 2, 3 and 4, one from each box. So **five is the answer: five always works, and four does not**. Both halves are needed for full marks.

**Version 1: count every possible choice** (Python)
```python
from itertools import combinations

numbers = range(1, 9)            # the whole numbers 1 to 8

def has_pair(chosen):
    # True if two chosen numbers add up to 9
    return any(a + b == 9 for a, b in combinations(chosen, 2))

for size in (4, 5):
    picks = list(combinations(numbers, size))
    safe = [p for p in picks if not has_pair(p)]
    print("Choose", size, ":", len(picks), "ways,", len(safe), "with no pair summing to 9")
```
It prints Choose 4 : 70 ways, 16 with no pair summing to 9 and Choose 5 : 56 ways, 0 with no pair summing to 9.

**Version 2: look inside the safe choices of four** (Python)
```python
safe4 = [p for p in combinations(numbers, 4) if not has_pair(p)]
print("Example:", safe4[0])
print("Every safe choice takes one from each box:",
      all(len({min(n, 9 - n) for n in p}) == 4 for p in safe4))
```
Run after Version 1, it prints Example: (1, 2, 3, 4) and Every safe choice takes one from each box: True.

The program shows both halves of the argument. Among the 56 ways to choose five, not one avoids a pair, which is the pigeonhole half. Among the 70 ways to choose four, exactly 16 avoid a pair, and Version 2 confirms that each of them takes one number from each box. That 16 is no accident: two choices per box, four boxes, 2 &times; 2 &times; 2 &times; 2 = 16.

For ONEM the lesson is practical. A closed-answer item asking "the least number that guarantees..." wants the number 5, and the boxes are how you find it in under a minute. An open problem at the regional or national stage wants the boxes, the argument and the example of four written out, because a jury marks the reasoning. We teach students to produce both, and to use code only to check their thinking afterwards.

## Five ONEM-style practice problems with exact answers (ours)

We wrote these for this page in the closed-answer format of the early ONEM stages: each answer is a single number. They are not ONEM problems and are not taken from any ONEM paper. The first four answers were checked by a short Python program.

1. **Quantity.** How many whole numbers from 1 to 500 are divisible by 4 or by 6 (or both)?
2. **Regularity and change.** The sequence 3, 7, 11, 15, ... goes up by 4 each time. Which term is the first one greater than 1,000? Give its position.
3. **Data and uncertainty.** Two ordinary dice are rolled. Of the 36 equally likely outcomes, in how many is the product of the two numbers even?
4. **Shape and location.** A grid of unit squares is 4 squares wide and 3 squares tall. How many rectangles, drawn along the grid lines, are not squares?
5. **Mixed, pigeonhole.** What is the smallest number of students in a class that guarantees three of them were born in the same month?

### Answers

1. **167**: 125 multiples of 4, plus 83 of 6, minus the 41 multiples of 12 counted twice. Python printed Q1 167.

2. **251**: the nth term is 4n &minus; 1, and 4n &minus; 1 > 1,000 first holds at n = 251, where the term is 1,003. Python printed Q2 term 251 value 1003.

3. **27**: the product is odd only when both dice are odd, 3 &times; 3 = 9 outcomes, so 36 &minus; 9 = 27 are even.

### Answers, continued

4. **40**: choose 2 of the 5 vertical lines and 2 of the 4 horizontal ones, 10 &times; 6 = 60 rectangles, of which 12 + 6 + 2 = 20 are squares. Python printed Q4 rectangles 60 squares 20 non-square 40.

5. **25**: twelve months are the boxes. With 24 students, two per month avoids any three sharing; the 25th student must make a third in some month.

Notice which problems a careful student could answer by listing cases and which need an idea. That judgement is what saves minutes, and minutes break ties.

## ONEM preparation in English: questions and answers

### Are the classes in Spanish?

No. Our classes are live, online and taught only in English, by mentors who speak clearly and adjust to each student's pace. ONEM papers are in Spanish, so we also train students to read Spanish statements precisely, but the teaching itself is always in English.

### Can you register my child for ONEM?

No. Registration is done by the school through MINEDU's systems, and families cannot enter directly. We are not connected with MINEDU, any UGEL or DRE, or Aceros Arequipa. Ask your child's school whether it takes part, ideally early in the school year.

### Can students at private or international schools take part?

ONEM 2026 was open to students in 1° to 5° de secundaria at public and private schools in Peru's regular basic education. Private-school students compete in the Beta category. Whether a given school enters is up to that school.

### Is there a penalty for a wrong answer?

Not in the 2026 school, UGEL and regional papers: a correct answer scored 10 points and a wrong or blank one scored 0. So students should answer every item. At the national stage, solutions are marked out of 25 points per problem.

### Do you use past ONEM problems?

None. Our mentors write all the problems we use, the ones on this page included. Official ONEM material belongs to MINEDU and the regions, and students should read it where they publish it.

### When is ONEM 2027?

Not announced yet: MINEDU had not published the 2027 bases when we checked in November 2026. In 2026 the school stage was on 8 July and the national stage on 11 October. Your school will receive the official dates.

### When are classes in Peru time?

Any day, any time, flexible: choose a batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at the hour that suits your family. Peru is UTC minus 5 all year and India UTC plus 5:30, so 08:00 in Lima is 18:30 in India.

### Will my child learn real problem solving or shortcuts?

Real problem solving. Shortcuts help with closed-answer items only until the problems change, and the later ONEM stages are marked on written reasoning. We teach the idea, the proof and then the fast version, never the fast version alone, and no AI tool solves problems for students.

### Do you promise a medal or a place in the next stage?

No. Results depend on the student, the paper and the quotas at each stage, which we do not control. We promise careful teaching and honest feedback. A finished course earns our own certificate of completion; ONEM awards are MINEDU's alone.

### How are fees paid?

By card in US dollars; your bank converts each payment into soles, so the amount can differ slightly between months. The first class costs nothing and needs no card.
