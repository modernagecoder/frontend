## How is a Math-U-See lesson structured?

Every lesson follows what Demme Learning calls its Four-Step Approach. The second step holds the method the curriculum is known for: Build, Write, Say.

**The Math-U-See Four-Step Approach (mathusee.com curriculum page, checked 31 October 2026), with what each step asks of a parent (our reading)**

| Step | What Math-U-See says | In practice at home |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Preparation | Look over the Instruction Manual, watch the lesson videos and get familiar with the Student Workbook pages | A few minutes for the parent before the child starts |
| 2. Presenting | Work examples together using the Build, Write, Say method | The heart of the lesson, done side by side |
| 3. Practice | Lesson Practice pages until mastery is achieved | The child works alone, with help when needed |
| 4. Progression | Systematic Review pages, then the Tests booklet (except for Primer) | Move on only when mastery is shown |

### Build

In Demme Learning's words, "Build: Use the manipulatives to demonstrate and model the problems." The integer blocks are color-coded by value, so a 7 and a 3 look different before they are counted.

### Write

Again from the curriculum page: "Write: Write down step-by-step solutions while working through the problems." The written record is what carries forward once the blocks are put away.

### Say

The third part asks the child to talk through why the concept works as they build and write. It is the step most likely to be skipped on a busy day, and the one a tutor can protect.

### What mastery means here

Math-U-See defines it carefully: "Mastery means that the student not only understands a new concept but can also apply the concept independently to solve a problem." It adds that the student can teach back the concept using the Build, Write, Say method.

That teach-back test is a gift to any tutor. It gives a clear, shared signal for when a lesson is done, which the parent, the child and the teacher can all check.

### The pages in the workbook

For Algebra 1: Legacy Edition, the site lists a Student Workbook with Lesson Practice, Systematic Review, and Application and Enrichment worksheets, a separate Test booklet, an Instruction Manual with detailed instructions and solutions, and lesson videos.

The site says a student who shows mastery quickly may not need every Systematic Review page, and that a student who has not yet mastered a concept may need more practice, reteaching and review, with extra resources on its Digital Toolbox.

## How do I know my child is ready for the next Math-U-See level?

Demme Learning publishes a short readiness list on each level page and offers an interactive placement tool with yes or no questions and example problems. Three of those lists are a useful map of where children tend to need help.

**Readiness points from three Math-U-See level pages (mathusee.com, checked 31 October 2026), summarized**

| Before starting | Math-U-See says the student may be ready if they can |
|---|---|
| Delta | Add or subtract multiple-digit numbers with regrouping; "Recall single-digit multiplication facts from memory"; "Demonstrate a multiplication problem by building or drawing a rectangle" |
| Pre-Algebra | Work fluently with multiple-digit whole numbers; "Proficiently add, subtract, multiply, and divide fractions", mixed numbers and decimals; convert between fraction, decimal and percent; choose the operation for a word problem |
| Algebra 1: Legacy | Do all four operations with whole numbers, fractions, mixed numbers and decimals; convert between forms; solve one-step equations; plot points; operate with integers; write an expression for a word problem |

1. Use the publisher's placement tool first. Math-U-See says its tools identify concepts already mastered and any conceptual gaps, and that its Placement Specialists can help with the results.
2. Read the readiness list for the next level aloud with your child. Ask them to show one item from each line with blocks or on paper.
3. Watch for the item that takes longest. That is usually the gap, and it is often from two levels back rather than the current one.
4. Book a free class with that item. We start there, inside Math-U-See's own order, rather than skipping ahead.

## Where do children commonly get stuck in Math-U-See?

Every curriculum has places where the work changes character. These are the Math-U-See transitions our teachers see most often in live lessons; they are general teaching guidance, not statistics.

**Common stall points in a Math-U-See sequence and what helps (our teaching notes)**

| Transition | What changes | What tends to help |
|---|---|---|
| Gamma to Delta | Division asks the child to run multiplication backwards | Rebuild the rectangle and ask what side is missing |
| Delta to Epsilon | Fractions mean a part of one, not a count | Name the whole first, every time |
| Zeta to Pre-Algebra | Negative numbers break the idea that adding makes bigger | Zero pairs, built and then drawn |
| Pre-Algebra to Algebra 1 | The blocks step back and symbols carry the meaning | Keep saying the why even after the blocks go away |
| Inside any level | Systematic Review pages feel like repetition | Treat review as the check that mastery has lasted |

### The quiet sign: build yes, write no

A child who can model a problem with blocks but freezes at the written version has the concept but not the notation. That is not a reason to return to an earlier level.

In class we put the build and the written step side by side, line by line, until the child can do the writing without the blocks and still describe what the blocks would show.

### The other sign: write yes, say no

The opposite also happens. The answers are right, but the child cannot explain them, which usually means the steps were copied from the video or the manual's examples.

Math-U-See's own mastery test asks the child to teach the idea back. We use that test in every class, gently, and it quickly shows whether a page of right answers is really understood.

## What does Build, Write, Say look like on real problems?

Original examples written by our teachers on topics from the Math-U-See sequence, from Delta to Algebra 1. None of them is taken from a Math-U-See book.

**Factoring x squared + 5x + 6 as a rectangle, an Algebra 1 topic (our example)**

| Part | What the student does | What the student says |
|---|---|---|
| Build | Lay out one x-by-x square, five x-by-1 strips and six unit squares, then arrange all of them into one rectangle | "I need the pieces to make a rectangle with no gaps." |
| Build | The only rectangle that works has sides x + 2 and x + 3 | "Two strips go down one side and three along the other, and the six units fill the corner." |
| Write | x squared + 5x + 6 = (x + 2)(x + 3) | "The sides of the rectangle are the factors." |
| Check | (x + 2)(x + 3) = x squared + 3x + 2x + 6 = x squared + 5x + 6 | "Multiplying the sides gives back the area I started with." |
| Say | Two numbers multiply to 6 and add to 5 | "The corner must be 6 units and the strips must total 5, so I need 2 and 3." |

**factor_pairs.py (our example)** (Python 3)
```python3
def rectangle_sides(b, c):
    """Find whole numbers p, q with p * q == c and p + q == b."""
    for p in range(1, c + 1):
        if c % p == 0:
            q = c // p
            if p + q == b:
                return p, q
    return None

print(rectangle_sides(5, 6))    # x^2 + 5x + 6
print(rectangle_sides(7, 12))   # x^2 + 7x + 12
print(rectangle_sides(4, 6))    # x^2 + 4x + 6
```
Output: (2, 3), then (3, 4), then None. The last trinomial has no whole-number rectangle, which is exactly what happens when a child tries to build it with blocks and always has pieces left over. We use small programs like this with older students who also learn to code.

### Long division by place value (Delta)

Our problem: 744 divided by 6. Build 744 as 7 hundreds, 4 tens and 4 units. Share the hundreds into 6 equal groups: each group gets 1 hundred, and 1 hundred is left over.

Trade the leftover hundred for 10 tens, so there are 14 tens. Each group gets 2 tens, with 2 tens left. Trade those for 20 units, making 24 units, and each group gets 4. Write it: 744 / 6 = 124. Say it: "Every trade keeps the value the same; only the block size changes." Check: 6 x 124 = 744.

### Negative numbers with zero pairs (Pre-Algebra)

Our problem: -7 + 4. Build seven negative units and four positive units. Each positive and negative unit together make zero, so remove four pairs. Three negative units remain.

Write it: -7 + 4 = -3. Say it: "Pairs that make zero do not change the total, so I can take them away." The same picture explains why 4 - 7 is also -3.

### A fraction of a number (Delta and Epsilon)

Our problem: three quarters of 28. Build 28 and split it into 4 equal groups of 7. Three of those groups make 21.

Write it: 28 / 4 = 7, then 7 x 3 = 21. Say it: "The bottom number tells me how many equal groups; the top number tells me how many groups I keep."

### A unit multiplier (Algebra 1)

Our problem: how many hours is 90 minutes? Multiply by a fraction equal to one: 90 minutes x (1 hour / 60 minutes) = 90/60 hours = 1.5 hours.

Say it: "One hour over sixty minutes is worth exactly 1, so multiplying by it changes the units but not the amount, and the minutes cancel."

## How can a live tutor support a Math-U-See lesson?

Math-U-See itself says the curriculum can be used for tutoring and that tutors should become familiar with its core principles. Our classes keep its lesson order and its Build, Write, Say rhythm; the teacher adds questions and fresh problems.

1. Tell us the level, edition and lesson. For Algebra 1 or Algebra 2, say whether it is the Legacy Edition or Principles of Secondary Mathematics.
2. Keep the blocks within reach. On camera, your child builds; the teacher watches the build and asks what each piece stands for.
3. Write together. The teacher asks for every written line to match a move with the blocks, until the blocks are no longer needed.
4. Say it back. Each class ends with your child teaching one idea to the teacher, which is the publisher's own mastery check.
5. Practise with new problems. Our teachers write fresh problems on the same lesson topic, so the Lesson Practice and Systematic Review pages stay yours to use as the book intends.
6. Move on with the book. When the teach-back is clear and the Tests booklet goes well, your child moves to the next lesson in Math-U-See, not in our plan.

## How a Math-U-See student moves from blocks to symbols

Our description of the stages we see as children move through the levels.

**From concrete to abstract (ours)**

| Stage | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 1. Building | Can model the problem with blocks, with help |
| 2. Matching | Writes each step next to the matching build |
| 3. Picturing | Draws the blocks or imagines them instead of using them |
| 4. Explaining | Writes the steps alone and teaches the reason back |

### For parents

Stage 3 is the turning point. A child who can describe what the blocks would show, without touching them, is ready for the symbol-heavy levels.
If the blocks have quietly disappeared before stage 3, bring them back for a week. That is not a step backwards.

### For students

When you are stuck, build it. When you are not stuck, say why.
If you can teach your parent the lesson, you have finished it.

## Math-U-See tutoring: common questions

### Can I get a Math-U-See tutor online?

Yes. Our teachers work live online with students who keep using their Math-U-See books, videos and blocks. Classes follow the lesson your child is on, in a batch of 15 to 20 or one to one.

### How many levels does Math-U-See have?

Demme Learning lists 13 levels: Primer, Alpha, Beta, Gamma, Delta, Epsilon, Zeta, Pre-Algebra, Algebra 1, Geometry, Algebra 2, PreCalculus and Calculus. The letters stand for subjects rather than grades.

### What is the Build, Write, Say method?

It is how Math-U-See presents each concept. The student builds the problem with manipulatives, writes the step-by-step solution, and says why it works. Math-U-See uses the same three parts as its mastery check, asking the child to teach the idea back.

### Does Math-U-See prepare students for college?

Math-U-See runs through Algebra 2, PreCalculus and Calculus, and Demme Learning says its Principles of Secondary Mathematics edition of Algebra 1 covers nationally expected topics. Whether a particular student is ready for college math depends on what they have mastered, which a placement check or an honest teach-back shows better than a level name.

### Where can I find Math-U-See extra practice?

Demme Learning points families to the Systematic Review pages, the Application and Enrichment worksheets and its Digital Toolbox. Our teachers also write fresh problems on the same topic so the book's own pages stay available for your child.

### Why is my child struggling with Math-U-See Pre-Algebra?

In our experience it is usually the move from building with blocks to working in symbols, together with negative numbers. Keep the blocks for zero pairs, and ask your child to say why each step is allowed even when the blocks are put away.
