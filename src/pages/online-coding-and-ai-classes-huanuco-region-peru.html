Equipment

How many Huánuco homes have a computer and internet?

Fewer than in most of Peru. For 2025 the national ENAHO survey found a computer in under one Huánuco home in five, about half the national share, while internet use has climbed steadily.

What changed and what did not

Home internet in Huánuco went from 9.3 per cent of homes in 2015 to 33.3 per cent in 2025, while the proportion of residents from age six upwards who use the internet anywhere climbed from 23.7 to 69.8 per cent. That is real progress.

Computers did not follow. In 2015 and in 2025 alike, fewer than one home in five had one. Most people in Huánuco who are online are online through a phone.

Our honest advice

Children of six to eight can start on a tablet with blocks. From about nine, learning to program properly needs a keyboard and a screen big enough to see code and the class together.

If you have no computer at home yet, tell us in the free class. Families often borrow a laptop for the hour, or use one at a relative's home or a parent's workplace, and one-to-one plans can be timed to when that machine is free.

In school

Is programming part of school in Huánuco?

No. Huánuco's colegios teach the national CNEB curriculum, in which digital skill is everyone's job and no teacher's subject: programming has no slot of its own.

The national framework

Under Law 28044 a pupil spends six years in primaria, then five in secundaria. MINEDU places pupils by their age on 31 March. Technology appears in the CNEB only as competency 28, a transversal competency that all areas share.

Under the regular day, secundaria has 35 weekly hours, with four for Matemática and two for Educación para el Trabajo. There is no programming area.

National coding schemes so far

MINEDU's Fábrica de Programadores (July 2025) took a first thousand 4° and 5° de secundaria pupils, every one of them from Lima Norte, and taught them Python with the PCEP exam as the goal. Huánuco pupils were not in that first group.

That is why our Python with PCEP course appears among the picks above: it gives a Huánuco teenager a similar goal, independently and in English.

After secundaria

What free options do young people in Huánuco have after secundaria?

More than many families realise. The DRE Huánuco has reported both free public technical training and a free regional pre-university academy. Our classes do not replace either; they add programming and English on top.

Public technology institutes

In April 2025 the DRE Huánuco said that ten public higher technological institutes (IEST) in the department were admitting students for 2025-I, offering 1,060 places. It described the programmes as lasting three years and charging no monthly fees, with an admission exam fee paid separately.

Applicants need to have finished secundaria and present their documents to each institute. Dates vary by institute, so families should check with the institute directly.

A regional pre-university academy

In January 2025 the DRE Huánuco reported that about 1,200 young people sat the entrance test for "Otro Level", a free pre-university academy run by the Gobierno Regional Huánuco for students of limited means who had finished secundaria. 815 were admitted for two months of preparation.

Admission exams are in Spanish and run by each university or institute. We never promise a place; we can strengthen maths reasoning and add skills a technical or university course will later expect.

  1. Finish secundaria. Every public route above requires it.
  2. Choose a direction. A three-year technical programme at an IEST, or a university course after preparation.
  3. Add programming early. A teenager who already writes Python arrives at either route with a head start.
  4. Add English. Programming documentation, error messages and most online courses are in English; learning code in English builds both at once.
  5. Keep a portfolio. Small projects, such as the inventory analysis below, show what a learner can do better than a list of courses.

A Huánuco data project

Why does the regional government seem to buy everything in December?

The Gobierno Regional Huánuco publishes its inventory of movable assets as open data: chairs, desks, monitors, printers and much more, each with a date. A first count suggests an extraordinary December. Looking closer separates a real year-end rush from a placeholder date.

The dataset

"INVENTARIO DE BIENES MUEBLES DEL GOBIERNO REGIONAL DE HUANUCO" is published on datosabiertos.gob.pe with an ODC-By (attribution) licence: reuse is allowed, by businesses too, so long as the regional government is credited. It was created on 26 May 2024; its description says the data comes from the final inventory report at the close of the previous financial year, and every row has a cut-off date of 31 December 2023.

Our copy, inventario_gorehco.csv, has 10,291 items. The commonest are chairs (SILLA, 1,787), monitors (770) and desks (767, plus 118 more spelled Escritorio). Each item has a PECOSA number and date: the stores-issue voucher that records the item, as the publisher defines it.

The question

In which month of the year are most items recorded? Public bodies are often said to spend their remaining budget at the end of the year, so a December peak would not be surprising. The first program simply counts PECOSA dates by month.

We never print the values in soles from this file. Counting items and dates is enough for the lesson.

December holds 4,019 of the 10,291 items, more than three times any other month. It is tempting to write "the regional government buys 39 per cent of its assets in December" and stop. But a month is a big bucket. A real buying rush spreads over many days and many vouchers; a date typed in when nobody knew the real one piles everything onto a single day.

Version 2 looks year by year. For each year it finds the single most common date and asks what share of that year's items sit on it. If one date holds at least 80 per cent of a year with ten or more items, the program prints the year, how many distinct dates it has, and the most common voucher number on that date.

Two very different stories come out. In 1997 every one of 326 items has the same date, 31 December 1997, and the same voucher text, TAS 1997; fifteen of the 17 items from 1998 carry that same voucher text on 31 December 1998. One label on one date for hundreds of different objects looks like a bulk entry of older assets, not a day of shopping. We do not know what TAS stands for, and we do not guess.

The 2005 pile is different. 228 items fall on 30 December 2005, but under 102 different voucher numbers, the most common holding only 49 items. Many separate documents on one day is what a genuine year-end rush looks like. Same shape in Version 1, opposite meaning in Version 2.

Data credit: Gobierno Regional Huánuco, movable-asset inventory at 31 December 2023, reused under ODC-By. Counts and readings are ours; the regional government has not confirmed them. In the whole file, 1,317 items are dated 31 December of some year.

  1. Under 8, with a calendar. If everyone in a class wrote "1 January" as their birthday, would that mean they were all born on the same day? What might have happened?
  2. Scratch, roughly 8 to 10. Build a list of ten birthdays where six are the same day. Write a loop that counts each day and shows the biggest pile.
  3. New Python coders, 10 to 12. Try Version 1, then alter it to count by exact date rather than by month, and print the five biggest dates.
  4. Ages 13 and over. Drop the 1997 and 1998 TAS rows and count December again. Is December still the biggest month, and by how much?
  5. Going further. Group items by the first word of their description after making it upper case, so that Escritorio and ESCRITORIO join. Which kinds of items cluster on 30 and 31 December?

Olympiads

Can Huánuco pupils take part in the ONEM?

Yes. The olympiad starts in each school, so a pupil signs up there. We are an independent tutor with no link to MINEDU, the DRE Huánuco or the contest.

The 2026 olympiad

The ONEM's school stage in 2026 was held across Peru on Wednesday 8 July 2026, in three levels by secundaria year; more than 1.7 million pupils took part, according to El Peruano.

We never use official ONEM problems. Our practice sets are written by us.

A practice problem of ours

Every pupil in a class writes down a birthday. If at least one date must be shared, what is the smallest class size that guarantees it in a year of 365 days? And how many pupils guarantee that some date is shared by three? (Ours. Answers: 366, and 731, because 365 times 2 plus 1 is 731.)

It is the pigeonhole idea behind spotting a suspicious pile of identical dates.

What time would classes be in Huánuco?

At a time your family chooses. Huánuco follows Peru's single time zone, and the mentor in India builds the day around the booked hour.

America/Lima in the tz database is UTC minus five and has kept no summer time since 1994; INACAL is the authority for Peru's legal hour. With India at UTC plus 5:30, Huánuco trails Indian time by 10 hours 30 minutes throughout the year.

As neither country changes its clocks, a booking keeps the same local hour all year. Families planning to borrow a computer for the class often choose the hour when it is reliably free, and keep that slot.

Coding and AI classes in Huánuco: questions and answers

Are the classes in Spanish?

No. Every class is live, online and taught in English only. Mentors speak slowly and clearly, adapt to each learner's pace and accent, and respond to Spanish answers with the English words.

Can my child join from Tingo María, Ambo or the highlands?

Yes. Classes are online, so Tingo María, Ambo, Llata, La Unión or Huánuco city all join the same way, given a connection and a suitable device.

We do not have a computer. Can we still start?

A young child can begin block coding on a tablet. From about nine a laptop or desktop is needed. Many families borrow one for the class hour, and one-to-one lessons can be timed around it.

What time are the classes?

Any day, any time, flexible: choose a batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at the hour that suits your family. India is ten and a half hours ahead, so 09:00 in Huánuco is 19:30 for the mentor.

Do schools in Huánuco teach programming?

Not as a subject. The national curriculum spreads digital skills across every area, and secundaria's regular timetable has no programming hours.

Will this help with an IEST or university?

It adds programming, maths reasoning and English, which help on any technical path. Admission is decided by each institute or university, in Spanish, and we never promise a place.

What is the PCEP?

An entry-level Python certification exam. MINEDU's Fábrica de Programadores lets its graduates take it. Our Python with PCEP and PCAP course prepares teenagers and adults for it independently; we are not linked to MINEDU.

Can adults in Huánuco take classes?

Yes, in adult groups or one to one. Many begin with Python or MySQL to handle records at work, or with AI tools.

How are fees paid?

Monthly, by card, in US dollars; your bank converts to soles. Visitors from India see rupees. The first class is free and needs no card.

Is there a certificate?

Courses that include one end with a Modern Age Coders certificate of completion. It is our own record, not a Peruvian qualification, and is not issued or endorsed by MINEDU, the DRE Huánuco or any school.
