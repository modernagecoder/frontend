### Reading the list like an admissions officer would

Four of the six courses are fixed: Advanced Functions (MHF4U), Calculus and Vectors (MCV4U), a Grade 12 university English, and one more university-level course of the student&rsquo;s choosing. The last two slots may be U or M courses. An M course is a university/college preparation course, which is why a course such as Grade 12 computer engineering technology can count while an open course cannot.

Waterloo&rsquo;s admissions FAQ settles the open courses directly: &ldquo;only U and M courses are included in the overall admission average.&rdquo; College-preparation and co-op courses are left out of that average as well, the same answer says.

### What the list leaves out

No Grade 12 science appears on Waterloo&rsquo;s Ontario list for Computer Science, and no computer science course is required. A student can meet the four named requirements without ever taking ICS3U or ICS4U, although Waterloo recommends computer science, as the next section shows.

The list also says nothing about Grade 11 math. Advanced Functions and Calculus and Vectors both build on Grade 11 Functions in the Ontario sequence, so in practice the math plan starts a year before the requirement is visible on paper. That planning point is ours, not Waterloo&rsquo;s.

The average is the line families ask about first, so it is worth reading slowly. Waterloo does not publish a cut-off for Computer Science. It publishes a range, a method and a warning.

| Question | Waterloo&rsquo;s words | Page |
|---|---|---|
| What is the range? | &ldquo;Individual selection from the low to mid-90s&rdquo; | Requirements tool, every province |
| Is that a cut-off? | Programs listed as individual selection use grades with supplementary forms or interviews, so &ldquo;there are no absolute cut-off grades to be admitted to those programs.&rdquo; | What does individual selection mean? |
| What else counts? | &ldquo;The Faculty of Mathematics evaluates students based upon both grades and supplementary information forms.&rdquo; | What does individual selection mean? |
| Will the range hold? | &ldquo;Admission averages depend on the number of applications received and the number of spaces available. The range listed above is based on previous years.&rdquo; | Requirements tool, Ontario |
| Which courses go in? | &ldquo;we will always include the courses which are required for admission in your admission average. We will then use your remaining courses with the highest grades to calculate your overall admission average.&rdquo; | Admissions FAQ |
| Are Grade 11 marks used? | Grade 11 marks and &ldquo;mid-term or predicted grades&rdquo; for Grade 12 courses can be used to make a conditional offer | Admissions FAQ |
| When are decisions made? | &ldquo;most of our admission decisions are made in May once we receive mid-term marks from semestered high schools.&rdquo; | Admissions FAQ |

A worked example, ours. The FAQ&rsquo;s method is precise enough to write as a function, and doing that is a good exercise for a student who wants to study computer science: turn a written rule into code, then test the edges. The plan below is invented. The output is arithmetic on invented marks. It says nothing about any real applicant&rsquo;s chances, because Waterloo reads the Admission Information Form alongside grades and sets no cut-off.

1. Keep only courses that can count. U and M courses stay; the open course is dropped, however high its mark.
2. Take the required courses first. Advanced Functions, Calculus and Vectors and the English course go in whatever their marks are.
3. Fill the slot for one other U course. Take the highest remaining U course. An M course cannot fill this slot.
4. Fill the last two slots by mark. The highest remaining U or M marks complete the six.
5. Average the six. Add them and divide by six. Round only at the end.
6. Test what breaks it. A missing required course, no other U course, and fewer than six courses should each return a reason, not a number.

**Python · six-course average (ours)** (python)
```python
# Six-course average, following the method Waterloo's FAQ describes (ours)
# Required for Computer Science (Ontario): MHF4U, MCV4U, a Grade 12 U English,
# and one other Grade 12 U course. Only U and M courses count.

REQUIRED = ["MHF4U", "MCV4U", "ENG4U"]

def six_course_average(courses):
    """courses: list of (code, level, mark). Returns (average, picked) or (None, reason)."""
    counted = [c for c in courses if c[1] in ("U", "M")]       # O, C and others never count
    by_code = {c[0]: c for c in counted}
    missing = [r for r in REQUIRED if r not in by_code]
    if missing:
        return None, "missing required course: " + ", ".join(missing)
    picked = [by_code[r] for r in REQUIRED]
    rest = sorted((c for c in counted if c[0] not in REQUIRED),
                  key=lambda c: c[2], reverse=True)
    other_u = [c for c in rest if c[1] == "U"]
    if not other_u:
        return None, "no other Grade 12 U course"
    picked.append(other_u[0])                                  # the best other U course
    rest.remove(other_u[0])
    picked += rest[:6 - len(picked)]                           # then the highest remaining marks
    if len(picked) < 6:
        return None, "fewer than six U or M courses"
    average = sum(c[2] for c in picked) / 6
    return round(average, 1), picked


plan = [
    ("MHF4U", "U", 91), ("MCV4U", "U", 88), ("ENG4U", "U", 84),
    ("ICS4U", "U", 95), ("SPH4U", "U", 86), ("SCH4U", "U", 79),
    ("MDM4U", "U", 90), ("TEJ4M", "M", 97), ("PAF4O", "O", 99),
]
average, picked = six_course_average(plan)
for code, level, mark in picked:
    print(f"{code} ({level}) {mark}")
print("Six-course average:", average)
```
Output: MHF4U (U) 91, MCV4U (U) 88, ENG4U (U) 84, ICS4U (U) 95, TEJ4M (M) 97, MDM4U (U) 90, then Six-course average: 90.8. The 99 in the open course and the 79 in chemistry never enter the calculation.

**Our edge cases for six_course_average (ours; each run in Python 3.13)**

| Input | What it returns | Why the case matters |
|---|---|---|
| MHF4U, MCV4U, ENG4U, TEJ4M (M) and a college course | no other Grade 12 U course | The fourth required course must be U level; M cannot stand in |
| MHF4U and ENG4U only | missing required course: MCV4U | A required course with no mark cannot be averaged around |
| The four required U courses and one M course | fewer than six U or M courses | Six is the minimum the requirements page states |

### What the example shows

The open course with the highest mark on the transcript does nothing for the average. The chemistry mark of 79 also drops out, because three higher U or M marks were available. A student who understands the method plans the Grade 12 timetable around it, and does not mistake a high mark in a course that cannot count for progress.

The required courses work the other way: they stay in even when they are a student&rsquo;s weakest marks. Here English at 84 is included while a 97 in an M course competes for a free slot. That is the real reason to start strengthening the required courses early.

### What the example cannot show

Waterloo describes Computer Science admission as individual selection, using grades together with the Admission Information Form. A function can reproduce the arithmetic of an average. It cannot reproduce a reading of a form, and it would be dishonest to pretend otherwise.

So we never compare a student&rsquo;s figure against the published range, and nobody on our team tells a family what a given average will do. Waterloo itself says the range is based on previous years and moves with applications and spaces.

Sources: [What does individual selection mean?](https://uwaterloo.ca/future-students/admissions/individual-selection), [Commonly asked admission questions](https://uwaterloo.ca/future-students/admissions/faqs-admissions) and the Ontario requirements page, read 22 October 2026. The function, the plan and its marks are ours.

## ICS3U and ICS4U: recommended, and not required

This is the line most often misquoted. Waterloo recommends high school computer science for Computer Science applicants, and in the same breath says leaving it out does not hurt.

**Waterloo on high school computer science, two pages (read 22 October 2026)**

| Waterloo page | Exact wording |
|---|---|
| Computer Science program page | Under the heading for a recommended course: &ldquo;Grade 11 U Introduction to Computer Science&rdquo; |
| Requirements for Ontario high school students | &ldquo;Grade 11 U Introduction to Computer Science and/or Grade 12 U Computer Science are helpful background for your studies; however, it will not impact your likelihood of admission if you do not take these courses.&rdquo; |
| The same tool, other provinces | The province&rsquo;s computer science course is described as helpful background that &ldquo;will not impact your likelihood of admission if you choose not to take the course.&rdquo; |

### Why take it anyway

Waterloo&rsquo;s own phrase is &ldquo;helpful background for your studies&rdquo;, and the first-year schedule explains why. A student in the fall term meets CS 135, Designing Functional Programs, while also taking two first-year math courses and a communication course. Arriving already able to break a problem into steps and test a program frees attention for the new ideas.

The program page is also frank that beginners are welcome: &ldquo;You don&rsquo;t need a background in computer science to get started here.&rdquo; Both statements are true at once. The background helps with the work; its absence does not count against the application.

### If the school does not offer it

Not every school runs ICS3U and ICS4U every year. Waterloo&rsquo;s wording means a student without those courses is not at a disadvantage for admission, and can still build the same habits outside school: writing programs from a blank file, tracing them by hand, testing edge cases.

That is the part we teach. A student who learns to reason about code, rather than copy it or paste it from an AI tool, arrives in first year with something no transcript line measures. Our [ICS3U help page](/ics3u-computer-science-help) walks through what the Ontario course itself covers.

## The Admission Information Form, in brief

Grades are only half of the file. For Computer Science, Waterloo requires an extra form and uses a second one for contest results.

Required for admission. Waterloo&rsquo;s AIF page gives February 15, 2027 for &ldquo;Programs other than those listed in the next rows&rdquo;; none of those rows names Computer Science, so that date applies unless the student also applied to Engineering.

### Supplementary Information Form

For Faculty of Mathematics applicants. Waterloo says it covers the Waterloo math contests a student has written, naming the Euclid and the CSMC.

Anyone who also applies to Engineering has to meet the earlier Engineering date, February 1, 2027, the AIF page says.

What we can do is teach the work that later becomes something true to write about, such as a project the student built and can explain line by line.

## What first year in Waterloo Computer Science looks like

The first-year timetable explains the requirements better than any average does. Half of it is mathematics.

### Why the math requirement is real

Two of the five fall courses are honours mathematics, and both continue into winter. Calculus and Vectors is the doorway to MATH 137, and Advanced Functions sits under all of it. A student who scraped through Grade 12 math by memorising procedures will feel that gap in October of first year.

Computer Science at Waterloo is in the Faculty of Mathematics, and the degree is listed as a Bachelor of Computer Science or a Bachelor of Mathematics in Computer Science. The program is offered as co-op and as a regular program.

### What this means in Grade 11 and 12

We teach Advanced Functions and Calculus and Vectors for understanding: why a rule works, where it fails and how to check an answer without a calculator. That is slower in the first week and much faster by the exam, and it is the kind of math first year assumes.

On the programming side, CS 135 opens with functional programs, which surprises students who have only written loops. Students who can already reason about what a function returns, and test it, adapt quickly whatever the language.

## A Grade 9 to 12 plan for a student aiming at university computer science

This plan is ours, written for Ontario students and adaptable to other provinces. It builds skills that matter whatever the admission decision, and it does not promise one.

1. Grade 9 and 10: protect the math sequence. Keep the path open to Grade 11 Functions, and treat every algebra unit as groundwork for Advanced Functions.
2. Grade 10: write real programs. Small projects from a blank file, in Python or Java. The aim is to explain every line, not to finish fast.
3. Grade 11: take Functions seriously, and ICS3U if the school offers it. Waterloo calls computer science helpful background; Functions is the course every later requirement stands on.
4. Grade 11 spring: try a contest through the school. Ask a teacher about the CCC or a CEMC math contest. Registration is the school&rsquo;s job, so ask months ahead.
5. Summer before Grade 12: preview Advanced Functions. A second look at polynomials, logarithms and trigonometry makes the school course a review rather than a first encounter.
6. Grade 12 fall: plan the six courses on paper. List the four required courses, then the U and M courses that could fill the last two slots. Run the method from the section above on your own expected marks if it helps you see the structure.
7. Grade 12 fall: apply, then write the AIF yourself. Use Waterloo&rsquo;s own questions and limits. Nobody else writes it.
8. Grade 12 winter: keep the required marks steady. Waterloo says most decisions are made in May, once mid-term marks arrive, so the second semester counts.

### Summer school, night school and repeats

Waterloo&rsquo;s page on repeated courses says that for September 2027 entry, a student who repeats a course &ldquo;will not be penalized in the application process&rdquo;, and that &ldquo;we&rsquo;ll use your highest grade&rdquo; in that course. It accepts day school, summer school, night school, online and private school courses, if the province accredits them.

The same page still recommends &ldquo;taking all required Grade 12 courses during the regular school year and doing well on your first attempt.&rdquo; Both statements stand side by side, and families should weigh them together.

## Four stages from strong student to ready for first-year CS

Our stages, drawn from teaching Grade 11 and 12 students who later started university computer science. They describe skills, not admission odds.

**Stages of readiness for first-year computer science (ours)**

| Stage | How it shows up in class |
|---|---|
| 1. Procedure follower | Gets calculus questions right when they look like the textbook, and stalls when the wording changes |
| 2. Reasoner | Explains why a rule works and checks answers another way, by sketch, estimate or substitution |
| 3. Builder | Writes a program from a blank file, tests the edge cases and fixes their own errors |
| 4. Ready for CS 135 | Moves between a math idea and code that tests it, and learns a new language without fear |

### For parents

Marks tell you a student&rsquo;s stage only roughly. Ask your teen to explain one calculus answer without the textbook open; the explanation tells you more.
The jump from stage 1 to stage 2 is where most of our Grade 12 math teaching goes, and it is also what first-year honours math demands.

### For students

Check every answer a second way before you call it done. Slow at first, it soon becomes the fastest habit you have.
Write one small program a week that tests a math idea, such as a sequence, a limit or a vector sum. Both subjects improve together.

## Waterloo Computer Science admission: common questions

### What courses does Waterloo require for Computer Science?

For Ontario students, Waterloo's requirements page read on 22 October 2026 listed Advanced Functions 4U, Calculus and Vectors 4U, a Grade 12 university English and one more Grade 12 U course, inside a minimum of six Grade 12 U or M courses. The Admission Information Form is also required. Other provinces have their own lists in Waterloo's requirements tool.

### What average does Waterloo publish for Computer Science?

Waterloo lists the admission average as individual selection from the low to mid-90s. It also says there are no absolute cut-off grades for individual selection programs, that averages depend on applications and spaces, and that the range is based on previous years. We do not predict any applicant's chances.

### Is ICS3U required for Waterloo Computer Science?

No. Waterloo's program page names Grade 11 U Introduction to Computer Science under its recommended-course heading, and its Ontario requirements page says ICS3U and ICS4U are helpful background but will not impact the likelihood of admission if a student does not take them.

### When is the Waterloo AIF due for Computer Science?

Waterloo's AIF page, read on 22 October 2026, gave February 15, 2027 for programs other than those listed in the next rows of its deadline table, and none of those rows names Computer Science or the Faculty of Mathematics. A student applying to Engineering as well must submit by the Engineering deadline, February 1, 2027.

### Do I have to write the Euclid or the CSMC?

Waterloo says the CSMC and Euclid are not required for admission, but writing one is required for some Faculty of Mathematics scholarships. Its FAQ adds that contest results are used in scholarship decisions and possibly admission decisions. A teacher at the student's school must register them; tutoring schools are not eligible.

### Does Waterloo look at Grade 11 marks?

Waterloo's FAQ says it can use Grade 11 marks with mid-term or predicted Grade 12 grades to make a conditional offer, and that most admission decisions are made in May once mid-term marks arrive. The final offer conditions list the grades a student must still reach.

### Can a required course be taken in summer school or online?

Waterloo's repeated courses page says that for September 2027 entry it accepts accredited day, summer, night, online and private school courses without penalty, and uses the highest grade for a repeated course. It still recommends taking required Grade 12 courses in the regular school year.

### What does Waterloo require from students in British Columbia or Alberta?

For British Columbia, Waterloo listed English Studies 12 or English First Peoples 12, Pre-Calculus 12, and Calculus 12 or AP Calculus. For Alberta it listed Mathematics 30-1, Mathematics 31 and English Language Arts 30-1. Both pages also require the Admission Information Form.

### Will you help write my Waterloo application or AIF?

No. Waterloo requires students to complete the AIF themselves, and no one on our team writes or polishes application text. We teach math and programming, and help students build projects they understand well enough to describe honestly themselves.

### Can you guarantee admission to Waterloo?

No one honestly can, and we do not. Waterloo selects Computer Science students individually using grades and supplementary forms. Our classes build real skill in Grade 12 math and programming, which helps in first year whatever the decision.

### When are the live classes held?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. We set each time in your own zone, Eastern time for most of Ontario, and check it again whenever the clocks change.
