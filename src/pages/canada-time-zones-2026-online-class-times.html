Canada time zones in 2026 and online class times

Until this year a Canadian family could assume two clock changes, one in March and one in November, almost everywhere except Saskatchewan and Yukon. That is no longer true. British Columbia made its last change on 8 March 2026 and now stays on UTC-7. Alberta's new Official Time Act fixes the province at six hours behind UTC. Saskatchewan has passed a new Time Act at UTC-6, and Manitoba stops at UTC-5 from 31 October 2026. Ontario, Quebec, the Atlantic provinces and Newfoundland still change. For anyone booking a live online class across provinces, the gap between two cities now depends on the season in a new way. This page quotes each rule from the government's own text and then shows, in tables, what one class time looks like in every zone.

In their own words

The four provincial rules, quoted

Each line below is copied from the government document named under it. Where a rule's start depends on a step we could not confirm, we say so.

British Columbia

The province's release of 2 March 2026 says "B.C. will adopt permanent, year-round daylight saving time after clocks shift forward an hour on Sunday, March 8, 2026" and "This will be the final time change in British Columbia."

On the offset it is exact: "Pacific time will be set seven hours behind co-ordinated universal time (UTC-7), matching the current offset used during daylight saving time." So BC families will not move their clocks this autumn, and will not in March 2027 either.

Alberta

The Official Time Act, in the consolidation "Current as of June 18, 2026", defines official time as the time that "means the time 6 hours behind Coordinated Universal Time".

It adds: "No person shall use or observe within Alberta any time other than official time." Alberta's summer offset was already UTC-6, so the visible change comes on 1 November 2026, when Alberta clocks do not fall back.

Saskatchewan

Bill 58, The Time Act, 2026, defines central standard time as one that "means UTC-6 hours", says "the time to be observed in Saskatchewan is central standard time", and says Saskatchewan "includes the City of Lloydminster".

The bill also says "This Act comes into force by order of the Lieutenant Governor in Council." We could not confirm that the order has been made. In practice the clock is the same either way: Saskatchewan has not changed its clocks in decades, and Alberta now sits at UTC-6 too.

Manitoba

Manitoba's release of 17 September 2026 says clocks "will not revert to standard time on Nov. 1". The proclamation then sets the date in law: "we name October 31, 2026, as the day on which The Official Time Amendment Act (S.M. 2023, c. 4) comes into force".

That Act defines Manitoba Standard Time as "five hours behind Coordinated Universal Time (UTC)", and adds "The official time may be referred to as "MBT"." The release also notes Manitoba "joins several other provinces and territories that have adopted or are moving toward a permanent time including British Columbia, Yukon, Northwest Territories, Alberta and Saskatchewan."

Sources: the four government documents named above, and the Manitoba proclamation, read 22 October 2026. We quote only the timekeeping lines; the releases' other reasoning is outside this page's purpose.

Why no single slot suits everyone

Read across one row of the table and the problem is plain. A 4:30 pm Eastern start lands at 2:30 pm in Vancouver in winter and 1:30 pm in summer, inside the school day. A 7:00 pm Eastern start is 8:30 pm in St. John's, late for a younger child.

That is why we do not publish one national batch time. A family picks a time that works in its own province, and the batch or the one-to-one slot is built around that.

Who moves, and who stays

From November 2026, families in BC, Alberta, Saskatchewan, Manitoba, Yukon and the NWT keep the same clock all year. If their class is set in their own local time, nothing moves for them on 14 March 2027.

What moves is everyone else's clock relative to them. A class fixed in Toronto time arrives an hour earlier in Calgary, Regina and Winnipeg when Eastern time springs forward. Fixing each class in the family's own time avoids that surprise.

Our converter

A short program that converts one class time into every zone

A worked example in Python, written for this page. It uses fixed offsets typed in from the laws above rather than a device's built-in time zone rules, and that choice is the lesson.

Why fixed offsets

Python's standard library can look zones up by name, such as America/Vancouver, but it reads those rules from whatever time zone data the computer has. Data older than the 2026 changes would still move BC, Alberta or Manitoba in November.

Typing the offsets in, with a comment saying where each came from, makes the program say exactly what the law says. The cost is that someone must update it if a rule changes again.

What a student learns from it

A time written without a zone is not a time; it is half of one. The program refuses to work with a bare "7:00 pm": every datetime gets a zone before it is converted.

Try it: change the Eastern start to 4:30 pm and check the output against our table above. Then add a row for Newfoundland in summer and predict it before you run it.

How we schedule

Our routine for booking a class across provinces

Five steps we follow with every Canadian family, so the class arrives when everyone expects it.

  1. We ask for your town, not only your province. That catches the exceptions above, from Golden to Lloydminster, before the first class.
  2. We agree the time in your clock. The confirmation names your local time and zone, for example "5:00 pm Alberta time (UTC-6)" for a family in Calgary.
  3. We send a calendar invitation that carries the zone. A calendar converts it for each device; a time typed into a chat message does not.
  4. We re-confirm around 1 November 2026 and 14 March 2027. Those are the two dates when Eastern, Atlantic and Newfoundland clocks move, and the dates when a family in two provinces can be caught out.
  5. We check your device if a time looks wrong. A phone or laptop that has not updated its time zone data can still show an old offset for BC, Alberta or Manitoba. Updating the operating system usually fixes it.

Families split across provinces

A parent in Toronto booking for a child in Winnipeg sees the same clock all winter and a one-hour gap from March. We write both local times on the confirmation, so neither has to work it out.

The same applies to a one-to-one slot shared between, say, Vancouver and Halifax: a three-hour gap all winter, widening to four hours from 14 March 2027.

Contests and exams

Canadian contests and exams set their own timing rules. The national computing contest, for one, is written at each school near the start of its own school day, not at a single national moment.

For anything with an official start time, we work from the organiser's published time and its zone, then convert, never the other way round.

Questions from Canadian families

Canada's 2026 time changes: common questions

Does BC change its clocks in November 2026?

No. BC made its last change on Sunday, March 8, 2026 and now stays on Pacific time at UTC-7 all year. Clocks in most of BC will not fall back on 1 November 2026 and will not spring forward in March 2027.

Is Manitoba on permanent daylight time?

Yes, in effect. The proclamation names 31 October 2026 as the day Manitoba's Official Time Amendment Act comes into force, and the Act sets Manitoba Standard Time at five hours behind UTC all year. The province has announced that clocks will not revert to standard time on 1 November 2026.

What time zone is Alberta in now?

Alberta's Official Time Act sets official time at six hours behind UTC, all year. That is the same offset Alberta used in summer, so the difference shows from 1 November 2026, when Alberta clocks no longer fall back.

Are Alberta and Saskatchewan on the same time?

Yes, from November 2026 both are at UTC-6 all year. Saskatchewan's Time Act, 2026 sets UTC-6 and includes Lloydminster. We could not confirm the date that Act comes into force, but Saskatchewan already kept one time all year.

When do Ontario and Quebec change their clocks?

Eastern clocks fall back on Sunday 1 November 2026 and spring forward on Sunday 14 March 2027, under the Canadian rule of the first Sunday in November and the second Sunday in March. The Atlantic provinces and Newfoundland change on the same dates.

Is Winnipeg on the same time as Toronto?

In winter, yes: from 1 November 2026 to 14 March 2027 both are at UTC-5. From 14 March 2027 Toronto moves to UTC-4 and Winnipeg stays at UTC-5, so Winnipeg is one hour behind.

What time is it in India compared with Canada?

India time is UTC+5:30 all year. In the Canadian winter it is 10 hours 30 minutes ahead of Toronto and 12 hours 30 minutes ahead of Vancouver. From 14 March 2027 it is 9 hours 30 minutes ahead of Toronto, while the gap to Vancouver stays 12 hours 30 minutes.

When are your online classes for Canadian students?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Every class is confirmed in your own local time.

Will my class time move when the clocks change?

Not if it is set in your own local time and you live in BC, Alberta, Saskatchewan, Manitoba, Yukon or the NWT. Families in Ontario, Quebec and Atlantic Canada move with their own clocks. We re-confirm every class around 1 November 2026 and 14 March 2027.

Why does my phone show the wrong time for a class?

A device that has not updated its time zone data may still apply the old rules for BC, Alberta or Manitoba. Updating the operating system usually fixes it. Our invitations carry the time zone, so a corrected device shows the right local time.
