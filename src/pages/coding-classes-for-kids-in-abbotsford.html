Coding classes for kids in Abbotsford

Can an Abbotsford child aged 6 to 12 learn coding with a live teacher?

Yes. Modern Age Coders runs live online coding lessons for Abbotsford children aged 6 to 12, privately or in a class of fifteen to twenty. Coding for Kids is the usual first course; puzzle lovers often add Problem Solving and Computational Thinking, and from about 9 most move to Python and AI for Kids. The first lesson is free.

British Columbia’s Grade 6 and 7 technology curriculum describes a robot in three words: it senses, it thinks, it acts. The thinking part is code, and it is the part a child can start on long before touching a robot. In Abbotsford, families are also a short drive from the Fraser Valley’s own Math Kangaroo sitting, which gives younger children a real contest to aim at. Our live online classes take children from first blocks to typed Python at a steady pace. Below: what BC’s ADST content says about robots and about judging what we find online, a parks project on the City of Abbotsford’s own data, how BC’s new permanent clock affects class times, and the Kangaroo date for 2027.

Coding at school in Abbotsford

Sense, think, act: BC’s robotics and digital literacy content for Abbotsford children

Abbotsford schools teach the British Columbia curriculum. In Grades 6 and 7, Applied Design, Skills, and Technologies (ADST) is taught through modules, at least three a year, chosen by the school. Two modules from the ADST 6-7 page, read on curriculum.gov.bc.ca on 22 October 2026, say a good deal about coding. Quotes are the curriculum’s; the right column is ours.

RoboticsA robot is “a machine capable of carrying out a complex series of actions automatically”, and the module includes “programming and logic for robotics components”.
Automatically is the key word. We show children that the robot only seems clever because someone wrote every rule it follows, then let them write the rules.
Robotics: senseThe sense parts are “the parts of the robot that allow it to gather information about its environment that guides its behaviour”.
In Scratch, a sprite senses with “touching colour” and key presses. Children build a sprite that stops at a wall, the simplest sense-and-react loop there is.
Robotics: think and actThink is “the part of the robot that determines the robot’s behaviour”; act is “the parts of the robot that do the work”.
We split every project into the same three boxes on paper: what it notices, what it decides, what it does. Most bugs live in the middle box.
Digital LiteracyLearning “how search results are selected and ranked”, judged for “accuracy, timeliness, appropriateness, credibility, and bias”.
Children who have sorted data themselves understand ranking from the inside. Our parks project below sorts and ranks, and we ask who decided what counts as first.

The same ADST page lists visual programming such as Scratch and simple algorithms under its Computational Thinking module, so an Abbotsford child might meet coding through robots, through computational thinking, or not at all in a given year, depending on the modules the school chooses. We have found no primary source for coding in BC Kindergarten to Grade 5 and claim nothing about those grades. Teenagers have Computer Studies 10 and Computer Programming 11 and 12 ahead of them, which our coding classes in British Columbia page explains.

No robot needed

We do not sell kits. Sense, think and act can all be practised on screen first, and children who later join a robotics club arrive knowing how to write the thinking part.

Search with judgement

BC asks children to weigh search results for accuracy and bias. We teach the same habit with code: before trusting an answer, check where it came from and how it was sorted.

Abbotsford data project

Which Abbotsford neighbourhoods have the most park records? A top five from the City’s file

The City of Abbotsford publishes its parks as open data, a file of every park and recreation area with a name, a type and a neighbourhood. Our older kids count records by neighbourhood, sort the counts and print the top five. Every figure below is ours, from the file downloaded on 22 October 2026.

Park records per neighbourhood in the City of Abbotsford Parks file (column DCC Park Neighbourhood), our count, top five of 21 named neighbourhoods
RankNeighbourhood as written in the filePark records
1TOWNLINE HILL21
2UPPER TEN OAKS19
3LOWER SUMAS MOUNTAIN18
4 (tied)MATSQUI PRAIRIE15
4 (tied)WEST CLEARBROOK15

The file has 238 records. 43 have no neighbourhood, and they hide a trap: 36 cells are truly empty, but 7 contain a single space. A program that checks only for empty text counts the spaces as a neighbourhood whose name is a space. Calling strip() first removes the space, so all 43 are counted as blank. Here is the program a 12-year-old writes in our class:import csv counts = {} blank = 0 with open("abbotsford-parks.csv", encoding="utf-8-sig") as f: for park in csv.DictReader(f): hood = park["DCC Park Neighbourhood"].strip() if hood == "": blank += 1 else: counts[hood] = counts.get(hood, 0) + 1 top = sorted(counts.items(), key=lambda pair: pair[1], reverse=True) for hood, n in top[:5]: print(hood, n) print("no neighbourhood:", blank)

Sorting by the count

counts.items() gives pairs such as (TOWNLINE HILL, 21). The key function tells sorted to compare the second item of each pair, the number, and reverse puts the biggest first.

Slicing the top

top[:5] keeps the first five pairs. Two neighbourhoods tie on 15, and children notice that the order between them comes from the file, not from any rule. Ties deserve a mention.

Records are not parks

Count what the file counts. 42 records are named UNDEVELOPED GREEN SPACE and 30 have no name at all, and there are 181 distinct Park ID values among the 238 records, so a record is not always a separate park.

Check the units with a second column

One column is labelled Area (m2), square metres. A second column, Shape__Area, holds the area of the drawn shape. For 218 records the drawn area is about 10,000 times the labelled value, which is what you would see if the labelled numbers were hectares, since one hectare is 10,000 square metres. Our reading is that the label is wrong and the values are hectares; 15 records do not fit the pattern. Comparing one column against another is a habit worth more than any single answer.

By type, the file holds 129 open space records, 64 neighbourhood parks, 25 community parks, 15 regional parks and 5 city-wide parks. Some columns record staff account names, and some park names honour people, so in class we print neighbourhoods, types and counts only. The City describes the layer as a “collection of all known naturally designated parks and recreation areas in the City of Abbotsford”. Fees and sign-up

Kangaroo questions reward the same habits as coding: read carefully, try a small case, look for the pattern, check the answer. We never reproduce contest questions; we write our own and label them ours. One of ours for a Grade 3 child: a kangaroo jumps 3 squares forward and 1 back, again and again; after how many jumps does it first land on square 10? Children act it out, then write a loop that counts the jumps. More on preparation is on Math Kangaroo Canada preparation. BC time

Abbotsford on permanent Pacific time: class times that no longer move

BC’s government release on the 2026 change says: “Pacific time will be set seven hours behind co-ordinated universal time (UTC-7), matching the current offset used during daylight saving time.” Abbotsford’s clocks did not fall back this autumn, and India never changes its clock.

Weekday, 4:00 pm

With Abbotsford at UTC-7 and India at UTC+5:30, India is 12 and a half hours ahead. A 4:00 pm class in Abbotsford is 4:30 am the next day in India, in every month of the year (our arithmetic).

Saturday, 9:30 am

A Saturday 9:30 am class is 10:00 pm the same day in India; an 8:00 am Sunday class is 8:30 pm. Weekend mornings are our easiest slots for BC children.

Abbotsford numbers

Local numbers use 604, with 778, 236, 672 and 257 in the same area. The +1 is already in the form, so type the ten digits.

One knock-on effect: with Ontario back on standard time from 1 November 2026, Abbotsford is two hours behind Toronto through the winter and three hours behind in summer. Families with cousins or classes in the east should check twice. We write every invitation in Pacific time. The full picture for every province is on Canada time zones in 2026.

Coding and AI

With AI everywhere, why should an Abbotsford child learn to code?

Because sense, think and act describes AI systems too, and the think part is where mistakes hide.

A program that skipped strip() would have reported a neighbourhood whose name is a space, and looked sure of itself. An AI system can be just as confident about a wrong answer. Children who have found such a bug with their own eyes learn to ask how an answer was produced before they trust it, which is the digital literacy BC asks for.

We keep adult chatbots out of lessons for children under 13. Instead, teacher-led activities show how AI learns from examples and why it fails. Most of each class is the child’s own thinking: splitting a problem into steps, trying one, and testing it.

BC puts judging search results for accuracy and bias into Grade 6 and 7 technology, and that judgement grows fastest in children who build things themselves. The longer argument is in why coding still pays off for kids.

FAQ

Abbotsford parents ask

The questions Abbotsford families send us most.

Do you have coding classes for kids in Abbotsford?

Yes, taught live online. Your child joins from home, either one to one or in a class of fifteen to twenty, so there is no centre to travel to.

Do Abbotsford schools teach coding?

In Grades 6 and 7, BC schools teach ADST through modules they choose. Robotics and Computational Thinking are among them and both involve programming, but not every child takes them every year.

Is my child too young at 6?

No. Six is a fine age to start with blocks and simple sense-and-react games. Our kids classes continue to age 12.

Does my child need a robot kit?

No. We teach the thinking and coding side on screen. Children who later join a robotics club already know how to program the decisions a robot makes.

When is Math Kangaroo in the Fraser Valley?

The UFV host-centre page lists Sunday 21 March 2027 at the Abbotsford Campus of the University of the Fraser Valley, open to Grades 1 to 12.

What times are classes for Abbotsford?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Times are written in Pacific time.

Will BC time changes affect class times?

Not any more. BC stays on UTC-7 all year from 2026, so an Abbotsford class time converts the same way every month.

Will my child use ChatGPT in class?

No. Children under 13 do not use adult chatbots in our lessons, and anyone under 18 needs a parent's permission to use ChatGPT. We teach young children about AI through teacher-led activities.

Does my child need to type?

Not to start. Younger children use blocks. Typing comes with Python, usually around age 9 or 10.

How can I see the fees?

Use the fees section on this page. It shows the free first lesson and the two monthly options, group and private, in your currency, and nothing is billed before the free lesson.
