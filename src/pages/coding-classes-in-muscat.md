## The Muscat student data project

The Oman Ministry of Education open-data catalogue lists a 2024/2025 dataset of student counts by government school, grade and sex. It also publishes school data by governorate. A student downloads the current files, filters the schools to Muscat and selects one grade cohort.

The ordinary question is how many coding groups are required if every group can hold eight students. A beginner divides the cohort by eight, stores the result as an integer and reports only that number of full groups. The calculation looks clean.

The trap is integer division and the remainder. If the cohort is not exactly divisible by eight, the remainder has vanished from the allocation. Rounding down loses students. Rounding up produces the required capacity, but it still does not explain that the last group is smaller.

A defensible result reports the quotient and remainder, uses ceiling division for the number of groups required and checks that total capacity is at least the cohort. The same bug appears in buses, hotel rooms, shipping cartons, database pages and work rosters.

| Step | What the student does | What it teaches |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Downloads current student and school files and filters to Muscat | Separate datasets need a checked join key |
| 2 | Sums the cohort and reconciles the grouped subtotals | Allocation begins with a verified total |
| 3 | Divides by eight and records quotient and remainder | Integer division has a precise meaning |
| 4 | Compares floor, ceiling and the actual final-group size | Full groups and required capacity differ |
| 5 | Tests zero, one, seven, eight and nine before the real cohort | Boundary cases expose silent loss |

Primary source: [Oman Ministry of Education open-data catalogue](https://home.moe.gov.om/region/opendata/days-day20). Students pull the current files themselves instead of copying a figure from this page.

## The arrangement

The first class is free and doubles as a placement check. Group teaching has five to eight learners. Private teaching has one student and one teacher. Both plans run twice a week for eight live classes each month.

The student uses a laptop or desktop with a microphone and a stable video-capable connection. The teacher is live. Learners write code, explain decisions, test failures and revise projects. The exact recurring slot is agreed before payment.

Oman uses Gulf Standard Time at UTC+4, one and a half hours behind India. Friday and Saturday form the Omani weekend. Weekday evening and weekend feasibility can both be discussed. Ramadan timing is reviewed with the household rather than fixed for every family.
