ALEKS math placement test prep

ALEKS PPL is the math placement assessment many colleges ask new students to complete before registration, often from home. It is short, open response and adaptive: about 25 questions decide which first math course you are allowed to take. It also comes with up to four more attempts and a learning module between them, which most students barely use. This page explains, from McGraw Hill's own pages, how the assessment works and how to use those extra attempts honestly, then works through the kind of exact-answer algebra the format demands, with problems we wrote.

In short

ALEKS PPL (Placement, Preparation and Learning) is McGraw Hill's math placement assessment. What ALEKS PPL is

Why so few questions can place you

ALEKS is built on Knowledge Space Theory. McGraw Hill's About ALEKS page says it identifies what a student has mastered "based on their answers to a small number of questions chosen by ALEKS based on their responses to all previous questions."

Topics depend on one another, so a correct answer on a harder topic is evidence about the easier topics beneath it. That is how 25 questions can stand in for 314 topics.

What that means when you study

A weak foundation shows up everywhere. If fraction arithmetic or exponent rules are shaky, ALEKS will keep probing the topics built on them, and the result drops more than one topic's worth.

The reverse is also true: fixing a prerequisite such as factoring can lift several topics at once on the next attempt.

Open response

Is the ALEKS placement test multiple choice?

No. That single fact changes how you should prepare. McGraw Hill's About ALEKS page puts it bluntly: "ALEKS Avoids Multiple Choice."

No answer choices to test

On a multiple-choice placement test you can often plug the options back into an equation and find the right one without solving it. On ALEKS PPL there are no options. You must produce the answer yourself.

McGraw Hill says ALEKS replaces "multiple guess with authentic problems that require the student to understand, learn, and master the topic."

Exact form matters

Open response means the answer must be in a form the system accepts: a reduced fraction, a simplified radical, an expression with factors cancelled, an interval written correctly.

Students who get the math right but leave 6/8 unreduced, or write a decimal where an exact root is asked, lose credit for understanding they actually have. Practising exact form is part of the math, not a trick.

Before the real assessment, the student should get comfortable with ALEKS's own answer tools. The About ALEKS page describes "ingenious student input tools" for entering math; a few minutes with them in the first attempt, or in the learning module, saves lost marks for typing, not knowledge.

Five attempts and the module

Can you retake the ALEKS math placement test?

McGraw Hill describes up to five assessment attempts in total, with a Prep and Learning Module to work in between them. Your college can set its own rules on top, so ask.

What McGraw Hill describes

Its K-12 page says that after the first Placement Assessment students "can compare results with their college goals and take action to prepare for a higher-level course", and that "ALEKS PPL allows for four additional placement assessments so students can track their progress."

The module emphasizes "skills needed for success in college-level math" and is available "for up to 12 months."

What colleges add

Colleges decide which ALEKS score places a student into which course, and a college may layer its own conditions on top, such as a wait between attempts, time in the module before a retake, or a deadline tied to orientation.

Any such rule is the college's, not McGraw Hill's, so it can differ from one campus to the next. Read your college's ALEKS page before your first attempt, not after.

  1. Take the first attempt honestly, under your college's conditions. Its job is to show what you know today, so the learning module can target the right topics.
  2. Compare your result with your program's required course. Find the score your college needs for the course your major starts with.
  3. Work in the Prep and Learning Module on the topics it assigns. Short daily sessions beat a single long weekend.
  4. Fix prerequisites first. Fractions, exponent rules and factoring sit under many later topics, so they move the result most.
  5. Reassess only when the module shows real progress. You have a limited number of attempts; spend each one when you are ready.

Proctoring and honesty

Is the ALEKS placement assessment proctored?

Often, yes. McGraw Hill offers colleges remote proctoring and a lockdown browser, and the college chooses which to use.

The tools McGraw Hill describes

Its Assessment Integrity page says McGraw Hill partners with Meazure Learning to offer remote proctoring through the ProctorU Platform, with two service lines, Review+ and Live+.

It also integrates Respondus LockDown Browser, which "prevents students from accessing other applications, copying, printing or visiting other websites during an ALEKS PPL Placement Assessment", and Respondus Monitor, which records the student by webcam.

Why we would not help anyone get around it

A placement score is only useful if it is yours. A student placed above their real level into calculus or college algebra usually struggles in the first weeks, and the course grade lasts much longer than the placement.

So we teach the math before and between attempts. We never sit, join or assist with a placement assessment while it is in progress, and we tell students to follow their college's rules exactly.

Source: ALEKS PPL Assessment Integrity, McGraw Hill, read 31 October 2026. ProctorU, Meazure Learning and Respondus are trademarks of their owners.

Worked examples

Three placement-level problems with exact answers

Our own problems at the level ALEKS PPL reaches between algebra and precalculus. None is an ALEKS item.

Worked example 1 (ours): simplify (x^2 - 9) / (x^2 + x - 6)
StepReasoning
1. Factor the topx^2 - 9 is a difference of squares: (x - 3)(x + 3)
2. Factor the bottomx^2 + x - 6: two numbers that multiply to -6 and add to 1 are 3 and -2, so (x + 3)(x - 2)
3. Cancel the common factor(x - 3)(x + 3) / ((x + 3)(x - 2)) = (x - 3)/(x - 2)
4. Note the restrictionx cannot be -3 or 2, because the original denominator would be zero
5. Check with a numberx = 0: original gives -9/-6 = 3/2; simplified gives -3/-2 = 3/2
Worked example 2 (ours): solve x^2 - 4x - 1 = 0 and give exact answers
StepReasoning
1. Try factoringNo two integers multiply to -1 and add to -4, so use another method
2. Complete the squarex^2 - 4x = 1, then x^2 - 4x + 4 = 5, so (x - 2)^2 = 5
3. Take both rootsx - 2 = root 5 or x - 2 = -root 5
4. Exact answersx = 2 + root 5 and x = 2 - root 5
5. Check the sumThe two roots add to 4, matching -b/a = 4 for this equation
Worked example 3 (ours): the line through (-2, 7) and (4, -2)
StepReasoning
1. Slope(-2 - 7) / (4 - (-2)) = -9/6 = -3/2
2. Point-slope formy - 7 = -3/2 (x + 2)
3. Slope-intercept formy = -3/2 x - 3 + 7, so y = -3/2 x + 4
4. Check the second pointx = 4: y = -6 + 4 = -2, which matches

Each answer above is exact and fully simplified, which is what an open-response system can recognise. The checks at the end of each table are the habit we drill hardest: with no answer choices on screen, the only way to know an answer is right is to test it yourself.

Questions students ask

ALEKS math placement: common questions

How many questions are on the ALEKS math placement test?

McGraw Hill says the Placement Assessment uses 25 questions or less. Because it is adaptive, the questions you see depend on your earlier answers.

What topics are on the ALEKS math placement test?

McGraw Hill says it measures knowledge across 314 topics, placing students into courses from Basic Math to Calculus 1. In practice that runs from arithmetic and algebra through functions, exponentials, logarithms and trigonometry.

Is the ALEKS placement test multiple choice?

No. ALEKS PPL is open response, so you type the answer yourself. McGraw Hill's About ALEKS page says ALEKS avoids multiple choice.

How long does the ALEKS placement test take?

McGraw Hill says the entire assessment can be completed in 90 minutes. Give yourself a quiet, uninterrupted block in case you need longer.

How many times can you take the ALEKS placement test?

McGraw Hill describes up to five assessment attempts, the first plus four more. Your college may set its own limits, waiting periods or deadlines, so check its rules.

What is a good ALEKS math placement score?

It depends on your college and your major. Each college decides which score places a student into which course, so find the score needed for the first course your program requires.

Is the ALEKS placement test hard?

It covers a wide range quickly, and the open-response format leaves no answer choices to fall back on. Students who practise exact, simplified answers and fix prerequisites such as factoring usually find it fair.

Is the ALEKS placement test proctored?

Often. McGraw Hill offers colleges remote proctoring through the ProctorU Platform and the Respondus LockDown Browser and Monitor. Your college decides which it uses.

When are your ALEKS prep classes?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays.

Are you connected with ALEKS or McGraw Hill?

No. We are an independent online school. We teach the math the assessment covers and never take part in an attempt.
