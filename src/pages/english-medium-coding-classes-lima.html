The short answer

Modern Age Coders runs English-medium coding classes for families in Lima: live online lessons taught entirely in English, for children from age 6, teenagers and adults. Learners start with Scratch or Python and move on to AI, Java, apps, and IB or IGCSE computer science. Mentors based in India teach a group of fifteen to twenty or one learner alone, at whatever hour suits your family in Peru time. Who it suits

Who are English-medium coding classes in Lima for?

Three kinds of family in Lima tend to look for coding taught in English. All three get the same classes, the same mentors and the same standard of teaching; what changes is the reason English helps.

Bilingual, Cambridge and IB pupils

Your child already learns maths and science in English at school. A coding class in Spanish would mean learning every technical word twice. In our classes, "variable", "loop" and "function" arrive in the same language as their school exams, so the vocabulary carries straight into IGCSE or Diploma work.

Spanish-first families who want both

Your child goes to a Spanish-medium colegio and you would like English to become something they use, not only study. An hour of coding gives a reason to listen, ask and explain in English, and the screen does half the explaining. It is English with a purpose: the game only works once the instruction is understood.

Teenagers and adults with plans

University reading lists, documentation, error messages and most programming help online are in English. A teenager preparing for university or an adult retraining for remote work learns Python, Java or AI the way the industry writes about it, and practises explaining technical ideas aloud.

We are honest about the other side. A child who understands very little spoken English will find the first weeks tiring, and for a six year old in that position we usually suggest starting one to one, where the mentor can slow down without holding a group back. We do not teach in Spanish, and it is fairer to say so here than after a free class.

English in Lima

How strong is English in Lima, and is it enough for a live class?

Stronger than in any other Peruvian city the most widely quoted ranking covers. The EF English Proficiency Index 2025 rates Lima "High" with a score of 555, while Peru as a whole scores 519 and sits in the "Moderate" band.

What the numbers say

EF tests adults who choose to sit its online test, so this is not a measure of Lima's children, and it is not a school result. It is still the clearest public signal that English is a working language for a large part of the capital.

The detail matters more than the rank. Across Peru, EF 2025 scores reading at 540 but speaking at 480, the lowest of the four skills. That pattern is familiar to anyone who learnt English from textbooks: you understand far more than you can say.

What we do about it

A live coding class is mostly listening and short spoken answers, which suits that profile. Mentors speak slowly, repeat an instruction in simpler words, and show each step on the shared screen before asking for it.

Speaking is built in small pieces: naming a block, reading a line of code aloud, then explaining in one sentence why a program failed. By the second month most children answer without being asked twice, and the code itself is the same in any language.

What school gives

How much English and computing does Peruvian school teach?

Some English, a few periods a week, and no computing subject at all. The national curriculum, the CNEB, sets the weekly timetable in teaching periods of 45 minutes.

A teaching period ("hora pedagógica") is 45 minutes; the minutes column is our own multiplication. Private bilingual and international schools set their own, usually much larger, English timetables.

English, on paper and in practice

For primaria, the CNEB itself is cautious: it says English is phased in "dependiendo de la contratación de estos docentes", depending on whether the school has hired a specialist English teacher, and where it has not, the hours become free-choice periods.

So two Lima classmates of the same age, at different schools, may meet very different amounts of English. One at a bilingual school spends most of the day in English; one at a public school may have three periods a week, or none in the early grades.

Computing, nowhere on the timetable

No area of the CNEB plan of studies is called computing or programming. Digital skills sit in competency 28, "Se desenvuelve en entornos virtuales generados por las TIC", which the CNEB lists among the "competencias transversales a las áreas": skills every subject is meant to touch, owned by none of them.

MINEDU has started to experiment. In July 2025 its Fábrica de Programadores pilot set out to train 1,000 pupils in 4th and 5th of secundaria in Lima Norte in Python, with the chance to take the PCEP exam at the end. That is a pilot, not a subject, and most Lima children will not meet it.

That is the gap these classes fill. A child at a bilingual school gets the English but rarely the coding; a child at a Spanish-medium school usually gets neither in any depth. Our classes add both, alongside school and never in place of it.

IB and Cambridge

Do IB and Cambridge students in Peru need computer science in English?

For a student who takes the subject in English, everything is in English: the coursework, the internal assessment and the final exam. And more Peruvian students take the IB than most parents realise.

The IB Diploma in Peru

MINEDU reported on 17 April 2026 that 2,502 students in Peru obtained the IB Diploma in 2025, and that students of the 25 public COAR schools made up 47% of them: 1,179 Diplomas from the November 2025 session, out of 1,740 COAR candidates for the full Diploma.

By our own subtraction, that leaves roughly 1,300 IB Diplomas earned in 2025 at schools outside the COAR network. The same MINEDU release adds that 515 COAR students in 5th of secundaria gained Cambridge English certificates, a reminder that English and the Diploma travel together.

What universities in Lima do with it

Two Lima universities publish an IB route in their own admission guides. The Universidad de Lima has an admission route for holders of the IB Diploma who apply within three years of earning it. PUCP has a Diploma de Bachillerato route and says Bachillerato courses can be recognised for up to 23 university credits.

Each university decides who it admits and what it recognises; we do not, and we are not connected with the IB, COAR, MINEDU or any university. What we can do is make sure a Diploma or IGCSE student understands their computer science and maths deeply, in the language they will be examined in.

A real lesson

What does a lesson look like? Sorting real English scores with Python

Here is a lesson we teach to learners from about 11, built on the EF city scores above. It teaches the two habits that catch most bugs in any program that sorts things into groups: put your checks in the right order, and test the edges.

The task

EF puts every score into a band, and its page data draws the band lines at 600, 550, 500 and 450. We give the learner the eight city scores and ask for a small function, band(score), that names the band for any score.

Most first attempts look like Version 1. It reads naturally from the bottom up: above 450 is low, above 500 is moderate, and so on. It runs without an error message, which is exactly why it is dangerous.

The surprise

Every city comes out "low", Lima included. The reason is that Python checks the conditions from the top and stops at the first true check. A score of 555 is at least 450, so the function answers "low" and never looks further.

The fix is not a cleverer condition; it is a better order. Version 2 keeps the band lines in a list, from the highest down, and walks through them.

Then comes the part that turns a coder into a careful one. We ask the learner to change >= to > and run it again. The count of cities does not change at all: high 1, moderate 3, low 4. Only the edge tests move, and 550 and 500 drop a band. Two rules can agree on every real city and still disagree at the edge, and only a test written on purpose at the edge will show it.

EF's page does not say which side of a line a score of exactly 550 falls on, so neither rule can be proved right from the data. The honest answer is to write the assumption down in a comment, which is what we teach. It is the same habit a Diploma student needs in an internal assessment and a developer needs at work.

  1. Ages 6 to 8, no screen. Sort ten toy cars into "small", "medium" and "big" boxes using a ruler. Then find a car exactly on a line. Which box does it go in, and who decided?
  2. Ages 8 to 10, Scratch. Make a quiz that says "cold", "warm" or "hot" for a temperature. Try the numbers right on your dividing lines before you show anyone.
  3. Ages 11 to 14, Python. Type Version 1, predict its output before you run it, then write Version 2 without looking at ours.
  4. Ages 14 and up. Add a fifth line for "very low" below 450 and write four edge tests of your own. Then explain, in two English sentences, why the city count could not catch the bug.

Thinking, not copying

Will my child learn to think, or just copy answers from AI?

To think. We teach learners to reason through a problem before any tool writes code for them, and we check that they can explain every line they hand in.

Explain before typing

Before a learner writes a program, they say in English what it should do and how they will know it works. The edge tests in the lesson above are part of that plan, not an extra.

AI with age rules

Children do not use adult chatbot accounts in our classes. Claude, for example, is for users aged 18 and over, and ChatGPT needs a parent's permission for anyone under 18. Older learners use AI tools to check and question their own work, not to replace it.

Depth over speed

We would rather a learner understand one program completely than copy ten. The same standard applies whether the learner is in Miraflores, Comas or Mumbai: one level of teaching, wherever you are.

Peru's own computing competition makes the same point. The Peruvian Informatics Olympiad, OPI, organised by UTEC and open free of charge to all secundaria pupils in Peru, bans generative AI for solving problems in its 2025 rules. A teenager who wants to try it needs to reason alone, in code, against the clock. We are not connected with UTEC or the OPI; we simply teach the kind of thinking it rewards, with problems we write ourselves.

A class needs a laptop or desktop computer, a stable connection and, ideally, a headset with a microphone. Across Peru, though, only 36.4% of households had at least one computer, so check the device before the first class. A phone is fine for watching, but not for typing code.

English-medium coding classes in Lima: questions and answers

Are the classes in Spanish?

No. All our classes are live online lessons in English, and only in English. Mentors speak clearly, adjust their speed and accent to each learner, and show every step on the shared screen, so a child whose English is still growing can follow. Scratch blocks and Python read the same whatever language you speak at home.

Who are these classes for?

Families in Lima who want coding taught in English: pupils at bilingual, Cambridge and IB schools, children at Spanish-medium schools whose parents want coding and English practice together, teenagers preparing for university, and adults learning Python, Java or AI for work.

What age can my child start?

From 6. Children of 6 to 12 start with Scratch, where blocks snap together and little reading or typing is needed. From about 9 they can move to typed Python. Teenagers and adults can start straight with Python or Java.

My child's English is still basic. Can they keep up?

Usually, yes, if they understand simple spoken instructions. Lessons are mostly short instructions and doing, and the mentor repeats in simpler words. For a young child with very little English we suggest one-to-one classes at first, so the mentor can slow down freely. The free first class will show you.

When are classes in Lima time?

Any day, any time, flexible: choose a batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at the hour that suits your family. Peru stays on UTC minus 5 all year and India on UTC plus 5:30, so India is always 10 hours 30 minutes ahead: 10:00 in Lima is 20:30 in India.

Do you help IB and IGCSE students with computer science?

Yes. We teach IB Diploma Computer Science at SL and HL, IGCSE Computer Science 0478, and IB and IGCSE maths, alongside the student's school. The school, Cambridge or the IB sets and marks all assessed work; we help the student understand it deeply.

Are you connected with MINEDU, COAR, the IB or a Lima school?

No. We are an independent online school with mentors based in India. We are not connected with MINEDU, COAR, the IB, Cambridge, any university or any school in Lima, and we do not award Peruvian qualifications. Learners who finish a course receive our own certificate of completion.

Does Peruvian school teach coding?

Not as a subject. The national curriculum, the CNEB, contains no computing area; it treats digital skills as a transversal competency that other subjects share. MINEDU ran a Python pilot, Fábrica de Programadores, for 1,000 pupils in 4th and 5th of secundaria in Lima Norte from July 2025.

Will my child use AI tools in class?

Only tools that suit their age, and only after they can solve the problem themselves. Children do not use adult chatbot accounts: Claude is for adults aged 18 and over, and ChatGPT needs a parent's permission under 18. We teach learners to think first and use AI to check their reasoning.

How are fees paid?

By card, in US dollars. Your bank converts each payment into soles, so the amount you see on your statement can vary slightly between months. The first class costs nothing, and we do not ask for card details to book it.

What does my child need at home?

A laptop or desktop computer, a stable internet connection and ideally a headset with a microphone. A phone is enough to watch but not to type code. Classes run on a normal video call, so no special software is needed for the first class.
