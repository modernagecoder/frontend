# Math placement tests explained: from MAP Growth to ACCUPLACER

American math has no single exit exam. Instead a student passes through a chain of tests owned by different organizations: a growth test a few times a year in elementary and middle school, a placement decision before Algebra 1, the SAT Suite or the ACT family in high school, and then a college placement or credit exam. This guide puts the whole chain in one table, says what each test decides, and links the page for your test.

## Which math tests does a US student take, and who runs each one?

Fourteen tests cover nearly every route from kindergarten to a first college math course. Every fact below comes from the organization that owns the test.

### Three kinds of test hide in that table

Growth tests (MAP Growth, i-Ready) follow a child through a school year. Readiness tests (the PSAT family, PreACT, SAT, ACT) report a scale score that colleges and scholarship programs read. Placement and credit tests (ACCUPLACER, ALEKS PPL, TSIA2, CLEP, AP) decide which college math course you may take, or let you skip one.

### Who draws the line

Publishers rarely set the line between tracks or courses. NWEA does not recommend MAP Growth "for grade-level advancement or accountability purposes", and CLEP says each institution "reserves the right to set its own credit-granting policy." So always ask: whose table turns my score into a decision?

## How do MAP Growth, i-Ready and placement tests shape a middle school math track?

They give the school evidence, and the school decides, usually by adding a district rule or a local exam.

### The two growth tests most districts use

NWEA describes MAP Growth Math as untimed and available from kindergarten to grade 12, with a child answering "around 43 questions" that adapt as they go. The RIT score means the same at every grade, which is how a teacher sees growth from fall to spring. Our [MAP Growth math page](/map-growth-math-test-prep) is for a parent holding a RIT report who wants home practice that is not item drilling.

Curriculum Associates renames its diagnostic i-Ready Inform from the 2026 to 2027 school year. The family report places each math domain at a level, down to Needs Improvement, and [our i-Ready page](/i-ready-math-help) works from the weakest domain rather than the overall number.

### Where tracking actually happens

The fork comes around grades 5 to 7. Texas now enrolls sixth graders automatically in advanced math if they reach the top 40 percent on grade 5 STAAR math or a local measure; other districts use RIT scores, teacher input or a local exam. [The placement test page](/middle-school-math-placement-test-prep) covers the move into accelerated grade 6 and the move into Algebra 1.

For a student already ahead, [the accelerated math page](/accelerated-math-program-online) shows which grade 7 and 8 ideas get compressed, so nothing is skipped on the way to early Algebra 1.

A word on preparation at this stage. NWEA calls its items secure test content and warns that helping a child answer compromises the data, and an adaptive test that is gamed simply places a child too high for the next unit. What helps is steady number sense: fractions, place value and word problems read slowly, which every later test on this page relies on.

## Which high school math courses sit underneath the readiness and placement tests?

Every test from the PSAT onward samples the same three or four years of math, whatever the course names on the transcript.

### Two routes through the same content

The Common Core standards "do not mandate the sequence of high school courses" and describe an "integrated course sequence (Mathematics 1, Mathematics 2, Mathematics 3)" beside the traditional one. North Carolina requires "NC Math 1, 2, and 3 and a fourth mathematics course", and NWEA offers MAP course tests for Integrated Math 1, 2 and 3.

Our integrated pages split the years: [Integrated Math 1](/integrated-math-1-tutoring) sets linear against exponential change, [Integrated Math 2](/integrated-math-2-tutoring) is the quadratics year, and [Integrated Math 3](/integrated-math-3-tutoring) brings polynomials, logarithms and inference, the content college placement probes most.

### The courses that finish the ladder

[Trigonometry tutoring](/trigonometry-tutoring-online) joins the right-triangle picture to the unit circle. [Statistics tutoring](/statistics-tutoring-online) is the non-AP course on variability and sampling, the data questions SAT and ACCUPLACER keep asking.

At the top, College Board describes [AP Precalculus](/ap-precalculus-tutoring-online) as "designed to be the equivalent of a first semester college precalculus course"; its exam is on Tuesday, May 11, 2027, with 42 multiple-choice and four free-response questions.

## What is the difference between the PSAT 8/9, PSAT/NMSQT, PreACT, SAT and ACT?

Two families, one ladder each. College Board runs the PSAT 8/9, PSAT 10, PSAT/NMSQT and SAT on linked scales; ACT runs PreACT 8/9, PreACT and the ACT on the 1 to 36 scale. Only the PSAT/NMSQT in grade 11 feeds National Merit.

**Score scales in the two readiness families (College Board score report table; ACT PreACT and score pages; read 1 November 2026)**

| Test | Total scale | Math scale | Grades | What it is mainly for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PSAT 8/9 | 240 to 1440 | 120 to 720 | 8 and 9 | A baseline at the start of high school |
| PSAT 10 | 320 to 1520 | 160 to 760 | 10 | A spring check-in on the SAT scale |
| PSAT/NMSQT | 320 to 1520 | 160 to 760 | 10 and 11 | National Merit entry when taken in grade 11 |
| SAT | 400 to 1600 | 200 to 800 | 11 and 12 | College admission and some placement |
| PreACT 8/9 | 1 to 36, top score 30 | Same scale | 8 and 9 | An early look at ACT-style items |
| PreACT | 1 to 36, top score 35 | Same scale | 9 to 11 | A predicted ACT score |
| ACT | 1 to 36 | 1 to 36 | 11 and 12 | College admission and some placement |

Sources: [College Board, understanding scores](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/scores/understanding-scores) (the score report table), the [fall 2026 PSAT/NMSQT student guide](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/psat-nmsqt-student-guide.pdf), [ACT PreACT page](https://www.act.org/content/act/en/products-and-services/preact.html) and [ACT, understanding your scores](https://www.act.org/content/act/en/products-and-services/the-act/scores/understanding-your-scores.html). Grade bands for PreACT are ACT's; for the SAT Suite they follow College Board's test names and guides.

All four College Board tests use "multistage adaptive testing" in the Bluebook app: the first module of each section sets the difficulty of the second. SAT Math is 44 questions in 70 minutes, with Desmos built in and an approved handheld allowed.

### The ACT ladder

ACT says PreACT gives "8th, 9th, and 10th graders an early experience with ACT test items" and a predicted score; its new Math section has 36 items, 32 scored, in 45 minutes. [The PreACT page](/preact-math-prep) explains what that predicted range can tell a ninth grader.

## What are ACCUPLACER, ALEKS PPL and TSIA2, and how are they different from CLEP?

ACCUPLACER, ALEKS PPL and TSIA2 are placement tests: they decide which math course a new college student starts in. College Board says no one passes or fails ACCUPLACER, and TSIA2 adds a Texas readiness benchmark on top of the placement. CLEP and AP are credit exams: a high enough score, by the college's own policy, replaces a course on the transcript.

**College placement and credit routes (ACCUPLACER, McGraw Hill, College Board TSIA2 and CLEP pages, Texas Higher Education Coordinating Board; read 31 October and 1 November 2026)**

| Route | What it decides | Retakes and validity |
|---|---|---|
| ACCUPLACER | Your first math course | Set by the college |
| ALEKS PPL | Your first math course, Basic Math to Calculus 1 | Up to five assessment attempts |
| TSIA2 | College-level readiness at a Texas public college | Results usable up to five years |
| CLEP College Algebra or Calculus | Credit for the course, by college policy | No retake of the same title within three months |
| AP Precalculus and AP Calculus | Credit or advanced standing | One exam each May |
| Dual enrollment | A college course taken in high school | By program; usually needs placement first |

### The three placement tests

[ACCUPLACER math](/accuplacer-math-prep) is three separate College Board tests (Arithmetic; Quantitative Reasoning, Algebra, and Statistics; Advanced Algebra and Functions), each 200 to 300 and usually untimed, with a calculator only on screen.

McGraw Hill's [ALEKS PPL](/aleks-math-placement-test-prep) is open response, covers 314 topics "in 25 questions or less" and "can be completed in 90 minutes"; retakes make the plan test, study, test again.

Texas public colleges use [TSIA2](/tsia2-math-prep): a 20-question math test, a 48-question diagnostic for some, and no handheld calculator "of any type".

### Credit, and college math in high school

[CLEP College Algebra](/clep-college-algebra-prep) is about 60 questions in 90 minutes with a TI-30XS MultiView built in; [CLEP Calculus](/clep-calculus-prep) is 44 questions with no calculator in Section 1. Both score 20 to 80, and ACE recommends credit at 50: 3 semester hours for College Algebra, 4 for Calculus.

With [dual enrollment](/dual-enrollment-math-help) a high school student takes a real college course. Washington's Running Start "allows 11th and 12th grade students to take college courses" at 34 community and technical colleges, usually after placing first.

## How do I find out which math placement test I need?

Start from the decision, not the test. The same five steps suit a fifth grader's parent and an adult returning to college.

1. Name the decision. A middle school track, a scholarship entry, a first college course or credit: each is a different row of the table.
2. Find the owner of the decision. A district office, a counselor or a college testing center. Ask which test they use, in writing.
3. Ask for the score-to-decision table. The test publisher reports a score; the school or college turns it into a course. That table is the target, and it differs from campus to campus.
4. Check for exemptions and alternatives. In Texas many students skip TSIA2 with other scores; colleges may accept SAT or ACT math for placement; some allow high school grades. One email can save weeks of study.
5. Take the owner's free practice, then plan. Sit the publisher's free official practice once, sort the misses by skill, and study skills, not items.

**Pace per question on the timed tests (our arithmetic from each publisher's stated question count and time; untimed tests left out)**

| Test | Questions and time | Seconds per question, ours |
|---|---|---|
| ACT Math | 45 in 50 minutes | 50 x 60 / 45 = 66.7, which ACT rounds to 67 |
| CLEP College Algebra | About 60 in 90 minutes | 90 x 60 / 60 = 90 |
| SAT Math | 44 in 70 minutes | 70 x 60 / 44 = 95.5 |
| CLEP Calculus | 44 in about 90 minutes | 90 x 60 / 44 = 122.7 |
| ALEKS PPL | 25 or fewer in about 90 minutes | At least 90 x 60 / 25 = 216 |
| ACCUPLACER, TSIA2, MAP Growth | Untimed | No clock: accuracy is the whole game |

A student comfortable on an untimed ACCUPLACER can still run short on the ACT, at about a minute a question, so know the pace before you practise.

## One idea, four tests: how the same math grows up the ladder

Our own example, not a test item: one idea, multiplying by a growth factor, from middle school placement to college placement.

**Worked example (ours): a club has 45 members and grows by 20 percent a year**

| Level | Question (ours) | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Middle school placement | How many members after one year? | A 20% rise means multiply by 1.2: 45 x 1.2 = 54 |
| Algebra 1 and the PSAT 8/9 | After a 20% rise there are 54. How many were there before? | Undo the multiplier: 1.2x = 54, so x = 54 / 1.2 = 45 (not 54 minus 20%) |
| SAT, PreACT and ACCUPLACER QAS | Write a model for members after t years and find year 2 | f(t) = 45(1.2)^t, so f(2) = 45 x 1.44 = 64.8, about 65 members |
| ACCUPLACER AAF, ALEKS PPL, CLEP College Algebra | When does the club double? | 1.2^t = 2, so t = ln 2 / ln 1.2, about 3.80 years |

The second row is the usual slip: taking 20 percent off 54 gives 43.2, not 45. Writing every change as a multiplier is a grade 7 habit that a college algebra placement test is still checking, so we look for that habit before drilling anything harder.

## US math placement and readiness tests: questions families ask

### What is a math placement test?

It is a test a school or college uses to decide which math course you start in. In middle school it may be a district exam or MAP and state scores; at college it is usually ACCUPLACER, ALEKS PPL or, in Texas, TSIA2. The score routes you to a course rather than giving a pass or a fail, though Texas sets a readiness benchmark on TSIA2.

### What is the difference between ACCUPLACER, ALEKS and TSIA2?

ACCUPLACER is College Board's multiple-choice, adaptive system with three math tests scored 200 to 300. ALEKS PPL from McGraw Hill is open response, 25 questions or fewer, with up to five attempts. TSIA2 is the Texas Success Initiative test used by Texas public colleges, with a 20-question math test and a diagnostic if needed.

### Which math placement test does my college use?

Only the college can tell you, so ask its testing center or math department. Ask also for its score-to-course table and its retake rule.

### Can I use a calculator on math placement tests?

It varies. ACCUPLACER and TSIA2 do not allow your own calculator and show one on screen only for some questions. The SAT has Desmos built in and allows an approved handheld; the ACT asks you to bring your own permitted calculator.

### Does the PSAT 8/9 or PSAT 10 count for National Merit?

No. College Board's 2026 student guide says only the PSAT/NMSQT counts, taken in the qualifying year, which for most students is grade 11. The earlier tests are useful baselines on the same family of scales.

### Should my teenager take the SAT or the ACT?

Colleges that ask for scores generally accept either. Try one official practice test of each, check which one your school gives in the school day, then prepare for one.

### How should I prepare for a math placement test?

Confirm the exact test and the college's placement table, take the owner's free practice test, and study the skills you missed rather than repeating practice tests.
