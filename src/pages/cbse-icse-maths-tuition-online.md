One thing about the boards themselves. CBSE and ICSE cover most of the same mathematics in Classes 6 to 10, in a different order and with a different emphasis: the ICSE paper leans harder on presentation and on commercial mathematics, and the CBSE paper, since the competency-based questions arrived, on applying a known idea in a new setting. Our batches are grouped by board as well as class, so a child is never taught the other board's exam. In Class 11 and 12 the same holds for CBSE and ISC.

For the board examination timetable itself, the boards publish their own dates each session and schools circulate them; we pace the Class 10 and Class 12 batches to the school's calendar rather than printing a date here that the board has not yet fixed.

Q2. Where do the marks actually go?

## The four places CBSE and ICSE students lose marks they could have kept

Ten thousand students later, the pattern is stable. Most lost marks are not from hard topics but from four habits, and each one is fixable in a batch small enough that the teacher sees it.

The Class 6 to 8 gap

### A confusion that compounds

A child unsure of integers, fractions or the first algebra in Class 6 carries it silently into Class 9, where it surfaces as "not good at maths." The batch for Classes 6 to 8 is built to catch it early: concrete first, then symbols, and a marked set after every class so the teacher knows within a week.

Rote before reasoning

### The formula without the why

A student who has memorised the quadratic formula and never seen why it works cannot handle the competency question that asks it sideways. We teach the idea, then the technique, then the exam question, in that order, because the board now asks in that order too.

The Class 11 jump

### A new subject wearing the old name

Functions, complex numbers, permutations and the first calculus are not harder Class 10 maths; they are different thinking. Students who treat Class 11 as more of the same fall in the first term. We teach it as a new subject from the first week, and the JEE foundation course runs alongside for those who want the harder problems.

Presentation and time

### The marks lost after the maths was done

On the ICSE paper especially, a correct answer with rough working loses marks, and a slow method loses the last question. Every batch practises writing a solution the examiner can follow, and the Class 10 and 12 mocks are sat under the board's time.

Q3. Why understanding beats memorising on a board paper

## The same Class 9 question, answered by two students

A polynomials question of the kind every board paper sets: "Find the remainder when x cubed minus 3x squared plus 4x minus 5 is divided by x minus 2." Watch what happens to a memorised method when the paper changes one word.

***The memorised answer**long division, every time*

```
Divide x^3 - 3x^2 + 4x - 5 by (x - 2)
by long division:
x^2 - x + 2, remainder -1.

# Then the paper asks:
# "For which value of k does x - 2
#  divide x^3 - 3x^2 + 4x + k exactly?"
# Student sets up the division again,
# with k in it, and stalls. Four marks
# gone on a two-line question.
```

Correct on the first question, lost on the second, because the method was a procedure and not an idea. This is the competency question doing exactly what it was designed to do.

***The understood answer**the remainder theorem, as an idea*

```
Remainder theorem: dividing p(x) by (x - a)
leaves remainder p(a).
p(2) = 8 - 12 + 8 - 5 = -1.  Remainder -1.

"For which k does (x - 2) divide exactly?"
Exactly means remainder 0, so p(2) = 0:
8 - 12 + 8 + k = 0, so k = -4.

# Two lines each. The second question
# is the same idea in a new costume,
# which is what the board now asks.
```

Same syllabus, same chapter, one idea understood instead of one procedure memorised. That is what the first class of every week is for, and why the marked set after it matters.

Every chapter has a version of this, from the number line in Class 6 to integration in Class 12. The plan below is built so the idea always comes before the technique.

Q4. How does the batch work through the year?

## Five steps, from the first week to the board paper

Two live classes a week, in a batch of five to ten from the same class and board, with the same teacher all year. One class teaches; the other practises, marks and rehearses.

1. #### A placement check in the first week, by class and board

  Twenty questions across the previous year's syllabus, sat alone and walked through together. It tells the teacher which earlier chapters are shaky, and it tells you honestly where your child stands before any tuition begins.
2. #### The syllabus in the school's order, one idea at a time

  We follow the sequence the child's school follows, so tuition and school reinforce each other. Each idea is taught before its technique, then met in the exam's own question shapes, attempted live and marked live.
3. #### A short marked set after every class

  Four or five questions, done alone, marked before the next class. The teacher opens every session with what the sets showed, so a confusion lasts days rather than a term.
4. #### The board year, paced

  For Class 10 and Class 12, the whole syllabus is finished with time to spare, case-study and competency practice starts in the first term, and the last weeks are sample-paper mocks under the board's time, reviewed question by question.
5. #### The Class 11 bridge and the JEE track

  Class 10 students who continue with us start the Class 11 syllabus early, as the new subject it is. Students who want more take the JEE foundation course alongside from Class 8, and the olympiad course if the school enters them for contests.

Batch timings are after-school and weekend slots in Indian Standard Time, fixed on the call; Gulf families on the CBSE syllabus join the same batches in their own evening, and the fee for families outside India is shown in your own currency on this page.

## About CBSE and ICSE maths tuition online

### Do you teach CBSE and ICSE maths, or one of them?

Both, and ISC for Classes 11 and 12, in separate batches. Classes are grouped by class and by board, so a CBSE Class 9 student and an ICSE Class 9 student are in different rooms with different papers in front of them. The mathematics overlaps heavily in Classes 6 to 10, but the order, the emphasis and the exam shape differ, and we teach to the paper the child will actually sit.

### How is the Class 10 board year handled?

The CBSE Class 10 batch follows our board prep course: all fourteen chapters of the 2026-27 syllabus in both Standard (041) and Basic (241), paced across ten months, with competency and case-study practice from the first term and sample-paper mocks under the board's time in the last weeks. ICSE Class 10 students get the ICSE syllabus and the presentation practice the ICSE paper rewards.

### Which class can my child start in?

Any class from 6 to 12. Most families start in Class 6 to 8, where the foundation gaps begin, or in Class 9, where the complexity jump arrives. A Class 11 student who scored well in Class 10 and is now struggling is one of our most common starts, and the honest answer is that they are not alone: Class 11 is a new subject.

### How many students are in a batch?

Five to ten, from the same class and board, with one teacher for the whole year. That size is small enough that every student is asked questions by name and the teacher knows which chapter each child is stuck on, and large enough that a student hears other people's questions, which are often better than the textbook's. A mini batch of three to four and one-to-one tuition are also available.

### Is it the 2026-27 syllabus?

Yes. The Class 10 batch is built on the CBSE syllabus published for session 2026-27, and every other batch follows the current syllabus for its class and board in the order the child's school teaches it, so tuition and school reinforce each other rather than compete.

### What happens if my child misses a class for a school test?

It is planned for. The teacher opens the next session with a short catch-up, the session notes and the marked set go out the same evening, and after a run of missed classes around school examinations we arrange a one-off catch-up slot. Classes are live and attended, not sold as recordings, and the catch-up is how continuity is kept.

### Does this help with JEE?

Class 11 and 12 batches teach the CBSE and ISC syllabus for understanding, which is the ground JEE stands on, and students who want the harder problems take the JEE foundation course from Class 8 to 10 alongside. We do not sell JEE ranks; we teach the mathematics properly and say honestly, after the placement check, whether a student is ready for competitive-level problems.

### We are a CBSE family in the Gulf. Does this work for us?

Yes, and many families in the batches are exactly that. The syllabus is the same CBSE syllabus your school follows, the batches run in Indian evening slots that fall in the early evening in the UAE, Oman, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait and Bahrain, and the fee for families outside India is shown in your own currency on this page.

Session notes and the marked practice set the same evening, yes; recordings as a substitute for attending, no. Every class is live because the teaching depends on the student being asked questions and answering them. If a class is missed, the catch-up described above is how it is recovered.
