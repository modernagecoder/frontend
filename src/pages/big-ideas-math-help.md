### Why the name matters for homework

Older editions and newer programs share topics but not always page numbers, chapter order or lesson titles. When you tell a tutor "chapter 4, lesson 3", the edition decides what that means.

Before the first class, photograph the cover and the table of contents. That lets us plan around the same sequence your child's teacher is following.

### What the publisher says about learning

The Concepts & Connections press release, dated February 5, 2026, says the program "emphasizes conceptual understanding as the foundation for procedural fluency and application" and supports rich discourse and productive struggle.

For a parent, that means some homework problems are meant to be puzzled over before a method is given. A tutor helps most by guiding that puzzling, not by short-cutting it.

## Where do students commonly get stuck in Big Ideas Math?

These are the topics our teachers most often meet when middle and high school students bring textbook homework to class. They are general teaching guidance from our lessons, not figures about any program.

**Common sticking points from grade 6 to Algebra 2 and what helps (our teaching notes)**

| Course | Where it sticks | What tends to help |
|---|---|---|
| Grade 6 | Ratios that are not written as fractions | Build a ratio table before any rule |
| Grade 7 | Proportional relationships and percent change | Find the constant first, then use it |
| Grade 8 | Equations with the variable on both sides | Collect the variable on one side, in words first |
| Algebra 1 | Systems of equations and function notation | Read f(x) aloud as "the output when the input is x" |
| Geometry | Writing a proof in order | List what is given and what is wanted before any step |
| Algebra 2 | Exponent and logarithm rules mixed together | Test each rule with small numbers |

### A sign worth noticing

A child who can do every problem in the lesson but freezes on the chapter review is usually matching problems to the example above them rather than recognizing what kind of problem it is.

The fix is practice at choosing a method: mixed problems where the heading does not tell you what to do.

### Another sign

Long homework sessions with lots of erasing often mean the arithmetic underneath, such as fractions or negatives, is not yet automatic.

We check that quickly in the first class, because repairing it makes every later chapter lighter.

## How does a tutor teach a Big Ideas Math topic without giving the answer?

Four original examples on topics from grades 7 and 8 and Algebra 1, written by our teachers. The aim in each is the same: read the structure of the problem, then choose the method.

**Solving 3(x - 4) = x + 6, a grade 8 topic, with the question the tutor asks at each line (our example)**

| Line | Working | Tutor's question |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3x - 12 = x + 6 | "What does the 3 outside the bracket do to each term inside?" |
| 2 | 3x - x - 12 = 6 | "Where should all the x terms live?" |
| 3 | 2x - 12 = 6 | "What is 3x take away x?" |
| 4 | 2x = 18 | "What undoes the minus 12?" |
| 5 | x = 9 | "What undoes the times 2?" |
| Check | 3(9 - 4) = 15 and 9 + 6 = 15 | "Do both sides really match?" |

### Unit rate and the constant (grade 7)

Our problem: 5 pounds of apples cost 7.50 dollars. Is cost proportional to weight, and what do 8 pounds cost? The unit rate is 7.50 / 5 = 1.50 dollars per pound, so the constant of proportionality is 1.5 and cost = 1.5 x weight.

Eight pounds cost 1.5 x 8 = 12 dollars. The structure to notice: one quantity is always the same multiple of the other, and the graph is a straight line through zero.

### Distance with Pythagoras (grade 8)

Our problem: how far apart are (1, 2) and (7, 10)? Draw the right triangle. Across is 7 - 1 = 6 and up is 10 - 2 = 8. Then distance squared = 6 squared + 8 squared = 36 + 64 = 100, so the distance is 10.

The structure to notice: any slanted distance on a grid is the long side of a right triangle you can draw yourself.

**Solving 2x + 3y = 12 and 4x - 3y = 6 by elimination, an Algebra 1 topic (our example)**

| Step | Working | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Spot the structure | +3y and -3y | Opposite y terms will cancel if the equations are added |
| Add | 6x = 18 | Left sides add, right sides add, so the equation stays true |
| Solve | x = 3 | Divide both sides by 6 |
| Substitute | 2(3) + 3y = 12, so 3y = 6 and y = 2 | Either original equation works |
| Check | 4(3) - 3(2) = 6 | The second equation must also hold |

**Mixed practice set: decide the method before you start (ours; answers in brackets)**

| Problem | Which method fits, and the answer |
|---|---|
| 4(x + 1) = 2x + 10 | Expand, collect x on one side [x = 3] |
| 3 notebooks cost 4.50 dollars; cost of 10? | Unit rate [15 dollars] |
| Distance from (0, 0) to (5, 12) | Right triangle [13] |
| x + y = 9 and x - y = 3 | Add to eliminate y [x = 6, y = 3] |
| If f(x) = 2x - 5, find f(4) | Substitute the input [3] |
| A price rises from 40 to 50 dollars; percent change? | Change over original [25 percent] |

## How does a live tutor fit around Big Ideas Math homework?

The school teacher and the textbook set the pace. Our lessons sit around them: before a hard chapter, or after a homework night that went badly.

1. Share the chapter and lesson. Snap the contents page and note the homework problem numbers; that is all we need.
2. Find the step that broke. The teacher asks your child to talk through the problem until the exact sticking line appears.
3. Teach on a fresh problem. The teacher works a new problem of the same kind, written by us, asking your child to choose each step.
4. Return to the school problem. Your child does the textbook problem alone, on camera if they like, and explains it.
5. Mix the methods. Near a test, we set mixed problems so your child practises recognizing which method to use.

### The textbook alone may do

For a student who reads the examples, finishes homework in reasonable time and does well on reviews.

### Book plus a batch may suit

For a student who learns from hearing others reason through the same topic, in a class of fifteen to twenty.

### Book plus 1-on-1 may suit

For a student with an older gap, or one who needs every lesson paced to the school calendar.

## From copying the example to choosing the method

Our description of how textbook students grow more independent.

**Stages of method choice (ours)**

| Stage | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 1. Copying | Follows the worked example line by line |
| 2. Adapting | Changes the numbers but keeps the same steps |
| 3. Recognizing | Names the kind of problem without the heading |
| 4. Choosing | Picks a method on a mixed review and explains why |

### For parents

Homework scores show stage 1 and 2 well. Chapter tests show stage 3 and 4.
Ask your child "what kind of problem is this?" before they start. The answer tells you a lot.

### For students

Cover the heading, then decide what the problem is asking.
If you can solve a problem two ways, you understand it.

## Big Ideas Math help: common questions

### Can I get live Big Ideas Math help online?

Yes. Students who use Big Ideas Math or Math & YOU at school join our teachers in live online classes. We explain the method behind the homework on fresh problems, in a batch of 15 to 20 or one to one.

### Who publishes Big Ideas Math?

Big Ideas Learning, which describes itself as a Larson Texts company founded in 2008. Big Ideas Math and Big Ideas Learning are registered trademarks of Larson Texts, Inc.

### Is Math & YOU the same as Big Ideas Math?

Both come from Big Ideas Learning. The student site still refers to Big Ideas Math users, while the publisher's main site leads with Math & YOU K-8 and Math & YOU: Concepts & Connections for high school. Check your child's cover or login to see which one the school uses.

### How do I access the Big Ideas Math textbook at home?

Go to bigideasmath.com and use Family Program Access: choose the grade or course, then your state, to open the Student Edition. A full student login needs an access code from the school, which the site says to request from the teacher or administrator.

### Do you give Big Ideas Math answers?

No. We do not complete graded homework or share answer keys. Our teachers teach the method on a new problem of our own, and your child then solves the school problem and explains it.

### Are there Big Ideas Math practice tests?

Ask your child's teacher what the school provides, since tests and their practice versions come with the school's program. We write our own mixed practice sets on the same topics, which is how we prepare students for chapter tests.

### Which grades use Big Ideas Math?

The student site lists kindergarten to grade 8, Algebra 1, Geometry and Algebra 2, and the publisher also offers Precalculus for AP. Our help focuses on grades 6 to 8 and the three high school courses.

### Why is my child struggling with Big Ideas Math?

In our experience, students who struggle can usually copy the worked example but cannot yet tell which kind of problem they are looking at, or are still shaky on fraction and integer arithmetic from earlier grades. Both respond well to steady, explained practice.

### When are classes?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays.

### Are you connected with Big Ideas Learning?

No. We are an independent online school. We describe the programs only from the publisher's own pages and use our own problems in class.
