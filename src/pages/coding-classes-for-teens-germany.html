Informatik in Klasse 7 to 10

How much Informatik does a teenager get at school in each German Land?

The answer changes at the Land border. The Gesellschaft für Informatik collects the rules of all sixteen Länder every year, and its 2026/27 Informatik-Monitor is the clearest single source for the years from Klasse 7 to Klasse 10.

What the table means for a fifteen year old

A teenager in Hamburg or Schleswig-Holstein will have met four hours of compulsory Informatik somewhere between Klasse 7 and 10. One in Nordrhein-Westfalen had theirs in Klasse 5 and 6 and may have nothing more until the Oberstufe. One at a Gymnasium in Hessen or Brandenburg may never have had a compulsory lesson at all.

According to the national overview of the Monitor, no Informatik is required of every pupil in six Länder: Berlin, Brandenburg, Bremen, Hessen, Rheinland-Pfalz and Sachsen-Anhalt.

What one hour a week can and cannot do

Even in the Länder that teach the most, one or two lessons a week shared with thirty classmates leaves little time to write and debug a program of any size. School lays the ground; fluency comes from hours at the keyboard.

That is the gap a teenager's coding class fills. Ours follows what the Land teaches when a learner asks for it, and otherwise goes as far as the teenager wants to go.

From Klasse 10 to the Abitur

Does Informatik count in the Oberstufe and the Abitur?

Yes, where the school offers it. The KMK places Informatik in the same field of subjects as maths and the sciences, and many Länder let it be taken as a Grundkurs or a Leistungskurs.

The two phases

KMK rules divide the Oberstufe into two parts: a one-year Einführungsphase followed by a two-year Qualifikationsphase. The marks that count towards the Abitur come from the four half-years of the Qualifikationsphase, called Block I, and from the Abitur exams themselves, Block II.

Before that comes Klasse 10, the year of the Mittlerer Schulabschluss. Berlin awards the MSA after Klasse 10, and Nordrhein-Westfalen sets central ZP10 exams at the end of Klasse 10 in German, maths and English; the decree for the 2027 exams is already published.

Who actually takes Informatik

The Informatik-Monitor 2026/27 puts the national figure at about 15 percent of pupils taking Informatik in the Qualifikationsphase. In Sachsen and Mecklenburg-Vorpommern it is about half; in Baden-Württemberg, Niedersachsen and Bremen it is roughly one pupil in twelve.

In Nordrhein-Westfalen Informatik can be taken in the Qualifikationsphase as a three-hour Grundkurs or a five-hour Leistungskurs, and the written Abitur in Informatik is done without a computer. Programming on paper is a skill of its own, and one we practise.

The same KMK agreement says Block I is worth at most 600 points and Block II at most 300, so the Abitur total can reach 900, and at least 300 are needed to pass. It also prints a formula and a table that turn the total into the average grade on the certificate. A teenager who codes can check one against the other, which is exactly the project below. We teach alongside school and award no grades; the school course and the Abitur are the ones that count.

A project on a real rule

Can a teenager write a program that reproduces the official Abitur grade table?

Anlage 4 of the KMK agreement gives the average grade as five and two thirds minus the total points divided by 180, and then prints a table of 31 rows from 900 points down to 300. A program that follows the formula should match the table at every one of those 601 whole scores. The first attempt does not.

The task

Write a function that takes the total points, a whole number from 300 to 900, and returns the average grade with one decimal, exactly as the table prints it. Then test the function against every score in every band of the table.

Typing the 31 bands by hand invites mistakes, so the program builds them: the top band is 823 to 900, then each band is 18 points lower than the one above, down to 301 to 318, and 300 alone gives 4.0. We compared this list with the table in the KMK document line by line and they agree.

What a teenager learns

How to turn a written rule into code, how to test that code against a published source rather than against your own expectations, and why "round to one decimal" is not always what a rule means.

None of this needs an account, an AI tool or anything beyond Python itself.

More than half of the 601 scores come out wrong. At 823 points the formula gives 1.094, Python rounds it to 1.1, and the table says 1.0. The table does not round: it cuts the number off after the first decimal. There is a second problem at the top. At 900 points the formula gives 0.67, but there is no grade better than 1.0, so the table stops there.

Version 2 multiplies the formula by ten and keeps it in whole numbers: ten times five and two thirds minus E over 180 is 1,020 minus E, divided by 18. Integer division with // cuts off the remainder exactly, with none of the tiny errors that decimal fractions carry inside a computer, and max(10, ...) stops the grade at 1.0. Every one of the 601 scores now matches the KMK table.

  1. Predict first. Before running Version 1, guess how many scores it will get wrong. Most teenagers guess a handful.
  2. Find the pattern. Print the first ten mismatches. Which part of each band goes wrong, the top or the bottom, and why?
  3. Try a third way. Replace round with int(n * 10) / 10 and test again. Does it fix both problems, or only one?
  4. Going further. Write the opposite function: given an average such as 2.3, return the lowest total that earns it. Test it on every band.

Formula and table: KMK, Vereinbarung zur Gestaltung der gymnasialen Oberstufe und der Abiturprüfung, Anlage 4, as amended on 6 June 2024, read on 3 November 2026. The code, the band list, the counts and the practice tasks are our own. A real Abitur certificate is issued by the school under the Land's own rules.

Contests for teenagers

The Bundesweite Informatikwettbewerbe, BWINF, run a ladder that starts with a quiz and ends at the International Olympiad in Informatics, which Germany hosts in 2027.

We are not connected with BWINF and never reuse its tasks. Our mentors invent fresh problems in a similar spirit: read a precise statement, find an algorithm, prove to yourself that it handles every case, then write it up so that someone else can follow. A teenager who can do that for the Abitur table above is already practising the habit these contests reward. Our German informatics competitions course works through it step by step.

AI and age

Do teenagers need ChatGPT or Claude for our coding classes?

Not in our teen classes. They are built so that a thirteen year old needs no account with any AI provider.

What the providers say

OpenAI's terms set 13 as the minimum age for ChatGPT and require a parent's or guardian's permission under 18. Anthropic's terms make Claude available to adults of 18 and over only. Our AI tools age guide quotes each provider's terms in full, with the date we read them.

What we do instead

Teenagers write their programs themselves and the mentor reads every line with them. When AI comes up, it comes up as something to build and question, in plain Python, never as a tool that writes the homework. Courses that use adult-only tools are kept for learners of 18 and over.

Germany keeps Central European Time, UTC+1, in winter and summer time, UTC+2, from the end of March, under the Einheitenzeitgesetz. The clocks went back on 25 October 2026 and go forward again on 28 March 2027. India stays on UTC+5:30, so it is 4 hours 30 minutes ahead in winter and 3 hours 30 minutes in summer.

These are examples, not fixed batches. When you book the free first class you choose a time in your own German clock. Many teenagers move to a weekend slot in the weeks before Klausuren or the ZP10, and a one-to-one learner can agree that with the mentor at short notice.

Coding classes for teens in Germany: common questions

Are the classes in German?

No. Every class is live and taught in English only, by a mentor who matches the teenager's pace and accent. For a pupil at a German school that means coding and English practice in the same hour.

My teenager has never coded. Is 15 too late to start?

Not at all. Our teen Python course starts from the first line, and teenagers usually move faster than younger children because they already know the maths and logic school has taught them.

Does my Land teach Informatik in Klasse 7 to 10?

It depends on the Land. According to the Informatik-Monitor 2026/27, Hamburg and Schleswig-Holstein require four hours across Klasse 7 to 10 and Sachsen one hour a week, while Berlin, Brandenburg, Bremen, Hessen, Rheinland-Pfalz and Sachsen-Anhalt have no Informatik that is compulsory for everyone. The table on this page lists all sixteen.

Will your classes help with Informatik at school?

Yes, if the teenager wants that. The mentor can follow the school topics, from algorithms in Klasse 7 to Java classes in the Qualifikationsphase. We teach alongside school and give no grades; the school course is the one that counts.

Which language should a teenager learn first?

Python, in most cases. It is short to write and easy to read. Many Oberstufe Informatik courses use Java, so a teenager heading that way usually adds Java after a few months of Python.

Can a teenager enter the Bundeswettbewerb Informatik?

Yes. BWINF says young people up to 21 who are not yet studying or working may take part. Round 1 of the 45th Bundeswettbewerb runs from 1 September to 23 November 2026, and group work is allowed in that round. We are not connected with BWINF.

Does my teenager need a ChatGPT or Claude account?

No. Our teen classes need no AI account at all. OpenAI's terms require a parent's permission under 18 and Anthropic's terms restrict Claude to adults, so we keep adult-only tools out of every class for under-18s.

When are the classes in German time?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Most teenagers come after school from about 14:00, or at the weekend.

How are fees paid from Germany?

The fee is shown on this page in euro at the European Central Bank reference rate and taken in US dollars, which your bank converts. Nobody pays for the first class, and we ask for no card.

Is it a group class or one to one?

Either. Group classes have fifteen to twenty teenagers of a similar level with one mentor; one-to-one classes follow a single teenager's plan and timetable.
