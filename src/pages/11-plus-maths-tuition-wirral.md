The lesson

## Negative numbers, counted through zero

The Year 5 curriculum asks children to "interpret negative numbers in context, count forwards and backwards with positive and negative whole numbers, including through zero". Temperature is the classic context.

| Night | Low | Night | Low |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monday | 3 | Friday | -4 |
| Tuesday | -1 | Saturday | -2 |
| Wednesday | -3 | Sunday | 1 |
| Thursday | 0 |   |   |

### Order

Which night was coldest? Friday, at -4. Children who compare only the digits pick Monday's 3 or say -1 is colder than -4. On a number line, further left is colder.

### Difference

How much warmer was Monday than Friday? From -4 up to 0 is 4 degrees, then up to 3 is 3 more: 7 degrees. Counting through zero in two steps avoids the error of 3 - 4 = -1.

### Change

On Saturday it was -2. By Sunday it was 1. By how much did it rise? 3 degrees: two steps up to 0, one more to 1.

The whole topic rests on one picture: a number line with zero in the middle. Negative numbers get smaller as they move left, which means -4 is less than -1 even though 4 is more than 1. A child who has the picture in their head answers these questions quickly and correctly; a child relying on the digits makes the same mistake every time.

Year 5 keeps negative numbers to whole numbers and to contexts like temperature, so the arithmetic is never hard. What is being tested is understanding, which is exactly what a five-option question can probe.

On a multiple-choice question, the wrong options are often the answers produced by the classic errors: 1 instead of 7 for the difference between 3 and -4, or -1 instead of 7 by subtracting the wrong way. A child who knows why those are wrong rejects them without calculating.

The same number-line picture helps elsewhere in Paper Two maths: sequences that count down past zero, and coordinates, which children meet more fully in Year 6.

Source: [national curriculum in England, mathematics](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/national-curriculum-in-england-mathematics-programmes-of-study/national-curriculum-in-england-mathematics-programmes-of-study), Year 5 number and place value (statutory requirements). ## Maths, then shapes: preparing for a shared paper

Paper Two mixes maths with non-verbal reasoning. We teach the maths part, and we prepare children for the switch.

### Nothing past Year 5

Because the assessment falls in September of Year 6, we build on what Year 5 teaches: place value, the four operations, fractions, measures, shape and data, each made reliable.

### Five options

With usually five choices per question, children learn to estimate first and reject options that cannot be right, before calculating.

### Short sections

A fifty-minute paper shared with non-verbal reasoning leaves limited time for maths. We practise short, timed maths sets so speed and accuracy go together.

We do not teach non-verbal reasoning. Families wanting help with it, or with Paper One's verbal reasoning, should look elsewhere; we say so at the start.

All the practice questions a child meets with us were written by our teachers. Quest's booklets stay Quest's, and we point families to them rather than copy them.

St Anselm's College's exam, as its FAQ describes it, includes a separate one-hour maths paper, and its list of things to bring mentions a scientific calculator. Because that FAQ is undated, families should check the current arrangements with the college directly. Our teaching covers written and mental methods either way.

Upton Hall School's 2027 test had no maths paper, so this page does not apply to it.

## What Wirral families ask us

### What is in the Wirral 11 plus?

Wirral Council says the assessment has two papers taken on the same day, with questions on verbal reasoning, non-verbal reasoning and mathematics. Quest says each lasts about 50 minutes, with Paper Two covering mathematics and non-verbal reasoning.

### Is the Wirral assessment multiple choice?

Yes. Quest says all questions are multiple choice, usually from five options, A to E, marked on an OMR sheet. The council says there are no long written answers.

### When do we register for September 2028 entry?

Wirral Council's timetable gives 1 May 2027 for registrations opening and 31 May 2027 as the deadline.

### When was the last assessment?

For September 2027 entry it was on Tuesday 15 September 2026, with results due on 21 October 2026.

### Which schools use the Wirral assessment?

Calday Grange Grammar School, West Kirby Grammar School, Wirral Grammar School for Boys and Wirral Grammar School for Girls.

### What about St Anselm's and Upton Hall?

They run their own tests. St Anselm's FAQ describes English, Maths and Verbal Reasoning papers of an hour each; Upton Hall's 2027 test was verbal reasoning and English, with no maths.

### What maths should my child know?

For a September test early in Year 6, the primary curriculum up to the end of Year 5: number, fractions, measures, geometry and statistics.

### Do you teach non-verbal reasoning?

No. We teach maths only, and we do not advise on applications or places.

### Do you use Quest papers?

No. We write our own questions and do not reproduce familiarisation material.
