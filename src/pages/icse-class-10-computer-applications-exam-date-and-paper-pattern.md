### Why the timetable comes too late to plan by

        A November timetable leaves roughly three months before the first paper. In that time a student cannot also learn arrays and strings for the first time and write twenty lab assignments. That is why our mini batch plans by the shape of the year: the Class 10 units in order from the start of the session, the lab file growing one program a week, and the winter kept for full papers.

### The fifteen minutes before writing starts

        The cover of the paper says that candidates may not write during the first 15 minutes, which are for reading. Computer Applications students should use that time to choose their four Section B programs out of six. In every full mock we rehearse this: read all six, choose four in under five minutes, then plan the order. A student who changes their mind halfway through a program loses time the paper does not give back.

## A 100-mark written paper and an internal assessment of equal weight

      Computer Applications is a Group III subject, which CISCE assesses half by its own external paper and half by internal assessment carried out in school. Both halves are worth 100 marks.

        - Part | What is asked | Marks

          - Section A, Question 1 | Twenty multiple-choice questions, one mark each, all compulsory | 20

          - Section A, Question 2 | Ten short questions of two marks: outputs, errors, Java expressions, rewriting code | 20

          - Section B, Questions 3 to 8 | Six programs offered, any four answered, 15 marks each | 60

          - Written paper total | Two hours of writing after 15 minutes of reading | 100

          - Internal assessment, subject teacher | Class design 10, variable description 10, coding and documentation 10, execution or output 20 | 50

          - Internal assessment, external examiner | The same four criteria, marked separately | 50

### Rules printed on Section B

          - Java in BlueJ or any Java environment. The examiner reads the program on paper; the IDE is not marked.

          - Variable description or mnemonic codes are expected with every program, so the logic is clear.

          - No flowcharts or algorithms are required.

          - No BufferedReader or DataInputStream. Input is taken with the Scanner class, as the specimen's own questions ask.

### Thinking skills the specimen labels

        The 2025 specimen tags every question with the thinking skill it tests: Recall, Understanding, Application or Analysis. Most of Section A is Understanding and Analysis, such as predicting an output or spotting why code fails, rather than recalling a definition. Every Section B program is tagged Understanding and Application.

        This is why a student who learns definitions by heart can still score poorly. Tuesday classes spend most of their time on tracing and analysis rather than on definitions. The paper's full design, solved question by question, is on [the specimen paper and mock tests page](/icse-class-10-computer-applications-specimen-paper-and-mock-tests).

## Six programs, six families, four of them to write

      The latest official specimen, for 2025, gives one program from each of six families. The 2027 syllabus is the same as 2026, so preparing every family keeps all six choices open on the day.

        - Specimen question | Family | What it tests | Syllabus unit

          - Question 3 | A class written to a specification | Member variables, an accept method with Scanner, a calculation from a rate table, display in a set format, and main | Class as the basis of all computation; user-defined methods

          - Question 4 | Searching | Binary search on a given sorted list of values | Arrays (search on single dimensional arrays)

          - Question 5 | String transformation | Converting case and replacing characters by a rule, keeping the others | String handling; Character methods

          - Question 6 | Double dimensional array | Accepting a 4 x 4 array and printing the sum of each row | Arrays (row, column and diagonal sums)

          - Question 7 | Number property | Checking a defined special number from its digits | Class IX revision: loops and digit extraction

          - Question 8 | Method overloading | Three versions of one method: a nested-loop pattern, a condition, and a formula | User-defined methods; polymorphism

## Five rules from the 2027 regulations and syllabus

### Internal marks cannot be improved later

        A student may take the Improvement Examination in up to two subjects in the same year, but only the theory paper is retaken; internal assessment marks already awarded are carried forward. The 100 lab marks are decided once, in school, which is the strongest reason to build the lab file properly from the start.

### The pass mark is 33% per subject

        CISCE's pass mark for each subject is 33%. In Computer Applications the written paper and the internal assessment both add to the subject result, so families aiming for a high percentage should treat the lab file as seriously as the paper.

### Who the external examiner can be

        The external examiner is chosen by the head of school and can come from the school's own staff, provided they are not the one teaching Computer Applications to your child's section. The syllabus gives the example of a computer teacher of Class VIII examining Class X. The examiner marks on the same four criteria as the subject teacher.

### Twenty lab assignments, and no set project

        What CISCE requires for 2027 is a record of at least twenty programming assignments written across the year. A separate project is not part of its scheme. When a school speaks of the computer project it usually means that finished record, though a few schools add a bigger program of their own, and their instructions decide that. The full plan is on [the project and internal assessment page](/icse-class-10-computer-applications-project-and-internal-assessment).

      A fifth rule decides how answers are written: Section B programs are expected with variable descriptions, and
      the internal assessment marks variable description as its own ten-mark criterion. A program without a
      variable description loses marks in both halves of the subject. Our students write the description table
      for every program from the first week, so by February it is a habit.

## Frequently asked questions

**When is the ICSE Computer Applications exam in 2027?** CISCE had not released the ICSE 2027 timetable when we checked on 13 October 2026, so there is no official date yet. Last year's timetable came out on 13 November 2025, and the 2026 examinations ran from 17 February to 30 March 2026. Expect the Computer Applications paper in the February to March 2027 window, and check cisce.org for the timetable.

**How long is the ICSE Computer Applications paper?** Two hours of writing, after 15 minutes in which candidates may read but not write. The paper is 100 marks. Students should use the reading time to choose their four Section B programs out of six, which our full mocks rehearse.

**What is the paper pattern for ICSE Class 10 Computer Applications?** Section A is compulsory and carries 40 marks: Question 1 has twenty one-mark multiple-choice questions and Question 2 has ten two-mark questions. In Section B students answer any four of six programs, Questions 3 to 8, worth 15 marks each, for 60 marks. A separate internal assessment carries another 100 marks.

**How are the internal assessment marks given?** On at least twenty lab assignments done during the year. Two people mark the record, the subject teacher and an external examiner, and each gives up to 50. Each 50 is made up of ten marks apiece for class design, the variable description and coding with documentation, plus twenty for whether the program runs and gives correct output. The head of school submits the combined mark out of 100 to CISCE.

**Can we use BufferedReader in the ICSE Computer Applications exam?** No. The specimen paper states that BufferedReader and DataInputStream should not be used in Section B programs. Input is taken with the Scanner class, which is how our students write every program from the first class.

**Is the ICSE 2027 Computer Applications syllabus changed?** No. The 2027 syllabus is the same as 2026: a revision of Class 9, then classes and objects, user-defined methods, constructors, library classes including wrapper classes, encapsulation, single and double dimensional arrays, and string handling. Recursion is not in the syllabus.

**Can Computer Applications marks be improved after the exam?** CISCE allows an Improvement Examination in up to two subjects in the same year. For a subject with internal assessment, only the theory paper is retaken and the internal marks already awarded are carried forward. So the 100 internal marks are decided once, in school.

**What is the pass mark in ICSE Computer Applications?** CISCE's pass mark is 33% for each subject. Computer Applications is a Group III subject, assessed half by the written paper and half by internal assessment, so a strong lab file supports the overall subject result.

**How do your live classes follow this calendar?** In India, the mini batch meets on Tuesday at 5:30 PM for Section A and theory and on Wednesday at 8 PM for Section B programs and the lab file. The target is twenty documented lab programs by November and every unit taught before the winter, then the specimen and three full two-hour papers. Doubts are answered live in every class. Outside India the same plan runs one to one.

**Is it too late to join for the 2027 ICSE exam?** Usually not, if your child joins soon. In October and November the mini batch can take a new student with catch-up help on the lab file. From December, personalised one to one is usually the better choice, because a private timetable can fit the remaining units, the lab programs and the full papers into the weeks left. The first class is a free demo.
