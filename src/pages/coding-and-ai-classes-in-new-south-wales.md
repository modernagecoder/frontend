Every NSW school publishes its terms as dates. Every planner, spreadsheet and app turns those dates into week numbers. And here is the trap our project is built on: depending on how you count, the same term starts and ends in different weeks. Term 1 for Eastern division schools in 2027 runs from Thursday 28 January to Friday 9 April. By the international ISO standard that is week 4 to week 14. By the obvious home-made method, day of the year divided by seven, it ends in week 15. Worse, the first three days of 2027 are not in 2027 at all by ISO reckoning: Friday 1 January belongs to week 53 of 2026. NSW learners write the code, find every disagreement and learn why international software insists on one definition.

## Which week does each 2027 term start and end in?

The NSW Department of Education's published 2027 term dates, turned into week numbers three different ways. Our calculation, checked day by day.

**NSW 2027 terms: ISO weeks against a home-made week count (our calculation)**

| Term (published dates) | ISO weeks | Home-made weeks | Weekdays |
|---|---|---|---|
| Term 1, Eastern: Thursday 28 January to Friday 9 April | 4 to 14 | 4 to 15 | 52 |
| Term 1, Western: Thursday 4 February to Friday 9 April | 5 to 14 | 5 to 15 | 47 |
| Term 2: Tuesday 27 April to Friday 2 July | 17 to 26 | 17 to 27 | 49 |
| Term 3: Monday 19 July to Friday 24 September | 29 to 38 | 29 to 39 | 50 |
| Term 4: Monday 11 October to Monday 20 December | 41 to 51 | 41 to 51 | 51 |

### 1. Count it the obvious way

Take the day of the year, subtract one, divide by seven and add one. It is simple, and across 2027 it disagrees with the ISO week on 157 of the year's 365 days.

### 2. Ask the standard

ISO 8601 starts weeks on Monday and calls week 1 the week that contains the year's first Thursday. Python's isocalendar does exactly that, and it says Friday 1 January 2027 is in week 53 of 2026.

### 3. Try the shortcuts

Python's strftime offers two more week numbers. The Sunday-start %U disagrees with ISO on 54 days of 2027; the Monday-start %W on only 3, the first three days of January, which it calls week 0.

### Weekdays are not school days

The weekday column counts Monday to Friday between the published first and last dates: 202 for the Eastern division across the four terms. It is not the number of days students attend. The department notes that its term dates include school development days, when students stay home, and public holidays fall inside some terms too. Learners have to say what their number counts before anyone uses it.

All week numbers and day counts on this page are our calculations from the department's published dates, made on 7 October 2026. The dates themselves are the department's; check its calendar page for any change before planning around them.

## Five habits for any program that handles dates

Learned on NSW school terms, then used on rosters, bookings, payroll periods and anything that says "week" in its column heading.

**How to stop week numbers from lying**

| Habit | What it looks like | What it prevents |
|---|---|---|
| Name the definition | Write "ISO week" or "school week", never just "week" | Two teams meaning different things by week 15 |
| Keep the year with the week | Store 2026-W53, not 53 | Filing 1 January 2027 under the wrong year |
| Use the library, then test it | Call isocalendar, then check it on 1 to 4 January | Trusting a function on the days it was not tried |
| Count what you mean | Separate weekdays, school days and calendar days | A plan built on days nobody attends |
| Re-read the source | Check the published dates again before relying on them | Planning against a calendar that has changed |

The second habit catches the most bugs. A spreadsheet that stores only the number 53 cannot tell week 53 of 2026 from a week that does not exist in 2027, which has only 52 ISO weeks. Storing the year beside the week fixes it for good.

## An AI tool will number the weeks for you. Why should a NSW student learn to check it?

Because "week 15" and "week 14" can describe the same Friday, and a confident tool will not say which it means.

Ask an assistant which week of 2027 Term 1 ends in and you may get 14 or 15, delivered with equal confidence, because the question never said which definition of a week it meant. Ask it to write a week-number function and it may hand back day-of-year arithmetic that is right for most of the year and wrong on 157 days. The code runs. The numbers look reasonable. The bug only shows when someone compares two systems that counted differently.

A student who has lined up three week-numbering methods against the NSW terms knows the questions to ask: which definition, which year does the week belong to, and what happens on 1 January. Those questions apply to every generated answer that involves dates, money periods or time zones, and they are exactly the questions an HSC Software Engineering student is expected to ask of their own code.

So the reason for a New South Wales student to learn to code in 2026 is not to out-type an AI tool. It is to be the person in the room who asks which week, and why. The longer argument is in [why teenagers should still learn to code in 2026](/blog/is-coding-worth-learning-2026).
