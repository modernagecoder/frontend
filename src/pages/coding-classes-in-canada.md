## Three cities, three national AI institutes.

The Government of Canada launched the Pan-Canadian Artificial Intelligence Strategy in 2017, and Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada names Amii, Mila and the Vector Institute as Canada’s three national AI institutes. We teach what sits underneath that work: Python, data and careful testing.

## Computing is taught differently in every province. We adapt.

Wherever your child goes to school, we set the plan beside the course that school teaches. We teach alongside the student’s school course and do not grant Ontario credits or credits in any other province. Each province below links to its own guide.

Ontario ONCoding in the Grade 1 to 8 math curriculum

Coding sits inside the Algebra strand of Ontario math, and high school adds the ICS computer studies courses. [Ontario guide](/coding-classes-in-ontario)

British Columbia BCADST · Applied Design, Skills, and Technologies

ADST modules arrive in rotations, and senior students can take Computer Programming 11 and 12. [BC guide](/coding-classes-in-british-columbia)

Québec QCQEP · 2026 digital framework

Classes in English on Quebec time, from Elementary 1 to CEGEP. [Quebec guide](/coding-classes-in-quebec)

Alberta ABCTS · Career and Technology Studies

The CTS computing science courses run as a chain, so arriving ready for each link matters. [Alberta guide](/coding-classes-in-alberta)

Manitoba MBComputer Science 20S to 40S

High school computer science runs from 20S to 40S, and the clock stays on UTC-5 from 1 November 2026. [Manitoba guide](/coding-classes-in-manitoba)

Saskatchewan SKComputer Science 20 and 30

FAQ

## Everything Canadian parents and learners ask in their first call.

Do classes run on my Canadian time zone?

Yes. Classes are set on your own clock: BC and Yukon stay on UTC-7 all year, Alberta, Saskatchewan and the Northwest Territories on UTC-6, Manitoba on UTC-5 from 1 November 2026, Ontario and Quebec on Eastern time, then Atlantic and Newfoundland time. Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays.

Do you follow my province’s curriculum?

We set each plan beside the course your child takes at school: coding in Ontario’s Grade 1 to 8 math curriculum, BC’s ADST and Computer Programming 11 and 12, Alberta’s CTS computing science courses, Quebec’s QEP, Manitoba’s Computer Science 20S to 40S, Saskatchewan’s Computer Science 20 and 30, and the courses in the other provinces and territories. We teach alongside the student’s school course and do not grant Ontario credits or credits in any other province. Tell us the course on the trial call.

Can you help with Waterloo, U of T, UBC, McGill or another Canadian CS programme?

We teach the programming and problem solving that first-year computer science assumes, prepare students for the CEMC’s Canadian Computing Competition, and explain each university’s published prerequisites in our guides to [Waterloo](/waterloo-computer-science-admission-requirements), [U of T](/uoft-computer-science-admission-requirements) and [UBC](/ubc-computer-science-admission-requirements). Admission decisions belong to the universities, so we make no admission promises.

Are classes taught in French?

Classes are taught in English, the language of most programming documentation. If your child learns math or science in French at school, tell us on the trial call so the teacher can connect the English terms to the ones used in class.

How are fees charged for Canadian families?

There is one monthly fee for eight live classes, two a week, in a group batch or 1-on-1. The pricing section shows the fee in your own currency. There is no registration fee, and you can cancel before the next billing cycle.

Are classes paused over Canadian school holidays?

You decide. You can stop for the December break, March Break or the summer by not renewing that month, with one message and no fee. Some families use a break for a focused project sprint instead; see our [March Break](/march-break-coding-camp-online) and [summer](/online-coding-summer-camp-canada) pages.

Will my child learn real AI?

Yes. Students build small AI apps from the ground up: prompting, structured output, retrieval, tool-calling agents and evaluation. The tools are chosen by age, so under-18s never use services that require users to be 18.

How is AI safety handled for younger children?

Under-13s never operate language-model tools on the open internet without a mentor present, and we choose tools by their own age rules: Claude is for users 18 and over, and ChatGPT needs a parent’s permission under 18. Parents see which tools are introduced each month.

Can siblings share a slot?

Siblings can join the same group batch when their levels are close; otherwise each does better in a class of their own. Ask on the trial call and we will suggest what fits.

How do we pay?

Payment is online by card when you enrol, billed monthly, with no joining fee. The pricing section shows the fee in your own currency.

Do you teach students from French-immersion and independent schools?

Yes. Tell us the course your child takes at school and the teacher plans around it, whatever kind of school it is, using the words your child already hears in class.

What's the cancellation policy?

The first trial is always free. After that, you can cancel before the next monthly billing cycle and you will not be charged again, with no fee and no contract. Refunds follow our [refund policy](/refund).

Inside a typical class

## A live one-hour lesson, minute by minute.

An example of ours: one 1-on-1 class for a Grade 9 Canadian student in their third month. Group classes follow the same shape.

00 to 03 min

#### Hi + warm-up

Three minutes of "how was the week", the human bit. Mentor scrolls back the chat history, cameras come on. Settles the student.

03 to 10 min

#### Recap and tiny quiz

A short recall test of last lesson. Not for marks, for the brain. The mentor watches for the lightbulb moments and the cobwebs.

10 to 22 min

#### New concept + live coding

About twelve minutes of new material. Live-shared VS Code. Mentor types two lines, student types the next two. Wrong moves welcome. Errors read aloud.

22 to 48 min

#### Project work · the heart of the class

The student leads. The mentor coaches. This is where the actual learning happens, feature shipped, bug fixed, function refactored. The mentor types nothing; only asks.

48 to 56 min

#### Code review & reflection

Several minutes to look back. What's good? What could be cleaner? The student names one thing they're proud of. This bit matters more than it sounds.

56 to 60 min

#### Next steps + goodbye

Mentor writes a 4-line note to the parent: what was covered, how the student felt, optional homework, plan for next class. Cameras off. Done.

Common Canadian parent worries

## Six honest answers to the questions Canadian parents type into Google at midnight.

The quiet ones. We'd rather just answer them in advance.

"My child already has too much screen time. Won't this make it worse?"

It’s a fair worry. Classes are one hour, twice a week, and most of that time the child is making something, not watching. There is a real difference between creating and scrolling, and the class is built around the first.

"Will my child learn fake 'kid Python' and not real programming?"

No. We start in Scratch for the youngest learners (Grade 1 to 3) and only as a bridge. Within the first months students write real, runnable Python that imports real libraries, handles real errors and pushes to a real GitHub repo. We deliberately avoid "Python for kids" tools that hide the real language behind a friendly mask.

"What if my child loses interest after a month? Am I locked in?"

No lock-in. You can cancel any month before the next billing cycle, no fee, no awkward conversation, no exit interview. If your child loses interest, we'd rather hear about it early and either pause for a few weeks or pivot the project track to something more exciting (game design, music programming, AI art) than have you grit your teeth through a year you paid for.

"Is AI safe for my 9-year-old? I've read worrying things."

Under-13s never use language-model tools on the open internet without a mentor present, and we follow each tool’s own age rules. The AI projects your child builds are educational and reviewable by you, prompt by prompt.

"My child is shy, won't a 1:1 video lesson be too much?"

Shy children often do well here. The first lesson is built around the code, not the camera: your child looks at VS Code, not at a stranger’s face. Mentors give long pauses, no pressure, and let the screen do the talking.

"Is this another bootcamp pretending to be a school?"

No. Bootcamps compress 12 weeks of intense, transactional study aiming at a job. We're the opposite: a slow, year-round mentorship that respects how Canadian school years actually work. We don't promise jobs after 12 weeks. We promise a real codebase, a real GitHub history, a real mentor relationship and a real shift in your child's relationship with maths, logic and computing.
