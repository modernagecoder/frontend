## Eight mukims, and the largest of them would be a small town anywhere else

The shape of the district is the whole argument, so it is worth setting out properly rather than gesturing at.

Tutong covers **1,166** square kilometres and recorded **47,210** residents at the 2021 census. Tutong Town, or Pekan Tutong, is the administrative centre. The 137 kilometre Tutong River runs the length of the district, **Bukit Bedawan** at 529 metres is its highest point, and **Tasek Merimbun**, Brunei's largest natural lake, has been an **ASEAN Heritage Park** since 29 November 1984. The population is Tutong, Dusun, Kedayan, Iban and Chinese.

## The midrange is not the median

Every page in this cluster works through one analytical mistake. Tutong's is a summary statistic that throws away most of the data by construction, and almost nobody notices because it has the word middle in its definition.

The learner is handed the eight mukim figures above and asked a reasonable question: how big is a typical mukim in Tutong?

A common first move is to take the halfway point between the largest and the smallest. Telisai has 13,253 and Rambai has 980, so halfway is 7,116.5. That number is called the midrange, it is a single keystroke in any spreadsheet, and it feels like the middle because it sits exactly between the extremes.

The trap

Five of the eight mukims are below that number

Three mukims are larger than 7,116.5: Telisai, Pekan Tutong and Keriam. The other five, Kiudang, Tanjong Maya, Lamunin, Ukong and Rambai, are all smaller, and the last two by a very wide margin. So the number offered as typical is above most of the things it is meant to describe, and nothing in the arithmetic warned anybody about that.

The learner then computes the two statistics that use all the data. The median, with eight values, is the average of the fourth and fifth once they are sorted: 3,772 and 5,063, giving **4,417.5**. The mean is the total divided by eight: 47,210 over 8, giving **5,901.25**. So three defensible-looking answers to one question, spread from 4,417.5 to 7,116.5, and the one that took the least effort is the outlier among them. To see how much that spread matters, note that the 2,699 between the midrange and the median is larger than the entire recorded population of Ukong, and nearly three times that of Rambai. The disagreement between two summary statistics is bigger than two of the places being summarised.

Then comes the demonstration that makes it stick. Delete the largest mukim and the smallest, Telisai and Rambai, and recompute both. The midrange becomes the halfway point between Pekan Tutong's 9,883 and Ukong's 2,029, which is 5,956: it has moved by more than a thousand. The median of the six remaining values is the average of 3,772 and 5,063, which is **4,417.5, exactly what it was before**. Not approximately. Identically.

That is the whole lesson in one line. **The midrange is computed entirely from the two most extreme values in the set, and the median is computed from everything except them.** The midrange is not a robust statistic that happens to be sensitive to outliers. It is a statistic made of nothing but outliers. And the reason it survives in real reports is that it is cheap, it sounds like the middle, and nobody checks how many of the underlying cases fall on each side of it. Counting them takes about ten seconds and settles the matter every time.

## Does coding still matter when AI can write it?

Here is a test worth two minutes of your evening. Give any assistant the eight Tutong figures and ask it for the typical size of a mukim in the district.

You will almost certainly get a fluent, well-structured answer. What you are unlikely to be told, unprompted, is which of the three possible numbers it chose, why that one, and how many of the eight mukims actually fall below the figure it just gave you. Ask it directly and it will usually explain all three correctly, because the statistics are not hard and it knows them. The gap is not knowledge. The gap is that nobody asked.

That is the honest description of where these tools sit. They answer the question they were given, extremely well, and they do not spontaneously interrogate whether it was the right question. Somebody still has to be the person who says wait, how many cases does that actually describe. Building that reflex is slow, it is done by being wrong repeatedly on data you can check, and it is the point of the project above.

### There is nothing like this near us in Tutong. Is online really equivalent?

For this subject it is better, and the district explains why. A worthwhile class needs five to eight learners at a similar level and a similar age, in the same subject, free at the same hour. In a district of 47,210 people whose largest mukim holds 13,253, the pool that satisfies all four conditions at once is usually empty. Online, the pool is our entire intake across twenty-five countries, so a learner is grouped with people at their level rather than with whoever else turned up.

### My child will go to Tutong Sixth Form Centre. When should they start?

Well before then. Sixth form is where a subject stops being an introduction and starts being a specialism, and a learner who arrives having already built three or four working things is in a different position from one who arrives having read about them. The years to use are the ones with no national certificate at the end. By the time A Level material begins, the sensible advice is usually to narrow what they are doing rather than to add to it.
