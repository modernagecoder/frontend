Qurayyat today

A coast of very unequal harbours

Qurayyat runs from a substantial fishing town down to landing points of a few boats, and that spread is what makes any league table here misleading.

A working fishing town

Qurayyat is known for its traditional fishing boats and for a quiet working waterfront looking out over the sea. A place where the boats are counted every day generates exactly the kind of small daily numbers this page is about.

Small landings along the same shore

The wilayat's coast carries settlements far smaller than the town, including the Dabab area where the Bimmah Sinkhole lies. Put a three boat landing and a town fleet in the same table and one column of that table stops meaning anything.

A history of standing against a fleet

The town's past is marked by fierce resistance to Portuguese invasion. That is a piece of history worth knowing here for its own sake, and a reminder that a place's size and its significance are two separate measurements.

A percentage hides the number it came from

Turning a count into a rate is a lossy operation. Thirty three per cent could be one boat out of three or a thousand out of three thousand, and once the conversion has happened nothing downstream can tell the difference. That is why the base belongs in the table beside the rate, every time.

Verified local facts

Qurayyat detail with a trail behind it

Located, measured and described, with the scope of each claim stated.

A sinkhole with a measured depth

The Bimmah Sinkhole lies in the Dabab area of Wilayat Qurayyat, around 600 metres inland from the coast, and is described as a pool roughly 65 feet deep. Geologists have confirmed it is a sinkhole; local legend holds that a meteorite made it.

Fresh and salt water in the same pool

The sinkhole holds fresh and salt water in roughly equal proportions, which is what gives it its turquoise colour. Two things mixed in one measurement is a good physical model for what a single averaged figure does to two populations.

A legend and a finding, side by side

The meteorite story and the geological confirmation coexist locally. A dataset that has room for both, with the source of each, is more useful than one that silently keeps only the confirmed version.

Part of Muscat Governorate

Qurayyat is one of the six wilayats of Muscat Governorate, alongside Muscat, Muttrah, Seeb, Bawshar and Al Amarat. Being counted inside the capital governorate while sitting well outside the city is its own kind of attribution problem.

Turquoise water and a coastal road

The coast draws visitors as well as supporting fishing, so demand here has two shapes laid over each other. Two audiences on one shoreline is two series that should not be summed without saying so.

A quiet town with a busy past

The description of Qurayyat as a quiet fishing village today sits beside its record of resistance. A place changes, and any figure about it needs the year attached before it means anything at all.

Nothing above implies a relationship with Modern Age Coders and none exists. Qurayyat is used because a coast with harbours of hugely different size makes the small numbers problem visible without any need to invent a scenario.

Signature project

One boat at Daghmar beats sixty at the town

Four landing points, one percentage column, and a ranking that rewards being small rather than being busy.

1. Count the boats honestly

Four landing points along the same coast: three boats, four, five, and a town fleet of a hundred and eighty. The learner records the counts, not the percentages, because a percentage has already thrown away the thing that matters.

2. Add one boat everywhere and compute the change

One extra boat is a 33.3 per cent rise at the three boat landing, 25 per cent at the four, 20 per cent at the five, and 0.6 per cent at the town. Every figure is correct and the column is now useless as a ranking.

3. Work out what the town would need

For Qurayyat town to post the same 33 per cent it would need about sixty more boats. The small landing did it with one. Anybody reading only the percentage column concludes the wrong thing about where the activity is.

The ranking is correct and it answers the wrong question

Every percentage above was computed before this table was written and every one is right. What is wrong is using them to rank. A percentage change measures movement relative to a base, so places with tiny bases will always crowd the top and the bottom of any such table, and they will swap places again next month for reasons that mean nothing.

Reliable pipeline

Publish the base, set a floor, and say what you did

Three habits that turn a misleading table into an honest one, none of which takes more than a line.

Reporting a rate computed on small counts
StageRuleWhat it preventsWhat it gives you
KeepStore the count, derive the rate at the endA percentage with its base thrown awayThe ability to recompute anything
ShowPrint the base beside every rateA 33 per cent nobody can sizeA reader who can judge it
FloorSet a minimum count before a place enters a rankingThe smallest place topping every tableA league that means something
BandGroup very small places rather than rank themMonth to month noise read as movementA stable comparison
Say itState the floor and the banding in the outputA reader recomputing and disagreeingA method somebody can check

For younger learners

Three counters in one pile and a hundred and eighty in another. Add one to each and ask which pile changed more. Children answer both ways and then argue about which answer was the question, which is the entire lesson.

For teenagers

Python computes the percentage change for every landing point, ranks them, then applies a minimum count and ranks again. The learner explains why the two orders differ and which one they would put in front of somebody making a decision.

For adults

Rates carry their denominator into every report, rankings apply a documented floor, and places below the floor are banded together rather than listed individually with numbers that will not repeat.

The boat counts in the project are teaching values, because no public per-landing-point record exists, and the page says so. The behaviour is a property of small denominators everywhere.

FAQ

Qurayyat coding class questions

Local facts, project method and service terms, kept apart.

Are the boat numbers in the project real?

No, and the page says so. The sinkhole, its depth and position, and Qurayyat's place among the six wilayats of Muscat Governorate are published. The boat counts are teaching values because no public per-landing-point record exists, and the arithmetic behaves the same on any counts you substitute.

Why is ranking by percentage change wrong here?

Because a percentage measures movement against a base, so a small base produces large percentages from tiny events. One extra boat at a three boat landing is 33 per cent; the town needs about sixty boats for the same headline. A table sorted that way will always be topped by the smallest place and will reshuffle next month for no reason.

Is there a Modern Age Coders centre in Qurayyat?

No. No premises are claimed in Qurayyat or anywhere in Muscat Governorate. Classes are live online and need a laptop or desktop, working audio, a current browser and a stable connection. The page describes where the service reaches, not a building.

What is the simplest fix?

Print the base beside the rate. That one habit lets any reader size a percentage for themselves and it costs one extra column. After that, set a minimum count before a place enters a ranking, and say in the output what the minimum was.

Can an adult here study rather than a school student?

Yes, up to 67. Adults take Python, databases, data analysis and automation. Anybody who publishes a comparison between places or branches or months will recognise this immediately, and their own table is the best exercise provided the data is public or theirs to use.

What does a learner have at the end?

A ranking computed two ways from one dataset, a documented minimum count, and the habit of never letting a percentage travel without its denominator. That last one prevents more bad decisions than any other single rule in reporting.
