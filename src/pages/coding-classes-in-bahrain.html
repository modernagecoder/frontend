Bahrain checks the school's homework, and that is the whole idea

The short answer

Bahrain has an independent authority, BQA, that examines students at Grade 9 and Grade 12 against the national curriculum separately from their own school, and separately reviews the schools themselves. Modern Age Coders teaches live online coding and mathematics to ages 6 to 67 in Bahrain, in groups of five to eight or one to one, at USD 100 and USD 150 a month. The first class is free.

Most education systems ask a school to mark its own work. Bahrain does not, and that single structural choice is more useful to a parent than any brochure. It means there is an outside number to compare against the inside one, and the gap between them is where the real information lives.

Modern Age Coders teaches from India. Bahrain runs two and a half hours behind, so the slot a family wants after school is still a working evening at the other end. Lessons are online and no Bahrain branch is claimed. If model training rather than programming foundations is what you are looking for, that is a separate page: AI and machine learning classes in Bahrain.

An authority whose job is to check, not to teach

Two separate functions, both independent of the institution being measured.

Bahrain's Education and Training Quality Authority, usually written BQA, does two things that matter to a family choosing anything extra for a child.

The first is examination. BQA runs national examinations at Grade 9 and at Grade 12, testing skills and knowledge against Bahrain's national curriculum, and it assesses student performance independently of the school the student attends. The second is review. BQA inspects institutions and publishes what it finds, so a school's performance is a matter of public record rather than a claim in a prospectus.

Its own sector performance report for the 2025 to 2026 academic year gives the scale. BQA reviewed 172 of the 547 institutions within its scope, which it states as 31 per cent, made up as follows.

Why an independent examiner changes what a parent should look at

When the same body teaches and marks, a good report tells you the school is pleased. When a different body examines, a good result and a good report are two pieces of information rather than one. If a learner is strong in class and weaker on an external paper, that gap is the most useful signal available, and it usually points at reasoning under unfamiliar conditions rather than at effort.

That is the specific thing a coding class can address, and the reason this page leads with it rather than with a course list. Reasoning under unfamiliar conditions is what programming is. A learner is handed a problem they have not seen, has to break it into parts, has to be wrong in a way they can detect, and has to fix it. Nobody marks their own work in that process either; the program either runs or it does not.

The student data project

One number, six different kinds of thing

Every market page here works through one analytical trap. Bahrain's comes straight out of the figures above.

The learner is given BQA's own published breakdown and asked a reasonable question: what share of Bahrain's education institutions were reviewed this year? The answer is printed in the report. One hundred and seventy-two of five hundred and forty-seven, which is 31 per cent. The arithmetic is correct and the source is primary. The learner has a defensible headline in about a minute.

Then they are asked to write the sentence that goes under the headline, and the whole thing falls apart.

The trap

A kindergarten and a university are not one unit

The 172 is not 172 of one thing. It is 50 government schools, 22 private schools, 51 kindergartens, 24 special education institutions, 22 vocational providers and 3 higher education institutions. Six populations, each with a different size, a different total in scope, and a different meaning attached to being reviewed.

So the 31 per cent answers a question nobody actually asked. A parent wants to know what share of schools were reviewed. A vocational trainer wants to know about vocational providers. Neither can get their answer from the single figure, and both would be misled by it: the kindergartens alone are 51 of the 172, so nearly a third of the headline is about institutions that most people reading the phrase "education institutions" were not picturing.

The fix is not more arithmetic. It is refusing to divide until you can say what the numerator and the denominator are made of. The learner ends up writing six rates instead of one, discovering that they cannot complete four of them because the report gives reviewed counts but not the in-scope totals per type, and then writing that limitation down instead of hiding it. That last step is the actual lesson.

This is deliberately different from a units problem. Adding tonnes to cubic metres is one quantity measured two ways. This is six different entities counted as though they were the same entity, which is harder to see precisely because every number involved is an integer and looks clean.

The question parents ask first

Will AI replace programmers before my child finishes school?

Bahrain already answered a version of this question at national scale, and the answer is the reason BQA exists.

A country could have let every school report its own results. It built an independent examiner instead, because a claim needs checking by somebody other than the party making it. An AI is a party making claims. Hand it BQA's table and ask for the share of institutions reviewed and it will return 31 per cent, formatted well, with no mention that the figure mixes kindergartens with universities. It is not lying. It answered the question asked, and the question was wrong.

The work that remains is deciding what to ask, noticing that the denominator is six things, and being unwilling to publish a rate you cannot describe. That is judgement, it is learnable, and it is what the project above trains. The syntax is the part the machine took.

Do the BQA national examinations affect what we should study?

They tell you something the school's own marks cannot. BQA examines students at Grade 9 and at Grade 12 against Bahrain's national curriculum, independently of the school. If a learner does well in class but less well on an external paper, that gap is the useful signal, and it usually points at reasoning rather than effort. That is the part a coding class can genuinely help with.

My child is at an Indian curriculum school in Bahrain on CBSE. Does that match what you teach?

Directly. Modern Age Coders teaches the CBSE Class 9 to 12 computer science sequence in India every day, so the vocabulary and the style of question are already familiar to the teachers. A learner in Bahrain on that board joins an existing group rather than becoming a special case.

No. No Bahrain office, branch or classroom is claimed. Every lesson is live online from India. A learner needs a laptop or desktop, stable internet, working audio and a current browser. Is coding still worth learning now that AI can write it?

Yes, and Bahrain's own system shows why. The country built an independent authority precisely because a claim needs checking by somebody other than the party making it. An AI makes claims. It writes a confident answer at the same speed whether the answer is right or wrong. Knowing how to check it is the skill, and it is the one the class trains.

What age should a child start, and do you teach adults?

Modern Age Coders teaches ages 6 to 67. A six or seven year old works on sequencing and precise counting before any syntax. A Grade 8 or 9 learner is usually ready for Python or a specific mathematics gap. Adults come for reporting, automation and data work, and they are a normal part of the intake.

My child finished a Scratch course. What comes next?

The free class answers that rather than the course title. Some learners who have completed blocks are ready for Python. Others have been dragging blocks without ever needing to name a variable, and going straight to syntax would waste a term. The teacher watches a short task and decides from what the learner actually does.
