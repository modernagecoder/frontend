The Ontario computer science pathway, from ICD2O to ICS4U

Ontario high school computer science is a short ladder with two rails: a university rail, ICS3U then ICS4U, and a college rail, ICS3C then ICS4C, with an open Grade 10 course in front of both. Beside the ladder runs the math line that universities care about most. This page puts every course on one map with the prerequisites the curriculum lists, shows four routes students commonly take, and sets the map against what two universities ask for. We teach students at every step of it, beside their school courses; we do not grant Ontario credits.

The pathway in brief

The Ontario computer science pathway runs from ICD2O in Grade 10, which needs no prerequisite, to ICS3U or ICS3C in Grade 11, then ICS4U or ICS4C in Grade 12, each needing its Grade 11 partner. Modern Age Coders teaches along it live online, one to one or in a batch, through Ontario Computer Science Companion.

Reading the map

Only two arrows on the map are real requirements: ICS3U to ICS4U, and ICS3C to ICS4C. Everything else is open. A student can start computer studies in Grade 11 with no Grade 10 course at all, and the Grade 10 course itself needs nothing before it.

That makes the Grade 11 choice the one that matters. Picking ICS3U keeps ICS4U open for Grade 12; picking ICS3C opens ICS4C instead. Our ICS3U guide covers the university Grade 11 course in depth.

Why ICS2O is in the last row

ICS2O is the Grade 10 course described in the 2008 document. The computer studies list on the current Ontario curriculum website shows ICD2O for Grade 10 and does not include ICS2O. We found no source stating that one replaced the other, so the map lists both and says where each appears.

Every course in the 2008 table carries the same value, stated in a note beneath it: “Each of the courses listed above is worth one credit.”

Sources: The Ontario Curriculum, Grades 10 to 12: Computer Studies (2008), course table and prerequisite chart; and the course list the Ontario curriculum website serves today (its content feed, read 22 October 2026). Modern Age Coders has no tie to the ministry and speaks only for itself.

The curriculum inside the courses

How the strands change from Grade 10 to Grade 12

Families searching for the Ontario computer science curriculum usually want to know what is taught in each year. Each course is organised into strands, and the 2008 document explains the pattern: “Each of the Grade 11 and 12 courses has four strands.” Laid side by side, the strands show how the subject grows.

The pattern in the curriculum’s words

From Grade 11 on, the first two strands keep the same names in all four courses, so the work deepens rather than changes. For the third, the document says: “The third strand (strand C) in each of the courses focuses on the programming environment, and the Grade 12 university preparation course features Designing Modular Programs in this strand.”

The fourth strand covers computers in society, and on the university rail it reaches further: “In this strand, the university preparation courses also explore new research in the field of information and computer science, and its implications for society.”

What a student feels, year by year

Grade 10 is broad: a little programming, a lot about the technology around the student. Grade 11 is the programming year, with arrays, functions, testing and a first project. Grade 12 on the university rail is the design year, with classes, recursion, analysis and a team project; on the college rail it is the year of object-oriented programs with graphical interfaces.

That is why the jump from Grade 11 to Grade 12 feels larger than the jump from Grade 10 to Grade 11. The names of strands A and B stay the same, but what they ask for does not.

The route that needs a fix

A fifth pattern is common enough to plan for: a student takes ICS3C in Grade 11, then decides on university computer science and wants ICS4U. The course table lists ICS3U as the prerequisite for ICS4U, so ICS3C alone does not open it.

The fix, if the school’s timetable allows, is to take ICS3U before ICS4U. The plan checker further down this page catches exactly this case.

Where route 2 needs care

A late start is allowed, but Grade 11 then carries the whole load of learning to program. In our experience, students starting ICS3U with no Grade 10 course cope far better when they write small programs over the summer before Grade 11, so that variables and loops are familiar by September.

The same applies to route 4 in reverse: a taster year in Grade 11 can turn into a Grade 12 course after all, as long as it was ICS3U or ICS3C, because each opens its own Grade 12 partner.

The rail beside the ladder

The math line: MCR3U, MHF4U and MCV4U

For university computer science, the math courses beside the computer studies ladder matter more than the ladder itself. Their prerequisites come from the same Ontario curriculum website, and they are stricter than the computer studies ones.

Grade 11 and 12 university math courses and their prerequisites, from the Ontario curriculum website’s course records (read 22 October 2026)
CodeCoursePrerequisite, as the record gives it
MCR3UFunctions, Grade 11“Principles of Mathematics, Grade 10, Academic”
MHF4UAdvanced Functions, Grade 12“Functions, Grade 11, University Preparation, or Mathematics for College Technology, Grade 12, College Preparation”
MCV4UCalculus and Vectors, Grade 12The prerequisite field is empty; the description says “The new Advanced Functions course (MHF4U) must be taken prior to or concurrently with Calculus and Vectors (MCV4U).”
MDM4UMathematics of Data Management, Grade 12“Functions, Grade 11, University Preparation, or Functions and Applications, Grade 11, University/College Preparation”

What the math line means for timing

MHF4U needs MCR3U first, and MCV4U needs MHF4U before it or alongside it. So a student who wants both Grade 12 courses needs Functions done by the end of Grade 11. The rule allows both in the same Grade 12 year, or MHF4U a term earlier so that Calculus and Vectors has a term to itself.

MCR3U in turn names a Grade 10 academic math course as its prerequisite. The current website lists a de-streamed Grade 9 math course, MTH1W, issued in 2021, so ask the school how that Grade 10 prerequisite applies to your teen’s own math sequence.

Why the math line comes first

Both universities on this page list Grade 12 math by name for computer science and neither lists a Grade 12 computer course. A student who misses MCR3U in Grade 11 can find the university rail closed in Grade 12, whatever they did in computer studies.

So when we help a family plan, we draw the math line first and fit computer studies around it. Our math tutoring for Canada covers these courses.

What the table says

The only computer course either university names is ICS3U, and only Waterloo names it, as a recommendation. ICS4U appears on neither page. Math and English carry the requirements at both.

ICS4U still has a place: as a 4U course it can fill one of the six Grade 12 university slots, which on our reading is what Waterloo’s “One other 4U course” allows. More importantly, it prepares a student for the pace of a first-year computer science course.

Two smaller lines worth noticing

U of T’s page adds: “The co-op component of Grade 12 courses will not be used for admission purposes”. Co-op credits count toward the diploma but not toward that admission average.

And the college rail sits outside both tables: ICS4C is a college preparation course, so it is not one of the 4U/M courses U of T counts. That is no criticism of ICS4C; it is built for a different destination. Requirements change each cycle, so check the live pages: Waterloo Computer Science and U of T Computer Science.

A worked example, ours

A prerequisite checker in Python: test a course plan before you choose it

Prerequisite rules are data, and data can be checked by a program. We wrote this checker for this page: it stores each rule in a dictionary and walks a plan term by term. It is also a neat example of the problem solving the Grade 11 and 12 courses teach. It was run in Python 3.13.

  1. Write each rule as data. Two dictionaries: one for courses that must be finished in an earlier term, one for a course that may run alongside its partner. MCV4U goes in the second, because the curriculum says MHF4U may be taken “prior to or concurrently” with it.
  2. Represent the plan in time order. A list of terms, each a list of course codes. Using terms instead of grades lets a semestered school put ICS3U and ICS4U in the same year, one after the other.
  3. Record when each course is taken. One pass builds a dictionary from course code to term number.
  4. Check every rule against that record. A course with a must-come-earlier rule fails if the needed course is missing or in the same or a later term. A course with a may-come-alongside rule fails only if the needed course is missing or later.
  5. Report problems in plain words. The function returns a list of sentences. An empty list means the plan works.

Where the checker simplifies

The MHF4U record also accepts Mathematics for College Technology, Grade 12, as an alternative prerequisite, and our rule lists only MCR3U. Adding it means letting a rule hold a choice of courses rather than one: a good extension exercise for a Grade 11 student.

The checker also knows nothing about what a school actually offers, or about equivalent credits a student may hold. It tests the rules on this page and nothing more.

Why write it at all

Because turning written rules into data is a core programming skill. The two dictionaries are the rules; the loop never changes when a rule is added. That separation is the modular design ICS4U asks for, in twenty lines.

Students who build this, then extend it, practise dictionaries, nested loops, edge cases and clear output in one small project that answers a question their family actually has.

OSSD, half credits and timing

ICS3U and ICS4U on the OSSD: credits, groups and half credits

Families searching for OSSD ICS3U or OSSD ICS4U usually want to know how the course counts toward the Ontario Secondary School Diploma. Here is what the sources say, and one thing we cannot do.

How the courses count

Each course on the 2008 table is worth one credit. On the current graduation requirements page, computer studies is one of the subjects students can use for the STEM-related course group, if they started Grade 9 in 2024 or later, or for Group 3, if they started in 2023 or earlier.

The 2008 Computer Studies document already pointed to Group 3 when it was written, in a footnote that lists computer studies beside technological education, an additional science course and cooperative education as ways to meet that requirement.

Half credits

The courses in the 2008 document may be delivered as two half credits, “with the exception of the Grade 12 university preparation course”, which is ICS4U. Half-credit courses carry “a minimum of fifty-five hours of scheduled instructional time”.

There is one condition worth knowing if ICS3U is split: “students must successfully complete both parts of the course to fulfil the prerequisite”. Half of ICS3U does not open ICS4U.

Where the OSSD credit comes from is simple: the school, night school, summer school or Ontario credit provider that teaches and evaluates the course. We are a teaching school working beside those courses, and we do not grant Ontario credits. For the current diploma rules, the source is Ontario’s graduation requirements page, updated September 02, 2026.

Planning with your teen

Planning the pathway in Grade 9 or 10: five steps

These steps are ours. They take about an evening, and they make the course selection form in Grade 10 much easier.

  1. Write down two possible destinations. One likely, one possible. For example, university computer science and a college IT program. The plan should keep both open as long as it can.
  2. Draw the math line first. If any destination is university computer science, plan Functions in Grade 11 and both Advanced Functions and Calculus and Vectors in Grade 12.
  3. Choose the Grade 11 computer course. ICS3U keeps ICS4U open; ICS3C opens ICS4C. If the destinations disagree, the counsellor can say which choice leaves more doors open at your school.
  4. Decide about Grade 10. ICD2O is optional. It helps most for students who have not written code before and plan ICS3U.
  5. Check the plan, then book a counsellor meeting. Run it past the rules on this page, or the checker above, then confirm with the school, which knows what it offers and when.

Pathway questions

Ontario computer science pathway: common questions

What computer science courses are there in Ontario high schools?

The current Ontario curriculum website lists five computer studies courses: ICD2O in Grade 10, ICS3U and ICS3C in Grade 11, and ICS4U and ICS4C in Grade 12. The 2008 curriculum document also describes ICS2O, a Grade 10 course not on the current list.

Do I need ICD2O to take ICS4U?

No. ICS4U needs ICS3U, and ICS3U has no prerequisite. ICD2O is optional, though it helps students who have never coded before.

Can I take ICS4U without ICS3U?

The course table in the 2008 curriculum lists ICS3U as the prerequisite for ICS4U, and the current curriculum website shows the same. Ask the school about any equivalent it accepts.

Can I take ICS3U and ICS4U in the same year?

Only if ICS3U is finished first. In a semestered school, ICS3U in the first semester and ICS4U in the second can work, if the school offers that timetable.

Does ICS3U or ICS4U count toward the OSSD?

Each is worth one credit. Ontario's graduation requirements page lists computer studies in the STEM-related course group for students who started Grade 9 in 2024 or later, and in Group 3 for those who started in 2023 or earlier.

Can ICS3U or ICS4U be taken as half credits?

ICS3U can be delivered as two half credits, and both parts must be completed to fulfil the prerequisite for ICS4U. ICS4U, the Grade 12 university preparation course, is the one course in the 2008 document that cannot be split.

Which math courses go with computer science?

For university computer science, plan Functions (MCR3U) in Grade 11, then Advanced Functions (MHF4U) and Calculus and Vectors (MCV4U) in Grade 12. The curriculum says MHF4U must be taken before or at the same time as MCV4U.

Do Waterloo or U of T require ICS4U?

On 22 October 2026 neither university's computer science page listed ICS4U. Waterloo recommended Grade 11 U Introduction to Computer Science, and both listed Grade 12 math and English. Check the pages in the year you apply.

Do you grant credits along the pathway?

No. We teach beside the school courses and do not grant Ontario credits. Credits come from the school or credit provider that teaches and evaluates each course.

When are classes held?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Most of Ontario keeps Eastern time, and we confirm each class time in yours.

How much do classes cost?

The first class is free. After that families pay a monthly fee and can stop after any month. The fees section on this page shows the current figure.
