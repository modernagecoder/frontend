Online coding and AI classes in the Moquegua region

Moquegua is one of Peru's smallest regions by population and one of its strongest in school results. Many homes already own a computer, and the regional government is placing tens of thousands of laptops and tablets in schools. What the school week still lacks is programming, and the chance to use English for real work. That is where our live online classes, taught in English, fit. This page sets out the evidence, explains who in Moquegua and Ilo gains most, and uses the regional university's own student data to teach a trap that catches journalists and politicians alike.

How well do Moquegua pupils do at school?

Among the strongest in Peru. MINEDU's 2025 census evaluation puts Moquegua at or near the top in both maths and reading.

Where Moquegua stands

In fourth grade, 52.0 per cent of Moquegua pupils reached the expected maths level in 2025; only Tacna, at 54.6, was higher. In reading, 48.8 per cent did, just behind Tacna's 49.0.

In second year of secundaria, Moquegua's 31.1 per cent in reading was the highest figure for any department in the table, ahead of Tacna (30.8) and Arequipa (29.0). Its 24.6 per cent in maths was second to Tacna's 27.7, and close to double the national 12.7.

What that means for us

Many Moquegua pupils are ready for more than the school week offers. They can handle algorithms, real programming projects and contest-style problems earlier than the average learner.

So our advice here differs from most regions. Rather than starting gently, we often place Moquegua teenagers straight into typed Python, then algorithms or web development, and offer olympiad-style maths alongside for those who enjoy it.

Even here, three quarters of second-year secundaria pupils did not reach the satisfactory level in maths in 2025. A strong regional figure says nothing about any single child, which is why the free class always begins with a short level check. The TIC 2 project

On 27 October 2026 the Gobierno Regional de Moquegua reported delivering 174 laptops to UGEL General Sánchez Cerro under its TIC 2 project, which it describes as "más de 30 mil equipos informáticos entre laptop y tablet, a favor de 180 instituciones educativas".

The same note lists the parts: 10,413 laptops for primaria, of which 8,005 had been delivered and 2,408 were still to come; 12,060 laptops for secundaria pupils; 2,354 for teachers; and 5,272 tablets for primaria pupils.

A device is not a curriculum

A laptop on every desk makes typed programming possible. It does not, on its own, teach anyone to program. The national timetable still has no programming hours, so the machine is often used for documents, research and presentations.

For a Moquegua family, that means the hardware question may already be solved at school, and sometimes at home: 45.3 per cent of households owned a computer in 2025, well above Peru's 33.9. The live mentor is the missing part.

Check with the school before using a school laptop at home; some are lent for home use, others stay in class. Our lessons need a working browser, a microphone and a steady connection, which 70.5 per cent of Moquegua households had at home in 2025.

Districts and towns

In 2025 the district of Ilo had 69,376 residents, Moquegua district 49,521 and neighbouring San Antonio 28,389, followed by Samegua 10,440 and Torata 6,376. In the highland province, Omate had 3,412 and Ichuña 3,312.

For 2017, INEI's table of department capitals gives Moquegua city 69,882 people. Its list of centros poblados that year puts Ilo at 66,118 and 33 metres above sea level, Moquegua town at 37,518 and 1,428 metres, and Omate at 1,042 and 2,169 metres.

An older population

Among the 179,214 people enumerated in the department in 2025, 15,609 were aged 6 to 11 and 17,039 were 12 to 17; 30,542 were 60 or older.

Our calculation from that table: people aged 60 and over make up 17.0 per cent of those enumerated in Moquegua, against 14.8 per cent in Peru as a whole. That makes adult and late-career learners a real audience here, not an afterthought.

Public school enrolment has been steady: public primaria went from 13.9 thousand pupils in 2015 to 14.7 thousand in 2025, and public secundaria from 11.4 thousand to 13.1 thousand. The department had 4,145 teachers in 2025. The town and district figures above come from different years and cover different areas, so we do not compare them.

A Moquegua data project

How much of each Moquegua university school is in its first year?

The Universidad Nacional de Moquegua publishes a register of its students by school and term, with each student's relative cycle. One count from it turns into two percentages that sound alike and mean very different things. Telling them apart is one of the most useful habits in statistics.

The register

UNAM publishes "Población estudiantil por sexo y escuela profesional - UNAM 2026" on the national open data portal with an Open Data Commons Attribution (ODC-By) licence. It was released on 31 October 2026 and modified on 2 November 2026.

Our copy, poblacion-unam-2026.csv, has 35,510 rows covering the terms 2020-1 to 2026-2. For term 2026-2 there are 3,468 rows, each with a different student code, 2,021 at the Moquegua campus and 1,447 at Ilo. The codes are identifiers, and we never print them. We use three columns only: school, term and CICLO_RELATIVO, the cycle a student has reached, and we count cycles 1 and 2 as first year.

Two questions that sound alike

Question A: of the students in Ingeniería de Sistemas e Informática, what share are in their first year? Question B: of all first-year students at the university, what share are in Sistemas e Informática?

Both start from the same number, the first-years in that school. A divides it by the size of the school, B by the number of first-years at the university. Mixing them up is how a report can make a school look like the place newcomers flock to, or the place they avoid, when neither is true.

Ninety-two of the 402 students in Sistemas e Informática are in their first two cycles: 22.9 per cent of the school. Those same 92 are 11.1 per cent of the university's 831 first-years. Neither number is wrong; they answer different questions. In a two-way table, the first is a row percentage, dividing across the school's row, and the second a column percentage, dividing down the first-year column. Version 2 builds both for every school and sorts by the first.

"First year" means relative cycles 1 and 2, our grouping. Shares are ours. The second column adds to about 100 across schools, the first does not and should not.

Read the columns separately. Down the first, Ingeniería Pesquera has the largest first-year share, more than a third of its students, yet it holds only 4.5 per cent of the university's first-years, because it is a small school. Gestión Pública y Desarrollo Social is the opposite: under a fifth of its students are first-years, but it is so large that it still holds the biggest slice of all first-years, 12.0 per cent. A school can rank high on one measure and low on the other; that is the whole point.

English

Is English a barrier for Moquegua learners?

Less than in many regions, judging by reading results, but it is still a new language of instruction for most. We teach only in English, live and online, and never run Spanish-language groups.

What we can say

EF's English Proficiency Index 2025 gives Peru 519, in its moderate band. EF scores sixteen Peruvian regions separately, and Moquegua is not among them, so we cannot quote a Moquegua English level.

Secundaria pupils on the regular school day have three hours of Inglés a week. Strong reading in Spanish helps: a pupil who reads carefully in one language brings that method to the next.

Who gains most

Able pupils in 3° to 5° de secundaria aiming at engineering or science. University students in systems, mining, environmental and civil engineering. Professionals in mining, port and public services who read manuals in English and want programming or data skills. Retired or late-career adults with time to learn properly.

Children under ten do well too, starting with blocks and moving to Python once reading is fluent.

Parents who prefer to read in Spanish will find our guide to clases de programación en inglés useful. For a learner set on university admission, our explainer on Peru's university admission exams describes how the main exams work.

Beyond school

Does Moquegua teach programming in school or after it?

Not in school as a subject. After school, the region invests in higher and technical education, though the sources we read mention no programming course for pupils.

The national school rules

Six years of primaria and five of secundaria, set by the General Education Law, with grades fixed by age on 31 March: a pupil who is twelve on that date starts secundaria.

Competency 28 of the national curriculum, on virtual environments, is shared across every area. The regular secundaria week has 35 hours, and none of them is labelled programming. MINEDU's Python pilot, Fábrica de Programadores, began in July 2025 for 1,000 pupils in Lima Norte, not in Moquegua.

What the region funds

The regional Beca Esperanza Joven scholarship, recognised internationally in October 2026, has generated around 1,750 places in higher education between 2023 and 2026, according to the regional government, at institutions in Moquegua, Arequipa and Lima.

In September 2026 the region equipped the public technological institute Los Andes in Carumas, where more than 140 students follow farming and civil-construction programmes, with tools from concrete mixers to a survey drone and a desktop computer.

Moquegua pupils can enter national contests on the same terms as everyone else. ONEM, the maths olympiad, holds its first round in each school; in 2026 that was on 8 July, and over 1.7 million secundaria pupils were called to take part. UTEC's computing olympiads, OPI included, are open to every secundaria pupil without charge. We are not linked with MINEDU, UTEC, UNAM, the regional government or any contest, and all our practice problems are our own.

Peru keeps UTC minus 5 hours the year round and has no daylight saving (tz database, America/Lima); India keeps UTC plus 5 hours 30 minutes. India is 10 hours 30 minutes ahead of Moquegua in every month.

With no clock changes in either country, a lesson hour fixed in March is still right in December. Book in your own time. Online coding and AI classes in Moquegua: frequent questions

Are the classes in Spanish?

No. Our lessons are live online lessons in English, and only in English. The mentor talks clearly and steadily, adapts to the learner's pace and accent, and when a learner falls back on Spanish, gives them the English to use; the lesson carries on in English.

My child is top of the class. Will the lessons be too easy?

They should not be. The free class includes a level check, and able learners can start with typed Python, algorithms or contest problems rather than the gentle introduction.

Can my child use the TIC 2 school laptop?

If the school lends it for home use, yes: lessons need a browser, a microphone and a steady connection. Ask the school first; some laptops stay in the classroom.

Do you teach learners in Ilo, Omate or Ichuña?

Yes. Lessons are online, so the port, the valley and the highlands are all equally close, as long as the internet is stable at your hour.

What time are the classes?

Any day, any time, flexible: choose a batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at the hour that suits your family. Moquegua is 10 hours 30 minutes behind India all year, so 09:00 in Moquegua is 19:30 in India.

Is programming taught in Moquegua schools?

Not as a subject. The national curriculum places digital skills in every area, and the standard secundaria week has no programming hours.

Are the classes useful for university students in Moquegua and Ilo?

Yes. Python, Java, data and statistics help in systems, mining, environmental and civil engineering, and in management. We teach alongside your degree, and the university alone sets your grades.

Do you teach AI?

Yes. Teenagers and adults build small models and learn where AI goes wrong. Children meet the ideas through games, and under-13s never use adult AI accounts with us.

How is payment made?

Monthly, by card, in US dollars, converted into soles by your bank. If you visit from India, prices show in rupees. The first lesson is free.

Do you give a certificate?

Courses that include one end with our own certificate of completion. It is not a Peruvian school or university qualification, and no Peruvian authority awards or endorses it.
