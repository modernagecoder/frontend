# GL Assessment 11 plus maths practice

GL Assessment is a test publisher that writes the 11 plus for many English grammar school areas, but it does not write one test. Each council or consortium orders its own paper, so the GL test in Medway, Buckinghamshire, Birmingham and Lincolnshire differs in timing, mix and weighting. Modern Age Coders teaches the maths for these tests live online, in a group or one to one, through our 11 plus maths preparation course. Before any practice paper, find out which version your child will sit: in one GL area maths is a separate fifty-minute paper, and in another there is no maths paper at all.

At a glance: Publisher: GL Assessment; Written for: Each area, to its own order; Format: Multiple choice, separate answer sheet; Maths content: National curriculum to the end of Year 5; Maths weight: From none to two fifths, by area; Calculator: Not allowed; We teach: Arithmetic, reasoning, answer-sheet habits; We never: Copy GL papers or promise a score. ## Is there one GL Assessment 11 plus test?

No. GL Assessment supplies tests to many areas, and each area decides what its own test contains. GL's main website returns HTTP 403 to automated readers, so everything below comes from GL's separate 11 plus shop and from the councils and consortia that use GL papers.

Parents searching for "GL 11 plus practice papers" are usually told the test is a brand, like a GCSE board. It is closer to a printer that takes orders. Medway Council says of its own paper: "These tests are written specifically for Medway Council." The Buckinghamshire grammar schools say the same of theirs: "Our test is produced specifically for Buckinghamshire." GL's own 11 plus site is plainer still, warning that "the exact timings, the combination of topics covered (for example, verbal reasoning, non-verbal reasoning, Maths and English) and the number of questions in your local 11+ test may differ from those in our familiarisation and practice materials."

### What stays the same across GL areas

In every GL area we read, questions are multiple choice and children mark answers on a separate sheet that a machine reads. Calculators are not allowed. The maths draws on the primary curriculum: the Buckinghamshire grammar schools say their maths "does not go beyond what the national curriculum expects children to know and be able to do by the end of Year 5".

Tests are sat in September of Year 6, after registration in Year 5, and scores are age-standardised so that a child born in August is not compared like-for-like with one born the previous September.

### What changes from area to area

The number of papers, the length of each, which subjects share a paper, whether maths is scored at all, and how much each section counts. In Buckinghamshire maths shares a paper with non-verbal and spatial reasoning. In Medway it has its own paper. In Lincolnshire the consortium sets two reasoning tests and nothing labelled maths.

Providers can change as well. Gloucestershire County Council says the county's grammar schools will use Future Stories Community Enterprise (FSCE) as their test provider for the September 2028 intake, so a family there should not assume a GL format for that year.

## How much does maths count in each GL area?

Anything from nothing to two fifths of the total, depending on where a child lives. This is the table we wish every family saw before buying a practice pack.

**Maths in GL Assessment 11 plus tests, as each area publishes it (September 2026 sitting)**

| Area and who runs the test | Papers | Where the maths sits | How much maths counts |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buckinghamshire: The Buckinghamshire Grammar Schools (TBGS) | Two papers, each about 45 minutes of questions, about an hour with instructions | Paper two, with non-verbal and spatial reasoning | 25 per cent of the Secondary Transfer Test Score |
| West Midlands Grammar Schools group, including every Birmingham grammar school | Two papers, each about 50 to 60 minutes including instructions | Individually timed sections spread through both papers | 25 per cent |
| Medway Council | Three papers: English 30 minutes, mathematics 50 minutes, reasoning 45 minutes | Its own paper, the longest of the three | Standardised score doubled; two of five weighted parts (our arithmetic) |
| London Borough of Redbridge | Two papers | Mathematics and Non-Verbal Reasoning paper | Not published on the council page we read |
| Lincolnshire Consortium of Grammar Schools | Verbal reasoning test and non-verbal reasoning test | No maths paper | None as a separate section |

The Medway figure is our own calculation from the council's published formula: the English standardised score times two, plus the mathematics standardised score times two, plus the reasoning score times one. Maths therefore supplies two of the five parts, or two fifths, before any rounding the council applies. Compare that with the Buckinghamshire and West Midlands weightings of 25 per cent, and with Lincolnshire, where the consortium's two papers are verbal reasoning and non-verbal reasoning. A child who is strong at maths and weak at reading is in a very different position in each of those places, even though the same publisher wrote all four tests.

### Qualifying lines differ too

Buckinghamshire treats a Secondary Transfer Test Score of at least 121 as qualified. Lincolnshire's consortium uses a total of 220 across both papers. Medway sets its line each year to take the top 26 per cent of its own maintained-school pupils; for 2027 admissions that was 478.

### Dates differ

Buckinghamshire's test was on 10 September 2026, with results on 9 October. Medway primary pupils sat on 15 and 16 September 2026. Redbridge tested on Friday 18 September 2026. Clashes are real, and Medway says there is no alternative date.

### Rules differ

Medway says plainly that "There is no resit and there is no remarking". The West Midlands group says children cannot go back to an earlier section once its time is up. Each rule changes how a child should practise.

## What is a GL maths question like to sit?

Short, multiple choice, timed by recorded instructions, and answered on a sheet a scanner reads. The content is ordinary primary maths; the conditions are not.

GL's own description of its maths practice papers lists five things: "Knowledge of times tables", "Quick mental arithmetic", "Understanding of shapes, space and measures", applying the four operations, and "Graph reading". That is a fair summary of what a Year 5 child needs. What the list leaves out is how the paper is run, and the councils fill that gap.

**Test-room conditions published for GL areas**

| Condition | What the area says | What it means for practice |
|---|---|---|
| Recorded instructions | West Midlands papers are timed by audio CD; Buckinghamshire uses a supplied audio file | Practise with a voice announcing start and stop, not a parent with a stopwatch |
| Sections locked by time | West Midlands candidates "cannot revisit a previous section" | Unfinished questions in a section are lost; pace has to be learned section by section |
| No scrap paper | West Midlands: "Rough/scrap paper cannot be used"; working goes in the question booklet | Practise doing working in the margin of the question page |
| Only the answer sheet counts | West Midlands: "Question booklets are not marked" | A right answer worked out but not marked on the sheet scores nothing |
| No calculator | Calculators are not allowed in or around West Midlands test rooms | Written methods and mental methods must both be quick |
| One sitting | Medway: no resit and no remark; Lincolnshire: tests taken once in the academic year | There is no second go, which is an argument for calm familiarity, not for cramming |

### The answer sheet is a skill

Every GL area we read uses a separate, machine-read sheet. The commonest disaster is not a hard question; it is a skipped question in the booklet that is not skipped on the sheet, so every later answer sits one row out.

We practise this deliberately. Children mark the question number beside each answer, leave a pencil dot on any row they skip, and check the row number every five questions.

### Mixed papers need switching

Where maths shares a paper with non-verbal and spatial reasoning, as in Buckinghamshire and Redbridge, a child moves between very different kinds of thinking inside one hour. Practice that only ever uses a whole maths paper misses that.

In the free lesson we ask which area a child will sit in, and set practice in that area's shape.

## Which GL practice papers should my child use?

Start with your own area's familiarisation booklet, then GL's free materials, and only then anything you buy. The order matters more than the quantity.

1. Find your area's own description of the test on the council or consortium site, and note the papers, timings and subjects. Buckinghamshire, for example, sends parents a familiarisation booklet specific to its test in the summer term of Year 5.
2. Use that local booklet first. The Buckinghamshire grammar schools say parents "should use those with their children" rather than relying on generic material.
3. Add GL's free familiarisation materials, which GL describes as 10 hours of downloads, including a maths set and a combined non-verbal reasoning and maths set. Download them from GL's own 11 plus site; GL asks that tutors do not reproduce them, and we do not.
4. If you buy practice packs, buy the right format. The Lincolnshire consortium tells parents to buy "the Multiple Choice Format, NOT the Standard Format" for its test, a warning worth applying in any multiple-choice area.
5. Keep full timed papers for the last few weeks. Before then, short sets on one skill at a time teach more than a whole paper marked once.

### What the free material is for

GL says its free materials are "particularly useful for verbal reasoning and non-verbal reasoning tests as children may not have encountered these types of questions before." Maths is different: a child meets maths every school day, so the gap is usually speed and the layout, not the topics.

Redbridge Council adds the caution that free materials "might not match the exact test your child will take", in the number, type or difficulty of questions.

## How should a child think through a GL-style maths question?

Three original questions at Year 5 level, in a five-option multiple-choice style. None is from a GL paper. The point is the route, not the answer.

### 1. Scaling a recipe

Six biscuits need 250 g of flour. How much flour for 15 biscuits? A 525 g, B 600 g, C 625 g, D 650 g, E 750 g. Estimate first: 15 is two and a half lots of 6, so the answer is two and a half lots of 250, which is 625 g. Check another way: 3 biscuits need 125 g, and 15 is five lots of 3, so 5 times 125 is 625. Answer C. Option E is the trap for children who treat 15 as 18.

### 2. Ordering mixed numbers

Which is largest: 0.6, five eighths, three fifths, 0.58, 61 per cent? Turn everything into thousandths. 0.6 is 600, three fifths is also 600, 0.58 is 580, 61 per cent is 610, and five eighths is 625, since one eighth is 125. Answer: five eighths. Knowing the eighths by heart turns a slow question into a ten-second one.

### 3. A perimeter without algebra

A rectangle has a perimeter of 36 cm and its length is twice its width. What is its area? Half the perimeter, 18 cm, is one length plus one width, which is three equal parts of the width. So the width is 6 cm, the length 12 cm, and the area 72 square centimetres. No Year 6 algebra is needed: a bar drawn in three equal pieces does the work.

**A practice set written by Modern Age Coders, with the reasoning we expect**

| Question (ours) | Answer | The thinking |
|---|---|---|
| 4.7 multiplied by 30 | 141 | Multiply by 3 to get 14.1, then by 10. Splitting a hard product into two easy ones is the habit to build. |
| A train leaves at 10:47 and arrives at 13:12. How long is the journey? | 2 hours 25 minutes | Count up to 11:00 (13 minutes), then to 13:00 (2 hours), then 12 more minutes. Never subtract times as if they were decimals. |
| What is three quarters of 56? | 42 | One quarter is 14; three quarters is 42. Find the unit fraction first, every time. |
| The temperature is minus 4 degrees and rises by 11 degrees. What is it now? | 7 degrees | Four degrees gets back to zero, leaving 7 more. A number line sketch in the booklet margin prevents sign slips. |
| A 45-minute session is cut by 20 per cent. How long is it now? | 36 minutes | Ten per cent of 45 is 4.5, so twenty per cent is 9, and 45 minus 9 is 36. |
| Next in the sequence 3, 7, 15, 31, ... | 63 | Each term is double the last plus one. Check the rule on two steps before trusting it. |

Each of these takes well under a minute once the method is automatic, and each hides one trap: the wrong unit, the clock treated as a decimal, the sign, or a pattern checked on only one step. In lessons we spend more time on the trap than on the answer, because the same trap reappears in a different costume on the day.

## Do GL areas say a child needs tutoring?

Two of the consortia we read say so directly: no. We think families should hear that from the schools before they hear anything from a tutor, including us.

### In their own words

The Buckinghamshire grammar schools: "TBGS does not endorse tutoring for the test." They add that "Tutors do not see our test papers or associated materials" and so cannot advise on the specific test.

The Lincolnshire consortium: "The Lincolnshire Consortium of Grammar School does not endorse tutoring", and "Too much preparation puts children under pressure and may be counter-productive."

### Where maths teaching still helps

Both statements are about coaching for the test. Neither says arithmetic does not matter. A child who is unsure of times tables, fractions or written methods by the end of Year 5 is short of things the national curriculum expects anyway, and that is worth fixing for secondary school whatever happens in September.

So our offer is narrow and honest: secure Year 5 maths, teach the habits of a multiple-choice, answer-sheet paper, and stop there. We do not teach to a leaked or imagined test.

## From Year 4 to the September test

Registration in GL areas falls in the spring or early summer of Year 5, and the test lands in September of Year 6. Plan backwards from registration.

**Four stages before a GL 11 plus**

| When | Stage | Secure when |
|---|---|---|
| Year 4 | 1. Number facts | Tables to 12 and fraction, decimal and percentage pairs recalled without counting |
| Autumn and spring of Year 5 | 2. Methods and problems | Written multiplication and division come out right with awkward numbers; word problems are read for the operation first |
| Summer of Year 5 | 3. Local format | The area's own familiarisation booklet worked through, answer sheet included |
| Holidays before Year 6 | 4. Timed sections | Short timed sections finished calmly, with skipped rows handled correctly |

A child who starts in the summer of Year 5 can still gain, but the order holds: fluency, then problems, then the clock.

## What parents ask about the GL 11 plus

### What is the GL 11 plus?

It is an 11 plus test written by GL Assessment for a particular council or group of grammar schools. GL's own site says tests vary from area to area in timing, subjects and number of questions, so there is no single GL test.

### Is the GL 11 plus maths paper the same everywhere?

No. Medway sets a separate 50-minute maths paper, Buckinghamshire puts maths in a second paper with non-verbal and spatial reasoning, and the Lincolnshire consortium sets no maths paper at all. Check your own council or consortium page first.

### How much does maths count in a GL test?

It depends on the area. Buckinghamshire and the West Midlands Grammar Schools both weight maths at 25 per cent. By our arithmetic on Medway's published formula, maths supplies two fifths of the weighted total there.

### What maths is in a GL 11 plus?

Primary maths up to Year 5. The Buckinghamshire grammar schools say their content does not go beyond what the national curriculum expects by the end of Year 5, and GL lists times tables, mental arithmetic, shape, space and measures, the four operations and graph reading.

### Are GL 11 plus questions multiple choice?

In every GL area we read, yes. Children mark answers on a separate sheet that is machine read, and in the West Midlands test only that sheet is marked, not the question booklet.

### Can my child use a calculator?

No. Calculators are not allowed in the test rooms the West Midlands group describes, and a GL maths paper is built for mental and written methods. Practise without one from the start.

### Where can I get free GL practice papers?

From your area's own familiarisation booklet first, then from GL Assessment's own 11 plus site, which offers free familiarisation downloads including maths. GL asks tutors not to reproduce those materials, so we point you to GL rather than copying them.

### Do grammar schools recommend tutoring for the GL test?

The Buckinghamshire grammar schools and the Lincolnshire consortium both say they do not endorse tutoring. Our lessons teach Year 5 maths and the habits of a multiple-choice paper; we do not claim to know a test we have never seen.

### When are lessons?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Lessons are fixed in UK time.

### Can you tell me what score my child needs?

Each area publishes its own line: Buckinghamshire uses 121, the Lincolnshire consortium 220 across two papers, and Medway sets its minimum each year. We do not predict scores or places.
