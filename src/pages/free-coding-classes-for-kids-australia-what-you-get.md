Plenty of Australian children learn their first lines of code without their family spending anything, and that is a good thing. Volunteer clubs, government resources and short global activities are all there for the taking. ## Where free coding actually happens in Australia

Four kinds of free coding are open to Australian children. They differ less in price, since all are free to the family, than in how much adult support comes with them.

### A government starting point

The Digital Technologies Hub, which describes itself as "brought to you by the Australian Government Department of Education", keeps a page for families called "After school activities and courses".

It notes that "some of these programs can be completed online in the comfort of your own home whilst some require a termly or holiday commitment", and lists Code Camp, Code Club, code.org, Grok, Hour of Code and Junior Engineers. Not every program on that list is free, so check each one before you plan around it.

### A global one

The Hour of Code has run worldwide for over a decade, and its organisers now also run the Hour of AI, which, in their words, builds "on over a decade of success with the Hour of Code".

Both are designed for a single hour. They are an excellent taste of coding and a poor substitute for a year of it, which is exactly how their organisers present them.

### Why it works

A Code Club puts children in a room with an adult and other children, every week, for free. For a curious eight-year-old that combination is hard to beat, and the projects are written to be followed step by step.

Its yearly Moonhack event also gives children a shared goal, which many find motivating.

## What free coding usually does not include

None of this is a criticism of free options. It is simply what a family gets for nothing, and what it does not.

### A teacher who knows your child

Volunteers change, clubs pause for holidays, and self-guided projects have no adult at all. A child who gets stuck often waits a week, or gives up.

### A plan that builds

Free projects are usually stand-alone. Finishing twenty of them is not the same as moving, step by step, from blocks to Python to a first real app.

### Feedback on their own work

Following instructions produces a working project. Explaining why it works, and changing it without instructions, is the part that needs someone to ask questions.

For many families the right answer is both: a free club or project library for fun and friendship, and a weekly class for the planned progression and the feedback. Others find free is enough for years. The point is to know which you are choosing. If you want to see what paid classes cost before deciding, our [guide to coding class costs in Australia](/coding-classes-cost-australia) sets out the arithmetic per lesson and per term.

**A rule of thumb** If your child is still asking to code after a term of free projects, the interest is real, and a teacher is likely to repay the cost. If interest fades after a few weeks, that is useful to know too, and it cost nothing to find out.

## A weighted decision matrix your child can write

Choosing between free options is a real decision with several factors, which makes it a perfect first program. A weighted decision matrix scores each option against each factor, multiplies by how much that factor matters to your family, and adds it up.

**Our example scores, out of 5, for one family's view of four options**

| Option | Cost to the family | A regular adult giving feedback | A planned sequence | Fits the timetable | Other children to build with |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A local Code Club | 5 | 4 | 3 | 2 | 5 |
| Self-guided projects at home | 5 | 1 | 2 | 5 | 1 |
| One-off Hour of Code or Hour of AI activities | 5 | 2 | 1 | 4 | 3 |
| A weekly live class | 2 | 5 | 5 | 4 | 4 |

**Two families, two sets of weights, two different winners (our arithmetic)**

| Option | Family A: cost matters most (weights 5, 2, 2, 3, 2; maximum 70) | Family B: feedback matters most (weights 2, 5, 4, 3, 2; maximum 80) |
|---|---|---|
| A local Code Club | 55 | 58 |
| Self-guided projects at home | 48 | 40 |
| One-off Hour of Code or Hour of AI activities | 49 | 42 |
| A weekly live class | 50 | 69 |

### What the numbers show

For Family A, the local Code Club wins comfortably. For Family B, who care most about regular feedback and a planned sequence, the weekly class wins. Same options, same scores, different priorities.

The scores above are our illustration, not a rating of anyone. The real value comes when your own family fills in the table.

### The program

A child stores the scores as a list of lists, the weights as a list, and multiplies and adds in a loop. Ten lines of Python, or a few Scratch lists, are enough.

Then the fun part: change one weight and watch the winner change. Children discover that a decision depends on what you value, which is a lesson far bigger than coding.

**Try it together** Sit down with your child, agree five factors and their weights, score each option honestly, and let the program decide. If the answer surprises you, look at which weight caused it.

## From free taste to real projects

A common path we see in Australian families, though every child moves at their own speed.

**Four stages from a free first taste**

| Stage | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 1. Taste | An Hour of Code puzzle or a first Code Club project, free |
| 2. Habit | A weekly club or a self-guided project every weekend, still free |
| 3. Plan | A structured course with a teacher, once the interest has lasted a term |
| 4. Own | Projects designed by the child, explained line by line, then shared |

### When to stay free

When your child is enjoying a club, finishing projects and not getting stuck for long, there is no reason to change anything.
Free is also the right choice while you find out whether coding is a passing interest or a lasting one.

### When a teacher helps

When projects start to feel repetitive, when the child wants to make their own game rather than follow one, or when they are stuck more often than they are building.
That is usually the moment a planned sequence and weekly feedback make the biggest difference.

## What Australian parents ask about free coding classes

### Are there free coding classes for kids in Australia?

Yes. Code Club Australia runs free, volunteer-led clubs for children aged 8 to 13 at schools and libraries, many children use free self-guided project libraries at home, and the Hour of Code and Hour of AI offer free one-off activities.

### What is Code Club Australia?

Code Club Australia is a national network of free, volunteer-led coding clubs for children aged 8 to 13. Volunteers use its step-by-step projects and free training, and it is part of the Telstra Foundation.

### What is a weighted decision matrix?

It is a table that scores each option against several factors, multiplies each score by how much that factor matters, and adds the results, so the option with the highest total suits your priorities. It also makes a good first coding project.

### How do I find a Code Club near me?

Code Club Australia's website has a "Find a club" page. Clubs are run by volunteers at schools and libraries, so whether one runs near you, and when, depends on your area.

### Is a free club enough to learn to code?

For many children it is a wonderful start and enough for a long time. Children who want a planned sequence, regular feedback on their own projects, or help when stuck usually benefit from a teacher as well.

### Can my child do Code Club and your classes at the same time?
