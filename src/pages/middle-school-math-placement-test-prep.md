# Middle school math placement test prep

Two decisions shape a child's middle school math: whether they start grade 6 on an accelerated track, and when they take Algebra 1. In many districts both decisions rest partly on a test, and families understandably want to prepare. The honest way to prepare is not to guess the questions. It is to find out exactly what your district measures, check the math that test is built on, and close the gaps properly. This page shows how placement works in published state and district rules, what the tests cover, and gives two original practice sets, one for each move.

## What is on a middle school math placement test?

There is no single national test. What it covers depends on the move: a placement at the end of grade 5 looks at grade 5 and early grade 6 math, while an Algebra 1 placement looks at the grade 7 and grade 8 standards that Algebra 1 is built on.

**The two placement moves and the standards they usually draw on (standard codes from the Common Core mathematics standards; the grouping is ours)**

| Move | Usual timing | Math it checks | Example standards |
|---|---|---|---|
| Move 1: into an accelerated grade 6 track | Spring of grade 5 | Multi-digit division, fractions, decimals, early ratio and negatives | 5.NBT.B.6 division with two-digit divisors; 5.NF.B.7 dividing unit fractions; 6.NS.C.5 positive and negative numbers |
| Move 2: into Algebra 1 in grade 8 | Spring of grade 7 | Rational numbers, equations, proportional reasoning, first linear functions | 7.EE.B.4 equations of the form px + q = r; 8.F.A.1 what a function is; 8.EE.C.8 pairs of linear equations |
| Move 2, early: into Algebra 1 in grade 7 | Spring of grade 6 | All of the above, often after a compacted course | 6.NS.A.1 dividing fractions by fractions; 8.EE.A.1 integer exponents; 8.G.B.7 Pythagorean Theorem |

### Why Algebra 1 placement looks back

Montgomery County Public Schools in Maryland states it directly: "Proficiency with all grade level math standards from Kindergarten through grade 8 ensures success in Algebra I." Whatever a district calls its test, the content it checks is the content Algebra 1 depends on.

That is good news for preparation. The skills are public, listed grade by grade, so a family can find them without ever seeing a test.

### Why the move into grade 6 is different

A grade 5 placement is often a test of fluency as much as knowledge: can the child divide 4,368 by 24 accurately, add fractions with unlike denominators and reason with a ratio table without slowing down? Children who know the ideas but work slowly can be placed below what they understand.

So for this move, we split preparation between closing content gaps and building calm, accurate speed on the grade 5 essentials.

Sources: the [Common Core mathematics standards](https://learning.ccsso.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf) and [MCPS mathematics](https://www.montgomeryschoolsmd.org/curriculum/math/), read 31 October 2026. States with their own standards use other codes for the same ideas. Modern Age Coders is independent and not connected with any state, district or test publisher.

## How do schools decide middle school math placement?

Usually with more than one measure. Texas sets a statewide rule with automatic enrollment; Seattle Public Schools uses MAP scores; San Francisco Unified uses eligibility criteria with routes for students who miss them. Here is what each publishes.

**Published placement rules from one state and two districts (read 31 October 2026)**

| Who | Measure | What happens | Family role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Texas (statewide) | STAAR Grade 5 Math, top 40% of scores statewide (60th percentile), or the top 40% on a local measure | Automatic enrollment in a grade 6 advanced math program aimed at Algebra I in grade 8 | Parents can decline the automatic place |
| Seattle Public Schools | "Eligibility is determined by math MAP (RIT) scores." | Three sixth-grade options, including direct enrollment in Compacted Math 7/8 | Families of eligible students choose an option with the district |
| San Francisco Unified | District eligibility criteria for grade 8 Algebra I | At most schools, eligible students automatically enrolled in Math 8 and Algebra I from 2026 to 2027 | Others "can choose to take Algebra I as an elective" |

### Texas: the details families ask about

The TEA FAQ says the cut is statewide, not set by each district: "Students whose scores place them in the top 40% of scores (60th percentile) across the state" must be automatically enrolled. It adds that "TEA will provide the scale score which indicates that a student is in the 60th percentile annually."

Timing matters too: "STAAR results are available in late May and early June each year." For a student with no grade 5 STAAR result, 19 TAC section 74.2101 says "A local measure shall be used to determine enrollment of Grade 6 students for whom there are no results on the state Grade 5 mathematics assessment."

### What a local measure can be

TEA leaves the local measure to districts and lists examples from districts: "a summative assessment, the average of unit assessments over the year, or a performance-based portfolio of student work." So in Texas, steady class work in grade 5 can count as much as one test day.

TEA also says a district cannot make a summer course a condition: every qualifying student must have the opportunity to participate "regardless of whether or not they are available to attend classes in the summer."

San Francisco's 26 March 2026 announcement adds a second path for strong students: families can request Algebra I as the only math class, skipping Math 8, "if they meet additional academic eligibility criteria", and this "requires a meeting with the school counselor and formal written consent from parents or guardians." For its 2026 program, now closed, Seattle said families of eligible students would receive details on Thursday, April 23.

Sources: TEA [Middle School Advanced Mathematics Program FAQ](https://tea.texas.gov/educators/subject-areas/middle-school-advanced-mathematics-program-faq) and [program page](https://tea.texas.gov/educators/subject-areas/middle-school-advanced-mathematics-program); [Seattle Public Schools](https://www.seattleschools.org/departments/highly-capable/highly-capable-summer-math-program/); [SFUSD](https://www.sfusd.edu/announcements/2026-03-26-board-education-approves-revised-math-placement-policy); read 31 October 2026. Rules change; always check your district's current page.

## What kinds of tests are used for math placement?

Three kinds turn up most often: a state test score from the previous spring, a computer-adaptive screener taken during the year, and a test or set of unit exams written by the district. Many districts combine one of these with grades and teacher input.

**Common placement measures (our summary; publisher and district wording quoted where used)**

| Measure | What it is | What preparation makes sense |
|---|---|---|
| State test score | For example, STAAR Grade 5 Math in Texas, taken in spring of the year before | Solid grade-level skills across the whole year; the state publishes its own practice tests |
| Adaptive screener | For example, NWEA MAP Growth: "computer-adaptive MAP Growth assessments" reported on "the grade-level independent RIT scale" | Breadth and accuracy; questions adjust to the student, so cramming one topic helps little |
| District placement exam | A test the district writes, often near the end of the year | Ask the district which standards it covers and whether a calculator is allowed |
| Course grades and unit tests | Class performance over the year, sometimes as a local measure | Consistent work and corrections all year, not a last-minute push |

### Can you use a calculator on a math placement test?

It depends entirely on the test. Some district exams allow a basic calculator for one part and not another, and an adaptive screener may show an on-screen calculator only on some questions. There is no general rule, so ask the school for the test's own instructions.

We prepare students to work without one either way. Fluent fractions and division are what placement measures are really checking, and a student who relies on a calculator for 4,368 divided by 24 will be slower everywhere else.

### Is the math placement test hard?

It is meant to separate students, so it will feel harder than a normal unit test, and an adaptive test is designed to reach questions the student finds difficult. Getting questions wrong is expected and is not a sign of failure.

What helps most is a calm student who reads carefully and checks answers. We practice that directly: estimate first, solve, then check by a second route.

## Math placement test examples to practice with

Two original practice sets written by our teachers, one for each move. They are not copied from or modeled on any real district or publisher test; they check the standards those tests are built on. Answers are in brackets.

**Practice set 1: the move into an accelerated grade 6 track (ours)**

| # | Problem | Skill | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 4,368 &divide; 24 | Division with a two-digit divisor | (182) |
| 2 | 3/4 + 5/6 | Unlike denominators | (19/12, or 1 7/12) |
| 3 | 1/3 &divide; 4 | Unit fraction divided by a whole number | (1/12) |
| 4 | 2.5 &times; 0.4 | Decimal multiplication | (1) |
| 5 | A drink uses 4 cups of juice for 6 cups of water. Water for 10 cups of juice? | Ratio reasoning | (15 cups) |
| 6 | What is 20% of 45? | Percent as a rate per 100 | (9) |
| 7 | &minus;8 + 13 | Positive and negative numbers | (5) |
| 8 | Volume of a box 3 by 4 by 5 units | Volume | (60 cubic units) |

**Practice set 2: Algebra 1 placement readiness (ours)**

| # | Problem | Skill | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2/3 &divide; 5/6 | Dividing fractions | (4/5) |
| 2 | &minus;2.5 &times; 4 | Rational number operations | (&minus;10) |
| 3 | 3(x + 4) = 27 | Two-step equation | (x = 5) |
| 4 | 4x &minus; 7 < 9 | Inequality | (x < 4) |
| 5 | A class of 40 students grows by 15%. New size? | Percent increase | (46) |
| 6 | Slope through (&minus;1, 4) and (3, &minus;4) | Linear functions | (&minus;2) |
| 7 | 32 &times; 3&minus;4 | Integer exponents | (1/9) |
| 8 | x + y = 12 and x &minus; y = 4 | System of equations | (x = 8, y = 4) |
| 9 | Right triangle with legs 5 and 12: hypotenuse? | Pythagorean Theorem | (13) |

### Worked: 4,368 divided by 24

Estimate first: 24 is close to 25, and 4,368 divided by 25 is a little under 175, so the answer is around 180. Then divide: 24 goes into 43 once, remainder 19; bring down 6 to make 196, and 24 goes in 8 times (192), remainder 4; bring down 8 to make 48, and 24 goes in exactly 2 times. The answer is 182.

Check: 24 &times; 182 = 24 &times; 180 + 24 &times; 2 = 4,320 + 48 = 4,368. The estimate, the working and the check all agree.

### Worked: 4x &minus; 7 < 9

Add 7 to both sides: 4x < 16. Divide both sides by 4, a positive number, so the sign stays the same: x < 4. The solution is every number less than 4, drawn as an open circle at 4 with the arrow pointing left.

Check with a number on each side. If x = 3, then 12 &minus; 7 = 5, which is less than 9. If x = 5, then 20 &minus; 7 = 13, which is not. Students who check like this rarely lose marks on inequalities.

How to use the sets: give the matching set without a calculator and without a time limit first, then look at which skills went wrong rather than the total. A wrong answer on problem 2 of set 2 and on problem 7 of set 1 points to the same thing, signs with negative numbers, and that is the domain to fix first. For official practice, use the free practice tests your state or the test publisher provides.

## How should my child prepare for a math placement test?

Start early, prepare for the math rather than the test, and keep it low pressure. This is the plan our teachers follow.

1. Find your district's published rule. Which measure, which grade, which date, and whether there is an appeal or a second route.
2. Check the content. List the standards for the year being tested and the year before; the tables above are a starting point.
3. Diagnose by domain. Use a set like ours to find which domain breaks: fractions, negatives, ratio or equations.
4. Fix the earliest broken link. A grade 7 equation problem often fails because of a grade 6 fraction skill.
5. Build accuracy, then speed. Untimed and correct first; then shorter, mixed sets with a gentle time limit.
6. Keep the week before calm. Light review, normal sleep, no new topics. A rested child shows what they know.

**A preparation timeline for a spring placement (ours)**

| When | Focus |
|---|---|
| Fall | Read the district rule; first diagnostic; start on the weakest domain |
| Winter | Close gaps one domain at a time; keep up with class work, which may count |
| Early spring | Mixed practice across the whole year's content |
| Final weeks | Short mixed sets, checking habits, rest |

### What we will not do

We do not obtain, copy or imitate real placement tests, and we do not teach test-taking tricks in place of math. Placement is meant to find the right course for a child, and a placement won by drilling can leave a student in a course they are not ready for.

We also never promise a placement. That decision belongs to the school, using its own measures.

### If your child does not place

It is rarely the end of the road. SFUSD lets students who miss automatic enrollment take Algebra I as an elective, and TEA notes that the Texas rule "is focused on students entering sixth grade" while districts can set policy for advancement in later grades.

A year of strong work at grade level, with gaps closed, often puts a student in a better position for the next decision point than a rushed placement would have.

## Middle school math placement: common questions

### What is on a middle school math placement test?

It depends on the district and the move. A placement at the end of grade 5 usually checks grade 5 division, fractions and decimals plus early ratio work, while an Algebra 1 placement checks grade 7 and 8 skills such as rational numbers, equations and linear functions.

### Is there math placement test tutoring near me?

Wherever you live in the US, the teacher comes to you by video. A free diagnostic class comes first, then weekly lessons with one consistent teacher.

### How do I find my district's placement rules?

Look on the district website for its math pathways or advanced math page, or ask the school counselor. The rule should say which measure is used, in which grade, and whether families can request a review.

### Can you use a calculator on the math placement test?

It depends on the test, so check its own instructions with the school. We prepare students to work accurately without one, since fluent fractions and division are what most placement measures check.

### Is there an Algebra 1 placement test practice PDF?

Some states and publishers post free official practice, and those are the right place for real-format questions. The practice sets on this page are our own, built on the grade 7 and 8 standards, and we do not copy any district test.

### How are students placed into advanced math in Texas?

By a state rule. A grade 5 student whose STAAR math score falls at or above the statewide 60th percentile, or who ranks high enough on the district's own measure, such as a year-end assessment or a portfolio, goes into the grade 6 advanced program automatically. Families can still decline the place.

### What if my child misses the placement cut?

Many districts offer later routes. SFUSD, for example, lets students who are not automatically enrolled take Algebra I as an elective, and Texas allows districts to set their own policies for advancement after grade 6.

### Can tutoring guarantee a higher placement?

No. Placement is the school's decision, made with its own measures. We build genuine readiness in the math those measures check, and we tell you honestly where your child stands.
