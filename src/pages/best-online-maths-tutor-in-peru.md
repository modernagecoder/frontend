## What makes an online maths tutor the best fit?

No organisation in Peru rates private maths tutors, and reviews mostly measure how pleasant a tutor is. A more useful definition: the tutor who leaves this learner able to work without them. That shows up in five habits a parent can watch for.

1. **They diagnose before they teach.** The first session should include questions designed to find the gap, not a lesson on whatever the homework says. Ours begins with a short diagnostic built around the four competencies below.
2. **They make the learner talk.** A good tutor asks "why does that step work?" more often than they explain. In our classes the learner explains each step aloud, in English, before moving on.
3. **They cover all of maths, not just sums.** Peru's curriculum has four maths competencies, and drilling arithmetic only touches one. Our plans rotate through all four.
4. **They separate checking from proving.** Trying examples is how mathematicians start; it is not how they finish. The lesson further down shows why, and we teach it from early secundaria.
5. **They report work, not hours.** Ask what the learner can now do that they could not before. Our mentors send short notes on problems solved and ideas understood.

## What maths does the Peruvian curriculum expect a learner to do?

The Currículo Nacional (CNEB) organises school maths into four competencies, each a kind of problem the pupil should be able to solve. A tutor who knows them can find gaps faster than one working from the textbook's chapter order.

**The four Matemática competencies of the CNEB, with a diagnostic question we might ask for each**

| CNEB competency (official name) | In plain English | A diagnostic question of ours |
|---|---|---|
| Resuelve problemas de cantidad | Numbers, operations, estimation, fractions and percentages | Which is larger, 3/8 or 0.4, and how could you tell without a calculator? |
| Resuelve problemas de regularidad, equivalencia y cambio | Patterns, equations, functions and algebra | A pattern goes 4, 7, 10, 13. What is the 50th term, and why? |
| Resuelve problemas de movimiento, forma y localización | Shapes, measurement, angles, coordinates and transformations | A square's sides double. What happens to its area, and can you show it with a drawing? |
| Resuelve problemas de gestión de datos e incertidumbre | Tables, charts, averages and probability | Two dice are thrown. Is a total of 7 or a total of 12 more likely, and by how much? |

Source: MINEDU, Currículo Nacional de la Educación Básica, area of Matemática (the competency names are copied from the CNEB; competency 23 is "Resuelve problemas de cantidad"). Read on 8 November 2026. The plain-English column and the diagnostic questions are ours.

## How much maths does school in Peru give, and what did ENLA find?

More hours than most families think, and steadier results than the headlines suggest. A tutor should build on school, not repeat it.

### Hours at school

The CNEB gives Matemática five periods a week in 1st and 2nd of primaria and four from 3rd to 6th. In secundaria it is four periods under the regular school day and six in Jornada Escolar Completa schools. A period, the CNEB says, is 45 minutes.

Our arithmetic: that is 225 minutes a week in the first two primaria grades, 180 minutes from 3rd of primaria through regular secundaria, and 270 minutes in full-day secundaria. A tutor adding one or two hours a week is a small fraction of that, so the hours have to be spent on what school cannot do: individual diagnosis and explanation.

## How should a tutor prepare a pupil for ONEM?

By teaching problem solving with original problems, not by drilling past papers. ONEM, the national school maths olympiad, rewards pupils who can reason about something unfamiliar, and the only preparation that builds that is unfamiliar problems.

### What ONEM 2026 involved

The school stage was held nationwide on Wednesday 8 July 2026, in three levels: Level 1 for 1st and 2nd of secundaria, Level 2 for 3rd and 4th, Level 3 for 5th. Its paper had ten closed constructed-response items in two hours, so pupils write answers rather than pick them.

Pupils go forward from school to UGEL, then regional, then national stages. El Peruano reports that ONEM 2026 began with more than 1.7 million secundaria pupils and that the national stage, on Sunday 11 October 2026 at Huampaní, brought together 237 finalists.

### How we prepare

Our arithmetic: two hours for ten items is twelve minutes each, long enough to need a plan and short enough to punish a wrong first approach. We practise choosing an approach quickly, then checking it.

Entry is through the pupil's school, which registers them; families should ask the school. We never reproduce ONEM problems or solutions. We have no connection with MINEDU, ONEM or its organisers, and we promise no result.

1. **Problem of ours, Level 1 style.** The sum of three consecutive whole numbers is 87. What are they, and can the sum of three consecutive whole numbers ever be 100? Explain.
2. **Problem of ours, Level 2 style.** How many two-digit numbers have digits that add up to 9? Find a way to count them without listing them all.
3. **Problem of ours, Level 3 style.** Show that the product of any four consecutive whole numbers is always one less than a perfect square, and then check your argument with 5 &times; 6 &times; 7 &times; 8.

## Is checking lots of examples the same as proving a maths rule?

No, and this is the most important habit a maths tutor can teach after arithmetic. Here is the lesson we use with secundaria pupils, with a little Python to do the checking.

### The pattern

Take the expression n &times; n + n + 41. For n = 0 it gives 41, then 43, 47, 53, 61, and every one is a prime number. A pupil checks the first ten by hand and announces a rule: this always makes primes.

The mentor does not argue. Instead the class writes a short program that checks the first forty values of n at once.

### The surprise

All forty are prime. The pattern looks overwhelming. Then the program tries n = 40 and gets 1681, which is 41 &times; 41: not prime.

Algebra explains it in one line. When n = 40, the expression is 40 &times; 40 + 40 + 41 = 40 &times; 41 + 41 = 41 &times; 41. The failure was built in from the start; forty successful checks just hid it.

**pattern.py: forty successes, then a failure** (Python)
```python
def is_prime(n):
    if n < 2:
        return False
    d = 2
    while d * d <= n:
        if n % d == 0:
            return False
        d += 1
    return True

values = [n * n + n + 41 for n in range(40)]
print(all(is_prime(v) for v in values), values[:5])

n = 40
v = n * n + n + 41
print(n, v, is_prime(v), v == 41 * 41)
```
It prints True [41, 43, 47, 53, 61], meaning all forty values from n = 0 to 39 are prime, and then 40 1681 False True: at n = 40 the value 1681 is not prime and equals 41 &times; 41.

This rule is famous, and mathematicians long ago noticed its forty primes. We use it because it fails so late. A pupil who has seen it never again says "it worked for every example I tried, so it is true". In ONEM-style problems and in IB or IGCSE proof questions, that sentence costs marks; in later life it costs more.

1. **Find it by hand.** Without the program, explain why n = 41 must also fail. (Hint: every term then has a common factor.)
2. **Prove a true rule.** Show that the sum of the first n odd numbers is n &times; n. Check n = 1 to 5, then give an argument that covers every n.
3. **Spot the gap.** A classmate claims every number of the form 2 &times; 2 &times; ... &times; 2 &minus; 1 is prime because 3, 7, 31 and 127 are. Use the program idea to test 2 to the power 11, minus 1.
4. **Say it in English.** Write two sentences explaining the difference between evidence and proof.
