Every age

Who takes coding and AI classes in Halle, and what does each age learn?

The same subject looks very different at seven, fifteen and forty. Here is how we split it.

Children, 6 to 12

Six to eight year olds start in Scratch, where blocks click together and a mistake shows on screen at once. From about nine, children type real Python. AI comes in as ideas a child can test: sorting pictures into groups, guessing the next item in a pattern, asking why a computer got it wrong.

Teenagers, 13 to 18

Teenagers write Python properly: functions, files, lists of thousands of rows. Then they build small machine-learning models and, more importantly, learn to check them. A teenager who can say why a model is wrong is worth more to a university than one who can only run it.

Adults

Adults come to us for two reasons: to learn programming from zero, or to understand AI well enough to use it with judgement at work. Either way we begin with Python, because a person who can read a ten-line script can also see when an AI tool has produced nonsense.

Halle schools

Is Informatik compulsory at schools in Halle (Saale)?

It depends on the school type, which is unusual. In Saxony-Anhalt the Sekundarschule teaches more compulsory Informatik than the Gymnasium does.

Sekundarschule and Gemeinschaftsschule

The Gesellschaft für Informatik (GI) reports in its Informatik-Monitor 2026/27 that at the Sekundarschule and the Gemeinschaftsschule, grades 6 to 10 each have one compulsory weekly hour of Informatik, "je eine Wochenstunde". One more hour is taken from the cross-subject allowance of grades 6 to 8 and moved forward into grade 5, so Informatik starts in grade 5. The same rules apply to grades 5 to 10 at the Gesamtschulen.

For a Halle child at one of these schools, that is a short weekly lesson every year from about the age of ten.

Gymnasium

At the Gymnasium the picture is thinner. Grades 9 and 10 may offer Informatik as a two-hour elective (Wahlpflichtfach). Since 2024/25, starting in grade 5, the Gymnasium follows the framework "Lernen in der digitalen Welt", but the GI notes that its share of Informatik content is "weniger als eine Wochenstunde", less than one weekly hour.

This is why the GI still lists Saxony-Anhalt among the six Länder without Informatik that is compulsory for every pupil.

In the Oberstufe, the same GI page says Informatik can be taken for two weekly hours in the Einführungsphase and for two weekly hours at the basic level (grundlegendes Anforderungsniveau) in the Qualifikationsphase. Only two Gymnasien with a mathematics, science and technology focus may offer it at the raised level. An oral Abitur exam in Informatik is possible if the pupil has taken the subject without a break since the Einführungsphase. We teach alongside all of this and award no German grades; the school course is what goes on the report card.

There is more on Saxony-Anhalt as a whole, including school types and the Abitur, on our Saxony-Anhalt page.

Taught in English

Are the classes in German or in English?

English, always, and we say so before the first class rather than after it.

Why English works here

Our mentors are based in India and every lesson is live, spoken and written in English. We have no German-language teachers. For a learner in Halle this usually turns out to be an advantage: Python, its error messages and almost every serious AI resource are in English anyway, so the language of the class is the language of the tools.

Children at German schools get a second subject for free, and adults who work with international colleagues get practice in exactly the vocabulary they need.

Pace and accent

Mentors slow down, rephrase and wait. They get used to a learner's accent within a few classes, and they never treat a German word in the middle of an English sentence as a mistake; they just offer the English one.

Every new idea is shown on the shared screen and tried straight away, so a learner who misses a word still sees what it means.

A real Halle data project

How does a recommender decide which Halle playground is "most like" another?

Streaming services and online shops recommend things by measuring how much two items have in common. We teach that idea on a file every Halle family can picture: the city's own register of its playgrounds.

The data

The city of Halle (Saale) publishes "Städtische Spielplätze 2026" on its KomGIS+ open data portal, dated 24 September 2026. Under the city's open data terms it is licensed Creative Commons Namensnennung 3.0 Deutschland (CC BY 3.0 DE), which allows any use, including commercial use, with the credit "Datenquelle: Stadt Halle (Saale)". We downloaded the CSV on 3 November 2026.

It has 135 rows, one per playground, with a name, the age group, a year of building or renewal, the net play area in square metres and, most usefully, a free-text column listing the equipment: "Wippe, 2 Federgeräte, Minikarussell, Hockerbank, Bank, Müllbehälter".

The question

A family likes one small playground, Spielplatz Am Tulpenbrunnen. Which other playground in Halle is most like it?

To answer, the program turns each equipment list into a set of items, drops the counts at the front ("2 Federgeräte" becomes "Federgeräte"), and scores every pair by overlap: the number of items both have, divided by the number of items either has. Two identical lists score 1; two with nothing in common score 0. Statisticians call this the Jaccard index.

The winner is Spielplatz Am Hohen Holz with 0.375. Now read why. Of the three things the two playgrounds share, two are a bench and a bin. Only the spring riders (Federgeräte) are something a child would actually play on. The recommender has learned that Halle playgrounds have bins, which is true and useless: in our count, Müllbehälter appear at 116 of the 135 playgrounds and Bänke at 93.

This is the most important lesson in recommendation systems, and it applies far beyond playgrounds. A feature that almost everything has cannot tell things apart. It only adds noise that happens to push some pairs up. So in Version 2 we remove the furniture and measure again.

The answer changes. Spielplatz Südstr., second before, now comes first, and what it shares with Am Tulpenbrunnen is real play equipment: spring riders and a small roundabout. Nothing about the data changed; only our decision about which features count. That is the honest truth about AI that we want every learner to leave with: the person who chooses the features is making the choice the machine then repeats.

Counts, scores and code are our own; the lists hold 391 different item names, and 135 playgrounds make 9,045 possible pairs. We are not connected with the city.

  1. Ages 6 to 8, no screen. Lay out three toy boxes. Which two are most alike? Now remove anything every box has, like a lid. Did your answer change?
  2. Ages 9 to 12, Python. Type the overlap function and test it on two short lists you invent, one pair identical and one pair with nothing in common, before you trust it on the city file.
  3. Teenagers. Version 2 still keeps "Sitzwürfel" (seat cubes) at Südstr. Is that furniture? Also look for "Federgerät" and "Federgeräte": the same thing, counted as two. Fix both and see whether the ranking moves again.
  4. Adults. Find the pair of playgrounds with the highest score in the whole city. Several Bolzplätze with only football goals score 1.0 against each other. Is a perfect match a useful recommendation, or just a duplicate?

AI, by age

Which AI tools can each age use in our classes?

The tool makers set the minimum ages, not us, and we follow them.

OpenAI's terms say a user must be at least 13 and that anyone under 18 needs a parent's or guardian's permission. Anthropic's consumer terms for Claude set 18 as the minimum. So in our classes, children work with AI ideas they build and test themselves rather than with chatbot accounts; teenagers use tools only where the tool allows their age and the family agrees; adults may use any tool, always with the habit of checking its output against something they can verify. Our AI tools age guide quotes the terms of many more tools.

Whatever the age, the rule in class is the same: an AI answer is a draft, not a result. The playground project above is a small proof of why. A program that looks clever can still rank benches above slides, and the only protection is a learner who reads the reasons, not just the score.

Questions from Halle

Coding and AI classes in Halle (Saale): questions and answers

Are the classes in German?

No. Every class is live and taught in English only, by mentors based in India. They speak clearly, adapt to each learner's pace and accent, and show every step on screen, so learners whose English is still growing can follow.

What ages do you teach in Halle?

Children from 6, teenagers and adults of any age. Children start in Scratch, move to typed Python from about 9, teenagers add machine learning, and adults learn Python with applied AI. Groups are always formed by age.

Do Halle schools teach Informatik?

It depends on the school type. The Gesellschaft für Informatik reports one compulsory weekly hour in grades 6 to 10 at the Sekundarschule and Gemeinschaftsschule, plus an hour moved into grade 5. At the Gymnasium, Informatik is a two-hour elective in grades 9 and 10.

Can an adult with no experience learn coding and AI?

Yes. Our adult courses assume nothing and start with Python from the first line. Many adult learners in Germany want to understand AI tools at work, and reading simple code is the quickest way to judge what those tools produce.

When are classes in Halle time?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Children often pick early afternoons or Saturday mornings; adults tend to choose evenings or Sundays.

Will my child use ChatGPT or Claude in class?

Not as a child. OpenAI requires users to be 13 and needs a parent's permission under 18, and Anthropic sets 18 for Claude. Children build and test their own small AI programs instead, which teaches more about how AI works than chatting with one.

What is the playground project on this page?

An original lesson of ours on the city of Halle's open register of 135 playgrounds. Learners score how alike two playgrounds are by their equipment and discover that benches and bins distort the answer until they are removed.

Do classes stop during the Saxony-Anhalt school holidays?

No, they continue unless you choose to pause. The 2026/27 holidays in Saxony-Anhalt include the Herbstferien from 19 to 30 October 2026 and the Sommerferien from 10 July to 20 August 2027, according to the KMK.

How big are the groups?

Fifteen to twenty learners of a similar age in a group batch. The one-to-one plan gives a learner two private classes a week with their own mentor, at times you agree together.

How is the fee paid?

Monthly. The fee is shown in euro at the European Central Bank reference rate, but the payment itself is taken in US dollars and your bank converts it, so the statement may differ slightly. The first class is free and needs no card.
