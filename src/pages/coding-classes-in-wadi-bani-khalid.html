Wadi Bani Khalid today

A valley that gets harder as it gets better

Difficulty of access here rises steadily with distance up the wadi, and so does almost everything worth measuring. That correlation is the whole problem.

Pools in a sequence

The water pools are the main draw and they run in a line up the valley. A sequence where effort and interest both increase together is the shape that guarantees a biased sample rather than merely permitting one.

A cave at the far end

Muqal Cave sits at the end of the upper pools, a limestone cave formed by the erosion of the rocks over thousands of years. The most distinctive feature in the wilayat is also the hardest one to reach.

Close enough to be busy

The valley lies some 200 km from Muscat and is popular with visitors. Being accessible as a destination and internally difficult is exactly the combination that fills the first pools and empties the last.

The sample stops where the path does

Nobody decides to exclude the far pools. The survey simply ends when the scramble starts, and the resulting dataset carries no record of where it stopped. What comes out is a complete-looking table describing the part of the valley with a path to it.

Wadi Bani Khalid facts with a trail behind them

Each claim traced, and each one held to what its source says.

Water pools as the main attraction

The water pools are described as one of the most prominent tourist attractions here. They are the thing being measured, which makes their arrangement along the valley the relevant fact.

Muqal Cave at the top

Muqal Cave is a natural limestone cave in the wilayat, reached at the end of the upper pools. Its position is the reason the far end of the valley is visited least and photographed most.

Carved by slow erosion

The cave was formed by the erosion of the rocks over thousands of years. A process that slow is a reminder that the valley was not designed for anybody's convenience, least of all a surveyor's.

Two hundred kilometres from the capital

The valley is described as some 200 km from Muscat and popular among visitors. That is close enough to be a day trip, which is what determines how far most people actually walk once they arrive.

A wilayat as well as a valley

Wadi Bani Khalid is a wilayat of Ash Sharqiyah North in its own right, not only a destination. One name covering a valley and an administrative unit is worth stating before anybody counts either.

Spelled more than one way

Published sources give both Wadi Bani Khalid and Wadi Bani Khaled for the same place. That is ordinary for Arabic names written in English and it is noted here rather than quietly standardised.

Modern Age Coders has no connection to the valley, the cave or anything else above and claims none. The wilayat is used here because access difficulty and the quantity of interest rise together along it, which is the exact condition that turns a convenient sample into a wrong one.

Signature project

A valley half as deep as it is, measured perfectly

Ten pools, four readings, every one of them accurate, and a conclusion that is out by fifty three per cent.

1. Walk up the wadi and measure what you reach

Four pools are beside the car park or a short walk from it. Three more need a scramble. The last three need a rope, and the cave is past those. The depths used here are teaching values, because no pool by pool survey is published, and the page says so.

2. Average what you measured

The four reachable pools average 1.77 metres. Nothing about that number is wrong. It is the correct mean of the readings that were actually taken.

3. Compare it against the whole valley

All ten pools average 3.80 metres. The convenient sample understates the wadi by 53 per cent, and the deepest pool anybody ever put a tape into is 2.6 metres against a real deepest of 6.9.

The bias is guaranteed because effort and depth increase together

If the far pools were shallow, stopping early would cost nothing. They are not, and they are deep for the same reason they are hard to reach: the valley steepens. Wherever difficulty of access correlates with the thing being measured, a convenient sample is not merely incomplete, it is wrong in a predictable direction. Are the pool depths real?

The pools, Muqal Cave, its formation by erosion over thousands of years and the distance of some 200 km from Muscat are published. The ten depths and the access categories are teaching values, because no pool by pool survey was found, and the page says so wherever they appear.

How is this different from people refusing to answer a survey?

That is the subject declining. This is the measurer not arriving. The distinction matters because the fixes differ: refusals are chased with follow-ups and weighting, while unreachable cases need somebody to go further, or an honest statement of where the data stops.

Is there a Modern Age Coders centre in Wadi Bani Khalid?

No. No premises are claimed in Wadi Bani Khalid or anywhere in Ash Sharqiyah North. Classes are live online and need a laptop or desktop, working audio, a current browser and a stable connection. The page describes where the service reaches, not a building.

Why is a convenient sample worse here than usual?

Because difficulty and depth rise together up the valley. When access correlates with the quantity you are measuring, stopping early does not just shrink the sample, it shifts the answer in a direction you could have predicted. A random sample of four pools would have been far better than the nearest four.

Do you teach adults here as well as school students?

Yes, to 67. Adults work in Python, analytics and reporting. If a dataset you rely on was collected wherever collection was easiest, this is the shape of the problem in it. Bring the real thing where it is yours to bring.
