What families in Puno want to know

For Puno children between 6 and 12, Modern Age Coders offers coding lessons held live online and taught in English. Younger pupils make games and stories in Scratch from coloured blocks; nine to twelve year olds type Python and tackle logic and number puzzles that suit Puno's strong young mathematicians. Each lesson has a real mentor on video, with a group of fifteen to twenty children of similar age or with your child alone, at days and hours you set. To begin, a child needs a computer, a reliable connection and a little English.

Puno's strength

Are Puno children good at maths, and does that help with coding?

On MINEDU's 2025 assessment, yes, and it helps a great deal. Coding asks for the same habits as maths: careful steps, patterns and checking.

Ahead of the country in maths

In 2025, 42.1 per cent of Puno's fourth-graders reached the satisfactory level in maths, against 30.0 per cent across Peru. In reading Puno was close to the national figure, 34.1 against 32.7.

A child who already enjoys numbers often takes to coding fast, because a program is a set of exact instructions, and a mistake shows itself straight away on the screen. Many of our projects for nine to twelve year olds are number puzzles in disguise.

But no coding at school

Puno follows the national curriculum, the CNEB, in which digital skills are a transversal competency spread across every area. The education law, Ley 28044, gives primaria six years, and none of its timetabled hours belongs to programming.

Under the 2026 school-year rules each regional office may set its own spread of teaching weeks, so Puno's calendar can differ from Lima's. Lessons with us run on the family's own calendar and carry on through school holidays unless you ask for a pause.

Who it suits

Can a child in Puno follow online classes in English?

Some children can start today, some should wait a little, and we would rather say which before you pay. Two things decide it: the connection at home and the child's English.

The connection

Only one Puno home in four had an internet service in 2025, and about one in five had a computer. Seven in ten people still went online, mostly on mobile phones. Learning to code takes a proper keyboard and a line steady enough for sixty minutes of video, so plenty of Puno families are not ready yet, and there is no shame in that.

If your home has a computer but a weak connection, try the free class at the hour you would use; the mentor will tell you honestly whether it held. A shared family computer, or a parent's laptop in the evening, works perfectly well.

The English

EF's English Proficiency Index 2025 scores the Puno region 417, in its very low band, second from the bottom of the 15 Peruvian regions it lists, above only Cajamarca. Peru as a whole scores 519; Lima city 555. Every class is in English only, never in Spanish, so for many Puno children English, not coding, will be the harder part.

Our advice: a child of 6 to 8 with no English yet is usually happier waiting a year. A child of 9 to 12 who has started English at school, watches cartoons in English or attends a school with more English hours can start now, and one-to-one classes let the mentor go as slowly as needed.

  1. Try the machine. Load any Scratch project in the browser; if it runs without stuttering, the computer will cope for several months.
  2. Try the line. Hold a video chat with a relative at the planned lesson hour. If the picture keeps stopping, test a different hour.
  3. Try a little English. Ask your child to follow three simple spoken instructions in English, such as "click the green flag". Hesitation is normal; total confusion means waiting or choosing one to one.
  4. Then book the trial. Afterwards the mentor tells you how the equipment held, how your child reasoned and how relaxed they seemed in English.

Families sometimes tell us the English becomes a reason to stay: a weekly hour of building, asking and explaining in English with a mentor who waits for the answer. Our page on learning coding and English together explains how mentors handle children who are still early in English.

Ages 6 to 12

What will a Puno child learn at each age?

The tool follows the age. Children are always taught with others of a similar age, never with teenagers or adults.

Six to eight

Lessons are brief and each ends with something on screen. A character walks, jumps and talks because the child arranged the blocks, and a few months in, every child has a short animation of their own. Instructions are read aloud and demonstrated before anyone tries them.

Eight to ten

Games with scores, timers and levels in Scratch, and for many children their first typed lines of Python. Children learn to plan before building: what should happen first, what repeats, and what decides the ending.

Ten to twelve

By now most of the work is in Python: programs that sketch shapes, hold a conversation, keep lists and open small files. Number lovers get puzzles a program can verify, and everyone meets the train project further down.

A Puno project

How many people rode the train between Cusco and Puno in 2025?

OSITRAN, the regulator for Peru's transport infrastructure, publishes how many passengers each railway carries every month, station to station. Children of ten to twelve work with the file and discover that a trip and a route are different things.

The data

The dataset is OSITRAN's Tráfico de Pasajeros, FERROCARRIL, released on datosabiertos.gob.pe with the ODC-By licence (Open Data Commons Attribution), which lets anyone reuse it with credit. OSITRAN describes it as passenger traffic counted by the company operating each line.

The file we saved on 10 November 2026, 202608-PDE-REP-00301.csv, has 4,955 rows from January 2019 to August 2026. Each row is a month, an origin station, a destination station and a kind of service, with the number of passengers. The southern line, marked SUR, links Puno with Cusco and Arequipa.

The question

The question for a class of eleven year olds: which train route in the south carried the most people in 2025? Most guess the line between Cusco and Puno, and they are right.

Version 1 adds up the passengers for every pair of stations, from one station to another, and prints the four biggest. Then the children look at the result and frown, because something is listed twice.

The program did exactly what it was told: it treated Cusco to Puno and Puno to Cusco as two different things, because the pair (Cusco, Puno) is not the same as the pair (Puno, Cusco). For a ticket that is true. For a question about routes, it is not: both trains run on the same track between the same two stations. The children need a way to make the two pairs into one key.

The trick is one line: tuple(sorted((a, b))). Sorting the two station names puts them in alphabetical order, so Cusco to Puno and Puno to Cusco both become (CUZCO WANCHAC, PUNO ESTACION), and their passengers land in the same box: 24,714 people in 2025. Version 2 also keeps the one-way counts, and they tell a second story. More people rode towards Puno, 14,413, than back to Cusco, 10,301. The file does not say why. Children suggest that some travellers leave Puno another way, by bus or by plane from Juliaca, which is a good guess to test, not a fact.

  1. Six and seven, paper and pencil. Draw Cusco and Puno with a line between them. Put five counters on trains going one way and three coming back. How many people used the line?
  2. Eight and nine, Scratch. Make a train sprite that goes to Puno and back. Count each trip with one variable, then with two, one for each direction.
  3. Ten to twelve, Python. Start with Version 1 and say in your own words why the same route appears twice. Then type the sorted line yourself before reading Version 2.
  4. A step further. Use the monthly rows to find the month in 2025 when the two directions were closest. What might make people travel both ways in the same month?

Class times

What time would classes be for a child in Puno?

Whenever suits your family. Puno is on Peru time all year, and India is a fixed 10 hours 30 minutes ahead.

Peru: UTC minus five hours throughout the year, without daylight saving (tz database, America/Lima; INACAL is the official timekeeper). India: UTC plus five hours thirty minutes, also unchanged through the year.

Young children usually concentrate more easily earlier in the day, so many families pick a morning slot during the January to March holidays and an after-school slot later. Neither country changes its clocks, so the hour you choose in February is the same hour in November. When you book, you pick the time in Puno time and the system shows the mentor theirs.

Coding for kids in Puno: questions and answers

Are the classes in Spanish?

No. Every lesson is given live and online in English, never in Spanish. Mentors talk slowly and demonstrate before explaining; if a child replies in Spanish, they hear the English version back, kindly. The trial lesson shows whether your child is ready.

Can children in Puno take coding classes?

Yes, from home. A child in Puno, Juliaca or a nearby district joins live on a computer with a mentor on video. Distance from the city makes no difference.

My child has little English. Should we wait?

For a child of 6 to 8 with no English yet, usually yes. Children of 9 to 12 with some school English can start, and one-to-one classes let the mentor go slowly. EF rated Puno region's English very low in 2025, so we check this carefully in the free class.

What do we need at home?

A desktop or laptop computer, a line that can carry an hour of video and a headset with a microphone; a phone alone will not do. In 2025 about one Puno home in four had an internet service (INEI), so please test first.

At what age can my child start?

From 6, with Scratch blocks and nothing to type, if your child is comfortable with some English. From about 9, children move to typed Python.

When would lessons take place?

Any day, any time, flexible: choose a batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at the hour that suits your family. India is always 10 hours 30 minutes ahead of Puno, so 09:30 in Puno is 20:00 for the mentor.

My child is strong at maths. Is there something for them?

Yes. Maths through coding and our maths competition course suit children who enjoy numbers, with problems we write ourselves. Puno's fourth-graders did well in maths in 2025, and coding builds on that.

How many children share a class?

Between fifteen and twenty, all close in age, with one mentor; private lessons are available if you would rather.

How much does it cost?

There is a single monthly fee. It appears here in US dollars and your card pays it in dollars, leaving your bank to work out the soles; visitors in India are shown rupees. Nothing is charged for the trial lesson.

Does my child get a certificate?

Courses that include one give our own certificate of completion, describing what the child built. It is not a school qualification in Peru and is not issued by MINEDU or any school.
