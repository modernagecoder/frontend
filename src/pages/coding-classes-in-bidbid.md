## A wilayat that counts its towers and not its villages

Bidbid describes itself confidently and incompletely, which is the normal way places are described and the reason so many published rates are fiction.

### Forty towers and no village list

The wilayat is credited with 40 towers scattered in different villages. The towers are counted. The villages are not. Anybody who wants a rate has to invent the half that is missing, and inventing it is easy enough that people rarely notice they did.

### A wadi that splits the banks in two

The wadi running through here divides its banks into two parts, lined with date palms. A settlement on two banks is one place or two places depending on who is filling in the form, and that choice quietly changes any denominator built from it.

### A souq that draws in the neighbours

People come to Fanja's souq from the surrounding villages to buy and to sell locally made handicrafts. Who counts as being from here is a question the market does not ask and a statistician cannot avoid.

A numerator is a fact and a rate is an argument

Forty towers is a fact somebody went and counted. Two towers per village is a claim about a quantity nobody counted at all. The two look alike on a page, they print to the same width, and only one of them can be checked against anything.

## Bidbid facts with a trail behind them

What is published, what is not, and which is which.

### Forty towers, counted

Bidbid is described as containing 40 towers scattered across its villages. This is the published number and the page uses it as published, without dividing it by anything.

### A fort rebuilt from local material

Bidbid Fort was among the first restorations to use local construction materials, with palm tree trunks holding up parts of the roof. A restored building is a useful reminder that the thing a record names can change while the record does not.

### Aflaj with names of their own

The wilayat is known for its aflaj, among them the Eastern Hammam Falaj, a western hot one flowing from the heights of the mountains, Falaj Al Rahah and Falaj Al Hakka. Four named channels is four records, and none of them is a village.

### A wadi lined with date palms

Wadi Fanja divides its banks into two parts and is surrounded by date palms and other trees. Geography that splits a settlement is geography that will eventually split a row in somebody's table.

### A market for the surrounding villages

Buyers and sellers come to Fanja's souq from neighbouring villages with locally made handicrafts. That the market has a catchment wider than the village is exactly why village counts and market counts should never be used interchangeably.

### A place filed under three governorates

One population database lists Fanja, in Bidbid, under more than one governorate across its own pages. Bidbid is a wilayat of Ad Dakhiliyah. That a reference work disagrees with itself about where a village sits is the plainest possible argument for checking a denominator before dividing by it.

None of this is a claim about Modern Age Coders and no association with any of it is implied. Bidbid is here because it publishes a clean numerator and no denominator, which is the exact shape of the mistake the project teaches.

## One published number, six honest answers, all different

Nobody has to lie, misread anything or make an arithmetic slip for this to go wrong. They only have to fill in the number that was never given.

### 1. Take the number that is actually published

Forty towers, scattered in different villages. That is the whole of what the source says. It does not say how many villages, and no other source found for this page says so either.

### 2. Reach for a rate, because a rate sounds better

Forty towers is a fact and towers per village sounds like an insight. Producing the second from the first needs a village count, so one gets chosen, usually silently and usually from a rough sense of how big the place is.

### 3. Try six reasonable guesses and compare

Between 8 and 35 villages the rate runs from 5.00 down to 1.14 towers per village. That is a 4.4-fold spread. Every value is arithmetically correct, every one would survive review, and the published data cannot distinguish between them.

| Assumed villages | Towers per village | Where the assumption came from | Would a reader query it? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 8 | 5.00 | A guess that the wilayat is small | No |
| 10 | 4.00 | A round number chosen because it divides neatly | No |
| 14 | 2.86 | Counting the villages somebody could name | No |
| 20 | 2.00 | A guess that produces a tidy answer | Least of all this one |
| 26 | 1.54 | Counting every hamlet on a map | No |
| 35 | 1.14 | Counting every named place at all | No |

### Two towers per village is the most dangerous row in the table

A rate of 2.00 looks like a finding. It is round, it is plausible, and it is the one a reader is least likely to question, which makes it the likeliest to be repeated and the hardest to retract. Nothing about it is more true than 1.14 or 5.00. Every figure above was computed before this was written, and no village count for Bidbid appears anywhere on this page, because none was found.

## Publish the numerator, and say out loud what you do not have

Five habits that cost nothing and keep an invented number off a page.

| Stage | Rule | What it prevents | What it gives you |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Source | Cite the denominator or do not divide | A rate resting on somebody's impression | A number with two halves you can defend |
| Name | Write the assumed value into the sentence | An assumption that reads as a measurement | A claim a reader can argue with |
| Range | Show the rate across plausible denominators | One convenient answer presented alone | An honest span instead of false precision |
| Prefer | Report the raw count where the rate is unsupported | Insight manufactured out of nothing | A statement that stays true |
| Mark | Flag any derived figure as derived | A calculation quoted later as data | A trail back to what was actually published |

### For younger learners

Forty counters and a handful of empty boxes, with the child choosing how many boxes before sharing them out. Doing it twice with different box counts makes the point faster than any explanation, and it needs no screen at all.

### For teenagers

Python computes the rate across every village count from 8 to 35 and prints the whole range. The learner then writes a function that refuses to return a rate unless it is given a sourced denominator, and tests that it refuses.

### For adults

Derived figures are marked as derived, assumptions are written into the text rather than the footnotes, and any rate without a cited denominator is reported as a raw count instead.

The tower count, the fort restoration, the named aflaj, the wadi and the souq are published. The six village counts in the project are explicitly assumptions and are labelled as such in the table itself, because no village count for Bidbid was found in any source consulted for this page.

## Bidbid coding class questions

Local facts, teaching method and service terms, answered separately.

### Is the 40 towers figure real?

Yes. Bidbid is described as having 40 towers scattered in different villages, and the page uses that number exactly as published. The six village counts in the project table are labelled assumptions, because no source consulted for this page gives a village count for the wilayat.

### Why is an invented denominator so hard to spot?

Because the finished rate carries no trace of it. Two towers per village and forty towers are printed in the same typeface and read with the same confidence. One of them can be traced to somebody who went and counted, and the other cannot, and nothing on the page tells them apart unless the writer chooses to say so.

### Is there a Modern Age Coders centre in Bidbid?

No. No premises are claimed in Bidbid or anywhere in Ad Dakhiliyah. Classes are live online and need a laptop or desktop, working audio, a current browser and a stable connection. The page describes where the service reaches, not a building.

### What should a learner do when the denominator is missing?

Report the count and say the rate is unavailable. If a rate is genuinely needed, give the range across plausible denominators and name the assumption in the sentence rather than burying it. Both options are more useful than one tidy number that cannot be checked.

### Can an adult in Bidbid study rather than a school student?

Yes, up to 67. Adults work in Python, analytics and reporting, and anybody who has been asked for a per-head or per-branch figure that nobody had the head count for has met this already. Their own report makes the best exercise where the data is theirs to use.

### What does a learner have at the end of this project?

A table showing how far one rate moves under reasonable assumptions, a function that declines to divide without a sourced denominator, and the habit of writing the assumption into the sentence. Those survive every change of tool.

### How much do Bidbid coding classes cost?

The first class is free. Standard group tuition is USD 100 per month for two live lessons each week, normally eight lessons monthly. Standard private tuition is USD 150 per month for one learner with one teacher on the same usual frequency. Course, format, recurring time and availability are confirmed before payment.

### What is the class size for Bidbid learners?

A group normally contains five to eight compatible learners. Compatibility includes current level, learning pace and objective, not age alone. Private tuition is one learner with one teacher. If no suitable group is ready, a learner can discuss private tuition or wait for an appropriate cohort.

### How are Bidbid class times arranged?

Oman is one and a half hours behind India. The teacher and learner agree a recurring time before payment, generally for two live lessons each week. Friday and Saturday options may be discussed subject to availability. School, work and travel schedules, examinations and Ramadan routines should be mentioned before confirmation.

### What happens in the free Bidbid class?

The teacher finds out what the learner is trying to become able to do and sets one task at that level. A child shares counters between a number of boxes they chose themselves, a teenager prints a rate across a range of denominators, and an adult is asked which numbers in a report they wrote were measured and which were derived. The teacher then recommends a course, a format and a time, and states the USD fee. That class is free.
