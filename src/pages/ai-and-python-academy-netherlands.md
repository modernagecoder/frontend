# The Netherlands is buying two hundred million euros of AI compute. Under a third of its schools offer informatica.

On 10 October 2025 SURF announced that the Netherlands would get its own AI factory in Groningen: 70 million euros from Europe, 70 million from the ministries, 60 million from the north's Nij Begun fund, an expertise centre opening in 2026 and a supercomputer at full capacity in 2027. In the same year the curriculum institute SLO published that informatica is offered at 238 of 774 havo schools and 236 of 761 vwo schools, and taken by 8.9 percent of havo and 12.6 percent of vwo exam candidates. The machine is being built; the ladder up to it is optional. This page is the ladder: hand-written Python, then data, then models with a measured error, then agents, then a public repository.

At a glance: Track: Python, data, ML, agents, GitHub; Format: Live video, never pre-recorded; Batch: 5 to 10 students, or one to one; Timing: Dutch afternoons and evenings, agreed on the call; Language: English; Ages: 6 to 67; Fees: From USD 100 a month; First class: Free, no card needed. ## A subject that a third of schools offer and a tenth of pupils take

These are SLO's own figures, published in July 2025 as the starting point for a two-year revision of the subject. They are the most useful piece of context a Dutch parent has about this field, and almost nobody states them.

Informatica in the Netherlands is a **profile elective**: in the Nature and Technology profile a pupil may pick it in place of wiskunde D, NLT or biologie, and in every other profile it can be taken in the free space. Schools are **not obliged to offer it**. There is **no central exam**; the subject ends with a school examination, which SLO notes leaves little view of what is actually taught beyond the results of those exams.

The current examination programme dates from **2019**. The subject carries **320 study-load hours** in havo and **440 in vwo**, a compulsory core with a skills domain and five conceptual domains, and elective themes. SLO describes it as a constructing discipline, a field in which making things is central, and observes that valid assessment of that kind of learning needs forms of testing that are not yet common in Dutch schools.

Now the arithmetic a parent actually needs. Of 774 havo schools, **238 offer informatica**, 30.7 percent. Of 761 vwo schools, **236 offer it**, 31.0 percent. SLO's phrase is that the subject is offered at almost a third of havo and vwo schools. So for roughly two pupils in three, the question of whether to take it never arises, because the timetable does not contain it.

Where it is offered, uptake is a minority choice. In 2024, **4,849 havo exam candidates** sat informatica, 8.9 percent of 49,234, and **4,506 vwo candidates**, 12.6 percent of 35,690, on DUO's examination data. Put the two constraints together and the share of a Dutch cohort that leaves secondary school having studied the subject formally is small, in a country that has just decided to build a national AI supercomputer.

None of this is a complaint about Dutch schools; the revision now under way is precisely because the field has moved. It is a description of a gap, and a gap is where a ladder goes.

## What two hundred million euros of compute is for, and who gets to use it

The Dutch AI factory is not a metaphor. It is a building in Groningen with a budget, a consortium and two dates, and the people it is being built for are exactly the people this ladder is meant to produce.

On 10 October 2025 SURF, the Dutch research computing cooperative, announced that the EuroHPC Joint Undertaking had awarded **70 million euros** to a Netherlands AI factory. The Dutch ministries add **70 million** and the north's Nij Begun programme **60 million**, for a total of **200 million euros**. The factory sits in **Groningen**. Its **expertise centre** opens in **2026** and its **supercomputer** reaches full capacity in **2027**.

The consortium is the Stichting Nederlandse AIfabriek, SURF, TNO, the Dutch AI Coalition and Samenwerking Noord, with Nij Begun and several ministries cooperating. SURF's own announcement lists who it is for: **startups, SMEs, researchers, education and government**. The Dutch AI Coalition's description of the facility puts the purpose plainly: to develop AI responsibly and in line with European rules, to keep sensitive data inside the Netherlands, and to reduce dependence on American and Chinese technology platforms.

Read those two paragraphs beside the SLO table above and the shape of the country's position is clear. The Netherlands is one of nineteen AI factories the EuroHPC programme has selected across Europe, with thirteen smaller antennas alongside them. It has decided that building models, not only using them, is a national capability worth two hundred million euros. And the school subject that would feed that capability is optional, offered by a third of schools and chosen by a tenth of pupils.

A supercomputer does not do anything by itself. Somebody has to write the Python that loads the data, define the model that trains on it, measure whether the model is any good, and wrap the result in something that can act. Those are the five rungs below, in the order they have to be climbed. A Dutch teenager who has climbed them arrives at a university, a startup or that expertise centre able to use the thing the country has paid for. One who has not arrives able to prompt it.

We are not connected to the factory, to SURF or to any consortium member, and we do not claim to be. We are the ladder a family can start on this month.

## The stage is small, the queue is short, and four Dutch pupils a year go to the world final

A national competition with four places at the top, a curriculum table with a third in it, and a budget line with nine zeros: between them they describe the opening in front of a Dutch student who can actually build.

The Nederlandse Informatica Olympiade is the national programming competition for secondary pupils, run by its own foundation with Windesheim, Eindhoven University of Technology, Leiden University and the University of Twente among its partners. It runs in three rounds, and its four winners go to the International Olympiad in Informatics, hosted by a different country each year. Four places, for a country of roughly 85,000 havo and vwo exam candidates a year.

That is not a lament. It is a description of how empty the top of the pyramid is. A pupil who arrives at the first round with a year of hand-written Python and a repository of working programs is not competing against thousands of peers with the same preparation, because the SLO table above says most of those peers were never offered the subject.

The same is true one level down. A university admissions tutor, a startup on the Groningen expertise centre's doorstep, an employer in Eindhoven or on the Zuidas: each of them meets far more applicants who have used an AI tool than applicants who can show a model they trained, a stated error rate, and a link. The second group is small enough that membership of it is noticed.

And delivery is not the constraint. The Netherlands is among the most connected countries in Europe, English is the working language of its universities and its technology sector, and a live class taught in English into a Dutch living room needs nothing the household does not already have.

## What comes after the basisschool, in the order it has to come

Five rungs, and the sequence is the point. Each one depends on the rung below it in a way that cannot be skipped, which is why a course that opens with the exciting-sounding top rung tends to produce students who can show something and cannot say why it works.

**The five rungs, and the Dutch school years they usually land in**

| Rung | Roughly | What the student actually does |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Python by hand | Groep 6 to 8, brugklas | Types real syntax instead of dragging blocks: loops, functions, files, and error messages the student reads and repairs alone |
| 2. Data | Onderbouw, years 1 to 3 | Opens a file that came from somewhere real, repairs it, draws it, and hunts down the single row that would have wrecked the answer |
| 3. Machine learning | Havo 3 to vwo 6 | Fits a model to data, reports how wrong it is in a unit a parent would recognise, and checks it beats a dumb guess |
| 4. Agents | Bovenbouw and beyond | Writes a program that receives a goal instead of instructions, chooses its own route, uses tools, and makes its mistakes where they can be seen |
| 5. GitHub and the write-up | Any of the above | Puts the code somewhere public with a plain paragraph on what it does, how well, and where it gives up |

### Why nobody gets to skip a rung

The mistakes that matter in a model live in the data, so a student who has never cleaned a file cannot judge a model. An agent that cannot measure its own output is a confident random number generator, so a student who has never evaluated anything cannot be trusted to build one. And work that lives only on a laptop persuades nobody: what a university or an employer responds to is a repository a stranger can open, run and check.
The order in which we teach every subject on the site, with the reasons, is on the [coding roadmap](/coding-roadmap). A Dutch-specific version, written by school year with what a parent should expect to see at home at each stage, follows in this series.

## Two clocks, only one of which moves

The Netherlands changes its clock twice a year and India, where the teaching operation sits, never does. So a Dutch afternoon is three and a half hours behind the teacher in summer and four and a half in winter, and the slots below are the ones that work at both ends. The recurring time is agreed on the free class, not promised on a page.

- **After school** Netherlands time, mid to late afternoon. Groep 6 to 8 and the onderbouw, while concentration is still there; late evening for the teacher.
- **Early evening** Netherlands time, after dinner. The main block for havo and vwo pupils and for university students; the teacher's late night.
- **Weekend** Saturday and Sunday, Netherlands time, morning or afternoon. Working adults, and families whose weekday evenings are already full.

- **The opening lesson** Free, and a lesson rather than a sales call. The learner is handed one problem and the teacher watches how they go at it, which fixes the starting rung better than any form could.
- **Between five and ten to a group** Grouped by what a learner has shown they can do, drawn from twenty-five countries, so a strong student in Almere is never held to a local average and a beginner in Leiden is never lost in one.
- **Or a teacher to yourself** For a narrow adult goal, an examination year that is already full, or the Copilot Studio courses, which are only ever taught one to one.
- **Ninety minutes, twice a week** Both screens shared, so a misunderstanding is caught while it is forming rather than discovered in homework a week later.
- **English, with the Dutch words kept** Groep, havo, vwo, profielkeuze and informatica are used as the school uses them. The libraries, the documentation and the error messages are in English anyway.
- **Committed as it is written** Every lesson's work goes into the student's own GitHub repository that day. What they leave with is a history, not a folder of files.

## Before the first class: what Dutch parents and students ask us

### Our school does not offer informatica. Is that unusual, and does it matter?

It is the majority position. SLO's July 2025 starting note gives 238 of 774 havo schools and 236 of 761 vwo schools offering the subject in 2024, just under a third, and 8.9 percent of havo and 12.6 percent of vwo exam candidates sitting it. Whether it matters depends on what the student wants: for a child aiming at anything involving models, data or agents, this track is the subject their school does not have.

### Our school does offer it. Is this track redundant?

No, and it is designed to sit beside the subject. The school programme is worth 320 study-load hours in havo and 440 in vwo, ends with a school examination rather than a central one, and is built around constructing digital artefacts. Rung 3 of this ladder is where a student learns to say how good an artefact is, with an error stated in a real unit. That is the part the school exam programme, by SLO's own account, finds hardest to assess.

### What is the AI factory, and can a student use it?

A 200 million euro facility in Groningen announced by SURF on 10 October 2025: 70 million from the EuroHPC Joint Undertaking, 70 million from Dutch ministries and 60 million from the Nij Begun programme, with an expertise centre opening in 2026 and a supercomputer at full capacity in 2027. SURF lists education among its intended users alongside startups, SMEs, researchers and government. We have no connection to it and make no promise about access; what we teach is what a person needs to know to make use of such a thing.

### How is this different from the coding club at school or the library?

A club teaches a child to make a program do a thing. This track teaches a student to build something that makes a decision, and then to find out whether the decision was right. That is a different education, and it has an order: Python first, then data, then a model with a measured error, then an agent, then a repository a stranger can open. Blocks are a good place to be at seven and a poor place to still be at fifteen.

### When you say agents, what do you mean by it?

A program that is handed a goal rather than a list of steps, works out its own steps, calls tools or other programs to carry them out, and can be stopped and corrected while it does so. Students build one in Python after the data and model rungs, and build it so that its wrong turns show up on screen rather than passing silently. The two dedicated agent courses are in the catalogue above; the Copilot Studio one is taught privately only.

### My child is in groep 8. Should they start this year?

Gently, or after the doorstroomtoets. The test sits between late January and mid-February with results in March, and a new subject in those weeks is a bad trade. A child who is already on rung 1 can keep a light routine through the year; a child who is not should start in the spring, when the school advice is settled and the brugklas is a known quantity.

### Machine learning at eleven or twelve: is that realistic?

Yes, for the right meaning of the words. A child does not need calculus to watch a program derive a rule from examples and then get a case wrong that the child chose. Below about ten the work is sequencing, prediction and argument; from groep 7 or 8 it becomes Python written by hand; and the mathematics turns up later to explain something the student has already watched happen on their own screen, which is the only order in which it ever sticks.

### What does the student have to show at the end?

A URL and a GitHub repository. Whatever the student finished is live somewhere a stranger can visit, its source is readable, and a short paragraph beside it says what it does, how well it performs and where it breaks down. That paragraph is the difference between engineering and a demo, and it is the part universities and employers actually read.
