The other two are chosen from eight optional papers, and Pearson allows ten different pairings. What 9FM0 is

Pearson Edexcel A level Further Mathematics (9FM0) is a linear A level of four 90-minute papers worth 75 marks each: Core Pure Mathematics 1 and 2, which every student sits, plus two optional papers chosen from Further Pure, Further Statistics, Further Mechanics and Decision Mathematics, in ten permitted pairs. Grades A* to E come from the 300-mark total. Four papers

What are the four Edexcel Further Maths papers?

Four, all 1 hour 30 minutes and 75 marks. Papers 1 and 2 are compulsory Core Pure; Papers 3 and 4 are the options.

The specification states that 9FM0 "consists of four externally-examined papers. Students must take Paper 1 and Paper 2, the two mandatory Core Pure papers, and two optional papers." It also says "Students must complete all assessments in May/June in any single year", so all four papers are sat in the same summer. "Calculators can be used in the assessment" on every paper, and the booklet Mathematical Formulae and Statistical Tables is provided.

Core Pure is not split by topic

The Core Pure content is proof, complex numbers, matrices, further algebra and functions, further calculus, further vectors, polar coordinates, hyperbolic functions and differential equations. Pearson says "Paper 1 and Paper 2 may contain questions on any topics from the Pure Mathematics content."

So there is no "Paper 1 topics" list to revise from. Either paper can ask anything in Core Pure.

Options carry half the marks

Papers 3 and 4 together are 150 of the 300 marks. Which two a student sits is therefore not a detail: it decides half the A level.

The Department for Education's subject content sets this shape for every board, with a prescribed core of about half and options for the rest. Edexcel delivers the options as separate papers.

Ten routes

Which Edexcel Further Maths options can be taken together?

Either any two Option 1 papers, or an Option 1 paper with its matching Option 2 paper. Pearson counts ten pairs in all.

Pearson's rule is short: "Students choose a pair of options, either: any two Option 1 papers, or a matching pair of Option 1 and Option 2 papers. This makes a total of ten different option pairs." There are four Option 1 papers, so six ways to pick two of them, and four matching pairs, which gives the ten. Further Statistics 1, Further Mechanics 1 and Decision Mathematics 1 can each be sat as Paper 3 or as Paper 4, which Pearson explains with the note that, for example, Further Statistics 1 "is also the Paper 4 option 4B paper".

  1. Ask the school which route it teaches. Many teach one route for the whole class.
  2. Check the route against the degree the student is aiming for. Engineering applicants often want mechanics; computing applicants often like decision mathematics.
  3. If the student wants a different option, ask whether the school can enter them for it while they are taught elsewhere. Entry is by route code, so the exams officer must know early.
  4. Plan teaching time: each option paper is a quarter of the A level, the same as a Core Pure paper.
  5. Confirm the final entry, 9FM0 plus the route code, before the entry deadline.

Grading

How is Edexcel A level Further Maths graded, and what if you take extra options?

On the 300-mark total of four papers, with thresholds that can differ by route. If a student sits more than two options, Pearson grades the combination giving the highest grade.

Pearson writes that 9FM0 "will be graded and certificated on a six-grade scale from A* to E using the total combined marks (out of 300) for the two compulsory papers and the two optional papers chosen. The different routes within the qualification may have different grade thresholds." A student below E receives U.

Taking more than two options is allowed: "Students are permitted to take more than the two optional papers if they want to extend their course of study." Then "the combination of papers that result in the best grade will be used. The combination of papers that is graded may not be the combination that gives the highest number of marks." That last sentence surprises people, but it follows from the previous one: if routes have different thresholds, a lower raw total on one route can still clear a higher grade than a larger total on another.

The matrix requirement is particular to Further Maths. A calculator that handles 3 × 3 determinants and inverses saves time on Core Pure, but, as on every A level, working that shows the method still earns the method marks.

Worked examples, ours

Core Pure and option examples we wrote

Two short Core Pure problems, an induction outline and a Further Mechanics 1 collision. None is a Pearson question.

Our Core Pure problem on complex numbers. Solve z2 − 4z + 13 = 0 and describe the roots on an Argand diagram.

Solving

The discriminant is 16 − 52 = −36, so z = (4 ± 6i) ÷ 2 = 2 ± 3i.

Real-coefficient quadratics always give conjugate pairs, which is a quick check.

Interpreting

The roots are reflections of each other in the real axis, at (2, 3) and (2, −3). Each has modulus √13.

Their sum 4 and product 13 match the coefficients, a second check worth one line.

Our Core Pure problem on matrices. For M = (2 1; 5 3), the determinant is 2 × 3 − 1 × 5 = 1, so M is invertible and M−1 = (3 −1; −5 2). Since det M = 1, M preserves area: a unit square maps to a parallelogram of area 1.

Our induction outline. Prove that the sum of r(r + 1) for r = 1 to n equals n(n + 1)(n + 2)/3. Base case n = 1: both sides are 2. Assume it for n = k. Adding (k + 1)(k + 2) gives k(k + 1)(k + 2)/3 + (k + 1)(k + 2) = (k + 1)(k + 2)(k + 3)/3, which is the formula for n = k + 1. Conclude in a sentence that the result holds for all positive integers n. Checked by us for n = 10: both sides are 440.

Our Further Mechanics 1 problem. A 3 kg sphere moving at 4 m s−1 hits a 1 kg sphere at rest, directly, with coefficient of restitution e = 0.5. Momentum: 3 × 4 = 3v1 + v2. Restitution: v2 − v1 = 0.5 × 4 = 2. Solving, v1 = 2.5 m s−1 and v2 = 4.5 m s−1, both in the original direction.

When the school's route does not fit

What if my school does not teach the 9FM0 option I need?

Talk to the exams officer first; an option is entered as part of a route, so the entry has to be planned. Teaching the option can then happen outside school.

Because Pearson enters students for 9FM0 "along with the entry code for the optional route that they wish to take", a student who wants, say, Further Mechanics while the class sits Decision Mathematics needs a different route code, not just extra lessons. Some schools can accommodate that; others cannot. Asking in Year 12 gives everyone time.

Where a school agrees, we teach the missing option as a 1-on-1 course alongside the school's Core Pure lessons, and we teach Core Pure too for students whose school does not offer Further Maths at all. We never enter students ourselves, and we cannot promise a school will change an entry. Our Further Maths tuition page covers the subject more broadly.

9FM0 questions

What students ask about Edexcel A level Further Maths

How many exams are in Edexcel A level Further Maths?

Four, each 1 hour 30 minutes and 75 marks. Core Pure Mathematics 1 and 2 are compulsory, and the other two are optional papers.

What are the Edexcel Further Maths options?

Further Pure 1 and 2, Further Statistics 1 and 2, Further Mechanics 1 and 2, and Decision Mathematics 1 and 2. Students take two of the eight, in an allowed pair.

Can I take Further Pure 2 with Further Statistics 1?

No. Pearson allows any two Option 1 papers or a matching Option 1 and Option 2 pair. Further Pure 2 only pairs with Further Pure 1.

How many option routes are there in 9FM0?

Ten. Six come from choosing two of the four Option 1 papers, and four are the matching pairs, such as Further Mechanics 1 with Further Mechanics 2.

Are the grade boundaries the same for every 9FM0 route?

Not necessarily. Pearson says the different routes may have different grade thresholds, so compare a mark with the boundaries for the route actually sat.

What happens if I sit three option papers?

Pearson grades whichever combination produces the highest grade. That may not be the combination with the highest raw total, because thresholds can differ between routes.

Do I need A level Maths to take Edexcel Further Maths?

Pearson sets no formal prior learning requirement, but says students who would benefit most are likely to have a Level 3 GCE in Mathematics. In practice it is taken alongside or after A level Maths.

What calculator do I need for 9FM0?

One with an iterative function, matrix calculations up to at least 3 by 3, and statistical distributions, without symbolic algebra or calculus. An exam mode is fine if it makes the calculator compliant.

When are your 9FM0 classes?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Times are in UK time.

Are you linked with Pearson or Edexcel?

No. Modern Age Coders is independent and has no connection with Pearson. Every example and practice question here was written by us.
