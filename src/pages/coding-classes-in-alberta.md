# Coding Classes in Alberta

> Alberta's CTS computing science courses are worth one credit each and are joined by prerequisites, so they form a chain rather than a menu. Reaching Recursive Algorithms 1 means passing through Structured Programming 1 and 2, Procedural Programming 1, Data Structures 1, Iterative Algorithm 1 and Object-oriented Programming 1 first. Modern Age Coders teaches live online coding and math to ages 6 to 67 in Alberta from USD 100 a month, and the first class is free.

## How is Alberta computing science actually structured?

Career and Technology Studies computing science is delivered as **1-credit courses**, and students usually aim to complete five in a semester. An Alberta High School Diploma requires a minimum of 100 credits, so one computing science course is one per cent of a diploma, competing with fine arts, second languages and physical education for the same optional space.

The important part is not the price. It is the dependencies.

| Course | Prerequisite | Depth in the chain |
|---|---|---:|
| CSE1110 Structured Programming 1 | None | 1 |
| CSE1120 Structured Programming 2 | CSE1110 | 2 |
| CSE2110 Procedural Programming 1 | CSE1120 | 3 |
| CSE2120 Data Structures 1 | CSE2110 | 4 |
| CSE3120 Object-oriented Programming 1 | CSE2110 | 4 |
| CSE3110 Iterative Algorithm 1 | CSE2120 | 5 |
| CSE3310 Recursive Algorithms 1 | CSE3110 and CSE3120 | 6 |

**Six links, six semesters, nothing spare.** If a prerequisite has to be finished before the next course starts, the deepest material sits six semesters from a standing start. Grade 10 to Grade 12 is six semesters. A student beginning Structured Programming 1 in their first semester of Grade 10, and never taking a wrong turn, arrives at Recursive Algorithms 1 in their last. Anyone starting a semester late does not get there at all.

Not every course sits on that chain. **Client-side Scripting 1 and 2 and the CSE project courses have no prerequisite**, so they are available immediately and are a sensible way to earn a credit or test whether a student enjoys this. They are just not the door to the deep end. Structured Programming 1 is.

Source: [Calgary Board of Education, CBe-learn computing science course listing](https://cbe-learn.cbe.ab.ca/computing-science.aspx), for the course codes, prerequisites, 1-credit value and the note that students usually aim to complete five courses in a semester. Verified 17 August 2026.

## What does that mean before Grade 10?

Structured Programming 1 has no prerequisite, which means Alberta expects a student to arrive knowing nothing. That is fair as policy. It is also a whole semester spent learning what a variable is, what a loop does, and why an error message is information rather than an insult.

A student who already knows those things does not skip the course. **They spend it consolidating instead of surviving**, and arrive at Structured Programming 2 fluent rather than shaky. Over six links that difference compounds, because every course in a chain assumes the one before it landed properly.

| Stage | What we would do |
|---|---|
| Grades 4 to 6 | Ordering, counting, predicting what a program will do before running it. No syntax, no deadline |
| Grades 7 to 9 | The target years. Blocks into typed code, aiming to write and debug a small program unaided before Grade 10 |
| Grade 10 | Take Structured Programming 1 in the first semester if the deep end is the goal. Scripting and project courses are good but off-chain |
| Grades 11 and 12 | Protect the diploma courses. Starting from nothing here, aim at scripting and a portfolio rather than recursion |

**What we will not claim:** nothing here carries CTS credit, appears on an Alberta transcript, or counts toward the 100.

## The Alberta student data project

Every market page here works through one analytical trap. Alberta's is a computer science concept applied to the student's own timetable: **capacity does not beat dependency.**

The learner is given the prerequisite table and one question. If you can take five courses in a semester, how many semesters to reach Recursive Algorithms 1?

Six courses. Five per semester. Almost everybody answers two.

But you cannot take Structured Programming 2 in the same semester as Structured Programming 1, because one is the prerequisite for the other. Nor the third, nor the fourth. **The chain is a queue, and no amount of spare capacity shortens a queue.** The answer is set by the depth of the longest path, which is six, not by dividing courses by seats.

This is the critical path, and it is a real algorithm rather than a metaphor. The learner builds the graph as a dictionary of course to prerequisites, writes a function returning the longest chain ending at any given course, and discovers that adding capacity changes nothing while removing one prerequisite changes everything. Then they run it on Object-oriented Programming 1, at depth four rather than five, and see why Recursive Algorithms 1 needing both branches is what makes it expensive.

**One more habit gets taught here, and it came out of researching this page.** A widely repeated summary of Alberta's diploma rules gives a per-grade credit breakdown that adds up to 105 against a stated minimum of 100. The parts do not reconcile with the whole, which means somebody has misread something, possibly us. The correct response is not to pick the number you prefer. It is to publish neither and say why, which is what this page does.

The course codes and prerequisites are Alberta's, cited above. The scheduling assumption, that a prerequisite must complete before the next course starts, is stated because it is an assumption rather than a rule we verified, and the learner is asked to test what happens if it is relaxed.

## What is left to learn if AI writes the code?

Look at what Alberta's chain builds toward. Data structures. Iteration. Object orientation. Recursion. Not one of those is a typing skill. They are ways of holding a problem in your head, and precisely what you need to look at generated code and say whether it is right.

Ask an AI how long it takes to reach Recursive Algorithms 1 at five courses a semester and there is a good chance you get two semesters, confidently, with the division shown. A reasonable-looking answer to a question with a dependency structure hidden inside it. Nothing in the reply flags that a constraint was ignored, because the question did not mention one.

Noticing that a problem has a shape the arithmetic does not capture is the skill.
