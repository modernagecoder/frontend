> AI literacy is five abilities: knowing what an AI system is, understanding at concept level why it produces what it produces, judging its output critically, using it responsibly, and creating with it. It is not the same as using a chatbot daily, which teaches fluency while quietly training a student to trust the exceptions where the machine is confidently wrong. School systems are moving it onto the timetable, with the UAE already mandating AI as a subject and CBSE bringing a compulsory Class 9 exam. ## The five abilities, by age

Know what AI is: at 8, spotting it in games and voice assistants; at 13, distinguishing a model from a database; at 16, explaining training versus programming. Understand why it errs: from "it learned from examples, so it can learn wrong" to naming where hallucination comes from. Judge its output: from asking "how does it know that" to verifying before using, by reflex. Use it responsibly: from knowing some things are private to navigating school and exam AI rules cleanly. Create with it: from a toy classifier with the teacher driving to training and evaluating real models. The columns advance by depth, not tools, because no mainstream platform permits accounts under 13 and the concepts do not need the accounts.

## The moment where learning happens

A model, asked for sources on the salt trade, produces three beautifully formatted references, one of which does not exist. The student without the habit copies all three into the bibliography and meets the teacher later. The student with the habit searches each title first, finds nothing anywhere for one, keeps the two real ones, and tells the class on Thursday. Same model, same wrong answer, thirty seconds of checking. In class we engineer this moment on purpose: being fooled once, safely, inoculates better than a hundred warnings.

## The five habits of an AI-literate student

1. Check anything that matters before using it: output that will be submitted, published, repeated or acted on gets verified against a source that is not the model.
2. Treat convincing as no evidence of true: ask where a thing came from before engaging with what it says.
3. Keep people's details out of prompts: names, addresses, faces, private circumstances, never, because prompts can be stored and reviewed.
4. Disclose the AI where honesty expects it: school rules, competition rules, workplace norms.
5. Know the tasks where the machine stays off: first drafts of your own thinking, practice that builds a tested skill, decisions about people.

## Where schools are heading

The UAE has made AI a mandatory school subject across grade levels. India's CBSE has built AI into its curriculum with a compulsory Class 9 examination from the 2027-28 cycle. Other systems are drafting frameworks. The direction is one way.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is daily ChatGPT use AI literacy?** It is fluency, which is different and slightly dangerous alone: heavy use teaches that the machine is usually right, and the risk lives in the exceptions.

**What age to start?** The questioning habit from 6 to 8, with no accounts; tool-based literacy from 13 with consent.

**Needed without a tech career?** Yes: like reading and arithmetic, its value does not depend on the profession. The coding tracks are optional extensions; the literacy is not really optional any more.

**Under 13 without accounts, how?** Teacher-operated demonstrations and unplugged games, which allow safe deliberate exploration of failures, the exact material that builds judgement.

**Deepfakes?** Covered as a habit rather than detection tricks, which age quickly: surprising content is unverified by default, source before substance, convincing is not evidence of real.
