Evidence

What the research says about maths tuition

The Education Endowment Foundation reviews school research in England. On one-to-one tuition it says it "can be effective, providing approximately five additional months’ progress on average", and adds that "Effects in mathematics appear to be substantially lower (+2 months) than in literacy (+6 months)."

On small groups it reports "an average impact of four months’ additional progress over the course of a year", and for maths that "The studies in mathematics show a slightly smaller positive impact (+ 3 months)".

Read honestly, that is a modest average effect, not a transformation. Averages hide a wide spread: some children gain a great deal and some gain nothing.

These studies are of tuition run in schools, often with trained tutors and set programmes. Private tuition varies far more in quality, which is why choosing the tutor matters more than choosing to have tuition at all.

When it helps

Signs maths tuition will be worth the money

  • There is a specific gap you can name, such as fractions, algebra or a GCSE tier
  • There is a goal with a date: the SATs, an 11 plus test, a GCSE or A level paper, a resit
  • Your child will practise between lessons, even for twenty minutes
  • You can find a tutor who works from your child's course and board
  • School has flagged a problem but cannot give one-to-one time
  • Confidence has collapsed and your child has stopped asking questions in class
  • A good first lesson leaves your child more willing, not less
  • You can afford it for long enough to matter, usually a term or more
When it does not

Signs it may not be worth paying for, yet

No time to practise

One lesson a week with no practice between rarely moves much. Fix the timetable before paying for tuition.

A child who refuses

Tuition forced on an unwilling child often deepens the dislike. A short honest conversation can matter more than a tutor.

Free help is unused

Many schools run catch-up sessions and boards publish many past papers free. Try those first if your child has not.

No clear target

Tuition "to help with maths" with no gap or goal tends to drift. Name the target first.

The wrong tutor

A tutor who does not know your exam board, or who teaches the same lesson to every child, is the most common waste of money.

Promises of a grade

Anyone guaranteeing a grade is selling, not teaching. Walk away from that offer, ours included if we ever made it.

Common questions

Is maths tuition worth it: questions parents ask

Does maths tuition actually work?

On average it helps, modestly. Research on tuition in English schools found about +2 months of progress for one-to-one maths tuition and +3 months for small groups. Individual results vary widely with the tutor, the child and the practice done.

Is maths tuition worth it for GCSE?

Often, when there is a named gap, a target grade and time to practise before the exam. It is less useful as general reassurance with no clear aim.

Is maths tuition worth it for primary children?

It can be for a specific need such as times tables, SATs confidence or an 11 plus test. Many primary children do well with school support and short daily practice at home.

How long before tuition shows results?

Give it about six weeks with a clear target and a check at the end. If nothing has moved by then, change the approach or stop.

Can tuition guarantee a grade?

No, and nobody honest will promise one. Tuition can find and fix gaps and build exam technique; the result still depends on the student.

Is group tuition worth it, or only one-to-one?

In the school research, small-group maths tuition did about as well as one-to-one at lower cost. See our group vs one-to-one guide.

What should we try before paying for tuition?

School catch-up sessions, free past papers from the exam boards, and twenty minutes of daily practice. If those are in place and the gap remains, tuition is easier to justify.
