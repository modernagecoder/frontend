AP CSP score calculator

AP Computer Science Principles is scored in a way that surprises a lot of students. Seventy multiple-choice questions decide 70% of the result, and just six rubric points, earned through a project and a short written section, decide the other 30%. That makes each of those six points very heavy, and it makes a careful estimate worth doing. This page lays out what College Board publishes about the weighting, works through an estimate method that is clearly ours, and is plain about the part nobody outside College Board can calculate: where the lines between a 3, a 4 and a 5 actually sit.

In short

AP CSP has 70 multiple-choice questions worth 70% and a Create performance task with written responses worth 30%, scored as six points of 0 to 1 each. So in our estimate a composite out of 100 is simply multiple-choice questions right plus 5 for every rubric point. College Board does not publish the raw cutoffs for each AP score, so treat any result as an estimate. The published weighting

How the AP CSP exam splits 100 percent

College Board says the end-of-course exam "has consistent question types and weighting every year". That consistency is what makes an estimate possible at all. Everything in this table comes from the AP CSP exam page on AP Central, read on 17 October 2026.

AP Computer Science Principles exam components, from AP Central (read 17 October 2026)
ComponentWhat it isTimeShare
Section I: multiple choice70 questions with 4 answer options: 57 single-select, 5 single-select with a reading passage about a computing innovation, 8 multiple-select where you choose 2 answers120 minutes70%
Section II: Create performance taskProgram code, a video of the program running, and a Personalized Project Reference9 hours in classPart of 30%
Section II: written response2 questions with four prompts, WR1, WR2(a), WR2(b) and WR2(c), about your own program60 minutes on exam dayPart of 30%

Two dates, not one

AP Central lists the deadline for submitting the Create performance task as final in the AP Digital Portfolio as Friday, April 30, 2027, at 11:59 PM ET, and the end-of-course exam as Friday, May 14, 2027, Session 1.

For an estimate, that means part of Section II is already decided two weeks before exam day. What a student submits in April is what gets scored.

Why the multiple-select items matter

Eight of the seventy questions ask for two answers. The pages we read do not say how partial answers are treated, so in our method we count a multiple-select question as right only when both choices are right.

That is the cautious assumption. If a student’s practice score depends heavily on half-right multiple-select answers, the estimate should be read as a little optimistic.

Source: the AP Computer Science Principles exam page on AP Central, read 17 October 2026. We are not connected with College Board.

Section II in detail

The six rubric points behind 30 percent of the score

The 2026 Scoring Guidelines split Section II into six reporting categories, each scored 0 to 1. Three are grouped as one block and three as another.

AP CSP reporting categories, from the 2026 Scoring Guidelines (read 17 October 2026)
CategoryScored fromWhat earns it, in short2025 mean (Set 1)
VideoThe video, or the code if neededThe program running, showing input, functionality and output0.94
Program RequirementsThe Personalized Project Reference, or the codeA student-developed procedure and a call to it, a list and a use of it, selection and iteration0.78
Written Response 1Your answer, with the video or codeIn 2026: a piece of documentation and how another programmer would use it0.84
Written Response 2(a)Your answer and the PPRIn 2026: what makes an iteration statement stop, and why0.43
Written Response 2(b)Your answer and the PPRIn 2026: a call with specific arguments that makes the procedure behave incorrectly0.57
Written Response 2(c)Your answer and the PPRIn 2026: how the list manages complexity, and the code needed without it0.57

Reading the means

College Board’s 2025 scoring statistics, Set 1, give the mean for each category out of 1 possible point. The video point is close to universal at 0.94; Written Response 2(a) is the hardest at 0.43, which means fewer than half of responses earned it.

Those are the published means for Set 1 of the 2025 prompts. Prompts change every year, and College Board says so directly: "The written response prompts on exam day will be different each year."

Small rules that cost whole points

Under the Program Requirements category the guidelines refuse the point when "The list is a one-element list." or when the use of the list is not connected to the program’s functionality, or when selection or iteration is trivial.

The general notes also say written responses "should be evaluated solely on the rationale provided", and that every criterion in a category must be shown to earn that category’s point. There are no half points: a response either meets all of it or scores 0 for that row.

Sources: 2026 AP CSP Scoring Guidelines and 2025 scoring statistics, Set 1, read 17 October 2026.

Our estimate method

A composite you can work out on a napkin

This method is ours. It uses only the published weights, and because of the way those weights fall, the arithmetic is unusually simple.

  1. Sit a full practice Section I. Seventy questions in 120 minutes. Count the questions you got right, counting a two-answer question only when both answers are correct.
  2. Score your own project against the six categories. Give the video and program requirements 1 or 0 from the rubric. Then answer four practice written-response prompts about your own code under a timer and score each 1 or 0.
  3. Convert Section I. Seventy questions carry 70% of the score, so each question is worth exactly 1 composite point: 70 / 70 = 1.
  4. Convert Section II. Six points carry 30%, so each point is worth 5 composite points: 30 / 6 = 5.
  5. Add them. Your composite is the number right plus 5 times the rubric points. A perfect paper is 70 + 30 = 100.

One point, five questions

The striking result: a single rubric point weighs the same as five multiple-choice questions. Student D answers ten more questions right than Student E, and E draws level by earning two more rubric points.

So the written response deserves far more practice time than its 60 minutes suggest. Four written-response points are worth 20 composite points, the same as twenty correct multiple-choice answers.

Ten points decided in class

The video and program requirements points are fixed by what you submit in April. Together they are worth 2 × 5 = 10 composite points before exam day begins, by our arithmetic.

Most students earn them, judging by the 2025 means of 0.94 and 0.78. The students who lose them usually lose them for a reason that could have been fixed in an afternoon: a one-element list, or a video that does not show input.

Run it yourself

The estimate as six lines of Python

AP CSP does not set one programming language, and many classes use Python. This short program does the same sum as the table above. We ran it before publishing.

It is a Create task in miniature

Look at what the program contains: a student-developed procedure named composite with two parameters, a list of rubric points, and a call to the procedure. Those are three of the program requirements the guidelines check.

What it lacks is selection and iteration. Adding a loop that prints the composite for several practice results, and an if statement that flags a result under your target, would cover those too.

A practice written response

Try answering a prompt about it in the 2026 style: name a call with specific arguments that would make composite behave incorrectly. One answer: composite(48, [1, 1, 1]) runs without complaint but silently treats the three missing categories as zeros, so the estimate comes out too low.

Explaining why that happens, in two or three sentences, is exactly the skill Written Response 2(b) asked for in 2026.

The cost of a zero

What happens to the estimate when part of Section II is missing

Two situations come up every year, and College Board’s exam FAQ answers both.

Why the first row is risky

The written responses are answered with the Personalized Project Reference open. The Student Handouts warn that a student who does not submit it by the deadline will not have it on exam day, so there is no code to point to and the four written points become very hard to earn.

The safer reading for an estimate: a missing Create submission leaves a student with Section I only, and 70 as the realistic maximum.

Protecting the 30 percent

Submit early, check that the Personalized Project Reference has no comments in it, and keep a record of any code you did not write and where it came from.

Why we cannot be precise

College Board sets the conversion from raw points to the 1 to 5 scale itself and does not publish it for the current exam. Any site that prints an exact raw cutoff is guessing, or using an old exam that no longer matches.

Our bands sit above where we expect the lines to be, by design. They are there to guide revision. They do not promise a score.

Questions from students

AP CSP scores: common questions

How is the AP Computer Science Principles exam weighted?

Section I, 70 multiple-choice questions in 120 minutes, is 70% of the score. Section II, the Create performance task plus two written-response questions, is 30%.

How many points is the AP CSP Create task worth?

Section II is scored as six categories of 0 to 1 point: Video, Program Requirements, Written Response 1, and Written Responses 2(a), 2(b) and 2(c). Together they make up 30% of the score.

How do I estimate my AP CSP score?

Our method adds the number of multiple-choice questions right to 5 times the rubric points earned, giving a composite out of 100. It is our estimate, not an official College Board conversion.

Is there an official AP CSP score calculator?

No. College Board publishes the weights and the rubric categories but not the raw score needed for each AP score on the current exam.

Can I get an AP CSP score without submitting the Create task?

Yes. College Board says students who take the end-of-course exam receive a score even if they do not submit the Create performance task, although they lose the points the task would carry.

What if I submit the Create task but miss the exam?

College Board says the exam score is cancelled in that case, and there is no refund.

Which AP CSP written response is hardest?

In the 2025 scoring statistics for Set 1, Written Response 2(a) had the lowest mean, 0.43 out of 1 possible point. Prompts change every year.

When is the AP CSP Create task due?

AP Central lists Friday, April 30, 2027, at 11:59 PM ET as the deadline to submit all components as final in the AP Digital Portfolio.

What times are AP CSP classes for US students?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible. Choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays.

How much do AP CSP classes cost?

The first lesson is free. Then families pay by the month, and this page shows the monthly amount in US dollars in its fees section.
