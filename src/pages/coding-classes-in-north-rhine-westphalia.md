## Is Informatik compulsory in NRW schools?

In grades 5 and 6, yes, at every general school type. After that it is an elective. Our summary follows the GI's Informatik-Monitor for 2026/27, a Land-by-Land survey of the rules; we have no tie to its authors or to NRW's Ministerium für Schule und Bildung.

After four years of Grundschule, an NRW pupil goes to one of five school types: Hauptschule, Realschule, Sekundarschule, Gesamtschule or Gymnasium. The KMK names the Sekundarschule as NRW's school combining the tracks. All five teach Informatik as a compulsory subject in grades 5 and 6. From grade 7, each school type has its own elective route.

**Informatik as an elective after grade 6 in NRW**

| School type | Grades | Wahlpflicht Informatik |
|---|---|---|
| Hauptschule | 7 to 10 | Up to eight weekly hours in total |
| Realschule | 7 to 10 | Within a 14-hour elective area, usually three hours a week throughout |
| Gesamtschule and Sekundarschule | 7 to 10 | Within a 12-hour elective area, usually three hours a week throughout |
| Gymnasium (nine-year route) | 9 and 10 | Offered as a required option within six elective hours, on its own or as a combined subject |

Source: Gesellschaft für Informatik, Informatik-Monitor 2026/27, Nordrhein-Westfalen page, read 2 November 2026. "Elective" means the pupil chooses it from what the school offers.

## How is the Informatik Abitur set in NRW, and when is the Abitur in 2027?

Centrally, with a twist: the school receives four tasks and the teacher, not the pupil, chooses which ones are written.

### Courses and exams

In the Einführungsphase, Informatik can be a three-hour Grundkurs. In the Qualifikationsphase it can be a Grundkurs of three hours or a Leistungskurs of five.

Informatik as a Leistungskurs always ends in a written exam; as a Grundkurs it may be written or oral. It cannot stand in for a natural science when pupils fulfil their course obligations.

### Four tasks, chosen by the teacher

The monitor explains that each school receives one task set for the Grundkurs and one for the Leistungskurs, each with four tasks. The teacher picks two of them for the Grundkurs and three for the Leistungskurs; pupils do not choose.

No computer is used. The permitted aids are a German spelling dictionary and a scientific calculator or a CAS or modular maths system.

The four-task system shapes good preparation. A pupil cannot avoid a topic by choosing around it, because the teacher does the choosing. So we cover the whole Oberstufe content evenly: object-oriented modelling, lists, trees and graphs, databases and SQL, automata and grammars, all written by hand. School grades and the central exam decide the Abitur; our role is the understanding behind them, and no German qualification is issued by us.

## How many NRW Oberstufen offer Informatik? A data project

The Schulministerium publishes, as open data, the courses each Gymnasium and Gesamtschule offers in its Oberstufe. Our pupils use it twice: once to answer a real question about their Land, and once to learn the habit that saves hours of confusion, opening a file and reading its first lines before writing any code.

### The data

The dataset is Kursangebot in der gymnasialen Oberstufe (Gymnasien/Gesamtschulen) in NRW, released with a Datenlizenz Deutschland Namensnennung 2.0 licence and fetched by us on 3 November 2026. It comes in two files: kurse.csv, one row per school and subject, and key_fach.csv, which explains the subject codes. The code for Informatik is IF.

Each row marks with a 1 or 0 whether the school offers a Grundkurs in the Einführungsphase and Grundkurs or Leistungskurs in Q1 and Q2. The ministry notes that it is based on the official school data survey, and that schools may offer more through cooperation with other schools.

### The question

Out of the Oberstufen in the file, how many offer Informatik, and how many an Informatik Leistungskurs? It looks like five lines of Python.

Version 1 is those five lines. It stops at once with an error about a column that is plainly in the file. The reason is on line 1.

**Version 1: read and filter** (Python)
```python
import csv

with open("kurse.csv", encoding="utf-8") as src:
    rows = list(csv.DictReader(src, delimiter=";"))

print("Columns Python found:", list(rows[0].keys()))
informatik = [r for r in rows if r["Fachschluessel"] == "IF"]
```
It prints Columns Python found: ['sep=', '', None] and then stops with KeyError: 'Fachschluessel'.

Python took the first line of the file as the header, and the first line is not the header. It is the text sep=;, an instruction some spreadsheet programs read to learn that the file uses semicolons. The real header is on line 2. Version 2 reads line 1 on its own, prints it so we can see it, and only then hands the rest of the file to the CSV reader.

**Version 2: read line 1 first** (Python)
```python
import csv

with open("kurse.csv", encoding="utf-8") as src:
    print("Line 1 of the file:", src.readline().strip())
    rows = list(csv.DictReader(src, delimiter=";"))

schools = {r["Schulnummer"] for r in rows}
informatik = [r for r in rows if r["Fachschluessel"] == "IF"]
print("Schools with an Oberstufe in the file:", len(schools))
print("Schools offering Informatik:", len({r["Schulnummer"] for r in informatik}))
print("...with a Leistungskurs in Q1:", sum(r["LKQ1"] == "1" for r in informatik))
```
It prints Line 1 of the file: sep=;, Schools with an Oberstufe in the file: 956, Schools offering Informatik: 654 and ...with a Leistungskurs in Q1: 105.

**Version 3 (continues Version 2): Informatik next to maths and physics** (Python)
```python
for key in ["M", "PH", "IF"]:
    offers = [r for r in rows if r["Fachschluessel"] == key]
    lk = sum(r["LKQ1"] == "1" for r in offers)
    print(key, len(offers), "schools,", lk, "with LK in Q1,", round(100 * lk / len(schools)), "% of all")
```
It prints M 956 schools, 892 with LK in Q1, 93 % of all, PH 834 schools, 241 with LK in Q1, 25 % of all and IF 654 schools, 105 with LK in Q1, 11 % of all.

**Oberstufe course offers in the NRW file, downloaded 3 November 2026 (our counts)**

| Subject (code) | Schools offering it | Schools with a Leistungskurs in Q1 |
|---|---|---|
| Mathematics (M) | 956 | 892 |
| Physics (PH) | 834 | 241 |
| Informatik (IF) | 654 | 105 |

Data: Ministerium für Schule und Bildung NRW, Kursangebot in der gymnasialen Oberstufe (Gymnasien/Gesamtschulen) in NRW, Datenlizenz Deutschland Namensnennung 2.0, via Open.NRW, downloaded 3 November 2026. Counts are ours. Courses offered through cooperation between schools may be missing from the file.

So in this file about two Oberstufen in three offer some Informatik course, and about one in nine lists an Informatik Leistungskurs in Q1. For a pupil set on a Leistungskurs, the school's course list matters as much as the pupil's own interest, which is worth knowing before choosing an Oberstufe.

1. **Grades 5 and 6, no computer.** A worksheet has a title line, then a line of column names, then the answers. A classmate starts reading the answers from line 1. What goes wrong?
2. **Grades 7 and 8, Python.** Run Version 1 and read the error. Before changing anything, open kurse.csv in a text editor and print its first three lines with Python.
3. **Grades 9 and 10.** Change Version 3 to compare Informatik with chemistry (CH) and biology (BI). Which subject has the largest gap between "offered" and "Leistungskurs"?
4. **Oberstufe.** key_fach.csv starts with the same line. Write one function that opens either file, skips a sep= line only if it is there, and returns the rows; then join the subject names onto kurse.csv.

## What time are classes in NRW?

An NRW afternoon is an Indian evening; a Saturday late morning is an Indian afternoon. The rows show the difference only; you choose your own slot.

**NRW time and India time**

| NRW | India in winter (to 28 March 2027) | India in summer (from 28 March 2027) |
|---|---|---|
| 14:45 | 19:15 | 18:15 |
| 16:15 | 20:45 | 19:45 |
| Saturday 11:30 | 16:00 | 15:00 |

German time follows the Einheitenzeitgesetz: MEZ at UTC+1 in winter, MESZ at UTC+2 in summer. The switch back happened on 25 October 2026; the next switch forward is on 28 March 2027. India stays at UTC+5:30, so the gap is 4 h 30 in winter and 3 h 30 in summer.

## Coding classes in North Rhine-Westphalia: common questions

### Are the classes in German?

No, never. Our teaching language is English, in every live lesson. The mentor slows down whenever needed, learns how your child speaks, and is happy to hear a German word like Leistungskurs in the middle of an English sentence.

### Is Informatik compulsory in NRW?

In grades 5 and 6. The Gesellschaft für Informatik's Informatik-Monitor 2026/27 states that Informatik is a compulsory subject in grades 5 and 6 at all general school types in NRW. After that it is an elective.

### Can my child keep Informatik after grade 6?

Usually, through the Wahlpflicht area. Hauptschulen, Realschulen, Gesamtschulen and Sekundarschulen can offer it from grade 7; nine-year Gymnasien offer it within the elective hours of grades 9 and 10. It depends on what the school offers and what your child chooses.

### How is the NRW Informatik Abitur set?

Centrally. According to the Informatik-Monitor, each school receives a set of four tasks for the Grundkurs and four for the Leistungskurs; the teacher chooses two or three of them, and pupils do not choose. No computer is used.

### When are the written Abitur exams in NRW in 2027?

The Schulministerium lists the written main dates from 13 April to 5 May 2027, each exam starting at 9:00, with the maths paper on Wednesday 5 May 2027. Resits run from 10 to 24 May 2027.

### Does every Oberstufe offer an Informatik Leistungskurs?

No. In the ministry's Oberstufe course file that we downloaded on 3 November 2026, 654 of 956 schools offer Informatik and 105 list a Leistungskurs in Q1, by our count. Cooperation between schools can add courses not in the file.

### What time are classes?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Because India runs four and a half hours ahead of NRW in winter, a 16:15 lesson in Düsseldorf is 20:45 for the mentor.

### Will my child get a certificate from you that counts at school?

No. Our classes support school work but sit outside the school system: German grades and qualifications come only from the school and from NRW's central exams.

### Where are the mentors based?

They work from India and meet pupils live on video; the mentor your child starts with is the one they keep.

### How is payment handled?

By card, once a month. The euro figures you see use the European Central Bank reference rate, while the charge itself is in US dollars and converted by your bank. Nothing is charged for the free first class.
