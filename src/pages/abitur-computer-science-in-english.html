Informatik Abitur, explained in English

Computer science is a full Abitur subject in Germany, and in several Länder it can be one of the written exams. But it is also the Abitur subject whose rules differ most from Land to Land: in one, the paper is written by a central commission and solved with a pen; in another, a teacher writes it and pupils may use a computer. This page sets out, in English, what each Land does, what that means for preparation, and works through one database task of the kind the official aids point to.

The subject

Can you take computer science in the German Abitur?

Yes, in every Land, but how far you can take it depends on the Land and on what your school offers.

How the Abitur is built

The KMK says the Abitur exam covers four or five subjects, at least two of them at raised level (the Leistungskurs, called Leistungsfach or profilgebendes Fach in some Länder), and two of them from German, a foreign language and maths. Informatik can fill one of the other places.

The written Abitur papers are set centrally in almost all Länder, according to the KMK. For Informatik that is only partly true, as the table below shows: several Länder still let teachers write the Informatik paper.

How many pupils choose it

The Gesellschaft für Informatik puts the share of Oberstufe pupils who take Informatik in the Qualifikationsphase at about 15 percent nationally in its Informatik-Monitor 2026/27. On its Land pages the 2024/25 figures run from 7 percent in Baden-Württemberg to 43 percent in Sachsen.

Choosing it is one step; finding a course is another. In Sachsen a Leistungskurs exists only at selected Gymnasien, and in Sachsen-Anhalt the raised level is offered at two Gymnasien with a mathematics and science focus.

In most Länder the Grundkurs runs two or three hours a week and the Leistungskurs five. Nordrhein-Westfalen, for example, offers a three-hour Grundkurs and a five-hour Leistungskurs in the Qualifikationsphase; Hamburg runs the raised level as a profile subject of at least four hours, Schleswig-Holstein as one of four.

Nordrhein-Westfalen in detail

NRW sends each school one set of four tasks for the Grundkurs and one for the Leistungskurs. The teacher, not the pupil, picks two of the four for the Grundkurs and three for the Leistungskurs. The permitted aids are a German spelling dictionary and a scientific calculator or CAS.

The written Informatik Abitur 2027 in NRW, Grundkurs and Leistungskurs, is on Friday 16 April 2027, inside a main exam period that runs from 13 April to 5 May 2027; NRW exams start at 9:00.

Three Länder that use a computer

In Sachsen the central paper has a compulsory Part B with practical work at an approved computer, and the aids include documentation for SQL, Python 3 and Java.

In Thüringen every optional task contains compulsory programming, pupils solve two of three, and tools used in class, such as a diagram editor or an automaton simulator, may be used if installed. Schleswig-Holstein caps computer-dependent work at a quarter of the marks.

Programming on paper

Why do most Länder test programming without a computer?

In Bayern, the Informatik-Monitor says, large parts of the written Informatik Abitur are programming tasks on paper with no work at a computer. Baden-Württemberg lets pupils answer in the language their course used but forbids a programming environment. Niedersachsen goes furthest: no calculator, no formula book, only a centrally issued information sheet.

No compiler to help

On paper, a missing semicolon or a wrong index is yours to find. Pupils who only ever test by running code meet this late, often in the mock.

Reading counts as much as writing

Many tasks give code and ask what it does, where it fails, or how to complete it. That is a reading skill, and it can be practised.

The sheet tells you the topics

Niedersachsen's information sheet summarises operations on abstract data types and the notation of database queries. Those two areas are worth practising by hand everywhere.

Is the Abitur written in English? Our sources do not say any Land offers the Informatik Abitur in English, and we do not claim it. The tasks will be in German, while the code itself uses English keywords. Our classes are in English, and every German exam term is taught next to its English meaning.

A worked exercise

A database task: predict the rows before you run the query

Our own exercise, not a real Abitur task. Four invented pupils, three courses and a table of who takes what. The first habit we teach for any SQL question on paper is to say how many rows the answer should have before writing a single line.

Trap one: the forgotten join

There are five enrolments, so a list of who takes which course must have five rows. The first query has 20. It links each enrolment to its course but never to its pupil, so every one of the four pupils is paired with every one of the five enrolments: 4 times 5.

A pupil who predicted five rows sees at once that 20 is wrong. A pupil who did not predict anything copies 20 rows into the answer.

Trap two: counting nothing as one

Dev takes no course. A LEFT JOIN keeps Dev in the result with empty course fields, so COUNT(*), which counts rows, says 1. COUNT(b.kurs_id) counts only fields that are filled, and says 0, which is the truth.

On paper there is no output to check against. The prediction is the only test you have.

  1. Count first. How many rows should the answer have? Write the number in the margin.
  2. One condition per link. Three tables need two join conditions. Count the tables, count the conditions.
  3. Ask about the empty case. Is there a pupil, a course or a customer with nothing? Decide what the query should say for them.
  4. Try it. Write the query that lists every course with the number of pupils in it, including courses nobody takes. How many rows will it return?

Beyond the exam

Can a competition count towards the Informatik Abitur?

Sometimes. Several Länder let a pupil bring in a besondere Lernleistung, a substantial piece of work outside the timetable, as part of the Abitur.

The Bundeswettbewerb Informatik

The organiser BWINF says the second round of the Bundeswettbewerb Informatik can count as a substitute for the besondere Lernleistung in the Abitur, and that whether this is possible depends on the Land and the school. The monitor names a besondere Lernleistung as an option in Informatik in Berlin, Brandenburg, Hamburg, Hessen, Mecklenburg-Vorpommern, Niedersachsen, Rheinland-Pfalz, Sachsen and Schleswig-Holstein.

Ask your school before you plan on it.

Oral and presentation exams

A Grundkurs pupil often meets Informatik as an oral or presentation exam rather than a written one. In Bremen, for example, the Grundkurs can be examined orally but not in writing; in Hessen a Grundkurs can be the fifth subject as a presentation with a colloquium.

An oral exam rewards the same skill as paper: explaining code in words, step by step.

Informatik Abitur in English: common questions

Can I take Informatik as an Abitur subject in Germany?

Yes. Every Land offers Informatik in the Oberstufe, as a Grundkurs and in most Länder as a Leistungskurs, but your school must run the course. About 15 percent of Oberstufe pupils take it nationally, according to the Informatik-Monitor 2026/27.

Is a computer allowed in the written Informatik Abitur?

It depends on the Land. Bayern, Baden-Württemberg, Hessen, Niedersachsen and Nordrhein-Westfalen, among others, use no computer. Berlin and Brandenburg allow one where a task needs it, Sachsen has a practical Part B at a computer, and Schleswig-Holstein caps computer work at a quarter of the marks.

When is the Informatik Abitur 2027 in NRW?

The written Informatik exam for Grundkurs and Leistungskurs in Nordrhein-Westfalen is on Friday 16 April 2027, according to the ministry's 2027 timetable. Exams start at 9:00.

Which programming language is used, Java or Python?

It depends on the school and the Land. Baden-Württemberg lets pupils answer in the language their course used, and Sachsen's exam aids include documentation for Python 3 and Java. We teach in whichever language your school uses.

Can I write the Informatik Abitur in English?

None of our sources says any Land offers it in English, so plan for German tasks. Program code uses English keywords anyway, and in class we pair every German exam term with its English meaning.

Does the Bundeswettbewerb Informatik count for the Abitur?

Sometimes. BWINF says its second round can replace the besondere Lernleistung in the Abitur, depending on the Land and the school. Ask your school early.

Are the classes in German?

No. All our classes are live and in English only, with mentors in India who match your pace and accent. That suits Informatik well, because the code is English; the German exam words are taught alongside.

When are the classes?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Our mentors' clocks in India run 4 hours 30 minutes ahead of German time in winter and 3 hours 30 minutes ahead from 28 March 2027.

Do you award a grade or certificate for the Abitur?

No. The Abitur and its marks come only from your school and Land. We teach alongside school and have no connection with any ministry, the KMK or BWINF.

How much are the classes?

You pay month by month. This page shows the amount in euro using the ECB reference rate; the charge itself is made in US dollars and converted by your card provider. Your first class costs nothing.
