## What is the Känguru der Mathematik, and when is it in 2027?

A multiple-choice maths contest written on one day, under supervision, at schools across Germany and in many other countries. In 2027 the date moves into April.

### The 2027 date

The contest is usually held on the third Thursday in March. The organisers explain that in 2027 that date falls directly before the Easter holidays in almost all Länder, so the Känguru takes place as an exception on 8 April 2027.

Schools register their numbers of participants online from 1 December 2026, and registration closes on 31 January 2027.

### How big it is

More than 920,000 pupils from more than 13,000 schools took part in Germany on 19 March 2026, according to the organisers, who note that the German figure has grown from 184 in 1995. Worldwide the organisers speak of about 6 million participants in over 100 countries.

The contest is prepared and marked centrally by the Mathematikwettbewerb Känguru e.V., based at the Humboldt-Universität zu Berlin, and run locally by the schools.

## How does a child in Germany enter the Känguru?

Only through school. The organisers rule out individual registration, and a school must be able to enter at least 15 pupils.

1. **A teacher takes it on.** Each school needs a responsible teacher who informs pupils and parents, registers, collects the per-pupil entry fee and runs the day.
2. **Consent.** Taking part is voluntary; the school needs the pupil's consent, or the parents', before entering them.
3. **Registration.** The teacher registers the number of pupils per Klassenstufe online between 1 December 2026 and 31 January 2027.
4. **Contest day.** 8 April 2027, 75 minutes, at school, under supervision, without calculators or other aids. Pupils keep the question sheet so it can be discussed afterwards.
5. **Answers and results.** Schools enter the answer letters online until Friday 16 April 2027 at 18:00. The organisers publish the correct letters the same day at 19:00, and the result packages reach schools from about three weeks after the contest.

Source: Mathematikwettbewerb Känguru e.V., Wettbewerb and Ablauf pages on mathe-kaenguru.de, read on 3 November 2026. The steps are our summary of the organisers' list.

## How are Känguru points counted, and should a child guess?

The organisers publish the rule in full. Turned into numbers it gives a clear answer: a blind guess is worth exactly nothing on average, and a guess after ruling out even one wrong option is worth something.

### The rule

Everyone starts with as many points as there are questions: 24 in Klasse 3 to 6, 30 from Klasse 7. Questions get harder through the paper. In the first third a right answer earns 3 points, in the second 4 and in the last 5. A question left blank scores 0, and a wrong answer costs a quarter of its value: 0.75, 1 or 1.25 points.

That gives a range from 0, if every answer is wrong, to 120 or 150 if every answer is right.

### What a guess is worth (our calculation)

Each question has five options, A to E. A blind guess is right one time in five. On a 4-point question it gains 4 points one time in five and loses 1 point four times in five: on average exactly zero. The rule was built so that blind guessing neither helps nor hurts.

Rule out one option and the guess is right one time in four. Now the average gain is positive. The program below works out every case exactly.

**The scoring rule, turned into expected gains** (Python)
```python
from fractions import Fraction

print("Most points, Klasse 3 to 6:", 24 + 8 * 3 + 8 * 4 + 8 * 5)
print("Most points, Klasse 7 to 13:", 30 + 10 * 3 + 10 * 4 + 10 * 5)

for value in [3, 4, 5]:
    gains = []
    for ruled_out in range(4):
        left = 5 - ruled_out
        p_right = Fraction(1, left)
        gain = p_right * value - (1 - p_right) * Fraction(value, 4)
        gains.append(str(gain))
    print(value, "point task, expected gain with 0 to 3 ruled out:", ", ".join(gains))
```
It prints Most points, Klasse 3 to 6: 120 and Most points, Klasse 7 to 13: 150, then 3 point task, expected gain with 0 to 3 ruled out: 0, 3/16, 1/2, 9/8, 4 point task, expected gain with 0 to 3 ruled out: 0, 1/4, 2/3, 3/2 and 5 point task, expected gain with 0 to 3 ruled out: 0, 5/16, 5/6, 15/8.

**Average points gained by answering instead of leaving a question blank (our calculation from the organisers' rule)**

| Question value | Nothing ruled out | One ruled out | Two ruled out | Three ruled out |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 points | 0 | 3/16 | 1/2 | 9/8 |
| 4 points | 0 | 1/4 | 2/3 | 3/2 |
| 5 points | 0 | 5/16 | 5/6 | 15/8 |

Rule: Mathematikwettbewerb Känguru e.V., Auswertung section of mathe-kaenguru.de/wettbewerb/, read on 3 November 2026; five answer letters A to E as accepted by the organisers' online entry. The expected gains are our own exact arithmetic with Python fractions.

The first two lines of output are a check on our reading of the rule: 24 starting points plus eight questions each at 3, 4 and 5 gives 120, and 30 plus ten of each gives 150, exactly the maxima the organisers state. The table then turns into one sentence a child can remember: never guess blind, always guess after crossing one option out. With two options gone, a 5-point question is worth five sixths of a point on average, more than many children expect. That is why our Känguru practice is really practice in elimination: spotting, fast, which answers cannot be right.

## What do Känguru-style questions train? Three practice puzzles of our own

These three multiple-choice puzzles are our own work, made for this page, one for each part of a paper. None is a Känguru question. Each is solved by ruling options out before calculating.

### Puzzle 1, a 3-point style question (ours)

The Känguru 2027 is on Thursday 8 April 2027. On which weekday is 8 May 2027? (A) Friday (B) Saturday (C) Sunday (D) Monday (E) Thursday

**Solution.** April has 30 days, so 8 May is 30 days after 8 April. Thirty days is four weeks and two days. Thursday plus two days is Saturday: (B). Option (E) can be ruled out at once, because 30 is not a multiple of 7.

### Puzzle 2, a 4-point style question (ours)

How many of the numbers 1 to 100 are divisible by 4 or by 6? (A) 29 (B) 33 (C) 35 (D) 37 (E) 41

**Solution.** 25 numbers are divisible by 4 and 16 by 6. Adding gives 41, but numbers divisible by both, which are the multiples of 12, are counted twice: there are 8. So 25 + 16 - 8 = 33: (B). Option (E) is the trap for anyone who forgets the overlap.

### Puzzle 3, a 5-point style question (ours)

Five different positive whole numbers have an average of 10. What is the largest possible value of the biggest one? (A) 36 (B) 38 (C) 40 (D) 42 (E) 46

**Solution.** Their total is 50. To make the biggest as large as possible, make the other four as small as possible: 1, 2, 3 and 4, which add to 10. The biggest is then 50 - 10 = 40: (C). Options (D) and (E) are impossible, since even the smallest four other numbers leave only 40.

### How we use puzzles like these

In class a child first says which options can be crossed out, and why, before working anything out. Only then do they calculate. On the real paper that habit turns a hard question from a blank into a well-informed guess.

Older children then check their answers with a few lines of code, below. On contest day there is no computer, so the checking is a training tool only.

**Checking the three answers by brute force** (Python)
```python
from datetime import date
from itertools import combinations

print("Puzzle 1:", date(2027, 4, 8).strftime("%A"), "->", date(2027, 5, 8).strftime("%A"))
print("Puzzle 2:", sum(1 for n in range(1, 101) if n % 4 == 0 or n % 6 == 0))
top = max(max(c) for c in combinations(range(1, 51), 5) if sum(c) == 50)
print("Puzzle 3:", top)
```
It prints Puzzle 1: Thursday -> Saturday, Puzzle 2: 33 and Puzzle 3: 40.

1. **Puzzle 1, varied.** Which weekday is 8 April 2028? Remember that 2028 is a leap year.
2. **Puzzle 2, varied.** How many of the numbers 1 to 100 are divisible by 4 or 6 but not by both?
3. **Puzzle 3, varied.** Now the five numbers may be equal. What is the largest possible biggest number?
4. **Timing.** Solve all three originals in under eight minutes, the time a Klasse 7 pupil has for three questions of the real 30.

## What does every Känguru participant receive?

Something, whatever the score. The organisers say no school or Land is compared with another; the aim is mathematical life at school.

|---|---|

| Longest run at each school | A Känguru T-shirt for the longest run of consecutive correct answers, the Känguru-Sprung |
| About the top 5 percent nationally | First, second and third prizes, set for each Klassenstufe in a ratio of about 1 to 2 to 3 |

Source: Mathematikwettbewerb Känguru e.V., Preise and Auswertung sections of mathe-kaenguru.de/wettbewerb/, read on 3 November 2026. From Klasse 7 the booklet usually comes as a digital file.

The Känguru-Sprung is a lovely detail for a practice plan. It rewards a long unbroken run of correct answers, which is easier to build at the start of the paper, where the questions are worth 3 points and are meant to be within reach of school knowledge. Our timed rounds teach children to bank those first questions calmly before moving on.
