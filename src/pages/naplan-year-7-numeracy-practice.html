NAPLAN Year 7 numeracy practice

Year 7 NAPLAN arrives a few weeks into secondary school, and it is the first numeracy test that splits in two. Students begin with a short section where no calculator is allowed, then move to the rest of the test with an online calculator, and they cannot go back. Proportion and percentages run through the Year 7 descriptions at every level from Developing up, so this page teaches one tool that makes them visible: the double number line. In short

The Year 7 NAPLAN numeracy test lasts 65 minutes. It opens with a short non-calculator section where students show arithmetic; after that an online calculator is available for the rest of the test. It runs inside the national window, 10 to 22 March in 2027. At the Strong level ACARA lists, among other things, calculating fractions and percentages of a quantity and solving problems involving a simple proportion.

Year 7 is the first year that NAPLAN tests secondary-school content for many students, but ACARA builds most questions on skills from earlier years. A small number reach into the current and following year, so a Year 7 student may meet an idea their class covers later in the year.

In states where Year 7 is the first year of high school, students sit NAPLAN in a new building, with new teachers, often in their first term. Familiarity with the format matters more in Year 7 than in any other year.

The test is tailored. Every student begins on the same first testlet, and their answers decide whether the next set is more or less complex. A slip in the non-calculator section can steer a capable student onto an easier path.

That is why checking in the first section is worth more than speed. Ten seconds spent confirming 15 per cent of 60 is 9 protects every question that follows.

The calculator rule

Why the first section changes how to prepare

ACARA's wording: "There is a short non-calculator section, in which students are required to demonstrate arithmetical calculation skills. For the remainder of the test, an online calculator is available."

What the rule means

The non-calculator section comes first and is short. Once a student moves past it, the online calculator appears for the remaining questions, and the first section is closed to them.

So mental and written arithmetic is tested directly, at the very start, when nerves are highest. Percentages of round numbers, fraction of a quantity, and order of operations are typical of what a student should be able to do without a device.

How we prepare for it

Build friendly benchmarks: 10 per cent, 5 per cent, a quarter, a half. Almost every non-calculator percentage question is a combination of these.

Practise estimating before using a calculator in the second section. An online calculator is only as reliable as the keys pressed, and an estimate catches a misplaced decimal point instantly.

Practice we wrote

The double number line

Draw two parallel lines. Mark one quantity on the top line and the matching quantity on the bottom line, starting from zero on both. Anything you do to one line (halve it, add a tenth) you do to the other.

Percentages

Top line 0 to 240 students, bottom line 0 to 100 per cent. Mark 10 per cent at 24, so 30 per cent is 72 and 5 per cent is 12.

Rates

Top line 0 to 150 km, bottom line 0 to 120 minutes. Halve both: 75 km in 60 minutes. Now any time can be found.

Scales

Top line centimetres on a map, bottom line kilometres on the ground. 2 cm matches 5 km, so 1 cm matches 2.5 km.

The first two questions are written for the non-calculator section on purpose. Both are answered entirely from 10 per cent and 5 per cent benchmarks, which a double number line makes automatic. The bus question looks harder, but on the double line it is three halvings and one subtraction; a calculator only confirms it.

A useful check for every proportion answer: the two lines must stay in the same order. If 50 minutes produced more than 75 km, the answer would have crossed the 60-minute mark, which cannot be right.

If your child is anxious, practise only the non-calculator benchmarks. Quick wins there make the rest of the test feel manageable.

Questions about Year 7 NAPLAN numeracy

Year 7 families often ask

How long is the Year 7 NAPLAN numeracy test?

65 minutes, sat after the conventions of language test within the national window.

Can Year 7 students use a calculator in NAPLAN?

Only after the first part. The test opens with a short non-calculator section; for the remainder an online calculator is available, and students cannot return to the first section.

When is Year 7 NAPLAN in 2027?

Within the national window of 10 to 22 March 2027, on a day set by the school.

What is a double number line?

Two parallel number lines that show two quantities growing together, such as students and per cent, or kilometres and minutes. Whatever is done to one line is done to the other, which makes proportion visible.

What does Strong look like in Year 7?

ACARA lists skills including calculating fractions and percentages of a quantity, solving problems involving a simple proportion, and locating points on the Cartesian plane.

What should my child practise for the non-calculator section?

Benchmarks such as 10 per cent, 5 per cent, a quarter and a half, fraction of a quantity, and order of operations. These cover most arithmetic a student must show without a device.

Is Year 7 NAPLAN harder than Year 5?

It covers more content and adds the calculator split, but ACARA builds most questions on skills from earlier years, with a few that reach ahead.

Do you cover reading and writing?

No. Our classes are mathematics only.

Can lessons guarantee a level?

No. We never promise a level or score; we teach the maths that the test draws on.

What time would classes be in Sydney?

We set one weekly time after the free trial. During daylight saving Sydney is five and a half hours ahead of India time, so 5 pm in Sydney is 11.30 am India time.
