Ans. The short version

Good class 10 AI project ideas for CBSE 417 are small, linked to one Sustainable Development Goal and built in one AI domain: Statistical Data, Computer Vision or NLP. Easy options include a leaf disease checker, a fruit ripeness sorter or a sentiment analysis project on clinic reviews; harder ones include coral bleaching detection or a complaint classifier. Each should follow the AI project cycle and report real evaluation numbers. The project carries 10 marks and its viva 5. Modern Age Coders guides projects from idea to viva in a live Monday and Wednesday 9:00 PM IST batch, or one to one.

Q1. What makes a good project?

What makes a good AI project for class 10 SDG work

A good Class 10 AI project answers one clear question about one real problem, uses data you can get, and ends with numbers you can explain. CBSE asks that it relate to the Sustainable Development Goals, and its own sample, penguin species prediction, shows how small the scale can be.

Grade on this pageDataToolTypical build time
EasyA ready open dataset, or a few dozen photos you takeOrange, no codeThree to four weekends
MediumAn open dataset that needs cleaning, or a survey you runOrange, or short PythonFive to six weekends
StretchData you collect and label yourselfPython, comparing two modelsEight weekends or more

Statistical Data

Rows and columns of numbers or categories: readings, surveys, records. The model predicts a label or a value from the columns.

Computer Vision

Images sorted into labelled folders. The model learns to tell the classes apart from pixel patterns.

Natural Language Processing

Short texts such as reviews, headlines or messages. The model scores or sorts them by their words.

Build times are from our batch's experience, not a CBSE rule. Easy does not mean fewer marks: a finished, honest easy project beats an abandoned ambitious one.

Q2. Fifteen ideas, easy to stretch

Easy AI project class 10 ideas, then medium and stretch

Each idea lists its goal, domain and a one-line run through the cycle: scope, data, explore, model, evaluate. All fifteen are written for this page; adapt them to your town and your data.

Easy: build in Orange with data you can get this week

  1. Leaf health checker. SDG 2, Zero Hunger · Computer Vision.
    Scope: small farmers notice leaf disease late. Data: your own photos of healthy and spotted leaves from one crop. Explore: view samples in Image Viewer. Model: Image Embedding with Logistic Regression. Evaluate: recall on diseased leaves, since a missed one spreads.
  2. Fruit ripeness sorter. SDG 12, Responsible Consumption · Computer Vision.
    Scope: shops throw away fruit that ripened unnoticed. Data: photos of bananas at three stages, taken over a week. Explore: are lighting and background consistent? Model: an image classifier in Orange. Evaluate: confusion matrix; which stages get mixed up.
  3. Sentiment analysis of clinic reviews. SDG 3, Good Health · NLP.
    Scope: what patients praise or complain about at local clinics. Data: public reviews, with names removed. Explore: Word Cloud after preprocessing. Model: Orange's Sentiment Analysis widget. Evaluate: label 50 reviews yourself and count agreement; 41 of 50 would be 0.82.
  4. Air quality band predictor. SDG 11, Sustainable Cities · Statistical Data.
    Scope: residents cannot read raw pollutant numbers. Data: an open city air-quality dataset. Explore: distributions of PM2.5 by band. Model: a classifier predicting the band from readings. Evaluate: accuracy, then which neighbouring bands it confuses.
  5. Sleep and screen-time survey. SDG 3, Good Health · Statistical Data.
    Scope: do students who use screens late feel less rested? Data: an anonymous survey of classmates, with consent. Explore: a scatter plot of screen time against sleep hours. Model: a classifier for "rested" or "not rested". Evaluate: accuracy, and an honest note on the small sample.

Medium: more cleaning, a harder question or some Python

  1. Coral bleaching detection. SDG 14, Life Below Water · Computer Vision.
    Scope: reef surveys need to flag bleached coral quickly. Data: open image sets of healthy and bleached coral on data-sharing sites. Explore: check the two classes are balanced. Model: Image Embedding with a classifier. Evaluate: recall on bleached coral (worked below).
  2. Misleading headline flagger. SDG 16, Peace and Justice · NLP.
    Scope: false headlines spread faster than corrections. Data: an open labelled headline dataset. Explore: frequent words in each class. Model: Bag of Words with a classifier. Evaluate: precision, because calling a true story false does harm.
  3. Hand sign alphabet reader. SDG 10, Reduced Inequalities · Computer Vision.
    Scope: few people can read sign language. Data: your own photos of five letter signs from several hands. Explore: are signs from different hands alike? Model: an image classifier. Evaluate: per-letter accuracy, and which letters look alike.
  4. Forest fire risk from weather. SDG 15, Life on Land · Statistical Data.
    Scope: forest staff need warning of high-risk days. Data: an open dataset of weather readings with fire or no-fire labels. Explore: temperature and humidity on fire days. Model: a decision tree. Evaluate: recall on fire days.
  5. Household electricity use. SDG 7, Clean Energy · Statistical Data.
    Scope: families do not know which habits raise their bills. Data: a survey of appliances and monthly units from twenty or more homes. Explore: units against AC hours. Model: classify "high" or "normal" use. Evaluate: accuracy, with the limits of a small sample.

Stretch: your own labelled data, two models compared

  1. Civic complaint sorter. SDG 11, Sustainable Cities · NLP.
    Scope: complaints reach the wrong department. Data: 150 or more short complaints you write or collect and label as water, roads or power. Explore: top words per label. Model: Bag of Words, two classifiers compared. Evaluate: confusion matrix across three classes.
  2. Pothole spotter. SDG 9, Infrastructure · Computer Vision.
    Scope: potholes are reported late. Data: your own road photos, labelled pothole or clear, taken safely from footpaths. Explore: variety of light and surface. Model: two classifiers compared. Evaluate: recall on potholes, then false alarms.
  3. Camera-trap filter. SDG 15, Life on Land · Computer Vision.
    Scope: volunteers sift thousands of empty trap frames. Data: open camera-trap images labelled animal or empty. Explore: night versus day frames. Model: image classifier in Python or Orange. Evaluate: recall on animal frames, so none are discarded.
  4. Solar output estimator. SDG 7, Clean Energy · Statistical Data.
    Scope: rooftop owners want to know what a cloudy week costs them in power. Data: open solar generation and weather records. Explore: output against cloud cover. Model: regression in Python or Orange. Evaluate: average error in units, compared with a simple guess.
  5. Mixed-language climate opinions. SDG 13, Climate Action · NLP.
    Scope: many posts mix Hindi and English, which English sentiment tools misread. Data: 200 or more posts you label positive, negative or neutral. Explore: words the English tool gets wrong. Model: your own Bag of Words classifier against the ready widget. Evaluate: which wins, and on what.

Questions about Class 10 AI projects

Class 10 AI project ideas: questions families ask us

What are some easy AI project ideas for class 10?

A leaf health checker from photos, a fruit ripeness sorter, a sentiment analysis of clinic reviews, an air quality band predictor or a survey on sleep and screen time. Each can be built in Orange without code, uses data you can get within a week and links to one Sustainable Development Goal.

Does the class 10 AI project have to be linked to an SDG?

CBSE asks that the Class 10 AI project relate to the Sustainable Development Goals. Name the goal in your problem scoping, explain who benefits, and keep the link specific: for example SDG 14, Life Below Water, for a coral bleaching project, rather than a general claim about helping the planet.

How do I do a sentiment analysis project in class 10?

Collect short texts such as public reviews, remove names, and load them into Orange with the Corpus widget. Clean them with Preprocess Text, score them with Sentiment Analysis and view the results in a Data Table. To evaluate, label about fifty texts yourself and count how often the tool agrees with you.

Is coral bleaching a good class 10 AI project?

Yes, as a medium-level Computer Vision project for SDG 14. Use an open image set of healthy and bleached coral, build an image classifier in Orange with Image Embedding, and report recall on bleached coral, since a missed bleached reef is the costly error. Check that both classes have similar numbers of images.

What is the class 10 AI project worth in marks?

The project carries 10 marks and the viva on it carries 5, out of the 50 practical marks in Part C of CBSE 417. CBSE allows a project, a field visit or a student portfolio for those 10 marks; your school tells you which it expects.

Can I make the class 10 AI project without coding?

Yes. Orange, which is part of the 417 curriculum, lets you build image, text and table models by connecting widgets. Every idea on this page can be built in Orange, including the stretch ones, though students confident in Python often find comparing models quicker in code.

Which AI domain should my project use?

One of the three in the curriculum: Statistical Data for tables of numbers, Computer Vision for images, or Natural Language Processing for text. Pick the domain whose data you can most easily get, and keep to one domain so the project stays finishable.

Can I use a dataset from the internet for my AI project?

Yes, open datasets are fine if you name the source in your report and can explain what each column or folder means. Data you collect yourself, with consent where people are involved, is often easier to defend in the viva because you know exactly how it was made.

How long does a class 10 AI project take?

An easy Orange project takes about three to four weekends of steady work; a stretch project with your own labelled data can take eight or more. Start in the first term, so the project is finished well before the school practical and the theory paper.

Do you help students with their AI project?

Yes. The project is guided inside the batch and one to one sessions: choosing an idea, checking the data, reviewing each stage and rehearsing the viva. The student builds the project themselves.
