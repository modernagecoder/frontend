Primary and lower secondary

VERA results are not marks. The IQB says no school or Land rankings come out of them; they show a teacher, a year early, where a class stands against the standards for grade 4 or grade 9. A weak VERA-3 result in May 2027 is a reason to look closely, not a verdict.

The MSK-Online-Check ("Mathe sicher können") is Hessen's own tool for Klassen 4 to 6. The ministry describes it as a digital diagnosis of basic maths skills that gives teachers support suggestions, free to every Hessian school since 2025/26. Its training themes are telling: natural numbers in Klasse 4 to 5, fractions and decimals in Klasse 6 to 7.

Where gaps usually open

Fractions and decimals are a common place for a gap to open, usually in Klasse 6 or 7, and the training themes of the Hessian check point at the same stage. A pupil who can add 2/3 and 3/4 without a calculator, and say why the answer is more than 1, has the base for equations in Klasse 7 and 8.

The second gap opens with functions and algebra in Klasse 8 to 10, just before the Realschule papers or the move into the Oberstufe. Tutoring before an exam year works far better than tutoring during one.

Hessen exams in 2027

How do the Hessen maths exams work in 2027?

Two moments matter most: the central papers at the end of the Realschule track in Klasse 10, and the written Landesabitur. Both are set by the ministry, not by the school alone.

Realschulabschluss, Klasse 10

In the Realschule track, the ministry writes the final papers centrally: "Die zentralen Abschlussarbeiten werden in den Fächern Deutsch, Mathematik und der ersten Fremdsprache geschrieben." A presentation based on a portfolio, in another compulsory subject, completes the procedure, and both count towards the overall grade.

The Realschulabschluss is awarded with an overall result of 4,4 or better. The qualifizierender Realschulabschluss, which opens the way to the gymnasiale Oberstufe, needs 3,0 or better including the papers in German, maths and the first foreign language. In 2027 the written papers fall between 10 and 14 May, with resits from 1 to 3 June 2027.

Landesabitur, Oberstufe

The Hessian Landesabitur combines central tasks from the ministry with parts written by the pupils' own teachers. The written exams in 2027 run from 15 April to 5 May 2027, and maths is the last of them: Wednesday 5 May 2027, for Leistungskurs and Grundkurs alike. The resit, with tasks that are also the same across Hessen, is on Thursday 3 June 2027.

On calculators, the ministry says the current rules in the Kerncurriculum hold up to the Abitur year 2029; from the Abitur year 2030, the requirements for extended calculator functions will follow the IQB. A pupil in Klasse 9 today should ask the school which calculator to buy.

Maths in English

Does maths tutoring in English help a pupil at a German school?

Often, if it is done carefully. The maths is identical; what changes is the language around it, and we take care that a pupil can say every method in the words their teacher uses.

Only English, said up front

We teach in English and not in German, and we tell families that before the first class. For a child at an international school in or near Frankfurt, English is already the language of maths lessons. For a child at a Hessian Gymnasium or Realschule, it means two languages for one subject, which takes some care.

Both sets of words

Mentors keep a running list of the German terms from the pupil's own worksheets beside the English ones: Nenner and denominator, Bruch and fraction, Steigung and gradient, Ableitung and derivative. Pupils write their working in German when the paper will be German.

Pace, not pressure

The mentor adjusts speed and accent to the pupil, explains one idea in two or three ways, and waits. A pupil who hides confusion in a full classroom often asks freely in a small online group or alone with a tutor.

Our mentors are based in India and teach live over video, so Frankfurt afternoons fall in their evenings. A pupil keeps the same mentor through the school year, which matters in maths more than in most subjects: the tutor who saw the fraction gap in Klasse 6 is the one who notices it again when it comes back inside an algebra problem in Klasse 8.

A Frankfurt maths project

How big is Frankfurt's Altstadt? The shoelace formula on the city's own map

The city publishes the exact boundaries of its 46 Stadtteile as corner coordinates in metres. With one formula from coordinate geometry, a Klasse 9 or 10 pupil can turn those corners into an area, check it against the city's figure, and then fall into the most common unit trap in school maths.

The data

Stadt Frankfurt am Main publishes WFS Stadtgebietsgliederung under the Datenlizenz Deutschland Namensnennung 2.0. Its Stadtteile layer, downloaded on 3 November 2026 in metres (ETRS89, UTM zone 32N), gives each district as one polygon: the Altstadt has 231 corner points, each an east and a north value in metres.

The layer also stores an area for each Stadtteil, which lets a pupil check the answer. We exported the corners to a small file, stadtteile.json.

The formula

For a polygon whose corners (x, y) are listed in order around the edge, multiply each x by the next y, subtract the next x times this y, add everything up, take the absolute value and halve it. In German it is often called the Gaußsche Trapezformel; in English it is the shoelace formula, after the criss-cross pattern of the products.

It works for any polygon without crossings, however many corners it has, which is why a computer can do in a moment what would take a page by hand.

Only after the formula passes a test we can do in our heads do we trust it on real data. Version 2 runs straight after the function from Version 1 and also does the conversion two ways: once by dividing by 1,000, the mistake we see most, and once properly.

The shoelace result for the Altstadt, 506,266 m², matches the city's stored area of 506,265.71 m² to the nearest square metre. For Sachsenhausen-Süd we get 30,520,652 m² against the city's 30,520,651.41; the one square metre comes from our rounding of each corner to the centimetre. The formula is right. The danger is the next line.

A kilometre is 1,000 metres, so a square kilometre is 1,000 times 1,000 square metres: a factor of a million. Dividing by 1,000 makes the Altstadt 506 km², and Sachsenhausen-Süd over 30,000 km². A quick check shows which answer can be true.

  1. Klasse 5 and 6, on squared paper. Draw an L-shaped room, count the squares, then split it into two rectangles and check that both ways agree.
  2. Klasse 7 and 8, by hand. Use the shoelace formula on a triangle with corners (0, 0), (6, 0) and (2, 5). Compare with one half times base times height.
  3. Klasse 9 and 10, in Python. Run Version 1, then find the area of Bahnhofsviertel from the file and convert it to hectares without looking at the table.
  4. Oberstufe. List the corners of a square clockwise instead of anticlockwise and print the sum before abs(). What does the sign tell you, and why does the formula need abs()?

Beyond the school course

Which maths competitions can a Frankfurt pupil enter in 2026/27?

Two national contests reach most schools, and both welcome pupils who simply enjoy problems.

Känguru der Mathematik

In 2027 the Känguru takes place on 8 April, later than its usual third Thursday in March, because that date would fall just before the Easter holidays in almost every Land. Schools register their pupils online from 1 December 2026 to 31 January 2027.

It lasts 75 minutes: 24 problems for Klassen 3 to 6 and 30 from Klasse 7. More than 920,000 pupils from over 13,000 schools took part in 2026.

Mathematik-Olympiade

The 66th Mathematik-Olympiade began with a school round in the autumn of 2026. The regional round is on 11 November 2026 and the Land round from 26 to 28 February 2027; the national final runs from 30 May to 2 June 2027 in Kaiserslautern.

Both contests enter through the school, so a pupil should ask the maths teacher. We are not connected with either organiser, and we train with original problems of our own.

Maths tutor in Frankfurt in English: common questions

Are the classes in German?

No. All lessons are live and in English only. The tutor speaks clearly, adjusts to your child's pace and accent, and keeps the German terms from school worksheets alongside the English ones, so your child can still write answers in German for a Hessian paper.

When is the Abitur maths exam in Hessen in 2027?

The Hessian ministry has set the written Abitur in Mathematik, Leistungskurs and Grundkurs, for Wednesday 5 May 2027, the last day of the written exams that start on 15 April 2027. The resit is on Thursday 3 June 2027.

When are the Realschule final papers in 2027?

In the Hessian Realschule track, the central written papers in German, maths and the first foreign language run from 10 to 14 May 2027, with resits from 1 to 3 June 2027. A portfolio presentation in another subject completes the final grade.

Can you help my child in Klasse 4 or 5?

Yes. Our Primary maths course places children by grade, and many Frankfurt families start around Klasse 4 or 5, before fractions and decimals arrive. The free first lesson includes a short check on a recent worksheet so we start in the right place.

Does an English-speaking tutor confuse a pupil at a German school?

Not if the tutor keeps both sets of words. Our mentors write the German term from the worksheet next to the English one, and pupils practise writing their working in German when the test will be in German. The methods themselves do not change.

Do you tutor IB, IGCSE or AP maths?

Yes, for pupils at international schools in and around Frankfurt. We have courses for IB Mathematics AA and AI, IGCSE Mathematics and AP Calculus, alongside the Hessian school course and the Abitur.

When are lessons in Frankfurt time?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Late afternoons are a common choice, or a weekend morning for a longer session.

Can you guarantee a better grade?

No, and nobody honestly can. The grade comes from the school and from the Hessian papers. What we promise is regular, careful teaching: secure methods, practice with problems we write ourselves, and an honest report on what your child can do.

What are the fees, and which currency do I pay in?

Lessons are paid monthly. Prices on this page are shown in euro at the European Central Bank reference rate, while the payment is taken in US dollars and converted by your bank, so your statement may differ slightly. The first lesson is free and needs no card.

Where are the tutors based?

In India. They teach live over video, so a 16:50 lesson in Frankfurt is 21:20 for the tutor in winter. Your child keeps the same tutor through the school year.
