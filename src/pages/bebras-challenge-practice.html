Bebras USA

What is the Bebras Computing Challenge in the USA?

It is a short, free online challenge of computational thinking tasks, taken at school under a teacher's supervision. It is part of an international initiative; bebras.org describes its organization as the "organizer of the International Challenge on Informatics and Computational Thinking".

From the US organizer

For this year the US organizer gives a two-week window, November 9 to 20, 2026, and its home page stresses two things: entry costs nothing, and children need no special preparation or study to tackle the tasks.

Students aged 6 to 18 get 45 minutes "to complete as many tasks as they can, they are not expected to finish them all." The site adds that the worldwide Bebras week for task solving "occurs during the second week of November."

What computational thinking means here

The organizer describes computational thinking as problem-solving skills that software engineers apply in programming, and lists "breaking down complex tasks, algorithm design, pattern recognition, generalization, and abstraction".

No coding is needed to take part. The tasks are short puzzles that use those ideas, which is why a child who has never programmed can still do well.

Reading the table

From Benjamins up, the students page describes the familiar format: "You get 15 multiple-choice questions and the maximum score is 180." The Kits and Castors groups have fewer tasks, so there is more time for each one.

Ages overlap at the edges (8 appears for both Kits and Castors). The coordinator places each student; when in doubt, ask the teacher which group they chose.

On the day

The students page says "You will participate online in your own school building. A teacher will supervise and it will take 45 minutes." It also says "you can use pen, paper and any calculator you want".

That pen-and-paper permission matters. Many tasks become easy once you draw the situation, and children who sketch tend to make fewer slips.

Scoring

How is the Bebras Challenge scored, and should you guess?

Every student starts with 45 points. Right answers add points, wrong answers take some away, and a blank costs nothing. That design changes how a sensible student should treat a question she cannot solve.

Bebras USA scoring rules (bebraschallenge.org Students page, read 31 October 2026)
CategoryTasksCorrectNo answerIncorrect
A (easier)5+60-2
B (medium)5+90-3
C (harder)5+120-4
Starting score45

Why start at 45? (our arithmetic)

If a student answered all 15 questions wrongly she would lose 5 x 2 + 5 x 3 + 5 x 4 = 10 + 15 + 20 = 45 points. Starting at 45 means the lowest possible score is exactly 0, never negative. The published maximum is 180.

In each category the penalty is one third of the reward: 2 of 6, 3 of 9, 4 of 12. That ratio is what makes the guessing question interesting.

To guess or not (our arithmetic)

Suppose a question has four choices and you have no idea. A blind guess in category A is right one time in four: on average 6 / 4 - 2 x 3 / 4 = 1.5 - 1.5 = 0. Guessing blind gains nothing on average, in every category.

Now rule out one choice. With three left, category A averages 6 / 3 - 2 x 2 / 3 = 2/3 of a point, B averages 1 point, and C averages 4/3. So: skip what you cannot touch, but guess once you can eliminate even one answer.

Scoring rules from the Bebras USA Students page, read 31 October 2026. The four-choice assumption and the averages are our own calculations, checked with exact fractions in Python; the number of choices can differ from task to task.

A puzzle

What does a computational thinking puzzle look like? A worked example

Here is an original puzzle of ours about a stack, one of the most useful ideas in computer science. It is not a Bebras task and does not imitate one; it simply uses the same kind of thinking.

The puzzle (ours)

Three rail cars, numbered 1, 2 and 3, arrive from the right in that order. Each car can either roll straight on to the left track or back into a short dead-end siding. A car can only leave the siding if it is the one nearest the exit, so the last car in is the first car out.

The left track must end up with the cars in a chosen order. Which of the six possible orders can be made? One of them cannot. Which, and why?

The reasoning

Try 3, 1, 2. To send car 3 first, cars 1 and 2 must both wait in the siding, with car 2 nearest the exit. The next car out of the siding must therefore be 2, not 1. So 3, 1, 2 is impossible.

Every other order works. For 2, 3, 1: park 1, send 2, send 3, then release 1. Five of the six orders are possible. Our Python check below confirms it, and for four cars it finds 14 possible orders out of 24.

  1. Draw it. Sketch the tracks and move numbered scraps of paper. A puzzle that is hard in your head is often easy on paper.
  2. Name the rule. "Last in, first out" is the whole puzzle. Computer scientists call a structure with this rule a stack.
  3. Look for the blocker. An order fails when a smaller car is trapped behind a bigger one that has to leave later.
  4. Generalize. Ask what happens with four cars. Generalization is one of the skills the organizer names.

Practice

How can my child practice for Bebras without drilling?

Try the organizer's own practice challenge once to meet the format, then spend the rest of the time on varied puzzles and a little code. The Examples page itself says there is "no need to prepare", only to get a feel for the style.

  2. Weeks 3 to 4. Algorithms: following and writing step-by-step instructions, shortest routes, sorting cards by hand.

Where your child is

Four stages of puzzle thinking

Our own description of how children grow as problem solvers.

Stages of solving a logic puzzle (ours)
StageWhat it looks like
1. GuessesPicks the answer that looks right without testing it
2. Tries casesTests choices one at a time until one works
3. Finds the ruleSpots the constraint that rules choices out quickly
4. Explains and extendsCan say why an answer must be right, and what happens with bigger cases

For parents

Stage 2 is a fine place to be at age 8. The step to stage 3 comes from talking about puzzles, not from doing more of them silently.

Ask "how do you know?" more often than "what is the answer?".

For students

If a puzzle feels stuck, draw it, or try the smallest version first: two cars before three.

A blank costs nothing in Bebras. Come back to hard questions at the end.

Bebras Challenge practice: questions answered

When is the Bebras Challenge 2026 in the USA?

The USA Bebras site says the 2026 challenge is scheduled for November 9 to 20, 2026. Schools choose one of the ten school days in that window to run it.

How do you prepare for the Bebras Challenge?

The organizer says no preparation or studying is needed. The useful step is trying the official practice challenge once to meet the format, plus regular logic puzzles that build pattern spotting, step-by-step planning and abstraction.

How long is the Bebras Challenge?

It lasts 45 minutes. Students try as many tasks as they can, and the organizer says they are not expected to finish them all.

How is Bebras scored?

Every student starts with 45 points. Easier A tasks give 6 points for a right answer and take 2 for a wrong one, B tasks give 9 or take 3, C tasks give 12 or take 4, and a blank scores 0. The students page gives a maximum of 180.

Should my child guess on Bebras?

By our own calculation, a blind guess among four choices gains nothing on average because of the penalty. Once one choice can be ruled out, guessing becomes worthwhile on average, so leave blanks only where nothing can be eliminated.

Is the Bebras Challenge free?

Yes. The USA Bebras site says participating in the challenge is free.

Can homeschoolers take part in Bebras?

Yes. A homeschool parent registers as a coordinator, and the registration page asks for a distinctive name such as Home School followed by your initials. Approval may take 1 to 3 business days.

Which Bebras group is for a 9-year-old?

Bebras USA lists Castors for ages 8 to 10, with four easy, four medium and four hard tasks. The coordinator makes the final placement.

What times are your classes?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays.

Are you connected with Bebras?

No. We are an independent online school with no link to Bebras USA or the international Bebras organization. Our puzzles are our own, and we never reproduce Bebras tasks.
