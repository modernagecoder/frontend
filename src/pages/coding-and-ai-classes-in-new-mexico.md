# Coding and AI classes in New Mexico

**Is computer science required to graduate in New Mexico?** No single computer science credit is required. Under HB171, for students entering high school in 2025 to 2026, New Mexico high schools must offer computer science as an elective within a two-unit pathway, and the state now teaches to the 2026 CSTA PK-12 standards. Modern Age Coders runs live online classes for New Mexico students, from computational thinking in third grade to AP Computer Science Principles.

New Mexico has taken the long road with computer science: a task force in 2019, a five-year strategic plan in 2021, and now a graduation law, HB171, that names computer science as an elective every high school must offer, starting with the class of 2029. In the same period the Public Education Department switched its standards to the newest national set, the 2026 CSTA PK-12 standards, which give artificial intelligence and data literacy a larger place. This page explains each of those pieces from the department&rsquo;s own documents, shows how access has grown from under a quarter of high schools to more than half, and describes how our live online classes reach a student in Las Cruces, Gallup or Taos as easily as one in Albuquerque.

## Where computer science sits in New Mexico&rsquo;s graduation rules

From the PED&rsquo;s HB171 Graduation Manual (April 2026) and the cohort 2029 flyer, read in October 2026.

**New Mexico graduation requirements beginning with cohort 2029, as PED summarizes them**

| Item | What PED says |
|---|---|
| Who it covers | Cohort 2029, the students entering high school in SY 2025-26 |
| Total | 24 units, including 4 in English, 4 in math, 3 in science and 4 in social studies |
| Electives | 5.5 elective units that must include a two-unit pathway concentration of the student&rsquo;s choice |
| Computer science | Financial literacy and computer science shall be offered as electives |
| What must be offered | The flyer states: Algebra 2, Financial Literacy, and Computer Science shall be offered. |
| Algebra 2 | Removal of Algebra 2 as a requirement, although it must still be offered as a math course |
| Local units | Two units set by each local school board or governing body |

### Offered, not required

HB171 guarantees that a New Mexico high schooler can find computer science on the timetable. It does not make every student take it, so taking it is a choice families make.

### A pathway, not a one-off

Because electives must include a two-unit concentration, a student who chooses computer science can build two connected units, for example an introduction followed by AP Computer Science Principles.

### Earlier cohorts

Students in cohorts 2025 to 2028 follow the earlier requirements listed on the PED graduation page; the HB171 changes start with those who entered ninth grade in 2025 to 2026.

The idea is older than the law. The PED&rsquo;s computer science strategic plan lists a goal to allow CS to satisfy a core graduation requirement dated April 2018, and a goal for all high schools to offer CS courses between August 2021 and August 2023. HB171 now writes the offering into the graduation rules themselves.

## New Mexico now teaches to the 2026 CSTA standards

The PED Math and Science Bureau says New Mexico&rsquo;s computer science instruction draws directly from the CSTA PK-12 Computer Science Standards (2026).

**Milestones in New Mexico computer science policy, from PED documents**

| When | Milestone | Source |
|---|---|---|
| December 2018 | Date given in the plan for the goal to adopt CS standards | PED Computer Science Strategic Plan |
| Fall 2019 | A computer science task force brought together to shape a state vision | Strategic Plan introduction |
| June 2021 | Strategic plan completed, a five-year plan of actions | Strategic Plan introduction |
| 2025 to 2026 | First HB171 cohort enters high school with CS as a required offering | HB171 Graduation Manual |
| 2026 | Instruction based on the CSTA PK-12 standards (2026), listed among adopted standards | PED Computer Science and Adopted Content Standards pages |

### AI moves to the front

The bureau says the 2026 standards reflect current shifts in the field, including the growing role of artificial intelligence and data literacy, with grade-level expectations from pre-K to twelfth grade.

### Three ways to teach it

Schools may deliver computer science through standalone courses, integrated instruction, or career and technical education pathways, depending on local context and student needs.

### A stated promise

The plan&rsquo;s vision statement reads: New Mexico guarantees a K-12 computer science education to ALL students. The bureau adds that access should hold regardless of location or background.

### Teachers are part of the plan

The strategic plan sets a goal of 30 individuals enrolled in a CS teacher preparation program and the PED offers a self-paced course, Computer Science Education for All. Growing the number of qualified teachers is how a state with many small, remote schools makes an offered course real.

## What does CABQ_PM mean? Decoding Albuquerque&rsquo;s bikeway data

Albuquerque&rsquo;s 2024 bikeway layer stores who maintains each segment as a short code. Students turn the codes into words with a Python dictionary, and discover what happens when the data uses a code the dictionary has never seen.

**Maintainer codes in the Bikeways Existing in 2024 layer, decoded with the layer&rsquo;s own domain (our counts, download of 18 October 2026)**

| Code in the data | Label from the layer&rsquo;s domain | Records |
|---|---|---|
| CABQ_SM | CABQ Street Maintenance | 1,905 |
| CABQ_PM | No label: not in the domain | 706 |
| BERNCO | Bernalillo County | 315 |
| HOA | Homeowner&rsquo;s Association | 275 |
| NMDOT | New Mexico Dept. of Transportation | 169 |
| (empty) | No value recorded | 68 |
| CABQ_OS | CABQ Open Space | 34 |

### 1. Build the lookup

The layer&rsquo;s metadata lists 12 maintainer codes with plain-English names. Students copy them into a dictionary, then replace each code in the 3,540 records with its label.

### 2. Meet the KeyError

The program crashes. Four codes in the data, CABQ_PM, Corrales, Los Ranchos and CABQ_Aviation, are missing from the domain, and between them they cover 726 records. Using dict.get() with a default keeps the program running and counts the unknowns.

### 3. Clean what is fixable

Jurisdiction names come in two spellings, CITY OF ALBUQUERQUE (3,010) and City Of Albuquerque (92). Converting to one case before counting merges them; surface values Gravel and UNK, also outside their domain, are flagged rather than guessed.

### Our counts, the city&rsquo;s data

All numbers in this section are ours, from one download of a static 2024 copy of the network. The City of Albuquerque provides the data as is, with no guarantee of completeness or accuracy, and we do not guess what an undocumented code such as CABQ_PM stands for.

Path types show the same idea from the other side: the domain lists 12 kinds of path, but only 10 appear in the data, with bike lanes the most common at 1,228 records. A lookup table tells you what could exist; only counting tells you what does. That distinction, between a schema and the records in it, is one of the most useful things a teenager can learn before working with any real database.

## Mountain Time across New Mexico

One time zone for the whole state makes scheduling simple, with the twice-yearly clock change to keep in mind.

### One zone

The IANA time zone database lists New Mexico under US mountain time, represented by America/Denver, from Farmington to Hobbs.

### India and Mountain Time

India runs 11 hours 30 minutes ahead of Mountain Daylight Time and 12 hours 30 minutes ahead of Mountain Standard Time, and does not change its clocks.

### Changeover dates

New Mexico falls back on November 1, 2026 and springs forward on March 14, 2027. Lesson times are always confirmed in Mountain Time.

**Code that knows the calendar** Students who write a lesson reminder using the America/Denver zone, rather than a fixed seven-hour offset, find that their program is still right on the morning after November 1, 2026. It is a small taste of why real software leans on shared databases like IANA tzdb.

## New Mexico&rsquo;s standards now put AI front and center. What should students learn?

How AI works, and how to check its output against the data.

Give an AI assistant the Albuquerque bikeway table and ask who maintains the most segments. It may answer confidently and invent a meaning for CABQ_PM, a code the city&rsquo;s own metadata does not define. A student who has built the lookup knows the honest answer is that the label is unknown.

That habit of checking a claim against the data is what the 2026 CSTA standards mean by data literacy. Our classes teach students to build models and tools themselves first, so that AI later becomes a helper they can audit rather than an authority they trust blindly.

New Mexico promises computer science to every student; the most valuable part of that promise is learning to ask where an answer came from. The longer argument is in [is coding still worth learning in 2026](/blog/is-coding-worth-learning-2026).

## New Mexico coding and AI questions

### Is computer science a graduation requirement in New Mexico?

No single computer science credit is required. Under HB171, starting with cohort 2029, high schools must offer computer science as an elective, and students choose a two-unit elective pathway.

### Who is cohort 2029?

PED defines cohort 2029 as students entering high school in SY 2025-26, so the ninth graders of fall 2025 are the first class under the HB171 requirements.

### How many units does a New Mexico diploma need under HB171?

The cohort 2029 flyer lists 24 units, with 5.5 elective units that must include a two-unit pathway and two units set by the local school board.

### Which computer science standards does New Mexico use?

PED says New Mexico instruction draws directly from the CSTA PK-12 Computer Science Standards (2026), which give more weight to artificial intelligence and data literacy.

### Does New Mexico have a computer science plan?

Yes. A task force met from fall 2019 and the PED completed a five-year computer science strategic plan in June 2021, with goals for standards, teacher endorsement and course access.

### How many New Mexico high schools offer computer science?

Code.org (2025) reports 126 of 235 New Mexico high schools, or 53.6%, offering computer science in 2023 to 2024, up from 23% in 2018 to 2019.

### How many New Mexico students take computer science?

The most recent Code.org count is 3,335 of 101,654 high school students, or 3.28%. Its yearly participation series ends at 3.3% for 2022 to 2023.

### What time are classes for New Mexico families?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience. All of New Mexico is on Mountain Time, and times are confirmed in it.

### Can your classes count as the HB171 computer science elective?

No. Elective units come from school courses. Our classes add depth, projects and AP Computer Science Principles preparation alongside a school pathway.

### Are you connected with the New Mexico PED?

No. We are an independent online school and are not connected with the PED, the City of Albuquerque, Code.org or the College Board.
