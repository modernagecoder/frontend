# AI and Python classes for teens in Denver

**Where can a Denver teenager learn AI and Python with a live teacher?** Modern Age Coders teaches Python, data science and machine learning live online to Denver students in grades 7 to 12, in level-matched groups or one to one. Colorado&rsquo;s computer science standards, adopted May 9, 2024, list Artificial Intelligence as one of six strands; our teen courses build that AI on real Python and real Denver data.

Colorado rewrote its computer science standards in 2024 and did something few states have done: it gave artificial intelligence a strand of its own, beside programming and cybersecurity. For a Denver teenager that is a signal about where the subject is heading, but standards are not a course, and in Colorado they are not even compulsory for districts. ## How many miles of bikeway does Denver have? It depends which mile you mean

The City and County of Denver publishes its bicycle facilities, existing and planned, as open data built for the Denver Moves: Bikes update. Teens query it, add up lengths by facility type, and discover that the dataset holds three different measures of length.

**Denver bikeway records with an existing facility type, by type (our sums from the city&rsquo;s Open Data Bike Facilities layer, queried 18 October 2026)**

| Existing facility type | Records | Our sum of CENTERLINE_MILES | Our sum of BIKE_LANE_MILES |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trail | 237 | 114.46 | 228.91 |
| Bike Lane | 203 | 91.24 | 156.72 |
| Shared Sidewalk | 119 | 56.78 | 113.13 |
| Buffered Bike Lane | 96 | 47.05 | 74.60 |
| Protected Bike Lane | 65 | 41.80 | 53.81 |
| Neighborhood Bikeway | 72 | 38.35 | 76.47 |
| Car-Free Street | 21 | 5.79 | 11.59 |
| Shared Street | 10 | 2.36 | 4.73 |

### 1. Respect the cap

A count request returned 2,025 records, more than the 2,000 the service hands back in one page. Rather than paging, students ask the server to group and sum for them with outStatistics, a real API skill.

### 2. Two kinds of mile

CENTERLINE_MILES measures the street once. BIKE_LANE_MILES counts each side that has a lane, and MILEAGE_MULTIPLIER is 2 on 1,833 records and 1 on 192. That is why lane miles are close to double for trails but not for protected lanes.

### 3. The hidden unit

Shape__Length looks like a third length, but the layer uses a Colorado state plane system measured in feet. The Trail rows sum to 604,328; divide by 5,280 and you get back 114.46 miles.

### A one-record puzzle

Group by display status and you find 822 existing bikeway records; group by existing facility type and you find 823. The odd one out is marked Future Bikeway yet already carries an existing type of Protected Bike Lane. A good analyst reports both numbers and the reason, rather than quietly picking one.

Every figure in this section is ours, computed from the city&rsquo;s layer on 18 October 2026. The City and County of Denver supplies the data as is, without warranty and not for engineering purposes, so our sums are a classroom result, not an official network length. The status field adds the forward-looking part of the story: 1,388 records are Unfunded and 500 Completed, with 60 in design or construction, which makes a good second question about what share of the plan already exists.

The Python is short: a requests call with groupByFieldsForStatistics, a loop that divides feet by 5,280, and a bar chart of centerline and lane miles side by side. The habit it builds is longer lasting. Before adding any column, find out its unit and its definition, and write both into the chart&rsquo;s caption.

## Colorado put AI in the standards. Why still learn Python first?

Because the AI strand is about understanding systems, and understanding comes from building them.

Hand the bikeway question to a chatbot and it may well sum the length column and call the answer miles. It would be off by a factor of 5,280 and nobody would notice unless the person asking knew where the number came from. Teens who wrote the query themselves catch it at once.

Our machine learning lessons follow the same rule. Students prepare the data, train a model, measure it on examples it has never seen, and then explain in plain words where it fails. AI assistants are allowed as helpers for drafting code, and every suggestion is read and tested before it is kept.

The Colorado AI strand asks teens to understand how computers learn; the reliable way to understand it is to make one learn something and check its work. The longer argument is in [is coding still worth learning in 2026](/blog/is-coding-worth-learning-2026).
