Why Python

Why learn Python first, and is it taught at school in Ayacucho?

Python reads almost like English, runs on any computer, and is used everywhere from engineering to AI. Peruvian schools do not teach it as a subject.

A first language that lasts

Python lets a beginner write something useful in the first hour: add up a list, read a file, draw a chart. The same language then carries a learner into data science, machine learning and AI tools, so nothing learned at the start is thrown away.

Its words are short and English: print, for, if, return. For a learner in Ayacucho, that is also steady English practice.

Not on the school timetable

The national curriculum makes digital skills transversal and gives secundaria no computing area. MINEDU has begun a Python pilot, Fábrica de Programadores, for 1,000 students in Lima Norte, ending with the PCEP certificate; it has not reached Ayacucho.

So a secundaria student in Ayacucho who wants Python learns it outside school, as university students often do before their courses require it.

Errors are the lesson

When a learner's program fails, the mentor does not fix it for them. The learner reads the error message aloud, says what they think it means, and tries a change. Most of the learning in an hour happens in those moments.

That is also how we keep AI in its place: a suggestion from any tool is tested like any other code, and the learner must explain why it works.

Group or private

A group of fifteen to twenty learners of a similar age suits most people, and other learners' questions help. A one-to-one hour suits shift workers, university students with changing timetables, and anyone who wants their own pace.

Either way, the mentor stays the same, and knows your code from week to week.

Equipment

What do you need at home in Ayacucho to learn Python online?

Less than people fear, but more than a phone. Here is our honest list.

  1. A computer with a keyboard. Any laptop or desktop from the last several years is enough. Python is free and light. Typing code on a phone is not practical, and we will say so.
  2. A connection that holds a video call. Speed matters less than steadiness. If home internet is not available, a university lab, a relative's home or a quiet internet café hour can work.
  3. A headset with a microphone. It makes the mentor clearer and lets you speak without the whole house hearing.
  4. A regular hour. Because we teach at any time you book, choose an hour when the computer and the connection are reliably free.
  5. Patience with English. Mentors slow down and rephrase. You do not need fluent English; you need to be willing to listen and ask.

INEI's 2025 figures put a computer in about one Ayacucho home in five. If you do not have one, tell us at the free class. We would rather plan around a shared or borrowed machine, or tell you a course will not work yet, than sell a place you cannot use.

A real Huamanga project

Why does a Huamanga university file break Python before it starts?

The Universidad Nacional de San Cristóbal de Huamanga publishes its undergraduate enrolment as open data. Our learners use it to meet a problem every Spanish-speaking programmer meets sooner or later: letters with accents, and the encoding a file was saved in.

The data

The dataset "Estudiantes matriculados de la Universidad Nacional de San Cristóbal de Huamanga" is on Peru's open data platform under the Open Data Commons Attribution Licence (ODC-By). Its file, matricula-2024-1-UNSCH.csv, has 11,993 rows, one per enrolled student, with the student's professional school, faculty and place of birth. Identifiers are anonymised.

Two small puzzles are already in the file. Its name says 2024-1, but every row's enrolment period says 2023-1, and the portal describes it as 2023 enrolment. And it was saved in an older text encoding, Latin-1, not UTF-8. We use only the school, faculty and birthplace columns; we have no connection with the university.

The question

A simple first task: how many students are there, and which professional schools are largest? Any beginner can write that in five lines.

Python 3 assumes UTF-8, the modern standard, unless told otherwise. That assumption is where the trouble begins.

Byte 0xcd is how Latin-1 stores the letter Í, as in INGENIERÍA. In UTF-8 that same byte means "a two-byte letter starts here", and the next byte does not fit, so Python refuses. Learners often search for a quick way past the error and find errors="replace". It makes the error go away, which is exactly the danger.

The program now runs and counts every student, but each Í and Ó has become the replacement mark �. Worse, a search for the Civil Engineering school answers False, as if no one studied it. Nothing crashed, and the answer is wrong. That is the lesson in one line: a program that runs is not a program that is right.

Counts are our own, from 11,993 rows across 28 professional schools. By birthplace, 11,620 students were born in the Ayacucho department, 204 in Cusco and 71 in Apurímac. Students' dates of birth, grades and other personal columns are never read.

With encoding="latin-1" every letter comes back and Civil Engineering is found. The last line counts the letters that needed care: 8,487 Í, 2,243 Ó and 108 Á in the school names alone. One more detail rewards a sharp eye: the faculty "INGENIERIA QUÍMICA Y METALURGIA" is typed without the accent on its first word, while the faculty next to it has one. So even with the right encoding, a search for "INGENIERÍA" in the faculty column misses 707 students. Real data needs checking twice.

  1. Ages 9 to 12. Type the words Perú, año and Huamanga in a text editor, save the file in two different encodings, and open each one as if it were the other. Which letters survive?
  2. Ages 13 to 18. Run Version 1, read the error aloud, and explain "byte 0xcd" in your own words before running Version 2.
  3. University students. Count students by faculty. Then write a check that warns when two faculty names differ only by an accent.
  4. Adults. Write a function that tries UTF-8 first and Latin-1 second, and reports which one worked, so your scripts never guess silently.

Class times

What time are live classes for learners in Ayacucho?

Any time the learner books. Ayacucho runs on Peru time throughout the year, and mentors plan around that.

Ayacucho hours converted to India time (illustrations, not a timetable)

Peru stays on UTC minus 5 hours all year, without summer time since 1994 (tz database zone America/Lima; INACAL keeps official time). India runs five and a half hours ahead of UTC, so it is always 10 hours 30 minutes ahead of Ayacucho.

With neither clock changing, a booked hour stays the same through the year. Learners book in Ayacucho time and the mentor sees India time. University students often switch to one to one at the start of each semester, when their own timetable changes.

Python and AI classes in Ayacucho: questions and answers

Are the classes in Spanish?

No. Our teaching happens live, over the internet, in English and nothing else. Mentors speak steadily, adapt to the learner's pace and accent, write key words in the chat and accept questions in mixed English and Spanish while giving the English terms.

Is there a Python course in Ayacucho?

Yes, live online. Learners in Huamanga, Carmen Alto, San Juan Bautista, Jesús Nazareno or anywhere in the department join from a computer at home, at a relative's or in a lab.

I am a university student. Is this useful alongside my degree?

Often, yes. Engineering, economics, biology and accounting students use Python for data and automation. We teach alongside your studies and have no connection with any university; your university decides what counts for your degree.

Do I need a computer?

Yes: any laptop or desktop, because code is typed on a proper keyboard and a phone will not do. In 2025 INEI counted a computer in 20.9% of Ayacucho homes, so many learners use a shared or borrowed machine; tell us and we will plan around it.

What is the youngest age for Python?

About nine. Younger children start with Scratch blocks. There is no upper age limit; adults of any age are welcome.

When can I have classes from Ayacucho?

Any day, any time, flexible: choose a batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at the hour that suits your family. India is 10 hours 30 minutes ahead of Ayacucho all year, so 08:00 in Ayacucho is 18:30 for the mentor.

Will I learn AI too?

Yes, after the Python foundations. Learners move on to data science, machine learning or generative AI, always testing AI output rather than pasting it. Under-18 learners only use tools their age allows.

What is the Huamanga project?

Learners open a real enrolment file from the Universidad Nacional de San Cristóbal de Huamanga, published as open data. It fails with a UnicodeDecodeError until they name its Latin-1 encoding, and a quick fix silently corrupts every accented letter. It teaches that a program that runs is not always right.

In what currency do I pay?

In US dollars, once a month, on a card; the soles amount is set by your bank. Someone browsing from India would see rupees. The trial lesson is free and asks for no card.

Do I receive a certificate?

Where a course includes one, we issue a completion certificate in our own name. No university or ministry issues it and it is not a Peruvian degree or school qualification. If you sit PCEP, that certificate is the Python Institute's, earned in its exam.
