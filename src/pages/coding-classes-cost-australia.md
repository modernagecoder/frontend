Behind the number

## Six things that change what coding classes cost

### Hours of real teaching

A two-hour session with a long snack break and a 50-minute focused lesson are not the same product. Divide the price by the minutes a teacher actually spends teaching.

### Children per teacher

A room of twenty with one instructor is cheaper per child than a group of six, and the child gets far less help. Ask for the ratio, not the room size.

### Venue or screen

Classes in a hired room carry the cost of the room and your own travel. Online classes remove both, which is a large part of why they can cost less per hour.

### Kits and hardware

Robotics and electronics programs often include a kit in the fee. Scratch and Python need nothing but a computer, so a pure coding class should not charge for equipment.

### Term or month

Paying for a whole term upfront is normal in Australia, and it means paying for weeks your child may miss. Monthly billing costs the same per lesson and is easier to stop.

### Continuity

The quiet cost of cheaper options is starting over: a new club, a new instructor, the same first lessons again. One teacher across terms is worth more than any single term's discount.

## The costs families tend to miss

**Upfront terms.** A term paid in advance includes the weeks of illness, sport carnivals and family trips. Ask whether missed sessions can be made up or credited.

**Enrolment and materials fees.** Some providers add a joining fee, a kit or a workbook. Ask for the total for the first term, not only the weekly figure.

**Camp extras.** Holiday camps can charge separately for early drop-off, late pick-up or lunch. Those add up over a week.

**Travel.** Two drives a week to a venue across a whole year is a real cost in time and fuel, especially in outer suburbs and regional towns.

**Software.** Scratch and Python are free to use, so a beginner course should not need paid software. Engines for older students may have their own licence terms.

**With us:** no enrolment fee, no kit, no term contract. You pay month by month and can stop at the end of any month.

## How to judge whether a coding class is worth the money

- Does your child build the project themselves, or copy steps from a screen?
- Can your child explain, in their own words, what their program does?
- Is it the same teacher from week to week, and do they know your child's name and level?
- How many children does each teacher look after at once?

- Is there a free class or a trial you can watch before paying?
- Is the full first-term cost clear, including any joining fees and kits?
- What happens if your child stops halfway through a term?
- Is there a plan for next term, or does every term start from scratch?

## Coding class costs: what Australian parents ask

How much do coding classes for kids cost in Australia?

It ranges from nothing at all, at volunteer clubs such as Code Club Australia, to paid term classes, holiday camps priced by the day and private tutors priced by the hour. Paid providers set their own prices and many only show them on a booking page, so compare the cost per hour of real teaching rather than the headline figure. The format table sets out how each option usually charges.

Are there free coding classes for kids in Australia?

Yes. Code Club Australia describes itself as free coding clubs for Australian children aged 8 to 13, and it is part of the Telstra Foundation. Clubs run in schools and libraries, usually led by volunteers, so availability depends on whether a club runs near you.

What is the cheapest way to start coding?

Scratch and Python are both free to use, and a free club or a free first lesson costs nothing. Paying makes sense when a child wants a teacher who knows them, regular feedback and a steady plan from one term to the next.

Is a group class worth it for coding?

Usually, for children who are happy to work alongside others and are roughly at the same level as the group. Our groups hold 5 to 10 children. A very quick learner, a shy child or a teenager with one large project often gets more from one-to-one lessons.

Are there extra costs on top of the class fee?

Sometimes. Watch for enrolment fees, robotics kits, paid software, payment for a whole term upfront and travel to a venue. With us there is no enrolment fee and no term contract, and the software used in early courses is free.

Do I have to pay for a whole school term?

Not with us; families pay month by month and can stop at the end of any month. Many Australian providers do bill by the term, so check what happens if your child stops halfway through.

Why are your prices in US dollars?
