# Coding classes in Thuringia

Thuringia has one of the most generous plans for school Informatik in Germany: the new compulsory subject Medienbildung und Informatik is meant to give six hours across lower secondary. It is arriving one grade at a time, though, and the first class to have it in grade 10 will get there in 2029/30. This page shows who has the subject now, how the central Informatik Abitur works, the holidays of 2026/27, how our live classes in English fit around school, and a data project on the Land's municipalities in which three towns vanish from a dictionary.

## Is Informatik compulsory in Thuringia?

Yes, as part of the new subject Medienbildung und Informatik (MBI), but only for the grades it has reached so far. The facts below come from the Gesellschaft für Informatik's Informatik-Monitor 2026/27.

**Medienbildung und Informatik in Thuringia: planned hours and roll-out**

| Grades | Planned weekly hours | Taught in 2026/27? |
|---|---|---|
| 5 and 6 | Two hours a week in each year of the pair | Yes |
| 7 and 8 | Two hours a week in each year of the pair | Grade 7 yes, grade 8 not yet |
| 9 | One hour a week | Not yet |
| 10 | One hour a week | From 2029/30 |

Source: Gesellschaft für Informatik, Informatik-Monitor 2026/27, Thüringen page, read 2 November 2026. MBI was introduced in 2024/25 at all general schools as a development of the earlier Medienkunde course; the monitor says it is currently taught in grades 5, 6 and 7 and grows one grade a year until grade 10 in 2029/30. A new curriculum for grade 7 came into force for 2026/27.

### Six hours, eventually

On paper Thuringia is ahead. The GI's national summary names Mecklenburg-Vorpommern, Saarland and Thuringia as the only three Länder that meet the medium-term recommendation of six compulsory hours of Informatik in lower secondary.

In practice a pupil in grade 8 in 2026/27 has not had MBI at all and will not, because the subject started with the younger grades. For those pupils, the electives are the only route: Informatik as a Wahlpflicht subject at the Regelschule, Gesamtschule, Gemeinschaftsschule or Gymnasium, where offered.

### Few teachers for a new subject

The monitor reports 149 Informatik teachers in Thuringia for 2024/25, 1 % of all teachers, and 10 newly hired in the year. A subject planned for six hours across six grades will need many more as it grows.

MBI is also a joint subject: the monitor describes it as a development of the older Medienkunde course, so its hours are shared between media education and informatics, and the time left for programming is smaller than the headline number.

Thuringia has 2,100,277 residents, 218,793 of them in Erfurt, the Land capital (Destatis, 31 December 2024). Children attend the Grundschule for four years, or a Gemeinschaftsschule from grade 1, then a Regelschule, a Gesamtschule, a Gymnasium, a Gemeinschaftsschule or a Förderschule. The KMK lists the Thuringian Regelschule as a school that combines two tracks. At the Gymnasium and the Gemeinschaftsschule grade 10 is the Einführungsphase of the Oberstufe; at the Gesamtschule it is grade 11.

## How is the Informatik Abitur set in Thuringia?

Centrally, by a Land commission, with programming in every optional task. These rules come from the Informatik-Monitor.

**Informatik in the Thuringian Oberstufe (Informatik-Monitor 2026/27)**

| Stage | What is offered | Exam |
|---|---|---|
| Einführungsphase | Informatik as an elective, usually three hours a week in grade 10 | None |
| Qualifikationsphase, basic level | Three hours a week | Oral exam only |
| Qualifikationsphase, raised level | Five hours a week, if the elective was taken before | Written exam, set centrally |

Taken from the GI's Informatik-Monitor 2026/27 (Thüringen page, consulted 2 November 2026). Informatik can stand in for a second science in a pupil's course choices, and as an exam subject it is treated as equal to the sciences. We did not obtain the 2027 Abitur dates from the Thuringian ministry, so this page prints none.

### What the exam looks like

Written tasks for the Leistungskurs are drawn up by a Land-wide exam commission and approved by the ministry, according to the monitor. Every optional task includes compulsory programming, and pupils must solve two of three optional tasks.

Practical tools may be used where they were part of the course and are installed on the exam computers, the monitor adds, giving a diagram editor or an automata simulator as examples.

### How to prepare

Because programming is in every optional task, a pupil cannot avoid it by choosing well. Fluency matters: writing a correct method quickly, explaining a state machine, modelling a class diagram.

We practise exactly those, with timed tasks we write ourselves. Only the school course and the school exam count for the Abitur; we teach alongside, never grade school work and have no role in the exam.

Two numbers from the monitor show how the Oberstufe works in practice. In 2024/25, 33 % of Thuringian upper secondary pupils took Informatik, from KMK figures, more than twice the national average of about 15 % that the GI quotes; yet the Land's education ministry counts only 1 % of Abitur candidates sitting an exam in it. Many pupils keep the subject without examining it.

## How many municipalities does Thuringia have? A name is not a key

The Land's geodata centre (GDI-Th) publishes all of Thuringia's administrative units as open data: the Land, its districts, its administrative communities and every municipality, each with an official code. Our pupils use it for a lesson that saves real programs from quiet errors: names can repeat, codes cannot, so you key your data by the code.

### The data

We requested the names, levels and official codes of all administrative units from the GDI-Th INSPIRE web feature service on 3 November 2026; its terms state the licence as Creative Commons BY 4.0 with the credit "© GDI-Th", which allows reuse with attribution. Our script saved the 729 units as einheiten_thueringen.csv with three columns: code, name and level (ebene).

624 of the rows are municipalities (Kommunales Gebiet). The others are 22 districts and towns with district status, 82 administrative communities and the Land itself.

### The question

A pupil wants a quick lookup from a municipality's name to its code, the kind of table a school app might use. In Python that is one line: a dictionary keyed by name.

Version 1 builds it and counts the entries. The count should be 624.

**Version 1: key by name** (Python)
```python
import csv

with open("einheiten_thueringen.csv", encoding="utf-8") as f:
    units = list(csv.DictReader(f, delimiter=";"))
gemeinden = [u for u in units if u["ebene"] == "Kommunales Gebiet"]

by_name = {g["name"]: g["code"] for g in gemeinden}
print("Municipality rows:", len(gemeinden))
print("Entries keyed by name:", len(by_name))
```
It prints: Municipality rows: 624 and Entries keyed by name: 621.

Three municipalities have vanished, with no error and no warning. A Python dictionary keeps one value per key: when the same name comes again, the new row silently replaces the old one. Version 2 keys the data by the official code instead and lists the names that appear twice.

**Version 2: key by code, find the repeats** (Python)
```python
by_code = {g["code"]: g["name"] for g in gemeinden}
seen, twice = set(), []
for g in gemeinden:
    if g["name"] in seen:
        twice.append(g["name"])
    seen.add(g["name"])
print("Entries keyed by code:", len(by_code))
print("Names used twice:", sorted(twice))
```
It prints: Entries keyed by code: 624 and Names used twice: ['Eichenberg', 'Elxleben', 'Lehesten'].

**Version 3: where are the twins?** (Python)
```python
from collections import Counter

kreis_name = {u["code"][:7]: u["name"] for u in units if u["ebene"] == "Landkreis/Stadtkreis"}
per_kreis = Counter(code[:7] for code in by_code)
top = per_kreis.most_common(1)[0]
print("Districts:", len(kreis_name))
print("Most municipalities:", kreis_name[top[0]], top[1])
for name in ["Eichenberg", "Lehesten", "Elxleben"]:
    print(name, "in", sorted(kreis_name[c[:7]] for c, n in by_code.items() if n == name))
```
It prints: Districts: 22, Most municipalities: Saale-Holzland-Kreis 91, Eichenberg in ['Hildburghausen', 'Saale-Holzland-Kreis'], Lehesten in ['Saale-Holzland-Kreis', 'Saalfeld-Rudolstadt'] and Elxleben in ['Ilm-Kreis', 'Sömmerda'].

**Thuringian municipalities that share a name, and their districts (our analysis)**

| Name | First district | Second district |
|---|---|---|
| Eichenberg | Hildburghausen | Saale-Holzland-Kreis |
| Elxleben | Ilm-Kreis | Sömmerda |
| Lehesten | Saale-Holzland-Kreis | Saalfeld-Rudolstadt |

Data: Thüringer Ministerium für Digitales und Infrastruktur, GDI-Th, INSPIRE WFS Verwaltungseinheiten, licence CC BY 4.0, credit © GDI-Th, retrieved 3 November 2026. The counts are of the units the service returned on that day; the district of each municipality is read from the first five digits of its official code. All counts and the table are our own analysis.

Each pair is two separate places in different districts, and a lookup by name would have sent anyone looking for one of them to the other. The fix is simple once you see it: use the identifier as the key, and keep the name as a label. The habit carries far beyond maps: pupils with the same first name in a class list, two products with one title in a shop, two files called final.docx.

1. **Ages 9 to 11, no screen.** Write a class list where two pupils share a first name, then try to find "the" one. What would make each entry unique?
2. **Ages 11 to 13, Scratch.** Store names in one list and codes in another, look up a name, and see which code Scratch returns when a name appears twice.
3. **Teenagers, in Python.** Run Version 1, explain the missing three in one sentence, then write Version 2 before reading ours.
4. **Going further.** Count municipalities per district from the code and rank the districts. Then check your total against the number of municipality rows.
