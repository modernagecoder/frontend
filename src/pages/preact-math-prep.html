PreACT test prep: PreACT and PreACT 8/9 math

PreACT is ACT's early test for students in grades 8 to 10, given by schools as a first look at the ACT and a predicted score. There are now four versions, and through the 2026-27 school year they are being rebuilt to match the enhanced ACT: math questions drop from five answer choices to four, the PreACT math section grows to 45 minutes, and science becomes a school-level option. This page sets out what ACT publishes about each version and the math section, explains how scores relate to the ACT, and offers problems we wrote. In short

PreACT is a family of ACT assessments for grades 8 to 10: PreACT 8/9, PreACT, PreACT 9 Secure and PreACT Secure. Each tests English, math, reading and science on the ACT's 1 to 36 scale, with a lower top score, and predicts ACT performance. The new PreACT math blueprint, from fall 2026, has 36 items in 45 minutes with four answer choices. Four versions

What are the different PreACT tests?

ACT now publishes four PreACT assessments. Two are for practice and planning; the two Secure versions can also be used for placement and accountability.

PreACT9 to 11Paper or online, multiple choice160 minutes35
PreACT 9 Secure8 to 9Online, multiple choice155 minutes32

Source: PreACT Assessments, ACT, read 31 October 2026. ACT's FAQ gives the new PreACT blueprint as 160 minutes with science and the earlier one as 130. PreACT and ACT are ACT trademarks; we are not connected with ACT.

What ACT says PreACT is for

ACT says PreACT assessments "offer 8th, 9th, and 10th graders an early experience with ACT test items" and provide a predicted ACT score. They are "Anchored on the ACT College and Career Readiness Standards."

Schools choose when to give PreACT 8/9 and PreACT, "anytime between early September to late May." The two Secure versions run in a four-week fall window in September and October and a six-week spring window in March and April.

Secure versions carry more weight

ACT says schools, districts or states can use PreACT 9 Secure and PreACT Secure "for program placement, high-stakes, or accountability purposes", and its enhancement page mentions uses such as dual enrollment.

If your child's school uses a Secure version for placement, the score matters more than a practice PreACT would. Ask the school which version it gives and how it uses the result.

The 2026 changes

Is the PreACT changing in 2026?

Yes. ACT says the PreACT assessments "are aligning with the Enhanced ACT", on a timetable that runs from spring 2026 to a PreACT 8/9 date ACT has not yet announced.

What changes for math

ACT lists "Reduced Answer Choices for Math Questions (5 to 4)". With four options, elimination is a little easier, and a blind guess has a 1 in 4 chance instead of 1 in 5: our arithmetic.

The new PreACT math section is 36 items, 32 of them scored, in 45 minutes, against 36 items in 40 minutes on the earlier PreACT blueprint, according to ACT's FAQ.

What changes for the Composite

From fall 2026 the Composite is "calculated using English+Math+Reading", the same as the enhanced ACT, so math is one of three parts of the Composite rather than one of four.

Science becomes optional, but ACT is clear that the choice is the school's or district's, "not at the individual student preference level."

ACT also explains that each section now includes embedded field test items: "Operational items are scored, while embedded field test items are not." They look like ordinary questions and are mixed in, so a student should treat every question as if it counts.

The math section

What is on the PreACT math test?

The same kinds of math as the ACT, at a lower level of difficulty. ACT publishes the reporting categories for the enhanced PreACT and PreACT Secure math sections.

Two kinds of math in one section

Preparing for Higher Math is the newer content: algebra, functions, geometry, statistics and number and quantity at the level a student meets in high school courses.

Integrating Essential Skills is the earlier math used in harder combinations: rates, percentages, proportional reasoning, area and averages, often several in one question.

Modeling runs through everything

ACT says items in the Modeling category "overlap with and are counted in other reporting categories." At least 7 of the 32 scored items ask the student to turn a situation into math and interpret the result.

That is the skill we teach most deliberately, because it is the one ordinary homework practises least.

Scores and reports

How is the PreACT scored, and what does the score mean?

On the ACT's own 1 to 36 scale, with a top score below 36, plus predicted ACT score ranges.

The scale

ACT says every PreACT version is "Aligned to the same 1-36 score scale as the ACT", with a top score of 30 for PreACT 8/9, 32 for PreACT 9 Secure and 35 for PreACT and PreACT Secure. Each also gives a STEM score.

A capped scale means a PreACT 8/9 score of 30 is the highest available, not a sign that 6 points were missed.

The prediction

ACT says reports for all four assessments include predicted ACT score ranges, and PreACT 8/9 and PreACT 9 Secure reports also include predicted PreACT score ranges.

A prediction is a range, not a promise, and it describes the student as they were on test day. Two years of algebra later, the picture can be quite different.

  1. Find out which version your school gives. PreACT 8/9 and PreACT are practice and planning tools; the Secure versions can be used for placement.
  2. Ask when and in which format. Paper or online for PreACT 8/9 and PreACT; online only for the Secure versions.
  3. Ask whether science is included. From fall 2026 that is the school's decision, and it changes how the Composite is calculated.
  4. Read the report by category, not only the Composite. The math reporting categories show where the points were lost.
  5. Keep the test booklet if you get it. ACT says schools using PreACT 8/9 and PreACT may retain booklets for the item response reports; Secure items are not released.

ACT also says students who take PreACT 8/9 or a paper PreACT "may even opt to have their information shared with colleges and scholarship agencies." That choice is the family's; nothing on the test requires it.

Worked examples

Three PreACT-level math problems, worked by reasoning

Our own problems for three of ACT's reporting categories. They are not ACT items, and ACT's own practice is linked from the school's test materials.

Modeling and Functions (ours): a streaming plan costs 12 units a month plus 0.5 units per film rented. Write the model and use it.
StepReasoning
1. Name the variablesm = films rented in a month, C = monthly cost
2. Fixed part and rateFixed 12, plus 0.5 per film
3. The modelC = 12 + 0.5m
4. Use it forward14 films: C = 12 + 7 = 19
5. Use it backwardA bill of 20.5 means 0.5m = 8.5, so m = 17 films
6. Interpret the slopeEach extra film adds half a unit; the graph rises 1 for every 2 films
Number and Quantity (ours): compute (3.2 x 10^4) x (5 x 10^-2) in scientific notation
StepReasoning
1. Multiply the numbers3.2 x 5 = 16
2. Add the exponents10^4 x 10^-2 = 10^2
3. Combine16 x 10^2
4. Normalise16 = 1.6 x 10, so the answer is 1.6 x 10^3, which is 1,600
Statistics and Probability (ours): weekly reading minutes for seven students are 20, 25, 25, 30, 35, 40 and 200. Which describes a typical student better, the mean or the median?
MeasureValueComment
Mean375 / 7, about 53.6Pulled up by the single 200
Median30The middle value, unaffected by the outlier
Better choiceMedianSix of seven students read 40 minutes or less

Each example asks for a decision as well as a calculation: which variable is which, which form is normal, which measure is fair. That is what ACT's Integrating Essential Skills and Modeling categories describe, and it is why we teach younger students to explain their choice in a sentence before they compute.

Questions families ask

PreACT math: common questions

What is the PreACT?

It is ACT's early assessment for students in grades 8 to 10, given by schools. ACT says it offers an early experience with ACT test items and a predicted ACT score, in English, math, reading and science.

What is the difference between PreACT and PreACT 8/9?

PreACT 8/9 is for grades 8 and 9, takes 130 minutes and has a top score of 30. PreACT is for grades 9 to 11 and has a top score of 35; its new blueprint takes 160 minutes with science.

How many math questions are on the PreACT?

ACT says the new PreACT blueprint, from fall 2026, has 36 math items, 32 of them scored, in 45 minutes. ACT lists 35 math items for PreACT 8/9, whose new blueprint is still to come.

Is the PreACT changing in 2026?

Yes. ACT is aligning PreACT with the enhanced ACT: math questions go from five answer choices to four, the Composite uses English, math and reading, and science becomes optional at the school or district level.

How is the PreACT scored?

On the ACT's 1 to 36 scale, with top scores of 30 for PreACT 8/9, 32 for PreACT 9 Secure and 35 for PreACT and PreACT Secure. Reports include predicted ACT score ranges.

Does the PreACT count for anything?

PreACT 8/9 and PreACT are mainly practice and planning tools. ACT says schools, districts or states can use the Secure versions for program placement, high-stakes or accountability purposes, so ask how your school uses it.

Can you use a calculator on the PreACT?

The ACT pages we read do not state the PreACT calculator rule. Ask your school's test coordinator, and practise with the calculator your child will be allowed.

How should a ninth grader prepare for the PreACT?

Lightly: one look at the format, steady school math, and a practice section under a 45-minute limit. The report is most useful when it reflects what the student really knows.

When are your PreACT classes?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays.

Are you connected with ACT?

No. We are an independent online school. We describe PreACT from ACT's published pages and teach with our own problems.
