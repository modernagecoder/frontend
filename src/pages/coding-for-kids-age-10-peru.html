Who this page is for

Who are coding classes for ten year olds in Peru for?

Families in Peru with a child of ten who reads English reasonably well, or who is keen to, and who wants more from a screen than games made by someone else.

Scratch veterans

They have built games with blocks and are starting to feel the limits. Typed code is the natural next step, and ten is when most children manage it comfortably.

Fresh starters

Ten year olds who have never coded can begin straight in Python. They lose nothing by skipping blocks at this age; they simply start with smaller programs.

Puzzle lovers

Some ten year olds care less about games and more about numbers. For them, code is a way to test a guess about a pattern hundreds of times in a second.

Our Spanish-language page programación para niños de 10 años covers the same age with a week-by-week plan from Scratch to Python and a closer look at AI. For the language itself, Python for kids in Peru lists the English words Python is made of and when children meet them.

One term of Python

What can a ten year old build in Python in one term?

More than most parents expect, as long as each project is small and finished. Here is a typical first term with us, described as the child would describe it.

  1. "I made the computer say my name in a sentence." Printing, text and variables. The child learns that a name in quotes is text and a name without quotes is a box holding something.
  2. "I drew a star with a turtle." Loops, angles and the surprise that 360 divided by 5 gives the turn for a pentagon, not a star; the star needs 144 degrees.
  3. "My quiz tells you if you are right." Input and decisions. The quiz breaks when someone types "Ten" instead of 10, and the child has to decide what to do about it.
  4. "The computer guesses my animal." A game of yes and no questions, built from nested decisions, which turns out to be how many real classifiers start.
  5. "I found a number that takes 111 steps." A while loop, a counter and some real mathematics, from the lesson on this page.

Why typed code at ten

Blocks hide the words; typing shows them. A ten year old who types while and if every week learns precise English and precise thinking together, because a single wrong letter stops the program.

Error messages are in English too, and they are short. Reading "NameError" and working out that a variable was misspelt is a small, satisfying act of detective work that ten year olds enjoy.

What still needs a teacher

Ten year olds rush. They type a line, run it, see an error and retype the line exactly the same way. A mentor watching live slows them down: read the message, point to the line, say what you expected.

They also over-trust working code. The program printed something, so it must be right. Our lessons always include one run where the output looks fine and is wrong, so that checking becomes a habit.

Online at ten

How does a ten year old's online life in Peru change before secundaria?

Sharply. INEI's household survey groups children of 6 to 11 and young people of 12 to 18, and the two groups live very different online lives. A ten year old is two birthdays from crossing that line.

What changes between the bands

Between April and June 2026, INEI counted 68.2% of the younger band online and 92.5% of the older band, a gap of 24.3 points by our subtraction. Phone use jumps by almost the same amount, from 67.8% to 92.2%.

The way young people get online changes too. Among internet users aged 6 to 11, 48.5% went online by phone; among those aged 12 to 18, 78.1% did. Somewhere around the start of secundaria, the phone becomes the main door.

Why that matters at ten

A ten year old who learns to make things on a computer now arrives at twelve with a habit of creating, not only scrolling. That is the quiet case for starting typed code before secundaria, when the phone takes over.

It also means setting rules early. We suggest that classes and practice happen on a laptop in a shared room, with the phone elsewhere; children who learn to code that way usually keep the habit later.

The end of primaria

What is left of primaria for a ten year old in Peru?

Fifth and sixth grade. Primaria lasts six years under Ley 28044, and on MINEDU's table of normative ages a child of ten belongs in 5° and a child of eleven in 6°, counted on 31 March.

Then secundaria

Peru's Reglamento of the education law says secundaria takes in adolescents "que hayan aprobado el sexto grado de Educación Primaria". So the move happens after 6°, usually at twelve.

Sixth grade can also bring a national test. In 2024 MINEDU's ENLA tested a sample of 105,534 pupils in 6° de primaria, alongside a census of fourth grade. In 2025 the ENLA tested 4° de primaria and 2° and 5° de secundaria. Whether a ten year old will sit it depends on the year and the school's selection.

Where coding touches the curriculum

The CNEB lists four mathematics competencies, one of them "Resuelve problemas de regularidad, equivalencia y cambio": finding and describing patterns. Today's lesson is exactly that, with a computer doing the arithmetic.

Its competency on virtual environments, shared by all areas rather than taught alone, includes building digital things "siguiendo un proceso de mejoras sucesivas y retroalimentación". That is a fair description of writing a program, running it and fixing it.

None of this makes our classes part of school. Modern Age Coders has no tie to MINEDU, any UGEL or any school, we do not prepare children for the ENLA, and we award no Peruvian grades. A child who completes a course receives our own certificate of completion.

An original Python lesson

What does a Python lesson look like at ten? A loop that might never stop

Our lesson for ten and eleven year olds in their first months of Python. It uses one simple rule, and it ends with a question that the world's mathematicians still cannot answer.

The rule

Pick any whole number. If it is even, halve it. If it is odd, multiply it by 3 and add 1. Do the same to the answer, and keep going until you reach 1.

The child tries it on paper with 6 first: 6, 3, 10, 5, 16, 8, 4, 2, 1. Eight steps. It feels like a game, and it is, until they try 27 on paper and give up somewhere past 300.

Why a while loop

A for loop needs to know how many times to repeat. Here nobody knows; we only know when to stop. That is what while is for: keep going while a condition is true.

To test whether a number is even, Python uses %, the remainder after division. n % 2 == 0 means "dividing by 2 leaves nothing over". Ten year olds learn this one symbol and use it for years.

Then we wrap the rule in a function, so it can be tried on many starting numbers, and we add one more thing to watch: the highest number the path reaches before it falls back to 1.

Children gasp at 27. Then comes the real question: does every starting number reach 1 in the end? Every number anyone has ever tested does, and nobody has been able to prove that all of them must. The idea is known as the Collatz problem. A ten year old has just written a program about an unsolved problem in mathematics, and understood why the program uses while and not for: we cannot promise how long it will run.

There is a second lesson hidden here. Five starting numbers all reaching 1 does not prove that every number will. Testing builds confidence; it is not proof. Children who learn that at ten are much harder to fool later, by an advert, a chart or an AI that sounds sure of itself.

  1. Our practice, predict. Before running it, guess how many steps 16 will take. Why is it so quick?
  2. Our practice, search. Use a for loop to try every start from 1 to 50 and print the one with the most steps.
  3. Our practice, careful. What happens if you start at 0? Why? Add a line that refuses numbers below 1.
  4. Our practice, explain. Tell your mentor, in English, why this program needs while.

AI at ten

Should a ten year old use AI to help with coding?

Not to write the code. At ten the point is to build the thinking that AI tools skip, and the main chatbots do not accept children this young anyway.

What the makers allow

OpenAI's terms for ChatGPT begin "You must be at least 13 years old". Anthropic sets 18 for Claude. A ten year old is too young for both, so we never use either with children of this age.

What we teach instead

How a model learns: with Teachable Machine, which trains in the browser without an account, a child trains a model on their own pictures and finds its blind spots.

Why it matters

A child who has written the 3n+1 loop knows that a program can be confident and still incomplete. That instinct is the right one to bring to AI later.

The ages each AI maker sets, with their own words quoted, are collected in our AI tools age guide.

For parents

Stage 4 is where today's lesson sits. Ask your child why 27 is special, and listen.

For your ten year old

Before you run a program, say what you think it will print. Then run it and compare.

An error message is a clue written in English. Read it all the way to the end before you change anything.

Parents in Peru ask

Coding at age 10: what parents in Peru ask us

Are the classes in Spanish?

No. Lessons happen live and online, and the only language used is English. Mentors speak plainly and at a steady pace, adjust to the way your child speaks and to how quickly they work, and write key words on the shared screen. Python itself is written in English words, so the language of the class and the language of the code are the same.

Is ten too late to start coding?

Not at all. Ten is one of the easiest ages to start, because a child can type, read error messages and hold a longer program in mind. Many children who start at ten overtake others who began with blocks years earlier.

Should a ten year old skip Scratch and go straight to Python?

Usually yes, if they read comfortably. A ten year old who has never coded can start Python with very small programs. If typing is still slow, a few weeks of blocks first is fine; the free class shows which suits.

What does 5° de primaria teach about computing in Peru?

There is no computing area in MINEDU's plan for primaria; digital skills appear as a competency running through all subjects. A number of private schools add coding in hours they control, so tell us what your child does at school and we will build on it.

How much English does my ten year old need?

Enough to follow short spoken instructions with help from the screen. The mentor slows down, repeats and shows. Children who start with modest English often improve quickly, because the code only runs once the instruction is understood.

What hours are possible from Peru?

Any day, any time, flexible: choose a batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at the hour that suits your family. India runs ten hours thirty minutes ahead of Peru all year, so 17:00 for your child is 03:30 the next day for the mentor.

Will my child use ChatGPT or Claude?

No. ChatGPT requires users to be at least 13 and Claude at least 18. Ten year olds in our classes learn how AI works with tools made for their age, after they have learnt to solve problems themselves.

Does coding help with school maths?

It often helps indirectly: children who code check their working, look for patterns and get used to precise steps. We make no promise about school marks, and our classes do not prepare for the ENLA.

What does my child need at home?

A laptop or desktop with a keyboard, a reliable connection and a headset with a microphone. A phone or tablet is not enough for typing Python.

Do you work with MINEDU or with Peruvian schools?

No. We are a private online school with mentors in India and no tie to MINEDU, any UGEL or any Peruvian school. We award no Peruvian grades; a completed course earns our own certificate.
