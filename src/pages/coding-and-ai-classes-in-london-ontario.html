Coding and AI classes in London, Ontario

Where does a London, Ontario student learn coding and AI from a live teacher?

Modern Age Coders teaches coding and AI live online to students in London, Ontario, from age 6 to adult, in a class of 15 to 20 or one to one. Senior students take our ICS3U and ICS4U companion or our MHF4U and MCV4U math course beside school; younger ones start with Python. The first lesson is free.

For a London, Ontario teenager, Grades 11 and 12 come down to two chains of courses. One runs from ICS3U to ICS4U in computer science; the other runs from MCR3U to MHF4U and on to MCV4U in math, and university computing programs ask for the second chain by name. We do not grant Ontario credits: those are earned at school, and our companion courses run beside them. This page sets out both chains in the ministry’s own words, what one university’s computer science page asks of an Ontario applicant, a project we built on the City of London’s park amenities file, the Waterloo contests that matter in Grade 12, and how our classes fit London’s Eastern clock.

Senior courses in London schools

Two course chains a London, Ontario student climbs in Grades 11 and 12

London schools follow the Ontario curriculum. We pulled each course record from the Ontario curriculum content service on 22 October 2026; quoted words are the ministry’s, the summaries are ours.

The two chains meet in university admission. The University of Waterloo’s computer science page, read on 22 October 2026, lists Advanced Functions, Calculus and Vectors, any Grade 12 U English and one other 4U course for Ontario applicants, and names Grade 11 U Introduction to Computer Science as recommended. In other words, a London student aiming at computing needs MHF4U and MCV4U, and ICS3U is the course that page encourages, not the one it demands. The ministry allows MCV4U to be taken at the same time as MHF4U, and a student who does that finds a weak function or a shaky limit costing marks in both.

Before Grade 11: ICD2O

Ontario’s current list also holds ICD2O, a 2023 open course for Grade 10 in which students apply coding concepts to hands-on projects and investigate artificial intelligence and cybersecurity. It is a gentle first look, not a prerequisite for ICS3U.

The Grade 9 math test

EQAO describes the Grade 9 Assessment of Mathematics as an online test of 54 questions over two sessions, using a multi-stage computer adaptive model, and says the results are included in the student’s final course mark. The 2027 window is 12 January to 5 February 2027.

Computer studies and the OSSD

For students who began Grade 9 in 2024 or later, one compulsory credit must come from a STEM group that includes computer studies, so a computer studies course such as ICS3U can count toward that requirement as well as being an elective.

Credit belongs to the school

We teach alongside a London student’s school courses, and we do not grant Ontario credits. ICS3U, ICS4U, MHF4U and MCV4U credits are earned only through the school. We are not connected with the Ontario Ministry of Education, EQAO, the University of Waterloo or any London school board.

London data project

Were 203 London park amenities really built in 2021? A project on the city’s file

The City of London publishes a Park Amenities file: soccer fields, ball diamonds, play structures, courts and more, each with an owner and a year built. Our students chart the years and find a spike that needs explaining. All counts below are ours.

This script prints the decade chart as rows of hash marks, then breaks down the 2021 rows by owner. Run it with Python 3 next to the downloaded CSV:import csv from collections import Counter decades = Counter() by_owner_2021 = Counter() no_year = Counter() sizes = Counter() with open("park-amenities.csv", encoding="utf-8-sig") as f: for row in csv.DictReader(f): year = row["YearConstructed"].strip() owner = row["Owner"] or "(blank)" if year.isdigit(): decades[int(year) // 10 * 10] += 1 if year == "2021": by_owner_2021[owner] += 1 else: no_year[owner] += 1 if row["AmenityType"]: sizes[row["AmenityType"].lower()] += 1 for decade in sorted(decades): print(decade, "#" * (decades[decade] // 10), decades[decade]) print("built in 2021, by owner:", dict(by_owner_2021)) print("no year, by owner:", dict(no_year)) print("peewee after lower():", sizes["peewee"])

1. Draw the decades

A text histogram, one hash mark per ten rows, shows the shape at a glance: almost nothing before 1990, then a broad plateau. Then one year inside the plateau stands out, with 203 rows dated 2021.

2. Cross the spike with owner

Split the 2021 rows by the Owner column and the spike changes character: 164 are school-owned, 32 city-owned and 7 private. Most are ball diamonds (124) and tennis courts (42).

3. Check the other side

The file lists 276 school-owned rows in total. Every one is dated 2021 or has no year at all (112). Not a single school row carries any other year.

What we think the spike means, and why it is only a guess

Our reading, and it is ours, not the city’s: 2021 is probably the year school fields were added to the city’s records, not the year 164 diamonds and courts were built. The file cannot prove that. A good student writes “dated 2021 in this file” instead of “built in 2021”, and says why.

The last line of the script teaches a smaller lesson. The AmenityType column spells one size as Peewee 25 times and as PeeWee once; lowercasing first gives the honest count of 26. For a stronger student, the next step is mapping the soccer fields (254 rows) and ball diamonds (194) with the X and Y columns, which hold projected map coordinates rather than latitude and longitude. The data comes from the City of London under its Terms of Use for City of London open data, which allow use for any lawful purpose; the file is publicly available from the City under those terms (dataset page). The City has not approved or endorsed this page or our classes.

CEMC at the University of Waterloo runs both. Facts below come from CEMC’s contest pages, read on 22 October 2026.

The Canadian Computing Competition at a glance, from CEMC’s CCC page (2026 means are CEMC’s table values)

The Senior average sitting near a fifth of the marks tells a London student something useful: the paper is meant to stretch even good programmers. CEMC lists the 2027 CCC for Wednesday 17 February 2027, and notes that with some language choices, Python or Java for example, a perfect score may not always be possible, which is why our Senior students learn to judge an algorithm’s speed before writing it. ICS4U students meet that same idea when the course asks for efficient, modular programs.

Euclid, for Grade 12 math

CEMC lists the Euclid for Tuesday 6 April 2027, for students in their final year of secondary school. Its full-solution questions reward the clear written reasoning MHF4U and MCV4U ask for.

Scholarships, not admission

Waterloo’s computer science page says a contest such as the Euclid is not required for admission, but is required for some Faculty of Mathematics scholarships. Its Admission Information Form, due 15 February 2027, asks about Waterloo contests written.

Only through a school

CEMC does not take entries from individual students; a London student writes through their own school, or through another local school that agrees to host them.

We prepare students with problems we write ourselves and point them to CEMC’s free past contests for the real thing; we never reproduce CEMC questions or solutions. For deeper reading see the CCC Senior guide, MHF4U help and MCV4U help. Modern Age Coders is independent of CEMC and of the University of Waterloo.

Coding and AI

Will AI make ICS3U pointless for a London teen?

No. It makes understanding the code more valuable, not less.

Give an AI assistant the London amenities file and ask how many were built each year, and it may well plot 2021 as a building boom. The tool did the counting correctly and the interpretation wrongly. Spotting that the spike is almost all school rows, and that school rows have no other year, is a human judgment that comes from writing the cross-tab yourself.

That is how we teach: reason through the problem, write the code, then challenge the result. Teens meet AI tools as part of the work, within each tool’s age rules, and use them to explain and check rather than to produce what they hand in.

ICS4U asks for programs that are fully documented, and documenting code well means understanding every line of it. The longer argument is in why understanding beats generating, in our longer essay.

London, Ontario coding and AI questions

The questions London families send us most often.

Do you offer coding classes in London, Ontario?

Yes, taught live over video rather than in a local centre. Students from age 6 join from home, in a class of 15 to 20 or one to one.

Can you help with ICS3U or ICS4U?

Yes. Our companion course runs beside the school course with practice, code review and projects in Python and Java. We do not grant Ontario credits; the credit is earned at school.

Do you tutor MHF4U and MCV4U for London students?

Yes. Our Grade 12 university math course covers Advanced Functions and Calculus and Vectors alongside school. Ontario lists MHF4U as needed before or at the same time as MCV4U, and we plan lessons in that order.

Does a London student need ICS3U for a computer science degree?

Not always. The University of Waterloo computer science page lists MHF4U and MCV4U as requirements and ICS3U as recommended. Each university sets its own rules, so check its admission page.

What is the Grade 9 EQAO math test like?

EQAO describes it as an online, adaptive test of 54 questions in two sessions, with results included in the final course mark. The 2027 window is 12 January to 5 February 2027.

Can my teen write the CCC from London?

Yes, through a school, since CEMC does not accept individual entries. CEMC lists the 2027 CCC for Wednesday 17 February 2027, at Junior or Senior level.

What age can children start coding classes in London, Ontario?

From 6, with block coding. Most move to typed Python between 9 and 11, and adults are welcome at any age.

When are classes held?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Times are always confirmed in Eastern time.

Will a 519 or 226 number work in the form?

Yes. London-area numbers with 519, 226, 548 or 382 all work; enter the ten digits without the leading 1.
