Teaching Textbooks math help, for when the app is not enough

Teaching Textbooks was built so a homeschool parent does not have to teach math: the app gives the lecture, reads problems aloud, offers hints, grades every answer and plays a worked solution for each one. For many families it does exactly that. The trouble usually starts quietly, around Pre-Algebra or Algebra 1, when a child can follow every solution and still cannot start the next problem. This page explains how the program works, from its own site, and what a live teacher adds that a recorded solution cannot.

Practical details

Teaching Textbooks says access to each course "is for 12 months, plus a 3-month pause feature" for breaks, and that the apps work on computers, tablets and phones, offline "for up to 6 lessons at once".

Students get a scratchpad area to work out steps on screen, which is saved so a parent can look at it later, and each app includes a full, searchable eBook that a parent can print.

Use their tutor line first

The publisher offers its own free tutor helpline: customers can call to set up an appointment with a math tutor about a problem the solutions did not clear up. If your child is stuck on one problem, that is the natural first step.

This page is for a different situation: a child who needs regular teaching, week after week, because the stuck moments have become the pattern.

Teaching Textbooks publishes a placement test for each course. If you are unsure, it says to "have them take two tests starting with the higher level" and to give the lower test only if very few questions are right on the higher one.

Warning signs

How can I tell my child needs more than the app?

A high average in the gradebook can hide a child who is leaning on hints and solutions. These are signals our teachers look for; they come from our own lessons and are offered as general guidance, never as figures.

Signs a Teaching Textbooks student may need a live teacher (our teaching notes)
What you noticeWhat it often meansWhat to try
Scores are fine, but lessons take much longer than beforeHints are doing the thinkingDo three problems a day with hints switched off, if your parent controls allow it
Your child watches the solution, then moves onFollowing is not the same as being able to startAfter a missed problem, redo it from a blank page
The scratchpad is empty for multi-step problemsSteps are being done in the head, or guessedAsk for every line written
Your child cannot say why a step worksThe method is memorised, not understoodAsk "why are we allowed to do that?" once a lesson
Pre-Algebra or Algebra 1 brings tearsFractions and negatives were never quite secureRepair those first, then go forward

None of these are faults in the program. A recorded solution is excellent at showing how. It cannot notice which part of the reasoning your child skipped, or ask a follow-up question. That is the gap a person fills.

The why

What does "explaining the why" look like in an algebra problem?

Our worked examples at Algebra 1 level, written by us. Each step carries a reason, which is what we ask students to say out loud in class.

Finding the line through (2, 5) and (6, 13), with the reason for each step (our example)
StepWorkingWhy it is allowed
1Slope = (13 - 5) / (6 - 2) = 8 / 4 = 2Slope is change in y over change in x between any two points on a line
25 = 2 x 2 + bAny point on the line satisfies y = mx + b
3b = 5 - 4 = 1Subtracting 4 from both sides keeps the equation balanced
4y = 2x + 1Slope and intercept fix the line
5Check (6, 13): 2 x 6 + 1 = 13The second point must also fit, or something went wrong

Clearing fractions

Solve x/3 + x/4 = 7. Our worked answer: the denominators 3 and 4 share the multiple 12, so multiply every term by 12. That gives 4x + 3x = 84, so 7x = 84 and x = 12.

The why: multiplying both sides by the same non-zero number keeps the equation true. The usual slip is multiplying only the left side.

Substitution

Solve y = 2x + 1 and x + y = 10 together. Our worked answer: replace y in the second equation with 2x + 1, so x + 2x + 1 = 10, 3x = 9, x = 3 and y = 7.

The why: both equations describe the same point, so y means the same thing in each. Check: 3 + 7 = 10 and 2 x 3 + 1 = 7.

App and teacher

How do Teaching Textbooks and a live tutor work together?

The app keeps the daily lessons and grading. The teacher takes the week’s hardest problems and turns them into a conversation.

  1. Keep the daily lesson in the app. Teaching Textbooks stays the curriculum; we do not replace or reorder it.
  2. Flag problems during the week. Your child notes any problem they got right only after a hint or a solution.
  3. Bring the flagged problems to class. The teacher asks your child to explain the solution in their own words and finds the missing reason.
  4. Practise with new problems. The teacher sets fresh problems of our own on the same idea, so the child shows they can start without help.
  5. Check the gradebook trend monthly. Fewer flagged problems over time is the sign that support can step back.

The app alone may suit

A student who rarely needs hints, can explain the steps and keeps a steady average. Many children are in this group.

App plus batch may suit

A student at Pre-Algebra or Algebra 1 who benefits from hearing classmates reason through the same kinds of problems.

App plus 1-on-1 may suit

A student with specific gaps, such as fractions or negatives, who needs them repaired at their own pace.

Teaching Textbooks help: common questions

Is there live math help for kids using Teaching Textbooks?

Yes. Our teachers work live online with students who keep doing their daily Teaching Textbooks lessons. We explain the reasoning behind the problems your child found hardest, in a batch of 15 to 20 or one to one.

How does Teaching Textbooks work?

Each course is an app that begins every lesson with an interactive lecture, gives hints, grades every problem and offers a step-by-step solution for each one. Problems and hints are read aloud to the student.

Does Teaching Textbooks have its own tutor?

Yes. Its site describes a free tutor helpline: customers can call to set up an appointment with a math tutor. That is a good first step for a single problem; regular live classes suit a child who needs ongoing teaching.

Which Teaching Textbooks courses are there?

The site lists Math 3, Math 4, Math 5, Math 6, Math 7, Pre-Algebra, Algebra 1, Geometry, Algebra 2 and Pre-Calculus, with optional printed textbooks.

How do I choose a Teaching Textbooks level?

Use its placement tests. Teaching Textbooks suggests trying the higher level first and says a student just under the recommended score is almost certainly ready for that level.

Why is my child struggling with Teaching Textbooks Algebra 1?

In our experience it is usually not the new algebra but older gaps in fractions and negative numbers, or a habit of following solutions without being able to start a problem alone. Both can be repaired.

Is a high gradebook average enough?

Not always. A child can score well while relying on hints and solutions. Ask them to explain one problem a day, or redo a missed problem on paper, to see what they can do unaided.

Will you replace Teaching Textbooks?

No. The app stays your curriculum. We add live teaching on the problems your child flags, with fresh practice written by our teachers.

When are classes?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays.

Are you connected with Teaching Textbooks?

No. We are an independent online school. Teaching Textbooks is a trademark of its owner, and we describe its program only from its own published pages.
