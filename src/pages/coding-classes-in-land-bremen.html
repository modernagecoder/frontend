Informatik in the Land Bremen

Is there compulsory Informatik in Bremen schools?

Not yet. The figures and quotations below come from the 2026/27 Informatik-Monitor of the Gesellschaft für Informatik, which surveys each Land's regulations. We are not connected to the GI or to the Senatorin für Kinder und Bildung.

What exists today

After four years of Grundschule, pupils in the Land Bremen go to an Oberschule or a Gymnasium. For both, the monitor states plainly that there are no curricular offers with an Informatik focus in the Sekundarstufe I.

The compulsory subject Wirtschaft, Arbeit, Technik in grades 5 and 6 has digital content, but the monitor notes that it aims at media skills, not Informatik. Some schools describe Informatik elements in their own ITG curricula for the Wahlpflicht area.

What is coming

Bremen is piloting a curriculum in 2025/26 and 2026/27. From the 2027/28 school year it will introduce Informatik as a compulsory subject at all schools, two to four weekly hours in total, step by step.

A pupil in grade 5 today will therefore meet the new subject later in Sekundarstufe I, while pupils now in grades 8 to 10 are likely to finish without it.

Oberstufe and Abitur

How does the Informatik Abitur work in Bremen, and what are the 2027 dates?

Bremen's written Informatik Abitur is not one paper for the whole Land. Each school writes its own, and the authority approves it.

Leistungskurs: written, decentral

A Leistungskurs in Informatik is examined in writing. The monitor explains the process: each school submits its exam tasks in two variants to the authority, which checks and approves them and decides which is used on the main date and which on the second date, sometimes after several rounds of revision.

Tasks are not worked on a computer. Schools apply for the aids they need, such as tables, formula collections, a dictionary or a calculator.

Grundkurs: oral only

A Grundkurs in Informatik can be an oral exam subject but not a written one. Informatik can be an exam subject at all only if it was taken without a break from the second half of the Einführungsphase through the Qualifikationsphase.

The monitor adds that Informatik cannot be chosen as the third exam subject, and it does not replace a natural science in the course requirements.

Because a Bremen Leistungskurs paper is written by the pupil's own school, the school's course and its teachers are the surest guide to what will be asked. Our part is the foundations behind any paper: clean code on paper, data structures, databases and the reasoning to explain them. A Land-wide project

Did the Land Bremen spend what it planned in 2025? A percentage project

The Senatorin für Finanzen publishes the Land's budget line by line, with the plan and the actual result, as open data. Our older pupils use it to learn the most common way a percentage goes wrong: dividing by a base that is zero, or so small that the answer means nothing.

The data

The dataset is Haushalt des Landes und der Stadtgemeinde Bremen 2025 (Ist-Daten), published under CC BY on the Transparenzportal Bremen. We use the file for the Land, downloaded on 3 November 2026. Bremerhaven keeps a budget of its own, so it is not in this file.

Each row is one budget line: its number, its purpose (Zweckbestimmung), the amount planned (Anschlag) and the amount actually booked in 2025, in the last column, headed with the German word for actual. The file is written the German way, with points for thousands and commas for decimals, which the euro() function converts.

The question

For each line, by what percentage did the result differ from the plan? The formula looks harmless: (actual minus plan) divided by plan, times 100.

Version 1 applies it to every line. It reads 8,620 lines and then stops, because some lines were planned at exactly zero.

A crash is a good result here, because it tells the truth: for a line with no plan, "percent of the plan" has no answer. Version 2 replaces the loop at the end of Version 1. It counts the zero-plan lines separately and computes the percentage only where a plan exists.

The program no longer crashes, but its headline is useless. A change of 94,034 percent sounds dramatic; it only means the plan for that refund line was tiny. A small base makes every percentage enormous. Version 3 asks the question again, only for lines planned at ten million or more.

  1. Grades 6 and 7, by hand. A class planned to read 0 books in August and read 3. A second class planned 1 and read 4. Which grew "more"? Can you give both a percentage?
  2. Grades 8 and 9, Python. Run Version 1, read the error, and write Version 2 yourself before you look at ours.
  3. Grade 10. Change the threshold in Version 3 from ten million to one million. Does the largest change look sensible now? Choose a threshold and defend it in two sentences.
  4. Oberstufe. Instead of a threshold, rank lines by the absolute difference in euro. When is that a better question than the percentage?

Two cities, one Land

Destatis gives the Land Bremen 704,881 inhabitants on 31 December 2024: 586,271 in the city of Bremen and 118,610 in Bremerhaven. It is the smallest Land by population.

Online lessons take the river out of the equation. A pupil in Bremerhaven and one in Bremen-Nord share the same live class.

Planning the Abitur spring

For a candidate in 2027, certificate-relevant teaching ends on 19 March, the Easter holidays fill 22 March to 3 April, and the decentral Leistungskurs papers can start on 13 April.

Easter is the natural place for a full practice paper written on paper and timed, followed by a week of work on whatever it uncovered.

Coding classes in Bremen and Bremerhaven: common questions

Are the classes in German?

No. All classes are live and taught in English only. Mentors adapt their pace and get used to each pupil's accent, and German school words such as Leistungskurs or Oberschule are fine in a question.

Is Informatik compulsory in Bremen?

Not yet. The Gesellschaft für Informatik's Informatik-Monitor 2026/27 names Bremen among six Länder without compulsory Informatik for all pupils. It reports that Bremen will introduce a compulsory subject of two to four weekly hours in total, step by step from 2027/28.

Does my child get any Informatik in grades 5 to 10 today?

According to the Informatik-Monitor, neither the Oberschule nor the Gymnasium has curricular Informatik in Sekundarstufe I at present. Some schools include Informatik elements in their own ITG electives.

Is the Bremen Informatik Abitur the same for every school?

No. The Informatik-Monitor explains that each school submits its Leistungskurs tasks in two variants to the authority, which approves them and decides which is used on the main and second dates. Tasks are not worked on a computer.

Can Informatik be a written exam at Grundkurs level?

No. The Informatik-Monitor states that a Grundkurs in Informatik can be examined orally but not in writing. A written Informatik exam is only possible in the Leistungskurs.

When is the maths Abitur in Bremen in 2027?

The Senatorin für Kinder und Bildung lists the written maths exam, LK and GK, on Wednesday 5 May 2027. German is on 27 April and English on 30 April 2027; the central resit dates run from 24 to 31 May 2027.

What time are the lessons?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. India is 4 hours 30 minutes ahead of Bremen in winter and 3 hours 30 minutes in summer.

Do you give grades or certificates?

No. We teach alongside school and award no German grades or qualifications. The school and the authority set and mark the Abitur.

Where do the mentors teach from?

From India, live over video and in English. A pupil keeps the same mentor from week to week.

How do I pay?

Monthly. Fees are shown in euro at the European Central Bank reference rate and charged in US dollars, so your bank converts the amount. The first class is free and needs no card.
