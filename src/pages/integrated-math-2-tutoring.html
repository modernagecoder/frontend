Integrated Math 2 tutoring: help with Integrated Math II

Integrated Math II is the year quadratics take over. A tenth grader factors, completes the square and graphs parabolas, then finds that some quadratics have no real solutions at all and meets the complex numbers. Between those algebra units sit similarity, the first trigonometry, circle theorems and a probability unit. Students who changed schools between Algebra 1 and Geometry and an integrated course often arrive missing one piece. This page shows what the course holds and teaches its core skill.

In short

Integrated Math II is the second course of the integrated high school pathway, usually taken in grade 10. Utah, which calls it Secondary Mathematics II, says its focus "is on quadratic expressions, equations, and functions", alongside complex numbers, conditional probability, similarity, right triangle trigonometry and circles. We teach it live online through our High School Mathematics course, either as a 15 to 20 student batch or in private 1-on-1 lessons.

The focus

What is taught in Integrated Math 2?

Quadratics first, with four other threads tied to them. Utah's course description spells out the links in a single paragraph, quoted here in parts.

How Secondary Mathematics II ties its topics together (Utah Core State Standards for Mathematics, High School, read 31 October 2026)
ThreadUtah's own words
Quadratics"THE FOCUS OF SECONDARY MATHEMATICS II is on quadratic expressions, equations, and functions and on comparing their characteristics and behavior to those of linear and exponential relationships"
Number system"real and complex numbers are introduced so that all quadratic equations can be solved"
Probability"The link between probability and data is explored through conditional probability and counting methods"
Similarity and trig"The study of similarity leads to an understanding of right triangle trigonometry and connects to quadratics through Pythagorean relationships"
Circles"Circles, with their quadratic algebraic representations, round out the course"

Source: Utah Core State Standards for Mathematics, High School (Utah State Board of Education). Other states and districts that teach an integrated pathway use their own course names and unit orders.

Why quadratics sit at the center

Almost every unit returns to a squared term. Right triangles bring a^2 + b^2 = c^2; a circle centered at the origin is x^2 + y^2 = r^2; the complex numbers exist, in this course, to finish off quadratics that have no real roots.

So a student who is weak at factoring and completing the square tends to struggle all year, not just in the first unit. That is where our lessons usually begin.

Where Integrated Math II sits

The Common Core lists an "integrated course sequence (Mathematics 1, Mathematics 2, Mathematics 3)" as one model alongside Algebra I, Geometry and Algebra II.

North Carolina treats the second course as close to essential: even a student exempted from its standard sequence "will be required to pass: NC Math 1 and Math 2 plus two additional courses identified on the NC DPI Math options chart."

Six areas

What are the units of Integrated Math II?

Utah divides the course into six critical areas. The right-hand column is our summary of what a student actually does in each.

Utah also offers an extended version, Secondary Math II Plus, which its 2021 pathways chart says adds "Depth in Complex Numbers", "Trigonometric Identities" and "Conic Sections." Students on the extended track need the core skills on this page first, then the extra topics.

Worked examples

How do you find the vertex and zeros of a quadratic?

Rewrite it in the form that shows what you need. Factored form shows the zeros, vertex form shows the turning point, and standard form shows the y-intercept. Every example below is our own.

One quadratic in three forms (ours): f(x) = x^2 - 6x + 8
FormExpressionWhat it shows at a glance
Standardx^2 - 6x + 8y-intercept (0, 8)
Factored(x - 2)(x - 4)Zeros at x = 2 and x = 4
Vertex(x - 3)^2 - 1Vertex (3, -1), a minimum, axis x = 3
A quadratic with no real roots (ours): solve x^2 + 4x + 13 = 0
StepWorking
1. Complete the squarex^2 + 4x + 4 = -13 + 4, so (x + 2)^2 = -9
2. Notice the problemNo real number squares to -9, so there are no real roots
3. Extend the number systemx + 2 = 3i or x + 2 = -3i, since (3i)^2 = -9
4. Solvex = -2 + 3i or x = -2 - 3i
5. Check with the graphVertex form (x + 2)^2 + 9 has vertex (-2, 9), above the x-axis, so the parabola never crosses it

The dartboard pair is our favorite way to teach independence with a geometric model, as Utah suggests. Because the circle is centered, knowing the dart is on the left tells you nothing about whether it hit the circle. Move the circle off center and the two probabilities split apart.

The quarter-by-quarter order is our reading of Utah's six areas. Your school may teach similarity before probability or start with quadratics; in the first class the teacher maps our plan onto your child's actual calendar.

Moving schools

Is Integrated Math 2 the same as Geometry?

No. It mixes the quadratic algebra of a traditional Algebra 2 year with part of a traditional Geometry year. Here is what a transferring student usually needs, in our experience.

Common gaps when a student switches pathway around grade 10 (our teaching notes, not an official list)
Coming fromGoing intoUsual gap to close
Algebra 1Integrated Math IICongruence by transformations and descriptive statistics from Integrated Math I
Algebra 1 and GeometryIntegrated Math IIComplex numbers and some probability; much of the geometry repeats
Integrated Math IGeometryLittle; but quadratics will be needed before Algebra 2
Integrated Math IIAlgebra 2Polynomials beyond degree 2, logarithms, and trigonometry beyond right triangles

Ask for the course map early

Each district publishes or can share its scope and sequence. Comparing it with the old school's course is the quickest way to find gaps.

A gap of one unit usually closes in three or four lessons when it is found at the start of the year.

Before Integrated Math III

Integrated Math III builds on quadratics with polynomial, rational and radical functions, so the forms of a quadratic need to be secure by spring.

Utah lists Secondary Math II as the prerequisite for its alternative math courses, which shows how much later work depends on this year.

Questions families ask

Integrated Math 2: common questions

What do you learn in Integrated Math 2?

Integrated Math II centers on quadratic expressions, equations and functions. It also introduces complex numbers, conditional probability and counting, similarity with right triangle trigonometry, and circles, as Utah's Secondary Mathematics II course description sets out.

Is Integrated Math 2 the same as Geometry or Algebra 2?

Neither. It combines the quadratics usually found in Algebra 1 and Algebra 2 with the similarity, trigonometry and circle work of a Geometry course, so a student switching pathways may meet some topics twice and miss others.

Is Integrated Math 2 hard?

In our teaching, students often find it the most demanding of the three, because so much depends on factoring and completing the square. When those two skills are secure, the rest of the course becomes far more manageable.

Why are complex numbers in Integrated Math 2?

Because some quadratic equations have no real solutions. Utah says real and complex numbers are introduced "so that all quadratic equations can be solved", and the graph of such a quadratic never crosses the x-axis.

What grade is Integrated Math 2?

Usually grade 10, after Integrated Math I in grade 9. Accelerated students may take it in grade 9, and some districts place it in grade 11.

Where can I find Integrated Math 2 practice tests?

Start with the student's textbook and teacher, since tests follow the school course. This page has twelve practice problems we wrote, with answers. We do not copy end-of-course test items or publisher answer keys.

Can you help with proofs in Integrated Math 2?

Yes. Utah says the similarity unit is where students develop facility with geometric proof. We teach how to choose the right criterion and set out each reason, then the student writes their own proof.

Will you do my child's Integrated Math homework?

No. We teach the method and check the student's reasoning, but graded work has to be their own. That way they can handle the test, where no tutor is present.

When are your Integrated Math 2 classes?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays.

Are you part of a school district or textbook company?

No. We are an independent online school with no link to any state, district or textbook publisher. Course names and documents on this page belong to their owners.
