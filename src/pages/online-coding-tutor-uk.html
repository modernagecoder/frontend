Online coding tutor in the UK

On 21 January 2026 something changed that most parents looking for a tutor have not yet heard about. Until then, a self-employed tutor could not apply for their own DBS check, because the system was built around organisations doing the applying. Now they can, and guidance for parents from the Government, updated in September, tells them how to ask. That turns choosing a tutor from a matter of price and reviews into something with a checkable step in it. This page explains the change, sets out the four very different things the word tutor covers in the UK, and then states our own position, which includes a limitation we would rather say out loud than let you discover halfway through.

In short

The word tutor covers four different arrangements in the UK and they are not equivalent. A self-employed tutor a parent finds and pays directly has, since 21 January 2026, been able to apply for an Enhanced DBS check and can be asked to share it. An agency, a platform and a teaching company each sit differently again. We are the fourth kind: a company whose teachers are employed and work from India. GOV.UK states that DBS "cannot access criminal records held overseas", so a UK check on our teachers would show nothing and we do not claim one. What we offer instead is lessons on a link a parent can join at any time, small groups as the default, work saved where a family can see it, and a named teacher who stays the same. The first lesson is free; after that a group place is USD 100 a month and one-to-one teaching USD 150.

What changed in January

A self-employed tutor can now be checked, and asked

This is recent enough that many tutors have not caught up with it either. Everything below is quoted from GOV.UK.

The DBS system was built around organisations. An employer applied, the certificate came back, the employer decided. A tutor working for themselves had nobody to apply on their behalf, which left a whole category of people working with children who could not be checked even if they wanted to be.

Guidance published on 16 January 2026 changed that, and states that it "is live from 21 January 2026". Self-employed people and personal employees can now apply for an Enhanced DBS check, and where the role is eligible, one that includes the children's barred list, through a DBS Umbrella Body.

In September the Government updated its leaflet for parents, and it goes as far as scripting the conversation. Its suggested wording is: "Before we go ahead, I'd love to see your DBS certificate. I understand you can now apply for an Enhanced one - would you be happy to share it?"

The leaflet's own worked example is a maths tutor, where a parent asks about qualifications, experience and references, and the tutor also chooses to share an Enhanced with Children's Barred List certificate.

What a parent can and cannot do, from the GOV.UK leaflet
QuestionThe answer
Can I apply for a check on my tutor?No. "Parents and carers cannot apply for a DBS check on behalf of an individual. Where someone is eligible for an Enhanced DBS check, they must apply for it themselves through a DBS Umbrella Body."
Can I ask to see one?Yes, and the leaflet suggests the words to use.
What if it shows a bar?"If a certificate shows that someone is barred from working with children or vulnerable adults, they are legally prohibited from carrying out certain types of regulated activity with that workforce."
Does a check cover everything?No. GOV.UK states that DBS "cannot access criminal records held overseas, so a DBS check may not provide a complete view of an applicant's criminal history, if they have lived outside of the UK."
Is a private arrangement regulated activity?Not necessarily. Regulated activity "excludes any activities carried out in the course of family relationships or as part of any personal, non-commercial relationships."

None of this is legal advice and we are not qualified to give any. It is a pointer to what the Government has published, with links below, because a parent who knows the question exists is in a better position than one who does not.

Sources, read 20 September 2026: DBS checks for self-employed people and personal employees, published 16 January 2026; A guide for parents and carers employing self-employed workers or personal employees, updated 17 September 2026; and DBS check requests: guidance for employers, updated 11 September 2026.

Four kinds of tutor

The same word, four different arrangements

When a parent types online coding tutor, four quite different things come back, and the questions that matter are not the same for each.

What you are actually dealing with
ArrangementWhat it isThe question that matters most
A self-employed tutorOne person, found and paid directly by the familyWould you share your DBS certificate, and can I take a reference from a family you teach now?
An agencyA business that matches you to a tutor and handles the moneyWho employs the tutor, who checked them, and what happens if the match does not work?
A marketplace or platformA listing site where individuals advertise and you chooseWhat has the platform verified itself, as opposed to what a tutor has typed into a profile?
A teaching companyAn organisation with its own teachers, curriculum and lesson structureWho exactly will teach my child, will it be the same person each week, and can I see the lessons?

There is a second difference that matters more for coding than for most subjects. A maths tutor can usefully work on whatever the school set this week. A coding tutor who only does that produces a student who can finish homework and cannot start anything, because programming skill is cumulative and homework is not.

The useful shape for coding is a sequence that goes somewhere, with the school's work handled inside it rather than instead of it. That is a class, even when it is delivered to one person.

So the questions worth asking a coding tutor are slightly different. What will my child be able to build in three months that they cannot build now? What happens in a lesson when their code does not work, and who does the typing? Will anybody read the code, or only the output?

Our answers to those are on how we teach, and the seven checks on the choosing a class page apply to a tutor just as well.

One more practical point. Hourly tutoring is priced by the hour and adds up quickly, while a monthly class is priced by the month whether the learner takes one lesson or three. Neither is better, but they suit different families, and a parent should know which one they are buying before the first invoice.

Our honest position

What we are, and the check we cannot offer

This section exists because we would rather lose a booking here than have a parent find out later.

We are a teaching company. Our teachers are employed by us and they work from India. That has real advantages, including a fixed timetable that never shifts with British clock changes and small groups that actually fill at a sensible hour, and it has one clear limitation.

A DBS check searches records held in the United Kingdom. GOV.UK states plainly that DBS "cannot access criminal records held overseas, so a DBS check may not provide a complete view of an applicant's criminal history, if they have lived outside of the UK". For a teacher who has always lived in India, a UK check would return nothing and would mean nothing. We therefore do not claim DBS-checked teachers, and any coding company with overseas staff that does should be asked exactly what it checked.

What we do instead is structural rather than documentary, and a parent can verify all of it in the first week.

Lessons happen on a link. A parent can join any lesson at any time without asking, and we never treat that as an intrusion. Small groups are the default rather than one-to-one, so a lesson is rarely one adult and one child. Every teacher is named before the first lesson and stays the same. Work is saved where the family can open it, which means you can see what was actually done. And for younger children we ask that an adult is in the house and reachable.

Questions about coding tuition in the UK

What UK families ask before booking a tutor

Are your teachers DBS checked?

No. Our teachers work from India, and GOV.UK states that DBS cannot access criminal records held overseas, so a UK check would return nothing meaningful. We would rather say that than use wording that sounds like a yes.

What changed for tutors in January 2026?

Guidance live from 21 January 2026 allows self-employed people and personal employees to apply for an Enhanced DBS check, and where eligible one that includes a barred list, through a DBS Umbrella Body. Before that, a self-employed tutor had no route to apply.

Can I apply for a check on a tutor myself?

No. The Government leaflet states that parents and carers cannot apply on behalf of an individual, and that an eligible person must apply themselves through an Umbrella Body. You can ask to see the certificate.

How should I ask?

GOV.UK suggests the wording itself: ask to see the DBS certificate and note that they can now apply for an Enhanced one. Most tutors will not mind being asked.

Can I sit in on my child's lesson?

Yes, any lesson, on the same link, without telling us beforehand. We treat that as completely normal.

Is it one-to-one?

Only if you want it to be. The default is a small group of five to ten learners at the same level, which most children find better than a one-to-one hour.

Do you charge by the hour?

No, by the month. A group place is USD 100 a month and one-to-one teaching USD 150, with nothing in advance and no minimum term.

Will you just help with homework?

We will handle school work inside a sequence that goes somewhere, but a tutor who only does this week's task produces a learner who can finish and cannot start. We say so at the outset.

Do you cover the Scottish, Welsh and Northern Irish courses?

Yes. Teachers work with National 5 and Higher, WJEC in Wales and CCEA in Northern Ireland as well as the English boards.

What if it is not working out?

Tell us and stop. There is no notice period and nothing has been taken in advance. We would rather you left after a month than stayed unhappily.
