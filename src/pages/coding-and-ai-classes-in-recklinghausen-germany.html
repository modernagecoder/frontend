One craft, three depths

A seven year old learns that a computer follows instructions exactly, in order, and that a loop saves writing the same step twenty times. A teenager learns the same idea when a program reads ten thousand rows of data. An adult learns it again when a script renames four hundred files that used to take an afternoon.

That is why one family can share the same mentor style without sharing a class. Each learner is grouped with people of a similar age and level.

Thinking before typing

We teach learners to work out a problem before they ask any tool to solve it: what is the input, what should come out, how would you check the answer by hand? AI tools are part of the course for the ages that may use them, but a learner who cannot check an answer has not learned anything from it.

Every learner on this page, from Scratch to machine learning, writes and runs their own code in class. The mentor guides; the learner drives.

NRW schools

What do schools in Recklinghausen teach about Informatik?

More than many parents remember from their own school days, though not much of it after grade 6 unless a pupil chooses the subject. Recklinghausen schools follow the rules of North Rhine-Westphalia.

Compulsory in grades 5 and 6

The Gesellschaft für Informatik sums up the NRW rule in its Informatik-Monitor 2026/27: Informatik is a compulsory subject in grades 5 and 6 at every type of general school. So a Recklinghausen child meets the subject soon after leaving Grundschule, whichever secondary school they attend.

From grade 7 on, Informatik is something a pupil opts into; at a G9 Gymnasium it can be picked as a Wahlpflicht subject in grades 9 and 10. In the Oberstufe it can be taken as a Grundkurs of three hours a week or a Leistungskurs of five hours in the Qualifikationsphase, where a school offers it.

The exam is written on paper

The written Informatik Abitur in NRW is set centrally, and the same monitor notes that no computer is used to answer the written tasks. A pupil has to read, trace and write code by hand, which rewards understanding over trial and error at a keyboard.

Across Germany only about 15 percent of pupils take Informatik in the Qualifikationsphase, the monitor estimates. Most young people in Recklinghausen will therefore leave school with two years of compulsory Informatik and no more.

A Recklinghausen data project

How powerful is a public charging point in Recklinghausen?

The Bundesnetzagentur publishes a register of public charging stations for electric cars, free to reuse under a Creative Commons licence. We use the Recklinghausen rows to teach a trap that hides inside the word "average", and that matters just as much when an AI model is scored.

The data

The register is called the Ladesäulenregister. The file we downloaded on 3 November 2026 is dated 1 September 2026 and lists every charging station whose operator has completed the Bundesnetzagentur's reporting process, so the true number in town can be higher. The first ten lines are notes; the column names only start on line 11.

We kept the 109 stations whose place is given as Recklinghausen, all marked as in operation, and saved four columns from them as recklinghausen_charging.csv: the kind of station, the number of charging points, the rated power in kilowatts and the date it went into service.

The question

A station can have one charging point or several. Its rated power is for the station as a whole, so we share it evenly across its points, an assumption we make for this exercise and say out loud.

Then we ask: what is the average power of one charging point in Recklinghausen? There are two honest-looking ways to calculate it, and they give different answers. The task is to find out which one answers the question we asked.

The first number treats every station as one vote. A single-point station at 22 kilowatts counts exactly as much as a station with two fast points at 300 kilowatts. The second number treats every charging point as one vote, which is what the question asked for. The gap of almost nine kilowatts is not a rounding error: it appears because the big fast stations tend to have more points than the small ones, so giving each station one vote quietly shrinks them.

Data: Bundesnetzagentur, Ladesäulenregister, file of 1 September 2026, rows with place Recklinghausen, licence CC BY 4.0, attribution Bundesnetzagentur.de; downloaded 3 November 2026. Sharing a station's power evenly across its points, and every figure in the table, are our own work.

Look at the last column. Fast stations are a third of the stations but deliver 104.4 kilowatts per point, more than five times the normal ones. More stations went into service in 2023 than in any other year, 46 of them, and the single most common setup is one point at 22 kilowatts, found at 45 stations. The overall 56.2 sits between the two kinds, pulled towards the fast ones because they carry 74 of the 168 points. That is the whole lesson in one line: an average of group averages is only right if every group is the same size, and here they are not.

The same trap appears in machine learning, where it has a name. A model that sorts photos into classes can be scored by averaging its accuracy class by class, the macro average, or over every photo, the micro average. With uneven classes the two disagree, exactly as the stations did, and a report that does not say which one it used can make a weak model look strong.

  1. Children, 9 to 12. Two classes take a quiz: one of 10 pupils averages 8 points, one of 30 averages 5. Is the average pupil score 6.5? Work it out with counters before using any code.
  2. Teenagers. Run both versions, then write a third that weights each station by its number of points and check it gives 56.2.
  3. Adults. Find an average in a report at work and ask what each row stands for. Recalculate it the other way and see if the story changes.
  4. Going further. Group the stations by the year they went into service and compute the power per point for each year. Which years pull the average up?

Children build, not chat

Children under 13 learn how AI works by making tiny models in Scratch and Python: a program that learns which answer goes with which example. They need no account anywhere, and they see that a machine only knows what its examples showed it.

Teenagers train and test

Teenagers write models on real data and measure them honestly against a simple guess. Pupils who want a target can aim at the Bundeswettbewerb Künstliche Intelligenz, which accepts projects in German or in English. We are not connected with it.

Adults put AI to work

Adults learn to call AI services from their own scripts and, from 18, to use coding agents such as Claude Code, always reviewing and testing what the tool wrote. The skill is judging the output, not typing the prompt.

Younger children can also enter a free puzzle contest this month without writing any code: the Informatik-Biber runs from 9 to 20 November 2026 for grades 3 to 13 and is entered through the school. Our Biber practice page has original puzzles in the same spirit, and the learn to think, not copy AI page explains how we keep AI from doing a learner's thinking.

Coding and AI classes in Recklinghausen: common questions

Are the classes in German?

No, never. We teach only in English, live, and the mentor adjusts speed and pronunciation to the learner. Python and Scratch are built from English words anyway, so learners in Recklinghausen pick up the language of code and the language of the lesson together.

How young can a learner start?

Six. Children of 6 to 8 start with Scratch blocks, which need no typing, and most move to typed Python at about 9. There is no upper age limit for the adult courses.

Can a parent and a child both learn?

Yes, but in separate classes. Children, teenagers and adults are grouped by age and level, so each learns at the right depth. Families often book the free first classes on the same day.

Do schools in Recklinghausen teach coding already?

Yes, briefly. The Gesellschaft für Informatik reports that every general school in NRW has compulsory Informatik in grades 5 and 6. After that it is an elective, and only about 15 percent of pupils nationally take it in the Qualifikationsphase.

Will my child use ChatGPT in class?

Not under 13, because OpenAI sets 13 as its minimum age, and from 13 to 17 only with a parent's permission. Children learn how AI works by building small models in Python and Scratch, which needs no account.

When are classes in Recklinghausen time?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Children usually come in the late afternoon and adults in the early evening.

Where are the teachers?

Our mentors are based in India and teach live over video. India is four and a half hours ahead of Recklinghausen in winter and three and a half in summer, which is why German afternoons and evenings suit both sides.

Which currency do I pay in?

The page shows euro, worked out from the ECB reference rate, while the card or bank payment is made in US dollars. Because your bank does the exchange, its figure may move by a few cents. Nothing is charged for the first class.

Can an adult with no experience learn AI?

Yes. The adult route starts with Python from the first line and moves to data and AI services once the basics are secure. Most adults begin with something from their own work, such as a spreadsheet they update every week.

What is the charging point project?

An exercise on the Bundesnetzagentur register of public charging stations, using the 109 Recklinghausen stations in the file of 1 September 2026. It shows why an average of station averages gives 47.6 kilowatts per point while the true figure is 56.2.
