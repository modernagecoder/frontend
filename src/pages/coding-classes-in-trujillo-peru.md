# Coding classes in Trujillo, Peru

Trujillo is a city of universities, and many of its children will one day study engineering, computing or economics a short bus ride from home. This page follows that path from the first Scratch game in primaria to the first year at university, compares the two school days a Trujillo teenager may have, and ends with a project on the electricity bills of Trujillo province.

At a glance: Teaching language: Live classes in English only, at the learner&rsquo;s own pace; mentors speak clearly and match their speed and accent to the learner; City: Trujillo and all its districts; Ages: 6 to adult, in age groups; Teens: Python, Java, data, contests; Undergraduates: Java, DSA and Python; Times: Any hour, Peru time; Trial: One free class, no card. Rated 4.9 across 547 Google reviews.

## Where most Trujillo learners begin

A first course for children, one for secundaria students who want more than school gives, and one for those already at university.

- [Python and AI for Kids](/courses/python-ai-kids-masterclass): For 9 to 12 year olds starting from nothing, over 8 to 10 months. They type real Python from the first week: drawings, small games, then tiny AI experiments. Younger children start with Scratch first.
- [Problem Solving for Teens](/courses/problem-solving-dsa-masterclass-teens): Logic, data structures and algorithms for 13 to 18 year olds who already know a little code. The thinking behind olympiad problems and, later, university computing courses.

## Does a Trujillo school timetable leave room for coding?

It depends on the school day. Public secundaria in Peru runs on two models, the regular day (JER) and the full day (JEC), and neither includes a computing subject.

**Weekly teaching hours in each secundaria grade, two school-day models (CNEB)**

| Area | Regular day (JER) | Full day (JEC) |
|---|---|---|
| Matemática | 4 | 6 |
| Educación para el Trabajo | 2 | 3 |
| Total per week | 35 | 45 |
| Computing or programming | None | None |

Source: MINEDU, Currículo Nacional de la Educación Básica, plans of study for secundaria. One teaching hour lasts 45 minutes. Digital competency 28 is transversal across all areas rather than a subject.

### What the longer day changes

A student on the full day has ten more teaching hours a week, with six of mathematics instead of four. That is good for maths, and it also means late afternoons are taken. Families on JEC often prefer a coding class early in the morning or at the weekend.

Neither model gives programming its own hours. Some schools use their free-disposal hours for workshops, which differ from one school to the next.

### Where coding fits

We do not replace school maths, and we do not teach for school marks. What a weekly coding class adds is a place where the maths a student already knows turns into something that runs, and where a wrong answer shows up on the screen straight away.

That is also why secundaria students in La Libertad who enter the ONEM often enjoy coding: in 2026, the regional education office reported 177,797 secundaria students from 936 schools taking part in the first round.

### Before university

Admission exams are set by each university, in Spanish, and decided by the university alone. We do not prepare students for them. What we can give a student in 4° or 5° de secundaria is a solid year of programming and reasoning, so that a computing or engineering degree starts from strength.

Students who enjoy competition can also try the OPI, the Peruvian computing olympiad run by UTEC, which is free for every secundaria student in the country. Its 2025 rules allowed only C++ in the in-person final and banned generative AI for solving problems.

### During university

First-year computing courses at most universities move fast through variables, loops and objects. Undergraduates who fall behind in Java or data structures often need someone who explains the same idea three different ways; a live mentor can do that.

We teach alongside the degree, never instead of it, and we do not write students' assessed work. Our page for [university students in Peru](/coding-courses-for-university-students-peru) sets out the Java, Python and DSA courses in more detail.

## Who uses the electricity in Trujillo, and what does a typical home use?

Hidrandina, the company that supplies electricity in La Libertad, Ancash and Cajamarca, publishes every customer's monthly consumption as anonymised open data. Our teenagers and adults use one month of it to learn why an average can describe nobody.

### The data

The dataset is "Consumo energético de clientes Hidrandina", published by Hidrandina (Distriluz) on datosabiertos.gob.pe under an Open Data Commons Attribution licence (ODC-By), first released on 28 December 2022 and updated on 18 June 2026. We took the July 2025 file on 9 November 2026: 1,046,671 rows separated by semicolons, about 207 megabytes.

Each row is one supply, usually one home or business, with its tariff, district, a "cartera" column that the data dictionary explains as Común (residential) or Mayor (business), and the kilowatt-hours billed. Names, addresses and ID numbers are masked by the publisher, and we use none of them.

### The question

How much electricity does a home in Trujillo use in a month? The quick answer is to add up all the kilowatt-hours in Trujillo province and divide by the number of supplies.

Version 1 does that. It also reads the file one line at a time, which matters: a file of this size would fill a school laptop's memory if it were loaded all at once.

**Version 1: total, count and average** (Python)
```python
import csv

supplies = 0
total = 0.0
biggest = 0.0
with open("DatosAbiertos_consumohdna_202507.csv", encoding="utf-8-sig") as bills:
    for row in csv.DictReader(bills, delimiter=";"):
        if row["Provincia"] == "Trujillo":
            kwh = float(row["ConsumosCalculados"])
            supplies += 1
            total += kwh
            biggest = max(biggest, kwh)

print("Supplies in Trujillo province:", supplies)
print("Average per supply, kWh:", round(total / supplies, 1))
print("Largest single supply, kWh:", round(biggest))
```
It prints Supplies in Trujillo province: 298472, Average per supply, kWh: 186.8 and Largest single supply, kWh: 578105.

The largest supply used 578,105 kWh in one month, more than three thousand times the average. A few supplies like that pull the average up a long way. So the next step is to sort every supply from largest to smallest and ask what share of all the electricity the top 1% uses.

**Version 2: how concentrated is it, and what is a typical home?** (Python)
```python
import csv
import statistics

kwh = []
homes = {}
with open("DatosAbiertos_consumohdna_202507.csv", encoding="utf-8-sig") as bills:
    for row in csv.DictReader(bills, delimiter=";"):
        if row["Provincia"] == "Trujillo":
            value = float(row["ConsumosCalculados"])
            kwh.append(value)
            if row["Cartera"] == "C":          # C = comun (residencial)
                homes.setdefault(row["Distrito"], []).append(value)

kwh.sort(reverse=True)
top = len(kwh) // 100
share = 100 * sum(kwh[:top]) / sum(kwh)
print("Top 1%:", top, "supplies use", round(share, 1), "% of the kWh")
print("Middle supply of all:", statistics.median(kwh), "kWh")
for district in ["Trujillo", "La Esperanza", "El Porvenir", "Victor Larco Herrera"]:
    print(district, "middle home:", statistics.median(homes[district]), "kWh")
```
It prints Top 1%: 2984 supplies use 36.0 % of the kWh, Middle supply of all: 84.0 kWh, then Trujillo middle home: 107.0 kWh, La Esperanza middle home: 80.0 kWh, El Porvenir middle home: 59.0 kWh and Victor Larco Herrera middle home: 120.0 kWh.

**One month of electricity in Trujillo province, July 2025 bills (our calculation)**

| Group of supplies | How many | Share of all kWh |
|---|---|---|
| Largest 0.1% | 298 | 20.4% |
| Largest 1% | 2,984 | 36.0% |
| "Mayor" (business) customers | 1,044 | 24.6% |
| All supplies | 298,472 | 100% |

Data: Hidrandina (Distriluz), Consumo energético de clientes Hidrandina, datosabiertos.gob.pe, ODC-By. July 2025 billing period, Trujillo province only. Shares, medians and counts are ours. 21,646 supplies were billed zero kWh that month. The file also contains billed amounts, which we do not use or print.

Three numbers now tell the story. The average supply used 186.8 kWh, but the middle supply, the median, used 84. Fewer than three thousand supplies, mostly businesses and large sites, account for more than a third of the electricity. And once we keep only residential supplies and compare like with like, homes in different districts differ too: the middle home in Víctor Larco Herrera used 120 kWh, in El Porvenir 59. The file cannot say why, whether household size, appliances or something else, and a careful analyst says so rather than guessing.

1. **Ages 9 to 12, on paper.** Ten children have 2, 2, 3, 3, 3, 4, 4, 5, 5 and 200 marbles. Work out the average and the middle value. Which one describes a typical child?
2. **Ages 12 to 14, in Python.** Put those ten numbers in a list, sort it and print what share of all the marbles the top child holds.
3. **Ages 14 and over.** Change Version 2 to print the share used by the top 10% and the top 0.1%. Where does the curve flatten?
4. **Going further.** Count how many supplies in each district were billed zero kWh, and decide whether they belong in the "typical home" figure.
5. **University students and adults.** Write the two-sentence summary you would give a journalist, with no number that could mislead.

## At what time can a Trujillo learner have class?

At the hour you choose. Trujillo runs on Peru time all year, and the mentor fits the class around school, university or work.

**Peru time in Trujillo and the same moment in India (conversions only)**

| Trujillo | India | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 07:30 | 18:00 | Same date |
| 09:30 | 20:00 | Same date |
| 12:00 | 22:30 | Same date |
| 14:30 | 01:00 | Next date in India |
| 17:30 | 04:00 | Next date in India |
| 21:00 | 07:30 | Next date in India |

Time zones: America/Lima is five hours behind UTC and Peru has had no daylight saving since 1994 (IANA tz database; INACAL holds the legal hour). Asia/Kolkata is five and a half hours ahead of UTC. The difference is a steady ten and a half hours.

A student on the full school day might take class at 07:30 before leaving home; an undergraduate might prefer the evening after lectures; a working adult might pick a weekend morning. The slot you book stays put all year because neither country moves its clocks, and you always choose it in Trujillo time.

## Coding classes in Trujillo: questions and answers

### Are the classes in Spanish?

No. Our classes are taught in English only, live and online. Mentors keep their language simple, repeat when asked and adapt to how each learner speaks, so a Trujillo child still building their English can follow. A word in Spanish is fine; the mentor answers with the English word.

### Is there a coding school I can visit in Trujillo?

We have no building in Trujillo or anywhere in Peru. Every class happens live over video, so learners in La Esperanza, Huanchaco, El Porvenir or the centre join in exactly the same way from home.

### What age is best to start?

Any age from six. Young children start with Scratch and move to typed Python around nine; teenagers and adults with no experience start with Python in their own age group. It is never too late: some of our learners are in their sixties.

### My child is on the full school day (JEC). When could classes fit?

Many JEC families choose an early morning slot, before school, or a weekend. Any day, any time, flexible: choose a batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at the hour that suits your family.

### Can you help a student at the Universidad Nacional de Trujillo?

We teach Java, Python, data structures and algorithms alongside any degree, at the student's pace. We are not connected with the UNT or any other university, and we do not complete assessed work for students.

### Do you prepare students for university admission exams?

No. Admission exams are set in Spanish by each university, which alone decides who is admitted. A year of programming and problem solving does build the careful reasoning that any maths paper rewards.

### Is there a computing olympiad for Trujillo students?

Yes, the OPI, organised by UTEC and free for all secundaria students in Peru. Under its 2025 rules the final allowed only C++ and banned generative AI. We teach with our own problems and are not connected with the organiser.

### What does a month cost, and in what currency?

The monthly fee appears on this page in US dollars and is charged in dollars; your bank turns it into soles. Visitors from India see the fee in rupees. The trial class costs nothing and needs no card.

### Will my child get a certificate?

Courses that offer one end with a Modern Age Coders certificate of completion. It is ours alone, with no link to MINEDU, a university or a school, and it is not a Peruvian qualification.

### Can adults in Trujillo join?

Yes. Adults learn in adult groups, usually starting with Python and moving to data analysis, databases or AI coding agents, at hours that fit around work.
