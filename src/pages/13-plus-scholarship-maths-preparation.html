What is the 13 plus scholarship maths paper?

For many independent senior schools it is the maths paper of ISEB's Common Academic Scholarship, CASE. ISEB's mathematics specification describes it as one paper of 90 minutes and 100 marks, "based on the 13+ CE specification for the Additional Paper", the harder optional level of Common Entrance. ISEB says senior schools enter pupils for CASE, choose the papers and mark them, usually at the senior school, in sessions in February and May. Some schools instead pick scholars from Common Entrance results or set papers of their own. The Additional topics include Pythagoras' theorem, simultaneous and simple quadratic equations, algebraic proof, reverse percentages and standard form. What CASE is

What is the ISEB Common Academic Scholarship exam?

A set of scholarship papers from ISEB in nine subjects, maths among them, which senior schools choose, enter pupils for and mark themselves. The questions rest on the Common Entrance 13+ specifications.

ISEB's page for schools puts it simply: "We offer a series of Common Academic Scholarship (CASE) assessments in English, mathematics, science, history, geography, theology, philosophy and religion, French and Latin." It goes on: "Pupils sitting CASE assessments are entered by the senior schools they have applied to, who choose which papers pupils will sit and also mark the papers. Pupils will usually sit the exam at the senior school." That division of roles matters. ISEB writes the paper, but there is no national scholarship pass mark: each senior school reads its own candidates' scripts and decides on its own awards.

Not every school uses CASE

ISEB itself notes that "Some senior schools select their scholars from candidates who sit CE in the autumn or spring term." In those schools the Common Entrance papers, often including the optional Additional maths paper, do double duty.

Other schools write their own scholarship papers, or buy a computer test such as Cambridge Select Insight, which Cambridge offers in versions for 11+ and 13+ entry. The school's own admissions page is the only reliable guide.

How CASE fits the year

ISEB runs "two CASE sessions each year, in February and May". Common Entrance at 13+ has three sessions, in November, January and May or June.

ISEB asks senior schools to tell prep schools CASE results "as soon as possible, so that decisions can be made about whether a candidate still needs to be entered for the CE examinations". For some pupils a scholarship result settles the place before Common Entrance is sat.

Sources: ISEB, CASE at 13+ information for schools; ISEB, CE at 13+ information for schools; Cambridge Insight, entrance assessments; all read 31 October 2026. Modern Age Coders is not connected with ISEB, Cambridge or any school.

The papers

How does the scholarship paper compare with Common Entrance maths?

It is longer per mark, harder in content and lighter on help. The table sets ISEB's 13+ maths papers side by side, as its specification lists them.

ISEB 13+ mathematics papers (CE and CASE Mathematics specification, for examinations from November 2022 onwards)
PaperMarksTimeWho sits it
Foundation non-calculator and calculator100 each60 minutes eachFor support in individual circumstances, by agreement with senior schools
Core non-calculator and calculator100 each60 minutes eachSuggested for all candidates
Mental arithmetic test4010 minutesFoundation and Core candidates, the same test
Additional Paper10060 minutes, calculators allowedOptional, "to offer challenge to the more able"
Common Academic Scholarship10090 minutesScholarship candidates entered by a senior school

Two lines of the specification define the scholarship paper. First, its content: "The Common Academic Scholarship Examination (90 minutes) will be based on the 13+ CE specification for the Additional Paper." Second, its time. By our own arithmetic, 90 minutes for 100 marks gives about 54 seconds a mark, against 36 seconds a mark on a 60-minute, 100-mark Common Entrance paper. That extra half again of time per mark is not a gift. It is room for longer chains of reasoning, and the specification says so in its list of changes: "Expectation of greater quality of thought and working, rather than quantity of questions".

Less scaffolding

The specification promises "Questions with less scaffolding": candidates "will less often be led through problems, step by step". A scholarship question may ask for the final result and leave the route entirely to the pupil.

More puzzles

It also lists "An increased number of thought-provoking puzzles", with "More unfamiliar problems" placed "towards the end of papers to encourage candidates to explore and experiment".

Units and working

Under the same heading, ISEB adds "Writing units in answers" and "Increased testing of core skills (with fewer words)". Marks follow the working, so a bare answer is a risk.

Source: ISEB CE and CASE Mathematics specification (for teaching from September 2021, examinations from November 2022), read 31 October 2026. ISEB sells past papers through its own shop; we do not reproduce any.

Topics

What maths topics are on the 13 plus scholarship paper?

Everything at Core level plus a list of further topics ISEB reserves for the Additional level. A scholarship candidate should know both lists well enough to use them without prompting.

What is out

The specification's "What's OUT" list removes some things at Core and Foundation, among them bearings with map scale drawing, accurate constructions and drawing pie charts.

At Additional level it removes all of those except significant figures, and adds one more removal: "Solving inequalities". A scholarship candidate should not spend hours on inequalities for this paper.

Calculators

The specification says: "A simple scientific calculator is recommended. Calculators with more advanced features, such as graph plotting and solving equations, are not permitted." The Additional Paper allows calculators.

For the scholarship paper itself, check the instructions each senior school gives with the entry; we do not assume a rule ISEB's specification does not state.

Worked examples, written by us

What does a strong scholarship answer look like?

Three original problems at Additional level, each answered the way a marker hopes to read it: every step written, units given, proofs closed with Q.E.D. None comes from an ISEB paper.

1. A short algebraic proof

Prove that the sum of any three consecutive whole numbers is a multiple of 3. Let the numbers be n, n + 1 and n + 2. Their sum is 3n + 3, which equals 3(n + 1). Since n + 1 is a whole number, the sum is 3 times a whole number, so it is a multiple of 3. Q.E.D. The marks are in the second-to-last sentence: saying why 3(n + 1) is a multiple of 3, not just writing it.

2. Simultaneous equations in context

Four apples and three pears weigh 1 kg; two apples and five pears weigh 920 g. Find each weight. Let a and p be grams: 4a + 3p = 1000 and 2a + 5p = 920. Double the second: 4a + 10p = 1840. Subtract the first: 7p = 840, so a pear is 120 g. Then 2a = 920 minus 600, so an apple is 160 g. Check in the first equation: 640 plus 360 is 1000. Working in grams keeps every number whole.

3. Average speed is not the average of speeds

A cyclist rides 12 km at 24 km/h, then 12 km at 12 km/h. What is the average speed? Times first: 0.5 hours and 1 hour, so 24 km takes 1.5 hours. Average speed is 24 divided by 1.5, which is 16 km/h, not 18. Multi-part journeys are on the Additional list precisely because the tempting answer is wrong.

We set these as full written solutions, never as answer-only drills. A scholarship marker is reading for the chain of reasoning ISEB's specification asks for, so in lessons a correct answer with a missing step is treated as unfinished, and a proof without its closing sentence is sent back.

A plan

How should a pupil prepare for 13 plus scholarship maths?

Secure Core first, then Additional, then practise writing full solutions to unfamiliar problems. Our plan below runs across Years 7 and 8; adjust it to the senior school's dates.

  1. Find out how the senior school chooses scholars: CASE, Common Entrance with the Additional paper, its own paper, or a computer test. Note the session, February or May for CASE.
  2. Check Core level is secure with ISEB's specification as a checklist, because scholarship questions assume it without warning.
  3. Teach each Additional topic from the ISEB list, starting with algebra (rearranging, simultaneous equations, brackets), since it feeds proof and graphs.
  4. Add proof early. Short "show that" and "prove that" questions, closed with a box or Q.E.D. as the specification asks, build the habit of justifying.
  5. Work unscaffolded problems weekly: a question with the final demand only, no parts (a), (b), (c) to guide the route.
  6. In the final term, sit full 90-minute papers under time, then review the working line by line rather than just the score.

Where competition maths helps

Problems from junior maths challenges ask for the same willingness to explore and experiment that ISEB describes. They train pupils to try a small case, spot a pattern and then prove it.

Scholarship questions

What parents ask about 13 plus scholarship maths

What is the CASE exam?

CASE is ISEB's Common Academic Scholarship, a set of scholarship papers in nine subjects including mathematics. Senior schools enter pupils, choose which papers they sit and mark them.

How long is the 13 plus scholarship maths paper?

ISEB's specification lists the Common Academic Scholarship maths paper as one paper of 90 minutes worth 100 marks. Schools that set their own scholarship papers may use different lengths.

What is on the scholarship maths paper?

ISEB says it is based on the 13+ Common Entrance specification for the Additional Paper. That adds topics such as Pythagoras' theorem, simultaneous equations, simple quadratics, algebraic proof, reverse percentages and standard form to Core level.

When are CASE exams sat?

ISEB runs two CASE sessions each year, in February and May. Pupils usually sit them at the senior school that entered them.

Is scholarship maths harder than Common Entrance?

Yes. It uses the harder Additional content, and ISEB's specification promises less scaffolding and more thought-provoking puzzles. By our arithmetic it also gives about half as much time again per mark as a Core paper.

Do all schools use CASE for scholarships?

No. ISEB notes that some senior schools select scholars from pupils who sit Common Entrance, and others set their own papers or use a computer test. Check each school's admissions page.

Can I use a calculator?

ISEB recommends a simple scientific calculator and bans graph-plotting and equation-solving models. The Additional Paper allows calculators; for the scholarship paper, follow the senior school's instructions.

Where can I find past scholarship papers?

ISEB sells past Common Entrance and CASE papers through its own shop. We do not copy them; our lessons use problems we write at the same level.

When are lessons held?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. We confirm each time in UK time.

Can you guarantee a scholarship?

No one honestly can. Senior schools mark their own candidates and decide awards, often with interviews and other subjects too. We teach the maths and the written reasoning.
