Why diagnosis comes first

A Grade 10 student who loses marks on quadratics rarely has a quadratics problem. Much more often the trouble is fractions, negative numbers or the meaning of the equals sign, met years earlier and never fully learned.

A tutor who teaches this week's homework without finding that earlier gap can raise one test mark and leave the next unit just as hard. A tutor who finds it can make the next three units easier.

Why answers are the line we will not cross

Students now carry tools that produce a worked solution from a photo. A tutor who does the same thing in a nicer voice adds nothing.

Wherever we teach, the promise holds: a student leaves able to reason through a problem alone, never trained to copy a worked answer or an AI's output. In practice the student holds the pencil and the tutor asks the questions.

Centres: routine and a room

Worksheet centres and tutoring centres give a child a place to go and a fixed rhythm, which suits families who want math to happen at the same time each week.

Their limit is the hour itself. In a centre built on worksheets the method is in the paper; in a busy tutoring centre the tutor may be sharing the hour with several students at once.

Online: reach and attention

Online tutoring reaches every province equally and shares a screen, so the tutor sees each line the student writes. For a student in a town with no centre, it is often the only choice that includes a specialist.

The trade-off between an online class and online one-to-one is the same as in coding: a class gives company and a lower monthly fee; one-to-one follows your child's own course and pace.

What the quotes reveal

Read side by side, the providers' own words describe very different hours. Thirty minutes of worksheets a day, a ninety-minute enrichment class with an entry bar, two one-hour sessions a week at a centre, a live screen-share: each is a reasonable product for a particular child.

The mistake is choosing on the brand rather than on the hour. Ask what your child will actually be doing for most of each session.

What the quotes leave out

Few of the pages we read print a fee or a maximum group size. Those two facts are the easiest way to compare like with like, and the hardest to find.

What a tutor should do with these dates

In Ontario, a Grade 9 student writes the EQAO math assessment in the first weeks of 2027, and the result counts in the course mark. A tutor who starts in October 2026 has about ten weeks to work with; one who starts in January has almost none.

In Alberta, three tenths of a Math 30-1 mark rests on one exam. That changes what tutoring should look like in the last months: timed practice and exam habits, not only new content.

Why the prerequisite chain matters most

The Grade 12 university courses sit at the end of a chain. In Ontario, MCV4U needs MHF4U before it or alongside it, and MHF4U needs Grade 11 Functions. A gap left in Grade 10 shows up two years later in a course a university asks for.

University computer science programs make this concrete: Waterloo lists Advanced Functions and Calculus and Vectors for Ontario applicants, and the University of Toronto lists MCV4U for computer science at St. George.

Our gap finder

A six-question gap finder you can try at the kitchen table

We wrote these six questions to find where a math gap starts. They are ours, not from any test, and they are not a grade-level exam. Ask your child to answer aloud and explain each one. The explanation tells you more than the answer, and a tutor worth hiring should be able to use the result in the first session.

The gap finder: six questions, answers, and what a wrong answer usually reveals (ours)
QuestionAnswerA common wrong answerWhat the wrong answer reveals
1. Which is larger, 0.7 or 0.65? Why?0.7, because 7 tenths is 70 hundredths, more than 65 hundredths0.65, "because 65 is bigger than 7"Place value: decimals read as whole numbers. Usually from Grades 4 to 6.
2. What is 3/4 divided by 1/2? Explain without the "flip" rule.1 and 1/2: one half fits into three quarters one and a half times3/8, by multiplying straight acrossDivision as "how many fit", never learned; the rule was memorised without meaning.
3. A jacket is 20% off, then 10% off the sale price. Is that 30% off?No: 0.8 times 0.9 is 0.72, so 28% off in totalYes, 30%Percent as a fixed amount rather than a fraction of a changing whole. Grades 7 to 9.
4. Solve 2(x + 3) = 14, and say what each step does to both sides.x = 4: divide both sides by 2 to get x + 3 = 7, then subtract 3x = 5.5, from 2x + 3 = 14The distributive property, and the equals sign as a balance. Grades 8 to 9.
5. If y = 3x + 2, what happens to y when x goes up by 1?y goes up by 3, every time"It goes up by 5"Slope as a rate of change. This is the bridge to Grade 10 and 11 functions.
6. If f(x) = 2 to the power x, how does f(x + 1) compare with f(x)?It is double: f(x + 1) = 2 times f(x)"It is one more"Exponential growth confused with linear growth. Grades 11 and 12, MCR3U and MHF4U.

How to read the result. Stop at the first question your child cannot explain, even if they get the next one right. That first question is where a tutor should begin, whatever grade your child is in. A Grade 11 student who stumbles at question 3 needs a few weeks on fractions and percent before functions will feel easier; that is not a step backwards, it is the shortest route forward.

Using the gap finder in a trial session

Give a tutor the result before the first session, and ask what they would do with it. A strong tutor will start at the earliest shaky question, not at this week's homework.

Then watch for check two in action: ask the tutor to explain question 2 to your child without the flip rule. If they can, they can teach.

What the questions are not

They are not a test of intelligence, and they are not tied to one province's curriculum. Six questions cannot cover everything a grade teaches.

They are a quick way to see whether the problem is the newest topic or an older idea underneath it, which is the single most useful thing to know before paying for tutoring.

Why contests suit tutoring

CEMC is clear on its own pages: "Our contests are not tests or exams. There is no passing or failing score." That makes them a low-pressure stretch for a student who is bored in class.

Contest problems also expose the difference between knowing a procedure and understanding it, because the procedure is rarely the obvious one.

A note for Grade 12 students

Waterloo says the CSMC and Euclid are not required for admission to computer science, "but writing one of these contests is required for some scholarships offered by the Faculty of Mathematics."

The first month

What the first month with a new math tutor should look like

Our view of a good first month, in order. If a tutor's first month looks very different, ask why; there may be a good reason, and you should hear it.

  1. Session 1: diagnosis. A short check, such as the gap finder above, and a conversation about the school course, the next test date and what the student finds hardest.
  2. Sessions 2 to 3: the earliest gap. Work on the first idea the diagnosis found shaky, even if it belongs to an earlier grade, explained with pictures or small numbers before any drill.
  3. Sessions 4 to 5: bridge back to school. Use the repaired idea on this month's homework, so the student feels the difference in class, not only in tutoring.
  4. Sessions 6 to 7: the student explains. The student teaches a method back to the tutor, out loud, and solves an unfamiliar question with the tutor only asking questions.
  5. Session 8: a short written update to the family. What was found, what has improved, what comes next, and how it lines up with the next provincial checkpoint.

Signs it is working

Homework takes less time and causes fewer arguments. The student starts sentences with "because" when explaining an answer. They attempt a question before asking for help.

Marks often follow, but they lag. A month is long enough to see the habits change and usually too short to see a report card move. We make no promise about marks; we show you the work.

Signs to change tutor

The tutor does most of the writing. Every session is this week's homework and nothing earlier. Your child cannot say what they learned. The tutor cannot tell you what the diagnosis found.

Changing is not a failure. The right fit between a tutor and a student matters more than any format or brand.

Where a student starts

Four stages of understanding in math

Our stages, used to place a student in the first class.

Four stages of math understanding (ours)
StageWhat you hear the student say
1. Recall"I know you flip it, I just do not know why."
2. Reason"It works because dividing by a half asks how many halves fit."
3. Connect"This is the same idea as the slope question last week."
4. Extend"If the rule changed like this, the answer would be..." on a problem never seen before

For parents

Report cards measure answers, so a student can reach a good mark at stage 1 and fall when the course moves on. Stage 2 is what lasts.

Listen to how your child explains homework. The words "because" and "so" are the best sign that tutoring is working.

For students

When you check an answer, check the reason too. If you cannot say why a step is allowed, mark it and ask.

If you use an app that shows worked solutions, cover the solution, try the question, then compare. Copying the steps teaches you to copy.

Choosing a math tutor in Canada: questions and answers

What makes the best online math tutor in Canada?

We give checks rather than a ranking. A strong tutor diagnoses before teaching, explains why methods work, knows your province's course and its checkpoints, states the group size, prints the fee, can stretch a student who is ahead, and never hands over answers.

Is online math tutoring as good as in person?

For most students it can be, because the tutor sees every line the student writes on a shared screen. In-person centres offer routine and a room. Judge the hour, not the setting: who is doing the thinking for most of it?

Should my child have a 1-on-1 tutor or join a group?

One-to-one suits a student with a specific course, a gap to repair or an upcoming checkpoint. A small group suits a student who likes company and is roughly at grade level. We teach batches of 15 to 20 and one to one.

How do I know where my child's math gap really is?

Try the six-question gap finder on this page and stop at the first question your child cannot explain. That question, not the newest topic, is usually where tutoring should start.

Can a tutor help with EQAO in Ontario?

Yes, by building the skills the assessments measure rather than drilling the test. EQAO lists the Grade 3 and 6 assessments for 4 May to 8 June 2027, and the Grade 9 math assessment for 12 January to 5 February 2027, where results count in the course mark.

Can you help with Grade 12 math such as MHF4U, MCV4U, Math 30-1 or Pre-calculus 12?

Yes. We teach alongside the school course and do not grant credits. Our Ontario Grade 12 University Math course covers MHF4U and MCV4U, and our Western Canada course covers Pre-calculus 12 and Math 30-1.

Do you teach Quebec secondary math?

We teach the math behind the Secondary 4 and 5 options. Quebec's program describes three options, Cultural, Social and Technical, Technical and Scientific, and Science, and says all three allow entry to a pre-university program.

My child is ahead in math. What should a tutor offer?

Harder problems, not just faster worksheets. The CEMC contests are a good path: Gauss for Grades 7 and 8, Pascal, Cayley and Fermat for Grades 9 to 11, and Euclid in the final year, all written at school.

Is it a problem if my child uses apps that show worked answers?

Only if the answer is copied instead of checked. We ask students to try a question first, then compare with any app or AI answer line by line. ChatGPT needs a parent's permission for anyone under 18.

What times do you teach for families in Canada?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Every time is confirmed in your province's own clock.

How soon should we see results?

Habits change first: less time on homework, more explanations that start with because. Marks usually follow later. We do not promise marks; we send short written updates so you can see the work.

Are you the best math tutor in Canada?

We will not make that claim. We publish our group sizes and our monthly fee, teach live, start with a diagnosis, and invite you to judge the first class against the seven checks on this page.
