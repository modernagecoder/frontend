What the activities look like

The hourofai.org list mixes coding with AI ideas. Examples include Dance Party: AI Edition and Music Lab: Jam Session from CodeAI, a Minecraft activity where an AI helper needs a child’s guidance, MakeCode Bug Arena from Microsoft, and Scratch face sensing blocks.

CodeAI’s page also keeps the older description: the Hour of Code as a global movement reaching tens of millions of students in 180+ countries, with 100 one-hour tutorials in over 45 languages.

Why one hour is not enough

An hour-long tutorial is designed to succeed: every step is laid out, and the child gets a finished result. That is exactly what makes it a great start and a poor finish.

Real coding begins when a child has a blank screen and an idea of their own. The gap between those two is small, but it needs a few weeks of practice, which the rest of this guide plans out.

The loop habit

What is the first real coding skill after an Hour of Code?

Spotting repetition and writing it once, with a loop. Most hour-long tutorials introduce a repeat block; the step after is a child seeing, on their own, that their twelve blocks are really one pattern four times.

How many blocks a repeating pattern needs, with and without a loop (our counting)
PatternBlocks without a loopBlocks with a loop
A 3-step dance move, 4 times124 (one repeat block holding 3)
A 2-step square side, 4 times83
A 3-step move, 10 times304
Any k steps, n timesk times nk plus 1

Why the table matters

Without a loop, a pattern repeated ten times takes ten times the blocks, and fixing one step means fixing it ten times. With a loop, the program stays the same size however many times it repeats, and one fix fixes every repeat.

Children feel this the first time they change "4" to "40" and watch the program still work. That moment is worth more than another hour of tutorials.

The fencepost trap

Loops bring the first classic bug. To draw 5 squares in a row with a gap between each, a child writes "repeat 5: square, gap" and gets an extra gap at the end. Five squares need only four gaps, as five fence posts need four rails.

The fix is to ask, every time, "how many times does this really happen?" It is a habit, not a rule, and it saves real programmers too.

Worked example (ours). A child’s animation makes a character step, jump and spin, then do it again three more times: 12 blocks. Asked to find the pattern, they spot the step, jump and spin trio and wrap it in a repeat 4 block, leaving 4 blocks. Asked for a longer dance, they change one number to 8 and get 24 moves with no new blocks. Then we ask them to add a bow at the very end, once. If the bow goes inside the loop, the character bows 8 times, which is a fencepost bug they can see and fix by moving one block.

First Python

When should a child move from blocks to Python?

When blocks feel slow rather than hard, often around age 9 to 11. The first Python program should do something the child already understands from blocks, so that only the typing is new.

What changes, and what does not

The ideas are the same: a repeat block becomes for side in range(4), and a custom block becomes def square(size). What is new is spelling, spacing and colons, which is why this step should come after the ideas are secure.

A child who can explain the block version first usually types the Python version in one lesson.

Signs a child is ready

They build projects in Scratch without a tutorial, they make their own custom blocks, and they get frustrated dragging the same blocks again and again. Those are signs, not an age.

Younger children, around 6 to 8, are usually better served by more block projects first. There is no prize for typing early.

Practice set

Five practice puzzles after the hour

Original puzzles, written by us, for a child aged about 8 to 11 who has finished an hour-long activity. Parents can read the answers aloud and ask the child to explain.

  1. Find the pattern. A program has: move, turn, move, turn, move, turn, move, turn. How few blocks can it be? Answer: 3: one repeat 4 block holding move and turn.
  2. Change one number. That loop draws a square. What shape does repeat 3 with a turn of 120 degrees draw? Answer: a triangle, because three turns of 120 degrees make one full turn of 360.
  3. Fence posts. A garden needs 6 posts in a line. How many rails join them? Answer: 5. A loop that adds a rail after every post would add 6, one too many.
  4. Inside or outside? A cat should say "Ready!" once, then jump 5 times. Where does the say block go? Answer: before the repeat loop, outside it. Inside, the cat would say it 5 times.
  5. Count the work. A loop repeats 4 times and holds another loop that repeats 3 times. How many times does the inner block run? Answer: 4 times 3, which is 12.

How to use them at home

Let the child guess first, then try it in Scratch or a CodeAI activity to check. Being wrong and then seeing why is the whole point.

Five puzzles take about twenty minutes. Spread over a week, they keep the habit going after CSEdWeek ends.

Six weeks

Keeping it the child’s

The game in week 6 should be the child’s idea, even if it is simple. A small game they invented teaches more than a big one copied from a video.

Our teachers ask questions rather than giving block-by-block instructions, so the child does the thinking.

After six weeks

Children who want more move into longer projects, then into Python around age 9 to 11. See our coding milestones by age for what is typical at each stage.

Others are happy to pause. One good experience of building something is a fine result, and the door stays open.

Ask your child to show you what they made and explain one block. Explaining is how they discover what they understand.

A week is long for a seven-year-old. The sooner after the hour the next session happens, the more of the excitement carries over.

A follow-up lesson the next week, asking students to change three things in their hour’s project, moves a whole class from step one to step two.

After the Hour of Code: what people ask

Is the Hour of Code still a thing?

Yes, under a new name. CodeAI, formerly Code.org, says the Hour of Code is now the Hour of AI, and hourofcode.com now leads to hourofai.org. Classic coding activities are still listed alongside the new AI ones.

When is CSEdWeek 2026?

The hourofai.org page gives CSEdWeek as December 7 to 11, which is Monday to Friday in 2026. CSEdWeek is run by the Computer Science Teachers Association, and its site highlights December 9, Grace Hopper's birthday.

What should my child do after the Hour of Code?

Keep building within a week, ideally something of their own. Scratch is free and suits ages about 8 to 12, and CodeAI offers free courses by grade, such as Computer Science Fundamentals for grades K to 5.

Is Code.org now called CodeAI?

Its website now uses the name CodeAI and says "Code.org" is a former trademark of CodeAI. The site address code.org still works and leads to the CodeAI pages.

What free coding courses are there for middle schoolers?

CodeAI lists Computer Science Discoveries for grades 6 to 10 and AI Discoveries for grades 6 to 8. Its student page also points ages 11 and up to App Lab, Game Lab and Web Lab.

What is the first coding skill to learn after the hour?

Loops: spotting a repeated pattern and writing it once. A 3-step move repeated 4 times is 12 blocks without a loop and 4 with one, and the loop version stays small however many repeats you add.

When should a child start Python?

When blocks feel slow rather than hard, often around age 9 to 11. The first Python program should repeat something they already built in blocks, so only the typing is new.

Are Hour of AI activities safe for young children?

Many are designed for classrooms from kindergarten up, but some use AI tools with their own age rules. Check each activity's teacher notes and the tool's terms before a child uses it at home.

Do you offer paid classes after the Hour of Code?

Yes. We teach live online coding classes for children aged about 6 to 12, in a group of 15 to 20 or one to one, and the first lesson is free.

When are your classes?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays.

Are you part of CodeAI or the Hour of AI?

No. We run our own classes and have no link to CodeAI, CSTA, the Scratch Foundation or any Hour of AI partner; their names and marks belong to them.
