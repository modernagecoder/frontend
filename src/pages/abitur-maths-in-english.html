Exam structure

How is the German Abitur maths exam structured?

Since 2017 the Länder have shared a common pool of Abitur tasks in maths, coordinated by the IQB, the Länder's institute for quality in education at Humboldt-Universität in Berlin. The structure below is the IQB's own description of the pool tasks.

Two parts, one exam

Every pool exam has two parts. In Part A (Prüfungsteil A) no aids are allowed: no calculator and no formula booklet. In Part B (Prüfungsteil B) the aids your Land permits may be used. Both parts contain tasks from all three areas: Analysis, Analytische Geometrie or Lineare Algebra, and Stochastik.

Part A is a set of short, unconnected tasks. Part B has longer tasks split into connected subtasks, and some of those subtasks can still be ones where aids give no real advantage.

You choose when to switch

At the start you receive both parts. You decide when to hand in Part A and receive your aids, but you must do so within the first 110 minutes at raised level (Leistungskurs) or the first 100 minutes at basic level (Grundkurs). The IQB says all Länder apply this rule from the 2025 exams.

That one rule shapes good preparation: Part A has to be fast and safe without a calculator, so that time is left for the long Part B tasks.

Each Land decides through its own commission which pool tasks it uses and how many of its own tasks it adds, so check your school's information for the exact rules in your Land.

Classes are in English, but the paper your school sets will use German terms. Every class pairs the English idea with the word you will read in the exam.

Most marks lost by pupils who think in English are not lost on the maths. They are lost on the operator: a pupil who is asked to "begründen" and writes only a correct number has not answered the question. We practise reading the operator first in every task.

Practice

Three Part A style tasks, solved in English

Our own tasks in the style of Part A: short, no calculator, one per area. They are not real Abitur tasks; the IQB publishes those after each exam year.

1. Analysis (Analysis)

Task. Given f(x) = x³ − 6x² + 9x. Bestimmen Sie the points where the graph has a horizontal tangent, and decide their type.

Solution. f'(x) = 3x² − 12x + 9 = 3(x − 1)(x − 3), so f'(x) = 0 at x = 1 and x = 3. f''(x) = 6x − 12 gives f''(1) = −6 < 0, a maximum, and f''(3) = 6 > 0, a minimum. With f(1) = 4 and f(3) = 0: high point H(1 | 4), low point T(3 | 0).

Where marks go. Many pupils stop at "x = 1 and x = 3". The question asks for points, so the y values are part of the answer.

2. Stochastics (Stochastik)

Task. A bag holds 3 red and 2 blue balls. Two are drawn without replacement. Berechnen Sie the probability that both are red, and that at least one is blue.

Solution. P(both red) = 3/5 · 2/4 = 6/20 = 3/10. "At least one blue" is the complement: 1 − 3/10 = 7/10.

Where marks go. "Mindestens ein" (at least one) is almost always quickest by the complement. Pupils who list every case run out of Part A time.

3. Geometry (Analytische Geometrie). Given A(1 | 0 | 2) and B(3 | 2 | 1). Task: berechnen Sie the length of AB, and prüfen Sie whether P(5 | 4 | 0) lies on the line g through A and B. Solution: AB = (2, 2, −1), so |AB| = √(4 + 4 + 1) = √9 = 3. The line is g: x = (1, 0, 2) + t · (2, 2, −1). For P, the first coordinate gives 1 + 2t = 5, so t = 2; then 0 + 2 · 2 = 4 and 2 − 2 = 0 also match, so P lies on g. Where marks go: checking only one coordinate. A point is on the line only if one value of t fits all three.

  1. Read the operator first. Underline berechnen, bestimmen, zeigen or begründen before you read the rest.
  2. Give the full object. A point has two or three coordinates; a probability is a number between 0 and 1.
  3. Check with a second route. In task 2, 3/10 + 7/10 = 1 is a one-second check.

Abitur maths in English: common questions

Can I get Abitur maths tutoring in English?

Yes. Our Abitur Mathematik in English course is live online tutoring for Leistungskurs and Grundkurs pupils across Germany, taught in English only. Mentors match your pace and accent and give every key term in German too, because your exam paper will use German words.

How long is the Abitur maths exam?

In the common pool of the Länder, the raised-level exam (Leistungskurs) lasts 300 minutes and the basic-level exam (Grundkurs) 255 minutes, including choice time. If a Land offers a choice of tasks in Part B, the IQB allows 30 minutes more.

Is a calculator allowed in the Abitur maths exam?

Not in Part A. The IQB pool exam has a Part A without aids and a Part B where the aids your Land permits are allowed. You hand in Part A within 110 minutes at raised level or 100 minutes at basic level, then receive your aids.

How many marks is each topic worth?

At raised level, Part A carries 30 of 100 marks; in Part B, Analysis carries 30, Analytische Geometrie or Lineare Algebra 20 and Stochastik 20. At basic level the figures are 25, 25, 15 and 15, out of 80.

How many marks do I need for 10 Notenpunkte?

In the IQB points table, 10 Notenpunkte need at least 70 % of all marks, 15 need 95 %, and 5 need 45 %.

When is the maths Abitur in 2027?

In Nordrhein-Westfalen, the written maths exam for Leistungskurs and Grundkurs is on Wednesday 5 May 2027, starting at 9:00. Other Länder publish their own dates; since 2022 maths has been held largely on common dates across the Länder.

Is the Abitur the same in every Land?

Not exactly. The Länder share a common task pool, but each Land's own commission decides which pool tasks it uses and how many of its own it adds. We prepare you for the rules of your Land and your school.

Do you use real Abitur tasks?

For practice we write our own tasks in the pool style, and pupils also work through the past pool tasks the IQB publishes after each exam year, which include the expected answers. We never present a real task as our own.

Who are the tutors?

Our mentors are based in India and teach live online in English. You see the same mentor every week, at times that fit German afternoons or weekends.

What does it cost?

Fees are monthly and shown on this page in euro at the European Central Bank reference rate. The payment is taken in US dollars and converted by your bank. The first class is free and needs no card.
