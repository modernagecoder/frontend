The contest

What is the Noetic Learning Math Contest?

Noetic Learning describes the NLMC as "a semiannual problem-solving competition for elementary and middle school students." Students solve 20 problems on their own in 45 minutes, or 50 minutes online, and each grade from 2 to 8 has its own problems.

Two and a quarter minutes a problem

Twenty problems in 45 minutes is 135 seconds each, or 2 minutes 15 seconds, by our arithmetic. Online, 50 minutes gives 150 seconds, or 2 and a half minutes. That is enough to read a word problem twice and draw it, but not enough to puzzle aimlessly.

For a second grader, that pace is a real stretch. Practice is less about speed than about having a reliable first move on every problem, which is where bar models help.

One grade, one problem set

Because "Each grade has its own problem set", a third grader competes only with other third graders nationally. Mixed-grade teams are allowed when necessary, but Noetic says they "must register under the highest student’s grade level", so a grade 4 student on a grade 5 team sits the grade 5 problems.

Check which level your child will sit with the team leader before practicing.

Awards

What awards does the Noetic math contest give?

The top 10 percent of participants in each grade earn a National Honor Roll medal, and the top 50 percent a National Honorable Mention ribbon. Each team’s highest scorer gets a Team Winner medal, and every participant gets a digital certificate.

How the team score works, with our numbers. Noetic says "The team score is calculated using the sum of the top six scores of a team". Suppose a grade 4 team of nine students scores 17, 15, 15, 14, 12, 12, 10, 9 and 6 out of 20. The team score uses only the top six: 17 + 15 + 15 + 14 + 12 + 12 = 85. The other three scores do not lower it. That is why Noetic notes that a team of fewer than six can still enter but weakens its chance at the team plaque, while individual awards are unaffected.

A reachable goal for most children

Half of each grade receives the ribbon. For a child entering a contest for the first time, that is a fair target that rewards steady practice rather than rare talent.

The medal for the top 10 percent is a stretch goal for children who already enjoy hard problems.

What we do not promise

We cannot promise a ribbon or medal, because awards depend on every student in the grade nationally. We teach the thinking and share honest progress with families.

A child who leaves the season able to draw any comparison problem as bars has gained something that lasts well beyond one contest.

At-Home Edition

Can my child take the Noetic contest if the school does not offer it?

Yes, through the At-Home Edition, an online version with its own window after each school contest. The parent acts as team leader. At-Home scores are not official and do not qualify for awards, but students receive a raw score and a national percentile.

  1. Ask the school first. Noetic’s advice to parents is to check with teachers and administrators. If the school hosts the contest, ask how your child can join the school team.
  2. Share the invitation letter. If the school does not take part, Noetic suggests sharing its NLMC invitation letter. Many participating teachers heard of the contest through parents.
  3. Register for the At-Home Edition if a school team is not possible. A parent registers as the team leader on the At-Home registration page.
  4. Give the contest in the window: November 27 to December 4, 2026 for fall, or April 16 to 23, 2027 for spring, online.
  5. Read the feedback. The raw score and national percentile show where the child stands; Noetic issues no medals or ribbons for this edition.

Why the percentile is useful

Noetic says the At-Home Edition gives students "valuable feedback, including their raw score and national percentile." For a homeschooled child or one whose school does not enter, that comparison is hard to get any other way.

Use it as a snapshot, not a verdict. A second snapshot in spring tells you far more than the first one alone.

Running it fairly at home

Give the full 50 minutes online in a quiet room, with paper for drawing. No hints, no calculator unless the procedure allows one, and no looking up answers.

Afterwards, go through the problems your child missed by type, not one by one, and bring the types to class.

The bar model

How do bar models help with Noetic word problems?

A bar model turns the words of a problem into rectangles whose lengths show the quantities. Comparison, part-whole and fraction problems, which fill elementary contests, become a picture with one unknown piece. This is our main teaching tool for the NLMC.

Which bar model fits which problem (our teaching guide)
Problem typeCue wordsHow to draw it
Part and whole"Altogether", "in all", "the rest"One long bar split into parts
Comparison"More than", "fewer than", "times as many"Two bars, one under the other, with the difference marked
Equal shares"Each", "equally", "groups of"A bar cut into equal units
Fraction of"One third of", "the rest"A bar in equal units, some shaded
Before and after"Gave away", "then had"Two bars for before and after, with the change shown

Worked example 1 (ours, grade 3): comparison with a total. Ava and Ben have 60 stickers together. Ben has 12 more than Ava. How many does each have? Draw Ava’s bar, then Ben’s bar the same length plus an extra piece labelled 12. Take the 12 away: 60 minus 12 = 48 is two equal bars, so Ava has 24 and Ben has 24 + 12 = 36. Check: 24 + 36 = 60.

Worked example 2 (ours, grade 4): times as many. Mia read 3 times as many pages as Leo. Together they read 36 pages. How many more pages did Mia read? Leo is one unit and Mia is three, so four units are 36 and one unit is 9. Mia read 27, Leo 9, and the difference is two units, 18 pages. Notice the question asks for the difference: the bar shows it directly as two units, with no need to find both totals first.

Worked example 3 (ours, grade 5): fractions of the rest. A bag of marbles: one third are red, and one half of the rest are blue. The other 15 are green. How many marbles are in the bag? Draw a bar in three units; one is red. The remaining two units split in half: one unit blue, one unit green. So one unit is 15 and the bag holds 45. The phrase "of the rest" is the trap: one half of the whole would give a different, wrong picture.

Why bars, not equations

A second or third grader cannot yet write 2x + 12 = 60, but they can draw it. The bar model is algebra in pictures, and it scales: the same drawing works with bigger numbers and fractions in grades 4 and 5.

In a 2 minute 15 second budget, a quick bar is often faster than any other first move.

How a lesson runs

The teacher reads the problem, the student draws, and nobody calculates until the bars match the words. Most mistakes show up as a drawing that does not fit the story, which is far easier to fix than a wrong number.

Then the student writes a new story for the same drawing. If they can, they understand it.

Practice set

Ten original Noetic-style practice problems for grades 3 to 5

Written by our teachers. They are not NLMC problems and not drawn from any Noetic test. Try them at the contest pace: about 2 minutes 15 seconds each.

  1. Sam has 8 more marbles than Tia. Together they have 50. How many does Tia have?
  2. A rope is cut into 3 pieces. The second is twice the first and the third is 3 times the first. The rope is 42 feet long. How long is the third piece?
  3. Jo spent one quarter of her money on a book and had 18 dollars left. How much did she have at first?
  4. There are 24 children in a class. There are 4 more girls than boys. How many girls are there?
  5. What is the sum of all the even numbers from 2 to 20?
  6. A box holds 6 rows of 7 cupcakes. 15 are eaten. How many are left?
  7. Four friends share 3 pizzas equally. What fraction of a pizza does each friend get?
  8. The perimeter of a square garden is 36 meters. What is its area?
  9. Pens come in packs of 6. How many packs are needed to give 1 pen to each of 40 students?
  10. Eli is 7. His mother is 5 times his age. How old will his mother be when Eli is 15?

Where students slip

Problem 9 catches students who divide 40 by 6, get 6 and something, and stop at 6. Problem 10 catches anyone who multiplies 15 by 5; the age gap stays the same, it does not scale.

Problem 3 tempts students to take a quarter of 18. The bar shows that 18 is three quarters, not the whole.

Moving on to official practice

After these, request Noetic’s free sample problems, or use the practice tests that come with the team registration, under contest timing.

In class we look at which type each missed problem was, and draw that type again with fresh numbers of our own.

For parents

At home, ask "can you draw it?" whenever your child is stuck on a word problem. It is the most useful question in elementary math.

Compare the fall and spring raw scores, but praise the drawings as much as the totals.

For students

Before you calculate, look at your drawing and the words side by side. Do they match?

If you finish early, check the problems where the question asked for "how many more".

Noetic math contest: what families ask

What is the Noetic Learning Math Contest?

It is a semiannual problem-solving competition for students in grades 2 to 8, run by Noetic Learning. Students solve 20 problems independently in 45 minutes, or 50 minutes online, and each grade has its own problems.

When is the Noetic math contest in fall 2026?

Noetic Learning lists the fall 2026 window as November 12 to 25, 2026, and the spring 2027 window as April 1 to 15, 2027. The team leader chooses the day within the window.

How many problems are on the Noetic math contest?

There are 20 problems, solved within 45 minutes on paper or 50 minutes online. By our arithmetic that is about 2 minutes 15 seconds per problem on paper.

How many students can be on a Noetic team?

A team has 30 or fewer students from the same grade and the same school, with no minimum. The team score is the sum of the top six scores.

What do you need to get a Noetic National Honor Roll medal?

National Honor Roll medals go to the top 10 percent of all participants in each grade. The top 50 percent receive a National Honorable Mention ribbon, and each team's highest scorer gets a Team Winner medal.

Can my child take the Noetic contest at home?

Yes, through the At-Home Edition, online, from November 27 to December 4, 2026 or April 16 to 23, 2027. Scores are not official and earn no medals, but students get a raw score and a national percentile.

Where can I get Noetic math contest practice problems?

Noetic Learning emails free sample problems on request and sells official past problems and solutions by grade. School teams also receive three practice tests with each registration.

Do you share Noetic past tests in class?

No. Noetic says its documents may not be distributed outside the buyer's home or classroom, so we teach with problems we write ourselves and help students review official sets they own.

What days and times are classes?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays.

Are you connected with Noetic Learning?

No. Modern Age Coders is an independent online school with no link to Noetic Learning or the NLMC. Families and teachers register for the contest with Noetic Learning directly.
