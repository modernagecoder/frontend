Are the Barr Al Hikman figures real? The coastline of over 160 km, the inland sabkha, the named boundaries and the nearly half a million waterbirds in winter are all published. The thirteen hourly areas in the project are teaching values, because no hourly survey was found, and the page says so wherever they appear.
Why is the average the worst single answer here? Because the minimum is genuinely true at high water and the maximum is genuinely true at low water, so each describes a real moment. The mean describes no moment in the cycle at all. It is still the figure most likely to be published, because it sounds balanced and it conceals that the question was incomplete.
Is there a Modern Age Coders centre in Mahout? No. No premises are claimed in Mahout or anywhere in Al Wusta. Classes are live online and need a laptop or desktop, working audio, a current browser and a stable connection. The page describes where the service reaches, not a building.
What is the right way to report a quantity like this? Give the range and name the phase. Four hundred and twenty square kilometres at mid ebb, or 140 at high water, are both usable statements. The area of Barr Al Hikman is not a statement at all until somebody adds a time to it.
Do you teach adults in Mahout as well as school students? Yes, up to 67. Adults work through Python, analytics and reporting. If a figure you report moves depending on what time the job runs, and somebody has already asked you why it changed, this is that problem. Bring the real numbers where they are yours to bring.
What does a learner take away from this? Measurements that always carry a timestamp, comparisons restricted to matching conditions, and a habit of publishing a range with its phase named instead of a single tidy figure. That survives any change of tooling.
