## A wilayat that changes size twice a day

Barr Al Hikman is flat, vast and tidal, which means its most basic property is not a constant but a schedule.

### A coastline measured in hundreds of kilometres

The peninsula carries a coastline of over 160 km bordered by an inland sabkha. On ground that flat, a small rise or fall in water covers or uncovers an enormous area, which is what makes the variation so large here and so small elsewhere.

### Birds that arrive on their own timetable

Barr Al Hikman is a haven to nearly half a million waterbirds in winter. A count taken in July and a count taken in January are both honest and describe different things, and neither is the number of birds.

### Bounded by four different waters

The peninsula borders the sands to the north, the Masirah Channel to the east, the Gulf of Masirah to the south and Hashish Bay to the southwest. Four boundaries described by four names is four chances for two datasets to draw the edge differently.

Some quantities are a function, not a value

The area of an intertidal flat is not a fact you look up. It is a curve over time. Recording it as a single number does not simplify the truth, it discards the only part of the truth that made the number meaningful.

## One hundred and forty square kilometres, or five hundred and twenty

The same place, the same week, the same method. Only the hour is different, and the answer changes by a factor of nearly four.

### 1. Measure the exposed flat every hour

Thirteen readings across one tidal cycle. The areas here are teaching values, since no hourly survey is published, but the behaviour is the ordinary behaviour of a tidal flat and the page says so.

### 2. Look at what the readings do

The exposed area runs from 140 square kilometres at the top of the tide to 520 at the bottom. That is a ratio of 3.7. Every one of the thirteen readings is correct at the moment it was taken.

### 3. Try to summarise it with one number

The mean across the cycle is 315 square kilometres. The flat is at that state in none of the thirteen hours. The average is not a compromise between the readings, it is a condition the place is never actually in.

| Measure | Value | True when? | What a single figure would do |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Smallest exposed area | 140 sq km | At high water only | Understate by 73 per cent |
| Largest exposed area | 520 sq km | At low water only | Overstate by 271 per cent |
| Ratio between them | 3.7 times | Always | The size of the problem |
| Mean across the cycle | 315 sq km | Never | Sound careful, describe nothing |
| Hours matching the mean | 0 of 13 | n/a | The average is not a state |
| A published single figure | any of them | about 8 per cent of the day | Be wrong the rest |

### The average is the answer that is wrong at every single moment

A minimum is true at high water and a maximum is true at low water, so both are at least honest about something. The mean is true at no point in the cycle at all. It is nonetheless the figure most likely to be published, because it sounds balanced and it hides the one fact a reader needed, which is that the question was incomplete. Every figure above was computed before this was written.

## Store the moment, then decide what you are actually asking

Five habits, none of them expensive, and each one keeps the clock attached.

| Stage | Rule | What it prevents | What it gives you |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Stamp | Never store a measurement without its time | A reading detached from its moment | A number that stays interpretable |
| Define | Say which state you mean, high, low or mean | Two teams comparing different things | A question with one answer |
| Range | Publish the span before publishing the average | A mean standing in for a curve | The size of the variation up front |
| Refuse | Do not average a cycle and call it the value | A state that never occurs | An honest summary or none |
| Match | Compare readings only at the same phase | A difference that is really a timing artefact | Comparisons that mean something |

Timestamps are mandatory on measurements, comparisons are restricted to matching conditions, and any quantity that varies on a cycle is reported as a range with its phase named rather than as a single figure.

### Why is the average the worst single answer here?

Because the minimum is genuinely true at high water and the maximum is genuinely true at low water, so each describes a real moment. The mean describes no moment in the cycle at all. ### What is the right way to report a quantity like this?

Give the range and name the phase. Four hundred and twenty square kilometres at mid ebb, or 140 at high water, are both usable statements. The area of Barr Al Hikman is not a statement at all until somebody adds a time to it.
