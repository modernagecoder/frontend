## When does a Munich child first get Informatik at school?

It depends on the school your child moves to after Klasse 4. The figures below come from the Bavarian ministry of education and the Gesellschaft für Informatik.

### Four years, then a fork

Bavarian Grundschule covers grades 1 to 4. Early in May of Klasse 4, the ministry says, every pupil receives the Übertrittszeugnis, the transfer report. What counts is the average mark in German, maths and Heimat- und Sachunterricht, and from there a child goes on to one of three school types: the Mittelschule, the Realschule or the Gymnasium.

For many Munich families that report is the first time school feels high stakes. It is also the reason this page talks about thinking skills before it talks about apps: a child who learns to break a problem into steps uses that habit in a maths word problem as much as in a program.

### Three school types, three timetables

The Informatik-Monitor 2026/27 of the Gesellschaft für Informatik lists Bavaria's computing hours by school type. At the Mittelschule, Informatik is compulsory for one lesson a week in every year from grade 5 to grade 10. At the Realschule, the subject is called Informationstechnologie and runs from grade 5 to grade 10, with a total load that depends on the branch the pupil chooses.

At the Gymnasium, computing starts inside Natur und Technik: one weekly lesson in grades 6 and 7 has an Informatik focus. The scientific and technological branch adds two hours of Informatik in grades 9 and 10, and in grade 11 every branch has two compulsory hours of computing.

**Compulsory computing in Bavarian secondary schools, school year 2026/27**

| School type | Subject | Grades | How much |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mittelschule | Informatik | 5 to 10 | One lesson a week in each grade |
| Realschule, branch I | Informationstechnologie | 5 to 10 | Eleven weekly hours in total |
| Realschule, branch II | Informationstechnologie | 5 to 10 | Eight weekly hours in total |
| Realschule, branch IIIa / IIIb | Informationstechnologie | 5 to 10 | Seven / nine weekly hours in total |
| Gymnasium, all branches | Natur und Technik, Informatik focus | 6 and 7 | One lesson a week |

Source: Gesellschaft für Informatik, Informatik-Monitor 2026/27, Bayern, compiled from the Mittelschulordnung, Realschulordnung and Gymnasialschulordnung; Bayerisches Staatsministerium für Unterricht und Kultus, Grundschule and Realschule pages. Read on 2 and 3 November 2026. The monitor's Bavarian tables begin at grade 5. The Bavarian Gymnasium has returned to nine years, so grade 11 is the Einführungsphase and the Abitur comes after grade 13.

Two details are easy to miss. The Realschule hours are totals spread over six years, not hours per week in every year, so the weekly dose is smaller than the headline number suggests. And since the exam round of 2025, the ministry says, every Realschule may offer a final exam in Informationstechnologie, which pupils take voluntarily. A child who arrives at grade 5 already comfortable with loops and variables starts these courses with confidence instead of catching up.

None of this is a reason to rush. Our classes for six to nine year olds do not try to teach the grade 7 syllabus early. They build the habits underneath it: following a sequence, spotting a pattern, testing an idea and fixing it when it fails.

## Are the classes in English, and can my child keep up?

Yes. All teaching happens in English, at a speed the mentor sets so that a child still learning the language never gets left behind.

### One language, said up front

We teach in English only and have no German-language classes. Munich families at international or bilingual schools find this natural; families at a German Grundschule often like the extra hour of spoken English that comes with the coding.

### A mentor who adjusts

Mentors keep sentences short, check that each step has landed, and say it a second way when a child looks unsure. They tune their ear to your child's accent within a few classes, and a German word in the middle of an answer is simply met with the English one.

### The screen does the explaining

Most of a lesson is shown, not told: the mentor builds, the child copies, changes and tests. Python keywords such as if, for and print are English words anyway, so a child picks up the language of code and of the class together.

Mentors work from India over live video, and a Munich afternoon falls in the Indian evening, so the timing suits both sides. The same mentor stays with your child week after week and learns whether they prefer a challenge, a hint or a little quiet before they answer.

## How many summer days did Munich have in 2025, and what was the longest run?

Every daily reading from the Deutscher Wetterdienst station München-Stadt is open data that anyone may reuse. With one year of it, a ten year old writes a streak counter: a small program with a big idea inside, a count that falls back to zero.

### The data

The station is number 3379, München-Stadt, at 515 metres. Its metadata file says it has stood at its present site since 8 July 1997. The daily file holds one line per day back to 1954, with the day's highest and lowest temperature, rain, sunshine and snow.

We cut out the 365 days of 2025 and give the child a small file, munich_2025.csv, with the date, the highest temperature and the lowest. All 365 days of 2025 have both readings, so this time nothing needs patching.

### The question

The DWD calls a day a Sommertag, a summer day, when the temperature reaches at least 25 °C. How many summer days did Munich have in 2025? And what was the longest run of them in a row?

Counting the days is easy. The run is the interesting part: the program has to keep a second counter that grows on every warm day and drops back to zero on the first cooler one, and it has to remember the highest value that counter ever reached.

**Version 1: count summer days and the longest run** (Python)
```python
import csv

summer_days = 0
run = 0
longest = 0
with open("munich_2025.csv") as data:
    for day in csv.DictReader(data):
        if float(day["tmax"]) >= 25.0:
            summer_days = summer_days + 1
            run = run + 1
        else:
            run = 0
        if run > longest:
            longest = run
            ended = day["date"]

print("Summer days in 2025:", summer_days)
print("Longest run:", longest, "days, ending", ended)
```
It prints: Summer days in 2025: 60 and Longest run: 19 days, ending 2025-07-06.

Sixty summer days, and a streak of 19 from 18 June to 6 July 2025. That run held the warmest day of the year, 2 July, when the station reached 34.3 °C. Children usually guess that August had the longest run; it had more summer days than July, fifteen against thirteen, but its longest streak stopped at nine.

The second program turns the question upside down. Instead of warm days it looks for frosty nights, nights when the lowest temperature fell below zero, and it also remembers on which date each run started. That is the step most children get wrong first: they record the start when the run ends, not when it begins.

**Version 2: the longest run of frosty nights, with dates** (Python)
```python
import csv

run = 0
longest = 0
start = None
with open("munich_2025.csv") as data:
    for day in csv.DictReader(data):
        if float(day["tmin"]) < 0.0:
            if run == 0:
                start = day["date"]
            run = run + 1
            if run > longest:
                longest = run
                window = (start, day["date"])
        else:
            run = 0

print("Longest run of frosty nights:", longest)
print("From", window[0], "to", window[1])
```
It prints: Longest run of frosty nights: 14 and From 2025-01-10 to 2025-01-23.

**Munich summer days, 2025 against the long-term average (station München-Stadt)**

| Measure | 2025 (our count) | 1991 to 2020 average (DWD) |
|---|---|---|
| Summer days, highest at least 25 °C | 60 | 53.3 |
| Summer days in June | 21 | 11.4 |
| Summer days in July | 13 | 16 |
| Summer days in August | 15 | 15.3 |

Data: Deutscher Wetterdienst, Climate Data Center, daily climate data and the 1991 to 2020 Sommertage averages for station 3379 München-Stadt, licence CC BY 4.0, downloaded 3 November 2026. Counts for 2025 are our own, from the daily maximum temperatures. The DWD defines a Sommertag as a day whose maximum air temperature is at least 25 °C.

1. **Ages 6 to 8, no screen.** Colour a paper calendar for two weeks: red for warm days, blue for the rest. Then count the longest row of red squares with your finger, starting again at each blue one.
2. **Ages 8 to 10, Scratch.** Keep two variables, a total and a streak. Make the cat say the streak each day, and set it back to 0 when a cool day comes.
3. **From about 10, typed code.** Run Version 1, then change it so it also prints the date on which the longest run began. Compare with Version 2 only after you have tried.
4. **Going further.** June 2025 had nearly twice the long-term number of summer days, July fewer. Use the whole file back to 1954 to find the year with the longest summer streak, and print the top three.

### A free puzzle contest right after the break

Straight after the Herbstferien, the Biberwochen run from 9 to 20 November 2026. The Informatik-Biber is a free online quiz of logic puzzles; its organisers, Bundesweite Informatikwettbewerbe, open it to pupils from grade 3 up to grade 13 and say nobody needs to have coded before. Over 580,000 children and teenagers joined in 2025.

Entry is through the school, so ask your child's teacher. Results follow in December 2026 and certificates in January 2027.

### How we use the calendar

Long breaks are a good moment for a short project: the summer weeks from 2 August 2027 suit a two-week Scratch game or the weather project above. Before the Übertritt in May of Klasse 4, many families slow coding down and keep the hour light.

We never use real Biber tasks in class. We write puzzles of our own in the same spirit, about order, rules and shortest paths.

## Coding classes for kids in Munich: common questions

### Are the classes in German?

No. All teaching is live and in English only; there is no German-language option. Mentors slow down, rephrase and adapt to each child's pace and accent, which is why children with only a little English manage well. The keywords of code are the same whatever language the lesson is in.

### At what age can my child start?

Six is a good age to begin. Younger children work in Scratch, where blocks snap together and almost nothing has to be typed, in a batch of children their own age. Around 9, most are ready for the typed Python in Python and AI for Kids.

### When do Bavarian schools start teaching Informatik?

It depends on the school type after Klasse 4. The Gesellschaft für Informatik lists one compulsory lesson a week from grade 5 at the Mittelschule, Informationstechnologie from grade 5 at the Realschule, and at the Gymnasium an Informatik focus within Natur und Technik in grades 6 and 7.

### Will coding classes help with the Übertritt in Klasse 4?

Not directly, and we do not claim they do. The Übertrittszeugnis rests on the marks in German, maths and Heimat- und Sachunterricht. Coding does train careful step-by-step thinking, which many children then use in maths, but the school's marks are what count.

### What time are classes in Munich?

Weekdays or weekends, flexible: choose your batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at your convenience, weekend mornings or weekdays. Most Munich children take a slot between about 14:45 and 18:00, or on a Saturday morning.

### Do classes stop during the Bavarian school holidays?

Not unless you ask. Classes continue through the Herbstferien, Christmas and the other breaks; many families pause for a trip and pick up again afterwards. The KMK table lists the summer holidays 2027 as 2 August to 13 September 2027.

### Where are the mentors?

In India, teaching over live video. Munich afternoons sit in the Indian evening: a 16:15 class in Munich starts at 20:45 India time until 28 March 2027. Your child keeps one mentor from week to week.

### How large are the groups?

A batch holds fifteen to twenty children close in age. Families who want the mentor's full attention choose one to one instead: two private lessons each week, at hours agreed with you.

### What does it cost, and in which currency?

You pay monthly. This page shows the fee in euro using the European Central Bank's reference rate, while the card is charged in US dollars and your bank converts it, so the final figure on your statement can move a little. Trying a first class costs nothing and needs no card.

### Does my child need any equipment?

A laptop or desktop computer with a keyboard, a webcam and a stable internet connection. Scratch runs in the browser, and we help you set up Python in the first typed lessons. A tablet alone is not enough for typed code.
