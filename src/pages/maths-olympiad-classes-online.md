Q2. How do we choose?

## Four questions that pick the contest

Families usually ask "which olympiad is best." The useful questions are narrower: how old is the child, where do you live, what do you want from the contest, and can your child write a proof yet.

By age

### From 6 to 18, the ladder is real

Ages 6 to 10: Math Kangaroo (grades 1 up), the SOF IMO (Class 1 up), the UKMT Primary Challenge, the AMT's primary divisions. Ages 11 to 13: AMC 8, MATHCOUNTS, Gauss, the Junior Challenge, IOQM from Class 8. Ages 14 to 18: AMC 10 and 12 and the AIME, Pascal to Euclid, the Intermediate and Senior Challenges, RMO and INMO, the British Olympiad. Start one rung below where you think your child is.

By where you live

### The entry route decides more than the maths

In India the SOF exam runs through the school and the IOQM pathway can be entered individually. In the US a parent can enter Math Kangaroo and, in some cases, MATHCOUNTS, while the AMC needs a school or centre. Outside the US and Canada the AMC needs an International Group Leader. CEMC and UKMT run through schools everywhere. Australian and Gulf families on international schools sit whatever the school orders.

By what you want

### A certificate, a ranking, or a pathway

For a first taste and a certificate: Kangaroo, SOF IMO, the Gauss contest, the Junior Challenge. For a national ranking: AMC 8, MATHCOUNTS, the UKMT Challenges, IOQM. For the road to a national team: IOQM to INMO in India, AMC 10 and 12 to AIME to USAMO in the US, the Senior Challenge to BMO in the UK. A student can sit the first kind as milestones on the way to the third.

By the kind of paper

### Answers, or arguments

Most contests on the list want an answer, quickly, and reward elimination and pace. RMO, INMO, the British Olympiads and the Waterloo full-solution papers want a written argument a marker credits, and that skill takes months to build. A child who tops the answer contests is usually ready to start proof writing at 12 or 13, not to sit a proof paper the following month.

Whichever contest you choose, the preparation has the same shape: the child's level first, then the question families the paper reuses, then timed practice on the organiser's own past papers. Students who also code often add [Maths Through Coding](/courses/maths-through-coding), and Class 8 to 10 students in India usually pair olympiad work with the [JEE foundation course](/courses/jee-foundation-maths-course-class-8-10).

Q3. Why "answer" and "proof" are two different preparations

## The same fact, asked by two contests

A question about factorials: how many zeros end the number 25 factorial? On an answer paper it is a number; on a proof paper it is an argument. Both students know the fact. Only one earns marks on each paper.

***The answer paper**AMC, Kangaroo, SOF, IOQM*

```
How many zeros does 25! end in?

Zeros come from factors of 10 = 2 x 5,
and there are far more 2s than 5s,
so count the 5s:
5, 10, 15, 20, 25 give one each: 5
25 gives a second: 1
Total 6. Bubble in 6.

# Twenty seconds. The paper asks
# nothing more, and that is the
# whole of the answer-paper skill.
```

Right, fast, and complete for AMC 8, Kangaroo or IOQM. On RMO or a Waterloo full-solution paper, this page would earn a fraction of the marks, because nothing here is shown.

***The proof paper**RMO, BMO, Fryer, Galois, Hypatia*

```
Prove that 25! ends in exactly 6 zeros.

The number of trailing zeros of n! is
the exponent of 10 in n!, which is
min(e2, e5) where e2 and e5 are the
exponents of 2 and 5. Since every
multiple of 5 up to 25 is preceded by
at least one even number, e2 > e5,
so the answer is e5.
e5 = floor(25/5) + floor(25/25) = 5 + 1 = 6.
Hence 25! ends in exactly 6 zeros.
```

Same fact, now with the reason e2 exceeds e5 stated, the formula justified, and the conclusion drawn. That paragraph is a different skill, and it is why the proof contests have their own preparation on this site.

The chooser table's "Paper" column tells you which kind your child faces. If it says proof, budget months, not weeks, and start with the answer contests as milestones.

## About choosing a maths olympiad, and the classes

### Which maths olympiad is best for my child?

The one your child can actually be entered for, at their age, in your country, with a paper that matches what they can do now. For most children under 11 that is Math Kangaroo, the SOF IMO or a primary challenge; for 11 to 13 it is AMC 8, MATHCOUNTS, Gauss, the Junior Challenge or IOQM; for 14 and up the AMC 10 and 12, the Waterloo senior papers, the Intermediate and Senior Challenges and the proof olympiads. The chooser table on this page gives the entry route and next date for each, and the mentor settles it with you on the call.

### Is the SOF IMO the same as the International Mathematical Olympiad?

No. The SOF IMO is a school-based multiple-choice exam for Classes 1 to 12 run by a private foundation, sat on a date the school chooses. The International Mathematical Olympiad is the contest between national teams, reached in India through IOQM, RMO and INMO. They share three letters and nothing else, and each has its own page on this site.

### Can a parent enter a child directly?

For three contests on our list, yes: IOQM in India, through its online portal; Math Kangaroo USA, at a public center or virtually; and MATHCOUNTS, as a non-school competitor when the school will not register. Everything else runs through a school, a registered centre or an invitation, including the AMC, CEMC, UKMT, SOF and the Australian Maths Trust. We prepare students for the paper; we do not enter them for it.

### How long does preparation take?

For an answer paper such as Kangaroo, AMC 8 or the UKMT Challenges, ten to twelve weeks of two classes a week takes a strong school student to a prepared one. For a proof paper such as RMO, the British Olympiads or the Waterloo full-solution contests, budget months, because writing an argument is a slower skill than finding an answer. The plan on this page counts back from the date your child is entered for.

### We live in the Gulf, Singapore or another country. Which contests are open to us?

Whatever your school orders: many CBSE and international schools in the Gulf run the SOF olympiads, CEMC contests and the UKMT Challenges. The AMC is available outside the US and Canada only through a regional International Group Leader. IOQM requires travelling to a centre in India. Our batches run in your evening hours and the fee is shown in your currency; ask your school what it enters students for, then ask us on the call.

### What is the difference between an answer paper and a proof paper?

An answer paper wants a number or a letter, quickly, and rewards elimination and pace; AMC, Kangaroo, SOF, MATHCOUNTS, the UKMT Challenges and IOQM are answer papers. A proof paper wants a written argument that a marker credits; RMO, INMO, the British Olympiads and the Waterloo full-solution contests are proof papers. A student who is excellent at the first is usually ready to start the second at 12 or 13, not to sit it the following month.

### Do you use the organisers' past papers?

Yes. Every organiser on this list publishes past papers, and we use them for placement, weekly practice and full rehearsals, then send families to the organiser's own archive rather than reproducing papers here. Every paper a student sits is reviewed question by question in the next class.

### At what age should a child start?

Six, on our elementary maths course and, from grade 1, Math Kangaroo or a primary challenge. Contest work before a child is comfortable with the school maths of their grade does more harm than good, so the base comes first and the contest layer sits on it. Proof writing usually starts at 12 or 13.

### What does a contest lead to?

Certificates and rankings for most; a qualification for the next round in the ladders, IOQM to RMO to INMO, AMC 10 and 12 to AIME to USAMO, the Senior Challenge to the British Olympiad; and, on the way, better mathematics. IARCS and the MAA publish university recognition for their top students, and we quote it on the relevant pages rather than promising it.
