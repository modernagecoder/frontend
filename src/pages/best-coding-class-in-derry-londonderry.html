The settlement NISRA calls Derry City had 85,279 usual residents at the 2021 census, in the council area of Derry City and Strabane. The Historic Environment Division lists the Guildhall on Shipquay Place at Grade A, and the town walls are a scheduled monument. Anyone here from six to sixty-seven can join our lessons, which a teacher in India leads live on a video call, one to one or with a group of five to ten working at one level, fitted around school and work in Northern Ireland. You pay nothing for the first session; staying on costs USD 100 a month in a group or USD 150 a month for private teaching.

Trains leave Derry~Londonderry for Coleraine about once an hour, and trains from Coleraine come the other way. Somewhere, each pair must pass. Our project finds where, using a time-distance chart: time along one axis, distance along the other, every train a sloping line. Where an eastbound line crosses a westbound one, two trains meet. A first, simple model, with one time per station, says the meetings happen out on the line between Bellarena and Castlerock. Add the minutes each train waits at a station, from the same timetable, and all 15 of Wednesday's meetings move to one place: the platform at Bellarena.

Signature project

Where do the trains pass?

On a time-distance chart, each train is a line that climbs as it travels and stays flat while it waits. Two trains going opposite ways meet where their lines cross. Finding those crossings is a problem of intersecting line segments, one pair at a time.

1. Rebuild each journey

From the timetable's patterns, work out when each train arrives at and leaves Derry~Londonderry, Bellarena, Castlerock and Coleraine. A typical eastbound train leaves at 11:38 and waits at Bellarena from 11:58 to 12:00.

2. Test every pair

For each eastbound and westbound pair, check first whether both are at the same station at overlapping times; if not, check whether their trips over the same stretch overlap in time.

3. Refine the model

The first version gave every train one time per station and put the meetings on the line. Using separate arrival and departure times moves all of them to Bellarena.

A small detail, a different answer

The two models disagree by only a minute or two, yet they tell a different story: one says trains pass in open country, the other that they meet, every hour, at one station. The waiting time at Bellarena, two minutes in the timetable, is what makes the meeting happen there. On Sunday, when trains leave Derry~Londonderry every two hours, the pattern is different again and no two trains meet on this stretch at all.

We use Translink's own coordinates to show that the stations are not evenly spaced: in a straight line, Derry~Londonderry to Bellarena is 27.56 km and Bellarena to Castlerock 11.40 km, so Bellarena sits much nearer Castlerock than Derry~Londonderry. The timetable says nothing about the track itself, so the page makes no claim about why the trains meet where they do; it reports only what the times show.

Coding and AI

AI can plan a timetable. Why should a teenager in Derry~Londonderry learn to check one?

Because a model that leaves out a two-minute wait can put a meeting in the wrong place, and it will still look perfectly reasonable.

Scheduling tools, including AI ones, work from models of time and space. If a model drops a detail such as waiting time, its output can be confidently wrong. A learner who has watched every meeting jump from open line to a station platform knows to ask what a model left out.

The project also practises saying what data cannot tell you. The timetable shows where trains meet; it does not show the track. Separating what the evidence supports from what it does not is exactly the judgement AI leaves to people.

So a teenager in Derry~Londonderry should keep learning to code in 2026, in a city whose town walls are a scheduled monument: AI can draft the plan, but people still have to check where things really meet. The longer argument is in why coding is worth learning in 2026.

What is a time-distance chart?

A chart with time along one axis and distance along the other, on which each train is a line. Where lines for trains going opposite ways cross, the trains meet. Finding those crossings is a problem of intersecting line segments.

Where do trains pass on the line to Coleraine?
