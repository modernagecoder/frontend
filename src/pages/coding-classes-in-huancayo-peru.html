Huancayo in figures

How many people live in Huancayo?

INEI counted 456,250 people in the city of Huancayo at the 2017 census, which made it the sixth city of Peru. The newer 2025 census reports departments, provinces and districts, but not yet cities, so each figure below keeps its own unit and year.

A city spread across districts

What people call Huancayo reaches well beyond the district of that name: El Tambo and Chilca hold a large share of the city, and towns such as Concepción, Chupaca and Jauja are a short drive up or down the valley. For a live online class none of that matters. A learner in El Tambo and one in Jauja log in at the same moment.

That is the practical case for online lessons in a valley city. Nobody waits for a combi after school, and an evening class does not end with a cold walk home at 3,000 metres.

How Huancayo scores in English

In the 2025 edition of EF's English index, Huancayo city scores 499, inside the low band. Lima reaches 555, in the high band, and Peru overall sits at 519, 52nd among 123 countries.

So in Huancayo English is usually a skill still being built. Our mentors plan for that: they speak slowly, check understanding often, and the free first class tests comfort in English as carefully as interest in code.

What school covers

Do schools in Huancayo teach programming?

Not as a subject. Junín schools work to MINEDU's Currículo Nacional (CNEB), whose secundaria timetable holds no informatics area; digital ability is meant to grow inside the other subjects.

A competency, not a lesson

The CNEB's ICT competency, number 28, about getting on in virtual environments, is listed as transversal: every area shares it and no area owns it. Among its four capacities are handling information found online and making digital objects, yet no standard mentions a programming language.

A teacher in Huancayo may well use a spreadsheet in maths or a slide deck in history. Nothing in the plan requires anyone to show a class how a loop works.

Where the week goes

On the regular school day, every secundaria grade has 4 hours of Matemática, 4 of Comunicación and 3 of Inglés a week, out of 35. Educación para el Trabajo has 2 hours. There is no slot for informatics or coding.

MINEDU's Fábrica de Programadores began in July 2025 with a first cohort of 1,000 public-school pupils from the two final secundaria years in Lima Norte, learning Python towards the PCEP exam. It is a pilot in the capital. A teenager in Junín who wants that path finds it outside school.

Coding beside CEPRE

Can a student preparing for university in Huancayo also learn to code?

Yes, and many should, as long as the hours are honest. A pre-university cycle is demanding; a coding class has to fit around it rather than compete with it.

What we do and do not offer

Admission exams in Peru are set and marked in Spanish by each university, and the university alone decides who gets in. We do not prepare students for any admission exam and we are not connected with the UNCP, its CEPRE or any other university.

What we teach is useful on either side of that exam: careful reasoning in maths, and programming in Python or Java, taught in English. A student who wants Ingeniería de Sistemas, for example, arrives at the first lecture already able to write and test a program.

How families fit it in

The simplest pattern is one class a week during the CEPRE months, then two once the cycle ends. One-to-one classes can move week by week around mock exams, which a fixed group cannot.

Students who finish secundaria in December and start a cycle in March sometimes use the summer break between the two for an intensive start, then keep a lighter slot.

  1. Count the real free hours. Write down a normal CEPRE week, including travel and homework, before choosing a plan.
  2. Pick one language. Python first for almost everyone; Java if the student is sure about a software degree.
  3. Keep the class short and regular. One live hour each week beats a long Saturday that gets cancelled before every exam.
  4. Build something that needs the maths. A program that checks a sequence or graphs a function revises algebra without feeling like revision.
  5. Review after the exam. Once admission results are known, decide together whether to speed up, pause or change track.

Taught in English

Are the classes in Spanish?

No. Each lesson is given live, over video, and only in English. We say so here, again in the free class, and on every page, so nobody discovers it late.

English at the learner's pace

Our mentors work from India over a video call. They slow down, rephrase an instruction in simpler words, and wait for an answer. A child who replies half in Spanish is understood and handed the English word without fuss.

Programming helps here. Python keywords are short English words such as if, for and while, so after a month a learner already reads a good part of every program on the screen.

English as a second gain

Huancayo families often see the English as a bonus: every coding hour doubles as practice in listening and explaining in English, one to one with a patient adult or inside a small conversation of learners.

Teenagers heading to university gain something extra. Error messages, documentation and most programming forums are in English, and they will have practised reading them long before their first course.

A real Huancayo project

Did Huancayo really sign up for CEPRE at two in the morning?

The Universidad Nacional del Centro del Perú publishes the list of people who registered for its 2024-II pre-university cycle on the national open-data portal. Older students learn from it a quiet trap in real data: a time written without a time zone.

The file

The dataset is the "Listado de inscritos en CEPRE" of the UNCP, released through the Plataforma Nacional de Datos Abiertos with an ODC-By licence (Open Data Commons Attribution). The file we downloaded, cepre-uncp-2024-II.csv, has 2,601 registrations with a cut-off date of 29 May 2024. Identity numbers are anonymised by the publisher, and we only ever print totals.

Each row has a date of registration, written like 20240401, and a time, written like 205848. The data dictionary says the time column is "Formato: hhmmss". It does not say which clock.

The question

At what hour of the day did people register? A student expects late afternoon and evening, when a secundaria leaver and a parent sit together at a computer, and almost nothing in the small hours.

Version 1 takes the first two characters of each time as the hour, counts registrations per hour and prints the busiest hour and two night and morning totals.

Read literally, 278 people registered between midnight and four in the morning, while across five whole morning hours, from six to eleven, only 4 did. A university office open in the morning with nobody signing up, and a crowd at two at night, is a strange picture of Huancayo.

There is a simpler explanation. Many web servers record time in UTC, and Peru's clock sits five hours behind UTC throughout the year. If the file's clock is UTC, every registration happened five hours earlier, in Huancayo time, than the file says. That is our hypothesis, not a fact: the file does not state its time zone. So we test it.

Data: the UNCP's Listado de inscritos en CEPRE 2024-II, from the national open-data platform, ODC-By. Every count here is ours, run with python3 -I on the downloaded file. Registrations run from 22 March 2024 to 17 May 2024; the busiest date in the file is 1 April 2024, with 222.

After the shift the day looks like a day: almost nobody between midnight and five, a climb from eight, a peak at eleven in the morning and a long tail into the evening. Two details matter. First, joining the date and time before subtracting is essential, because 297 registrations written just after midnight move back to the previous date. Second, the shift proves nothing on its own. A good analyst writes "the pattern fits a UTC clock" and asks the publisher, rather than quietly rewriting 2,601 rows.

  1. Under 9, on paper. If it is 4 in the afternoon in Huancayo, what time is it in London, five hours ahead? What happens to the date if it is 9 at night?
  2. Scratch, about 8 to 11. Make a sprite that says the time in Huancayo and in India. Add 10 hours 30 minutes, and handle the jump past midnight.
  3. First Python, 10 to 13. Run Version 1 on the table above by hand: which hours look wrong, and why?
  4. Secundaria. Write Version 2 yourself, then try shifts of 4 and 6 hours. Which shift leaves the fewest registrations between midnight and five?
  5. Going further. The file also has 37 careers. Medicina Humana leads with 459 registrations, Ingeniería de Sistemas has 135. Chart the top ten, then decide whether the hour of registration differs by career.

Computing contest

Is there a programming olympiad a Huancayo student can enter?

Yes: the Olimpiadas Peruanas de Computación, organised by UTEC. We are not connected with the organiser; we describe it from its own pages.

UTEC runs the OPI at no cost to any secundaria pupil in the country. The 2026 edition stopped taking sign-ups on 18 March 2026 and held its final on 11 April 2026. The 2025 rulebook let first-round entrants write Python or C++, kept the in-person final to C++ alone, and forbade generative AI. A Huancayo student who starts Python now and moves to C++ later has time to prepare properly for a future edition, with problems we write ourselves rather than the organiser's.

A practice problem of ours. A clock in a file is 5 hours ahead of Huancayo. A registration is written as 20240401 and 031500. On what date and at what time did it happen in Huancayo? (Ours. Answer: 31 March 2024 at 22:15, because subtracting five hours crosses midnight and the month.)

Class times

What time are classes in Huancayo?

Whatever time you choose. Huancayo keeps Peru time, the same clock as Lima, and your mentor plans around it.

Clock facts: Peru runs on UTC minus five with no daylight saving (tz zone America/Lima; INACAL publishes the legal time) and India on UTC plus five and a half, so the gap is a fixed 10 hours 30 minutes.

With no clock changes on either side, a class time agreed in April still holds in November. At booking you pick times in your own clock and the system shows the mentor the India equivalent; this is the same kind of conversion the CEPRE project above asks students to reason about. MINEDU opened the 2026 school year on 16 March 2026, with 6,225,000 pupils returning to 53,631 schools; our classes carry on across the school breaks unless you ask for a pause.

Coding classes in Huancayo: questions and answers

Are the classes in Spanish?

No. Lessons happen live on video and only in English. Mentors keep their speech slow and plain, follow each learner's speed and accent, and when a reply comes in Spanish they supply the English words.

Can children in Huancayo take coding classes?

Yes, live online from home. Children in Huancayo, El Tambo, Chilca or anywhere in Junín start Scratch at 6 and typed Python at about 9, in groups of their own age.

Can my child learn coding while doing CEPRE?

Yes, if the hours are planned honestly. Many families choose one short class a week during the cycle and more afterwards. We do not prepare anyone for an admission exam, which is set in Spanish by each university, and we are not connected with the UNCP or its CEPRE.

At what age can someone start?

From 6 with Scratch, from about 9 with typed Python, from 13 with Python or Java at teenage level, and at any adult age. The youngest we teach are 6, the oldest 67.

Do schools in Huancayo teach coding?

Not as a subject. The national curriculum spreads digital skills across all areas, the regular 35-hour secundaria timetable includes no informatics, and MINEDU's only Python pilot so far runs in Lima Norte.

What time are the classes?

Any day, any time, flexible: choose a batch, or for a very personalised plan choose 1-on-1 at the hour that suits your family. Huancayo uses Peru time all year and the mentor in India is ten and a half hours ahead: 08:00 in Huancayo is 18:30 there.

Is my English good enough?

Usually yes. The free first class checks it gently. Mentors simplify their language for beginners, and code itself uses only a small set of English words.

Can adults in Huancayo join?

Yes. Students over 18 and people in work learn in adult groups or one to one, usually starting with Python and moving to data analysis or data structures.

How much does it cost?

It is a monthly fee, quoted here in US dollars and billed to your card in that currency, with your bank converting it to soles. Visitors browsing from India are shown rupees. The trial class costs nothing.

Do you give a certificate?

Where a course includes a certificate, it is ours and records the work completed. No Peruvian school, university or MINEDU issues it, and it is not a Peruvian qualification.
